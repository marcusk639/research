### Why each column is there

- **Inv. item no.** — A-002 requires the claim be "limited to the destination descriptions of damages
  for each item **on the inventory logs**." A-006 numbers everything 1–85. Anchoring to those numbers
  ties each damaged item to the inventory the carrier's own foreman signed at origin.
- **Est. weight** — this is what determines recovery. Option 2 on A-005 was initialled "M. K.",
  electing settlement at **the individual article's weight × $0.60**. A 200 lb piano computes to
  $120 under that election regardless of what it cost. Weigh what you can; estimate the rest and mark
  estimates as estimates. A-002 separately requires proof of "value, **and weight**."
- **Value** — required by A-002 ("proof of ownership, value, and weight") and needed to show the gap
  between the 60¢ figure and actual loss. Attach receipts, order confirmations or listings where you
  have them.
- **Damage description** — be specific and physical: "left rear leg snapped at the joint," not
  "damaged." Vague entries are the easiest to refuse.
- **Photos** — name files so they map to the row, e.g. `item-63-piano-01.jpg`. Include at least one
  wide shot showing the whole item and one close-up of each distinct point of damage.
- **Noted at delivery?** — A-002 states "**All damages and missing items must be noted on the
  inventory logs.**" Record honestly whether each item was noted, including where it was not.

## Before you throw anything away

A-002 states: _"Carrier, or its representative, **has the right to inspect and repair any alleged
damage**."_ Do not discard, repair, or replace a damaged item, and keep the packing materials and
debris, until that inspection right has been addressed in writing. Disposing of an item first is one
of the few things that can defeat an otherwise good claim outright.
