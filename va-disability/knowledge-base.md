## 1. How VA Assigns Ratings

VA bases each rating on: the evidence you submit (doctor's reports, test results),
the results of the VA claim exam (C&P exam), and information from other federal
agencies. A rating is expressed as a percentage representing how much the disability
decreases overall health and ability to function.
[Source: va.gov — About disability ratings, https://www.va.gov/disability/about-disability-ratings/]

**Volatility: stable.**

## 2. Combined Ratings & "VA Math" (38 CFR 4.25)

VA does **not** add ratings. It uses the "whole person theory" — a person can't be
more than 100% able-bodied. Example: two disabilities each rated 10% combine to 19%,
not 20%.
[Source: va.gov — About disability ratings]

Process:

1. Rank ratings highest → lowest.
2. Look up values in the combined ratings table (left column = highest, top row = next).
3. Find the intersection.
4. Round to nearest 10% (1–4 down, 5–9 up).
5. Repeat using the **unrounded** combined value against the next rating, progressively.
   [Source: va.gov — About disability ratings]

- **Bilateral factor:** applies when disabilities affect paired extremities (both
  arms, both legs); VA adds an additional factor before rounding. _(General rule per
  38 CFR 4.26; exact computation `UNVERIFIED` to primary text — eCFR fetch was
  bot-blocked; verify at 38 CFR 4.25/4.26 before relying on a precise figure.)_

## 4. Claims Process End-to-End

**Filing methods:** online (fastest; sets effective date automatically), mail
(VA Form 21-526EZ to the Janesville, WI intake center), in person, fax, or via an
accredited representative.
[Source: va.gov — How to file a claim, https://www.va.gov/disability/how-to-file-claim/]

**Claim types:** standard; Fully Developed Claim (FDC — all evidence submitted
upfront); supplemental and secondary claims.
[Source: same]

**Evidence:** VA and private medical records, lay/"buddy" supporting statements, DD214
and service treatment records. Evidence is not strictly required to file but speeds
decisions. Claimants have a **one-year window** to submit additional evidence.
[Source: same]

**Effective date / Intent to File:** filing online sets the effective date
automatically; submitting an Intent to File first can establish an earlier effective
date and enable retroactive payments.
[Source: same]

**Average processing:** ~72.3 days (as stated on the page; `volatility: volatile` —
this figure drifts; treat as indicative). [Source: same]

**Volatility: stable (process); the processing-time number is volatile.**

## 5. Decision Reviews / Appeals

Three lanes after a decision:

- **Supplemental Claim** — submit **new and relevant** evidence not previously
  considered.
- **Higher-Level Review** — a more senior reviewer re-examines; **no new evidence**
  allowed.
- **Board Appeal** — appeal to the independent Board of Veterans' Appeals.
  [Source: va.gov — Decision reviews and appeals, https://www.va.gov/decision-reviews/]

- **Deadline:** the standard window to choose a review option is generally **one year**
  from the decision date. _(`UNVERIFIED` to the exact page text — the fetched excerpt
  omitted the deadline; confirm on va.gov/decision-reviews before relying.)_

## 6. TDIU — Total Disability based on Individual Unemployability

Pays at the **100% rate** even when the schedular combined rating is below 100%, when
service-connected disability prevents **substantially gainful employment** (marginal
"odd jobs" don't count). The rating itself is unchanged; only the payment amount rises.
[Source: va.gov — Individual Unemployability, https://www.va.gov/disability/eligibility/special-claims/unemployability/]

**Schedular thresholds (one of):**

- One service-connected condition rated **≥ 60%**, OR
- Two+ conditions with **at least one ≥ 40%** and a **combined ≥ 70%**.
  Lower ratings may still qualify in special cases (e.g. frequent hospitalization).
  [Source: same]

**Forms:** VA Form 21-8940 (veteran) + VA Form 21-4192 (employer). [Source: same]

**Volatility: stable.**

## 7. SMC — Special Monthly Compensation

Higher-tier payments **above** the standard 100% rate for especially severe
conditions or care needs.
[Source: va.gov — SMC rates, https://www.va.gov/disability/compensation-rates/special-monthly-compensation-rates/]

- **SMC-K** (+$139.87/mo, 2025 rate) — adds on top of basic/other SMC for specific
  losses (e.g. loss/loss of use of a creative organ, one hand/foot, etc.).
- **SMC-Q** ($67.00/mo) — protected historical rate, no longer awarded.
- **SMC-L through SMC-S** — increasing severity (e.g. SMC-L veteran alone $4,900.83/mo;
  SMC-R.2 up to $11,271.67 with spouse, 2025 rates).
- **Qualifying categories:** limb amputation / loss of use, blindness or eye loss,
  **Aid & Attendance** (needs daily help with eating, dressing, bathing), **Housebound**,
  and bedridden status.
  [Source: same]

**Volatility: ANNUAL (dollar levels) + stable (categories).**

## 8. Presumptive Service Connection & the PACT Act

A **presumptive** condition is one VA assumes was caused by service — the veteran need
only meet the service/exposure requirements, not prove causation.
[Source: va.gov — PACT Act, https://www.va.gov/resources/the-pact-act-and-your-va-benefits/]

PACT Act ("largest health care and benefit expansion in VA history") added 20+
presumptive conditions and expanded eligibility:

- **Burn pit / Gulf War & post-9/11 cancers:** brain, GI, glioblastoma, head, kidney,
  lymphoma, melanoma, neck, pancreatic, reproductive, respiratory.
- **Respiratory/systemic:** asthma (post-service onset), chronic bronchitis, COPD,
  emphysema, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis, sarcoidosis, granulomatous
  disease.
- **Agent Orange additions (Vietnam):** hypertension, MGUS.
- **Exposure locations/dates:** post-9/11 (from 2001-09-11) — Afghanistan, Djibouti,
  Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Uzbekistan, Yemen; Gulf War era (from 1990-08-02) —
  Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, UAE; Vietnam-era Agent
  Orange (1962-01-09 – 1975-05-07) — Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Guam, American
  Samoa, Johnston Atoll; new radiation response efforts (Enewetak, Palomares, Thule).
  [Source: same]

**Volatility: VOLATILE — presumptive lists change with regulation/legislation;
re-verify the current presumptive list at strategy time.**

## 9. Secondary Service Connection

A **secondary** condition is one caused or aggravated by an already service-connected
condition (e.g. depression secondary to chronic pain; sleep apnea secondary to a
service-connected condition). Establishing the medical **nexus** between the primary
and secondary condition is the key to these claims.

## 10. Evidence Types That Move Outcomes

- **C&P (Compensation & Pension) exam** — VA-ordered exam; a major input to the rating.
  [Source: va.gov — About disability ratings]
- **Nexus letter / private medical opinion** — links the condition to service or to a
  primary condition.
- **DBQ (Disability Benefits Questionnaire)** — structured clinical form mapping
  symptoms to rating criteria.
- **Lay / buddy statements** — first-hand accounts of onset and progression.
  [Source: va.gov — How to file a claim, evidence section]

**Volatility: stable.**

## 11. Representation & Fee Law

- **VSO representatives** (DAV, VFW, American Legion, state veterans affairs offices):
  accredited and **always free** for VA benefit claims.
- **VA-accredited attorneys** and **accredited claims agents:** may **charge fees**.
  [Source: va.gov — Get help filing a claim, https://www.va.gov/disability/get-help-filing-claim/]

- **Fee timing (38 CFR 14.636):** fees may generally only be charged **after** VA
  issues an initial decision and the veteran enters a decision review — i.e. **no fees
  for initial claim preparation**. Fee amounts are regulated; figures commonly cited as
  presumed reasonable (≤ 20% of past-due benefits) and presumed unreasonable (> 33⅓%)

  exact percentages and conditions at 38 CFR 14.636 before advising on fees.**
