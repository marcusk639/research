# Pre-Service vs. Post-Service Severity Contrast

> **Status:** active · **Created:** 2026-07-05
> **Purpose:** an honest, affirmative framing of the pre-service-vs-post-service distinction, for use in your personal statement and to inform Cherie's nexus letter — built from the complete Starlite record, not a curated subset of it.
> **Why this exists:** see `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §1.4. The record shows pre-service experimental substance use that never rose to a diagnosis, followed by post-service escalation to a severe, formally diagnosed disorder. That's a legitimate, persuasive contrast on its own — it doesn't require omitting anything, and it holds up if VA independently obtains the full Starlite file. **Verified 2026-07-06** (`citation-verifier` pass against 38 CFR § 3.159(c)): this isn't literally independent of you — VA's duty to seek "relevant records" from a private facility like Starlite is triggered by you identifying the custodian and signing a release (VA Form 21-4142/21-4142a), not something VA does unprompted. Once that release is signed, though, facilities typically disclose the full episode record rather than redacting for relevance, so a complete authorization does tend to produce the complete file in practice — which is why the underlying advice below still holds.

## The honest version of the argument

**Pre-service:** experimental substance use (kratom, beginning around age 18) — never diagnosed as a disorder, never treated, and by your own account not something that affected your functioning at the time. This is consistent with the record: nothing in your service treatment history, your 2015 VA intake, or any pre-2016 document shows a substance use _diagnosis_ — the earliest formal diagnosis (Opioid Use Disorder) doesn't appear until years after separation.

**NEW (2026-07-06, per the veteran):** there was also a separate pre-service episode where a medication was prescribed for an unrelated reason, which you state you didn't know was an opioid until told so later, during treatment. Per you, this caused zero SUD or associated problems at the time — taken as directed, no misuse. This is a distinct fact from the Kratom experimentation above (not a replacement for it), and it strengthens the same underlying point: no pre-service disorder existed under either account, and the actual disorder only began after discharge, after the 2012 assault. This isn't yet reflected in how the Victory/Starlite records phrase your self-reports, so it's worth saying plainly, in your own words, rather than assuming the records already capture it.

**Post-service:** escalation to opioid use beginning around age 25 (2014, roughly a year after separation), progressing over the following decade to **at least nine distinct treatment episodes** (Acadiana 2016, Acadiana 2017, Victory Addiction Recovery Center 2018, the five Starlite Recovery Center admissions 2022–2025, and Harmony Junction Recovery in 2026) — **corrected 2026-07-06; "five" undercounted this by treating the Starlite series as one entry rather than five separate admissions, and omitted the 2026 Harmony Junction stay entirely** — each formally diagnosing Opioid Use Disorder, and, from 2023 onward, alongside formally diagnosed GAD, MDD, and OCD. Your own repeated statements to treating clinicians across that decade **most often** attribute relapse to psychiatric symptoms and the stress of managing them, not to the earlier experimental use — **but not uniformly: several records also attribute a given relapse to "work stress," "recently laid off," or use "recreational"/"compromised judgment" language rather than a psychiatric-symptom framing (see `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §5) — corrected 2026-07-06, this document previously overstated uniformity here.** That's a real complication for the PTSD-secondary theory specifically (it doesn't undercut MDD/GAD-secondary theories the same way, since work stress and psychiatric symptoms aren't mutually exclusive).

**Why this is a stronger argument than hiding the pre-service history:** the contrast itself is the evidence. A single, brief, non-clinical period of substance experimentation with no diagnosis and no functional consequence, followed by a severe, chronic, repeatedly-hospitalized disorder that only emerged once the psychiatric conditions took hold — that sequence supports the theory that the _current_ disorder is driven by the _current_ psychiatric conditions, not by the earlier experimentation. If VA only saw the favorable page, it would look exactly like what it would be: a hidden fact. Seeing the complete, honest sequence is what actually makes the argument work.

## What NOT to do

Don't submit a version of the Starlite records that includes the trauma-disclosure page but omits the pages showing pre-service use. If VA requests the complete file directly from Starlite (a standard part of the duty-to-assist process under 38 CFR § 3.159(c) — see the header note above on how that request actually gets triggered) and it doesn't match what you submitted, that discrepancy will look like exactly what it would be — and it would damage the credibility of the entire claim, not just the SUD contention. Submit the complete, relevant clinical record, and let the honest contrast above do the work.

**Curation vs. cherry-picking, made explicit:** curating out clinically-irrelevant administrative bulk (medication logs, meal notes, insurance paperwork) is normal and expected — nobody submits 600 pages of that. Omitting a _clinically material fact from within_ the same record set (the trauma-disclosure page while dropping the pre-service-use pages) is the actual risk. Note this distinction isn't a codified VA rule — no regulation or M21-1 section formally defines "material" vs. "administrative bulk" — it's an ordinary evidence-weighing consequence (consistency with other evidence of record, the same factor behind _Caluza v. Brown_'s credibility framework), not a named legal standard. **Also confirmed:** this scenario is a credibility/weight-of-evidence issue, not a fraud issue — VA's forfeiture-for-fraud rule (38 CFR § 3.901) requires a specific, narrow finding through its own separate proceeding, not a mere evidentiary discrepancy. Keep this framed as a credibility risk, as it already is above — don't let any future edit drift toward calling it fraud.
