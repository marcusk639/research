# Pre-Service vs. Post-Service Severity Contrast

> **Status:** active · **Created:** 2026-07-05
> **Purpose:** an honest, affirmative framing of the pre-service-vs-post-service distinction, for use in your personal statement and to inform Cherie's nexus letter — built from the complete Starlite record, not a curated subset of it.
> **Why this exists:** see `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §1.4. The record shows pre-service experimental substance use that never rose to a diagnosis, followed by post-service escalation to a severe, formally diagnosed disorder. That's a legitimate, persuasive contrast on its own — it doesn't require omitting anything, and it holds up if VA independently obtains the full Starlite file (which it can, via a standard records release).

## The honest version of the argument

**Pre-service:** experimental substance use (kratom, beginning around age 18) — never diagnosed as a disorder, never treated, and by your own account not something that affected your functioning at the time. This is consistent with the record: nothing in your service treatment history, your 2015 VA intake, or any pre-2016 document shows a substance use _diagnosis_ — the earliest formal diagnosis (Opioid Use Disorder) doesn't appear until years after separation.

**Post-service:** escalation to opioid use beginning around age 25 (2014, roughly a year after separation), progressing over the following decade to five separate inpatient/residential treatment admissions (2016, 2017, 2018, and the Starlite series 2022–2025), each formally diagnosing Opioid Use Disorder — and, from 2023 onward, alongside formally diagnosed GAD, MDD, and OCD. Your own repeated statements to treating clinicians across that decade attribute the escalation and every relapse to psychiatric symptoms and the stress of managing them — not to the earlier experimental use.

**Why this is a stronger argument than hiding the pre-service history:** the contrast itself is the evidence. A single, brief, non-clinical period of substance experimentation with no diagnosis and no functional consequence, followed by a severe, chronic, repeatedly-hospitalized disorder that only emerged once the psychiatric conditions took hold — that sequence supports the theory that the _current_ disorder is driven by the _current_ psychiatric conditions, not by the earlier experimentation. If VA only saw the favorable page, it would look exactly like what it would be: a hidden fact. Seeing the complete, honest sequence is what actually makes the argument work.

## Suggested language for your personal statement (adapt into your own words — don't copy verbatim)

> "I want to be direct about my full substance use history, including the parts that aren't part of my service-connected claim, because I think the honest picture is more persuasive than a partial one. I experimented with kratom around age 18, before I joined the Air Force. That use never became a problem — I wasn't diagnosed with anything, I never sought treatment, and it didn't affect my life. It was exactly what it sounds like: experimentation, not a disorder.
>
> What happened after my discharge in 2013 was different in kind, not just degree. Starting around age 25, my opioid use escalated to the point where I've now been through five separate inpatient or residential treatment admissions over the past decade, each one diagnosing a severe substance use disorder. Every time I've talked with a treating clinician about why I relapse, the answer has been the same: I use to manage anxiety, depression, and the aftermath of what happened to me in service — not because of anything that happened before I enlisted."

## What this means for Cherie's letter

Cherie can only speak to what falls within her own clinical knowledge and the treatment relationship she has with you (since Feb 2023). If, based on what you've reported to her and her own clinical assessment, she agrees that:

- the current substance use disorder is clinically distinct from — and more severe than — any pre-service use, and
- the current disorder's onset/escalation and relapse pattern track the psychiatric conditions rather than the earlier experimental use,

...then her letter is stronger for saying so plainly, in her own words. If her clinical judgment doesn't support drawing that distinction as confidently, she should say what she actually believes — a modest, accurate statement from her serves the claim better than an inflated one.

## What NOT to do

Don't submit a version of the Starlite records that includes the trauma-disclosure page but omits the pages showing pre-service use. If VA requests the complete file directly from Starlite (a standard part of the duty-to-assist process) and it doesn't match what you submitted, that discrepancy will look like exactly what it would be — and it would damage the credibility of the entire claim, not just the SUD contention. Submit the complete, relevant clinical record, and let the honest contrast above do the work.
