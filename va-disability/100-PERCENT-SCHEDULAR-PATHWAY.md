## 1. The exact 38 CFR 4.130 criteria

All mental disorders (DC 9201–9440) are rated under one General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders. Verbatim text, current as of this research:

**100%:**

> "Total occupational and social impairment, due to such symptoms as: gross impairment in thought processes or communication; persistent delusions or hallucinations; grossly inappropriate behavior; persistent danger of hurting self or others; intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene); disorientation to time or place; memory loss for names of close relatives, own occupation, or own name."

**70%:**

> "Occupational and social impairment, with deficiencies in most areas, such as work, school, family relations, judgment, thinking, or mood, due to such symptoms as: suicidal ideation; obsessional rituals which interfere with routine activities; speech intermittently illogical, obscure, or irrelevant; near-continuous panic or depression affecting the ability to function independently, appropriately and effectively; impaired impulse control (such as unprovoked irritability with periods of violence); spatial disorientation; neglect of personal appearance and hygiene; difficulty in adapting to stressful circumstances (including work or a worklike setting); inability to establish and maintain effective relationships."

The controlling phrase for 100% is **"total occupational AND social impairment"** — both prongs, not just one. Sources: [38 CFR 4.130 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.130); [eCFR 4.130](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRfa64377db09ae97/section-4.130).

## 2. Case law on how "total" impairment is actually assessed

- **Mauerhan v. Principi, 16 Vet. App. 436 (2002).** The "such as" language means the listed symptoms are examples, not an exhaustive checklist. VA is not required to find all, most, or even some of the enumerated symptoms — it must weigh all evidence bearing on occupational/social impairment and assign whichever rating most closely reflects the actual level of impairment.
- **Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki, 713 F.3d 112 (Fed. Cir. 2013).** The counterweight: a veteran qualifies for a given rating by showing the specific listed symptoms _or others of similar severity, frequency, and duration_ — but those symptoms must actually **cause** the specified level of impairment. It's not a symptom head-count.
- **Bankhead v. Shulkin, 29 Vet. App. 10 (2017).** Suicidal ideation — passive or active — is itself a symptom; it doesn't require intent, a plan, or preparatory behavior. The Board erred by demanding "more than thought," because actual danger of self-harm is a 100% criterion, not 70%. Caveat: ideation doesn't automatically grant 70% or 100% — the question is always whether it's severe/frequent enough to produce "deficiencies in most areas" or total impairment.

**Combined doctrinal takeaway:** _Mauerhan_ + _Bankhead_ mean you don't need every 100%-tier example symptom; _Vazquez-Claudio_ means whatever symptoms exist must genuinely produce _total_ impairment. The winning frame is a holistic argument (paired with 38 CFR 4.7's "more nearly approximates" rule) that the overall disability picture reaches total impairment — not a checklist exercise.

## 3. How 100% mental-health ratings are actually granted in practice

This section is evidentiary-tendency, not black-letter law — there's no published numeric threshold; treat it as informed pattern-recognition, not a guarantee.

- **The single most outcome-determinative item is the C&P examiner's own global-impairment selection on the mental disorders DBQ.** A 70% grant typically corresponds to the examiner checking "deficiencies in most areas"; 100% almost always requires the examiner (or a competing private DBQ/IMO) to select "total occupational and social impairment."
- **Both prongs need to be documented** — not just inability to work, but genuine, near-total social isolation. Veterans who are unemployable but maintain some relationships tend to land at 70%, not 100%.
- **Concrete, corroborated severity** (hospitalization, near-continuous symptoms, consistency between the C&P exam and the treatment record) moves toward 100%; divergence between what the examiner finds and what the records show tends to produce 70% plus a remand, not 100%.
- Explicitly invoking the 38 CFR 4.7 "more nearly approximates" standard, tied to _Mauerhan_/_Bankhead_, is the standard legal frame for arguing up from 70% to 100%.

## 4. Comorbid diagnoses under one rating

**Verified rule:** 38 CFR 4.130 assigns one evaluation for overall occupational/social impairment, regardless of how many mental-disorder diagnoses exist. 38 CFR 4.14 (anti-pyramiding) bars rating the same disability or the same manifestation under multiple codes.

- More diagnoses do not automatically produce a higher rating. What matters is the aggregate, **distinguishable** symptom burden and its functional effect.
- **Mittleider v. West, 11 Vet. App. 181 (1998):** when symptoms of a service-connected condition can't be medically separated from symptoms of a non-service-connected (or not-yet-connected) condition, VA must attribute the whole picture to the service-connected disability. This is directly useful for the SUD theory: if an examiner can't cleanly separate substance-use symptoms from the PTSD/GAD/MDD/OCD picture, all of it gets attributed to the service-connected condition. _(Widely cited in BVA decisions and M21-1; exact primary-opinion pin cite is **UNVERIFIED** here, but the holding is standard and reliable.)_
- M21-1, Part V, Subpart iii, Ch. 13 directs examiners to differentiate symptoms by diagnosis where possible, but to evaluate them together under the single formula where they can't be separated.

**Bottom line:** the value of having five diagnoses (PTSD, GAD, MDD, OCD, SUD) is in breadth and severity of _distinguishable_ symptoms feeding one score, plus the Mittleider attribution rule for the SUD — not in the raw diagnosis count.

## 5. OCD specifically

**Verified:** OCD has its own diagnostic code (DC 9404) but is rated under the same General Rating Formula as PTSD (9411), GAD (9400), and MDD (9434) — same 0/10/30/50/70/100 scale, not a separate scale.

- **"Obsessional rituals which interfere with routine activities" is a 70%-tier symptom**, textually, in the regulation itself. Genuine OCD compulsions/rituals that interfere with routine activities are a concrete, favorable anchor at the 70% level.
- Intrusive thoughts and, if severe enough, compulsions that prevent independent functioning can feed toward the 100%-tier framing (gross impairment, inability to perform ADLs) if the severity genuinely supports it.
- This is a regulatory-text-level fact, verified; the practice-pattern observation (BVA decisions citing OCD symptoms this way) is evidentiary-tendency, not a guaranteed outcome.

## 6. Unemployment as evidence, without filing TDIU — the _Rice_ mechanics

This is the key strategic nuance for your stated goal (100% schedular, not TDIU).

**Rice v. Shinseki, 22 Vet. App. 447 (2009):** TDIU is not a freestanding claim — it's part of the rating determination whenever unemployability is reasonably raised by the record during a pending increased-rating claim. VA **must** consider TDIU if the record raises it, whether or not the veteran asks for it.

**What this means practically:**

1. You **can and should** cite unemployment as evidence of "total occupational impairment" — that's literally half of the 100% schedular test. Nothing prevents using it as severity evidence.
2. Raising unemployment in the record **does** trigger VA's duty to consider TDIU — you can't force VA to ignore evidence in the file. You _can_ expressly decline or withdraw a TDIU claim, but that doesn't stop VA from considering the underlying evidence.
3. **Why this is low-risk to your actual goal:** TDIU and schedular 100% both draw on the same occupational-impairment evidence — they aren't a fork you have to choose between at the evidence stage. Worst case, VA grants TDIU as an automatic fallback floor (paying at the 100% rate) while you continue pursuing schedular 100%.
4. **The one real tension:** if VA treats a TDIU grant as fully resolving the "total impairment" question, it may not separately reach schedular 100% (the payment rate is the same). To keep the schedular-100% argument alive, frame the case as **total occupational AND social impairment** under 4.130 — not just "can't work," which is the TDIU frame.
5. There is no verified authority letting a veteran bar VA from _considering_ Rice-raised TDIU while still using the same evidence toward schedular 100% — the defensible strategy is to let unemployment support the "occupational" prong of the schedular argument and not fight TDIU consideration as a fallback, rather than trying to suppress it.

## 7. Combined-rating math to 100% (38 CFR 4.25)

**Method:** disabilities combine by remaining efficiency, not by addition. Rank highest to lowest; each successive rating applies only to the _remaining_ percentage; round to the nearest 10 at the very end. Reaching 100% combined requires a computed value of about 95 or higher (95 rounds up).

**Scenario A — 70% MH anchor + a realistic secondary stack:**
MH 70, sleep apnea 50, ankle 20, tinnitus 10, GERD 10 → combines to **90%**, not 100%. Shows how hard 100% combined is from a 70% MH anchor — it needs roughly 80%+ worth of _other_ conditions.

**Scenario B — a heavier stack that does reach 100% from a 70% anchor:**
MH 70, sleep apnea 50, GERD 30, migraines 30, ankle 20, tinnitus 10, hearing loss 10 → combines to approximately 95%, **rounds to 100%.**

**Scenario C — 90% MH anchor (the efficient path):**
MH 90 + sleep apnea 50 → combines to 95%, **rounds to 100%.** A single 50% secondary on top of a 90% mental-health rating clears 100% — far more efficient than stacking many small conditions from a 70% anchor.

**Takeaway:** if the mental-health rating lands at 90%, pursuing one high-value secondary (sleep apnea, if genuinely secondary to PTSD) is the highest-leverage move. If it lands at 70%, expect to land around 90% combined absent a large stack of additional conditions.

**Bilateral factor (38 CFR 4.26):** applies only to disabilities in _paired_ extremities (both legs, both arms). A single ankle claim gets no bilateral-factor uplift unless the other leg is also compensably service-connected.

## 8. Previously-open regulatory gaps — now verified

- **Attorney/agent fees (38 CFR 14.636):** fees ≤20% of past-due benefits are presumed reasonable (where representation continued through the award decision); fees >33⅓% are presumed unreasonable. Direct VA payment to the attorney from past-due benefits is available only when the fee is contingent and doesn't exceed 20%.
- **Bilateral factor (38 CFR 4.26):** combine the paired ratings normally, then add 10% of that combined value before combining with everything else. A favorable-to-veteran exception exists — if calculating the paired disabilities separately (outside the bilateral rule) yields a higher combined result, that's used instead.
- **One-year deadline (38 CFR 3.2500):** within one year of an AOJ decision notice, a claimant may elect Higher-Level Review or a Board appeal. A Supplemental Claim may be filed at any time, but filing within one year of the decision notice preserves the original effective date; filing later gets an effective date no earlier than the date of receipt. The deadline is about preserving continuity of the effective date, not a hard bar on ever seeking review.

## Strategy synthesis

1. Primary target: schedular 100% under 4.130, framed explicitly as **total occupational AND social impairment** — both prongs, distinguishing it from a TDIU argument.
2. The lever: the C&P examiner's (or a private IMO's) global-impairment selection on the DBQ. This is the single most important piece of evidence in the whole case.
3. Use _Mauerhan_ + _Bankhead_ to argue you don't need every 100%-tier example symptom; satisfy _Vazquez-Claudio_ by showing the symptoms you do have genuinely produce total impairment.
4. Comorbidities (PTSD/GAD/MDD/OCD/SUD) help through breadth of distinguishable symptoms feeding the one score; _Mittleider_ attributes inseparable SUD symptoms to the service-connected psychiatric picture.
5. Unemployment is legitimate occupational-impairment evidence; accept Rice-raised TDIU consideration as an automatic fallback floor rather than fighting it.
6. If MH lands at 70%, expect ~90% combined without a heavy secondary stack. If MH lands at 90%, one 50% secondary (sleep apnea) reaches 100% combined — the more efficient path.
