## Part 2 — Realistic rating outlook, stated honestly

Given everything above, here's the honest range, not the hoped-for one:

- **70% mental health is very likely achievable** on the current evidence, even accounting for the credibility friction points above — the symptom picture (panic attacks 4–5x/week, chronic depression, DASS-21 ceiling scores, documented occupational collapse, corroborated social withdrawal) maps cleanly onto the 70% tier's "deficiencies in most areas" language, and nothing in the record overreaches into unsupportable territory (ADLs and reasoning/judgment are consistently charted as intact, which is actually a credibility strength — the claim isn't claiming more than the evidence shows).
- **90% mental health is a real but harder stretch** — it would need the C&P examiner (or a private IMO) to find near-total impairment in both domains, likely anchored on documented suicidal ideation frequency/severity (which needs the "every day" vs. "denies SI" discrepancy resolved first) and the OCD/compulsion evidence once it's properly incorporated into your prep materials.
- **100% schedular mental health alone is a genuine stretch, not a likely outcome**, on the evidence reviewed here — none of the records found in this review use "total occupational **and social** impairment" language or 100%-tier symptoms (no gross impairment in thought/communication, no persistent danger to self/others, ADLs preserved). Getting there requires the C&P examiner to affirmatively check the "total" box, and the current record's strongest quantitative evidence (DASS-21) maps to 70%, not 100%.
- **100% combined (schedular, not TDIU) is achievable, but the math matters:** from a 70% MH anchor, reaching 100% combined needs roughly 80%+ worth of _other_ conditions stacked on top — a heavy lift. From a **90%** MH anchor, a single **50%** secondary condition (most plausibly obstructive sleep apnea, if it's established as secondary to PTSD) clears 100%. This means the single highest-leverage move, if 90% MH is reachable, is pursuing the sleep study — not accumulating many small secondary conditions.
- Since you're currently unemployed, that fact is legitimate, real evidence toward the "occupational" half of the 100% test — cite it. Just be aware (per _Rice v. Shinseki_, 22 Vet. App. 447 (2009)) that VA must consider TDIU once unemployability is reasonably raised by the record; you can decline to pursue TDIU as a claim, but you can't force VA to ignore the evidence. In practice this is low-risk: worst case, TDIU becomes an automatic fallback floor while you continue to argue for schedular 100% by framing the case around **both** occupational _and_ social impairment, not just "can't work."

**Bottom line:** treat 70% mental health as the confident floor, 80–90% combined as the realistic target, and 100% schedular as a genuine stretch goal that depends heavily on how the evidence is presented before the C&P exam and how that exam actually goes — not a guaranteed outcome to build the whole plan around.
