### 3.1c The law on a stale address is against you

🟢 **38 C.F.R. § 3.1(q)**: "*Notice* means written notice sent to a claimant or payee at his or her

## 9. ✅ Health insurance — the Texas coverage gap is GONE

> **This section previously described the Texas coverage gap as an unsolved problem. The move solves
> it.** In Texas, a childless non-disabled adult couple under 100% FPL got **neither** Medicaid **nor**
> an ACA subsidy. In Louisiana they get **Medicaid** (§8.1).

| | **Texas (before)** | **Louisiana (now)** |

| Household under 100% FPL | ❌ No Medicaid, no subsidy | ✅ Medicaid |
| VA compensation counted? | n/a | ✅ **No** |
| Spouse's coverage | ❌ Uninsured or full price | ✅ **Free Medicaid** |
| Asset test | n/a | ✅ **None** |

**If income later exceeds the Medicaid limit** — e.g. once unemployment or contract income starts — the
ACA marketplace applies:

- **Open Enrollment: Nov 1 – Jan 15**; enroll by **Dec 15** for January 1 coverage. HealthCare.gov
  currently states Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage starts **November 1**. 🟢
- ✅ **A permanent move to a new ZIP or county is itself an SEP trigger** — 60-day window 🟢. Loss of
  job-based coverage is a separate trigger. **Your move already opened one; do not let it lapse.**
- ⚠️ The expiration of the enhanced/ARPA premium tax credits on 2025-12-31 and the resulting 2026
  premium increase remains 🟡 (not confirmed on a .gov page). Read it as: subsidies got **less**
  generous for 2026, which makes Medicaid eligibility **more** valuable, not less.

**The earlier advice that "filing for UI helps by lifting you into subsidy eligibility" is now
obsolete** — in Louisiana you do not need to clear 100% FPL to get covered. File for UI because
$15,730 is worth having, not for the coverage side-effect.
