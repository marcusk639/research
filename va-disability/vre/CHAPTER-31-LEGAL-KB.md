## 1. The two doors — 38 U.S.C. § 3102

There are exactly two paths to Chapter 31 entitlement. Discharge date does **not** create a
third; that variable lives entirely in § 3103.

**§ 3102(a)(1)** — VERIFIED verbatim (govinfo):

> (a)(1)(A)(i) "a veteran who has a service-connected disability **rated at 20 percent or more**
> which was incurred or aggravated in service on or after September 16, 1940" … (a)(1)(B) "is
> determined by the Secretary to be in need of rehabilitation because of an **employment
> handicap**."

**§ 3102(a)(2)** — VERIFIED verbatim:

> "(2) the person is a veteran who— (A) **has a service-connected disability rated at 10
> percent** which was incurred or aggravated in service on or after September 16, 1940; and
> (B) is determined by the Secretary to be in need of rehabilitation because of a **serious
> employment handicap**."

> ⚠️ **Do not blur statute and regulation.** The **statute** says "rated at 10 percent"
> (exactly 10). The **regulation**, 38 C.F.R. § 21.40(b)(1), says "**less than 20 percent**."
> Attributing either phrase to the other source is a misquote. (Latent tension: the reg's "less
> than 20 percent" reads on its face to include 0%, but § 21.42(a) requires a "qualifying
> **compensable**" SC disability, which VA reads as closing the gap.)

**§ 3102(a)(1)(A)(ii)** — a separate pre-discharge pathway for a servicemember still hospitalized
or receiving outpatient care whose disability "**will likely be** compensable at a rate of 20
percent or more." Relevant only to pre-discharge claimants.

## 2. Definitions — 38 U.S.C. § 3101

**§ 3101(2), "employment handicap"** — VERIFIED verbatim:

> "an impairment, resulting in substantial part from a disability described in **section
> 3102(1)(A)** of this title, of a veteran's ability to prepare for, obtain, or retain employment
> consistent with such veteran's abilities, aptitudes, and interests."

> ⚠️ **That internal cross-reference is stale.** It is printed exactly that way in the current
> Code, but Pub. L. 112-56 (2011) added subsection (b) and redesignated the pre-existing text as
> subsection (a); Congress never conformed § 3101. The correct present-day locus is
> **§ 3102(a)(1)(A)**. Quote it as printed — do **not** represent the cross-reference as current.
>
> (There is an editorial footnote in § 3101 reading "So in original. Probably should be '(1)'."
> It attaches to a paragraph designation in the 2023-added definition, **not** to this
> cross-reference. Do not cite it as the Code flagging this error.)

**§ 3101(8), "serious employment handicap"** — VERIFIED verbatim:

> "a **significant** impairment, resulting **in substantial part from a service-connected
> disability rated at 10 percent or more**, of a veteran's ability to prepare for, obtain, or
> retain employment consistent with such veteran's abilities, aptitudes, and interests."

**The operative distinction — two differences, not one.**

1. **"Significant."** SEH requires a *significant* impairment where EH requires only an
   impairment. This is the heightened severity finding.
2. **A different rating predicate, and SEH's is _broader_.** § 3101(2) ties EH to "a disability
   **described in section 3102(1)(A)**" — the **20%-or-more** clause. § 3101(8) ties SEH to "a
   service-connected disability **rated at 10 percent or more**." These are different predicates,
   not the same predicate with an adjective bolted on.

> An earlier draft of this file said EH and SEH "share an identical underlying test" and that
> *significant* is "one word that does all the work." **That understated the second difference and
> is corrected here.** The shared core is the "prepare for, obtain, or retain employment
> consistent with abilities, aptitudes, and interests" language — not the whole test.

**Paragraph numbering note:** Pub. L. 117-333 (Jan. 5, 2023) added an "emergency situation"
definition at (1) and redesignated former (1)–(9) as (2)–(10). Current order: (1) emergency
situation, (2) employment handicap, (3) independence in daily living, (4) program of education,
(5) program of independent living services, (6) rehabilitated to the point of employability,
(7) rehabilitation program, (8) serious employment handicap, (9) vocational goal, (10) vocational
rehabilitation program.

## 3. Period of eligibility — 38 U.S.C. § 3103

**§ 3103(a)** — VERIFIED: no rehabilitation program after "the end of the **twelve-year period**
beginning on the date of such veteran's discharge or release," except as provided in subsections
(b), (c), (d), (e), (g), or (h).

**§ 21.42(a)** — VERIFIED verbatim:

> "The basic period of eligibility **does not commence until the day VA notifies a veteran of a
> rating determination** by VA that the veteran has a qualifying compensable service-connected
> disability **under § 21.40**."

This is the "later of separation or first notice of a qualifying rating" rule — implemented as a
_deferral of commencement_, not as a separate statutory clause.

**Tolling and extension:** § 21.42(b) (character-of-discharge bar), § 21.42(c) (medical
infeasibility ≥30 days, with alcoholism sub-rules at (c)(2)–(5)), and **§ 21.44** — a CP or VRC
**may extend** the basic period for a veteran with a **serious employment handicap**.

### 3.1 ⚠️ THE ISAKSON CORRECTION — read this before citing anything

**38 U.S.C. § 3103(g)** — VERIFIED verbatim, and it **is** subsection (g):

> "Subsection (a) shall not apply to a veteran who was **discharged or released from active
> military, naval, or air service on or after January 1, 2013**."

**Added by: Pub. L. 116-315, Title I, § 1025(a)(2), 134 Stat. 4960, enacted January 5, 2021** —
the _Johnny Isakson and David P. Roe, M.D. Veterans Health Care and Benefits Improvement Act of
2020_. Section heading: "Elimination of period of eligibility for training and rehabilitation for
certain veterans with service-connected disabilities." Verified against the enrolled text at
govinfo (page marker `[[Page 4960]]`).

> ⚠️ **Pub. L. 115-48 is NOT the Isakson Act.** It is the _Harry W. Colmery Veterans Educational
> Assistance Act of 2017_ (Forever GI Bill), enacted 08/16/2017. Its Title IV § 402, 131 Stat.
> 996, amended § 3103**(f)** — the tolling rule for veterans recalled to active duty — which has
> nothing to do with the 2013 carve-out. **Both public laws amend § 3103**, which is why blogs
> and even VA-adjacent explainers conflate them. This corpus's own source prompt had it wrong.
>
> Two further precision points: § 402 did **not create** the (f) tolling (Pub. L. 112-56
> § 233(b), 2011 did) — it only expanded the call-up authority list, which now covers 10 U.S.C.
> §§ 688, 12301(a), (d), (g), 12302, 12304, 12304a, 12304b. And the exact pincite for the
> carve-out is **§ 1025(a)(2)**; § 1025(a)(1) is the conforming change to § 3103(a).

**Effective date:** § 1025 contains **no separate effective-date clause** — it took effect on
enactment, **January 5, 2021**. This can be stated affirmatively.

**Drafting quirk:** § 3103(g) says "or air service," without "space." Pub. L. 116-283 substituted
"air, or space service" only in subsections (a) and (b)(2)(A), four days before (g) was added.
The asymmetry is real — do not "helpfully" insert "space" when quoting.

## 4. The entitlement determination — 38 C.F.R. §§ 21.50–21.53

**§ 21.50(b)(1)–(3)** — VERIFIED verbatim. ⚠️ _Pincite is § 21.50(b), not § 21.50 generally._

> "(b) _Determinations to be made by VA during the initial evaluation._ A counseling
> psychologist (CP) or vocational rehabilitation counselor (VRC) will determine: (1) Whether the
> individual has an employment handicap …; (2) Whether an individual with an employment handicap
> has a serious employment handicap …; and (3) Whether the achievement of a vocational goal is
> currently reasonably feasible as described in § 21.53."

Full structure: (a) entitlement to an initial evaluation; (b) determinations; **(c) factors for
assessment** — separately load-bearing, incorporated by reference into § 21.51(c)(2); (d) need
for cooperation.

**§ 21.51 — employment handicap.** Requires **all** of: (a) a vocational impairment; (b) the
effects not overcome through employment; (c) SC contribution. VERIFIED verbatim at (c)(1):

> "the service-connected disability(ies) **must contribute in substantial part to the
> individual's overall vocational impairment**. This means that the disability(ies) must have an
> **identifiable, measurable, or observable causative effect** on the overall vocational
> impairment, but **need not be the sole or primary cause** of the employment handicap."

**§ 21.51(b)** is the sleeper provision — it expressly covers a veteran

> "who qualifies for a suitable job, but who **does not obtain or keep the job for reasons beyond
> his or her control**."

That is the statutory home for a documented pattern of getting hired and not retaining work.

**§ 21.52 — serious employment handicap.** Requires a **significant** vocational impairment, not
overcome through suitable employment, with SC disability contributing in substantial part to the
overall _significant_ vocational impairment. ⚠️ _It contains **exactly nine** enumerated factors
at § 21.52(b)(1)–(9) — not "roughly nine."_ VA "will develop and assess":

1. Number of disabling conditions
2. Severity of disabling condition(s)
3. Existence of **neuropsychiatric** condition(s)
4. Adequacy of education or training for suitable employment
5. Number, length, and frequency of periods of **unemployment or underemployment**
6. Pattern of reliance on government support programs (welfare, SC compensation, NSC pension,
   worker's compensation, Social Security disability)
7. Extent and complexity of services and assistance needed to achieve rehabilitation
8. Negative attitudes toward individuals with disabilities and other restrictions on suitable
   employment — **expressly including "alcoholism or other substance abuse"**, discrimination
   based on age/race/gender/disability, and labor market conditions
9. Other factors relating to preparing for, obtaining, or keeping employment consistent with
   abilities, aptitudes, and interests

**§ 21.53 — reasonable feasibility.** Two propositions, both VERIFIED verbatim:

- **§ 21.53(c):** "…**Any reasonable doubt shall be resolved in favor of a finding of
  feasibility.**"
- **§ 21.53(e)(2):** "A finding that achievement of a vocational goal is infeasible **_without a
  period of extended evaluation_** requires compelling evidence which establishes infeasibility
  beyond any reasonable doubt."

> ⚠️ **The most commonly overstated provision in this area.** The "compelling evidence / beyond
> any reasonable doubt" standard does **not** govern _any_ infeasibility finding — by its terms
> it governs infeasibility found **without first conducting a period of extended evaluation**,
> and the pincite is **(e)(2)**, not (e). § 21.53(c) is the cleaner, stronger citation.

Full § 21.53 structure: (a) requirement; (b) definition; (c) expeditious determination;
(d) feasibility met; (e) criteria not met; (f) independent living services; (g) responsible staff.

## 5. Pending claims and the rating percentage

Both § 3102 and § 21.40 speak in the **present tense** — a veteran who "**has**" a disability
"rated at" a percentage. No provision was found permitting a VRC to treat a _pending, undecided_
claim as an existing rating for basic-entitlement purposes. (The one "will likely be" exception,
§ 3102(a)(1)(A)(ii), applies only to servicemembers still hospitalized pending discharge.)

**But that limitation is about the percentage, not the evidence.** Nothing prevents the VRC from
weighing the medical evidence underlying a pending claim in the § 21.52(b) severity analysis.
The distinction is worth holding precisely: _a pending claim cannot supply a percentage today,
but the records behind it can inform how significant the impairment is._

> **UNVERIFIED:** no CFR or M28C provision was found directly on point for how a pending claim is
> weighed in § 21.52. The above is a textual inference, not an express rule.

## 6. Review of an adverse decision — 38 C.F.R. § 21.416

VERIFIED. Within **one year** from the date VA issues notice of a decision, a claimant may elect:

1. **Supplemental Claim** — § 21.416(b)(1), in accord with § 3.2501, except no complete written
   application form is required and no hearing is provided (VA Form 20-0995)
2. **Appeal to the Board of Veterans' Appeals** — § 21.416(b)(2), see 38 C.F.R. part 20
   (VA Form 10182)
3. **Higher-Level Review** — § 21.416(b)(3), in accord with § 3.2601 (VA Form 20-0996)

**Caveat:** § 21.416(a) limits the section to decisions noticed on or after the AMA effective
date (**2019-02-19**), or legacy claims where the claimant elected the modernized system.

> ⚠️ **38 C.F.R. § 21.98 NO LONGER EXISTS.** "Appeal of disagreement regarding development of, or
> change in, the plan" — which provided an internal VR&E Officer / VR&E Director review — was
> **REMOVED effective 2019-02-19** by the VA Claims and Appeals Modernization final rule
> (**84 FR 138**, RIN 2900-AQ26). Confirmed via the eCFR versioner API, which returns
> `removed: true` for that date. **Do not cite it.**
>
> This took three passes to resolve, and the reason is worth recording: a _removed_ CFR section
> returns **404** on Cornell LII and on govinfo's current-year CFR, which reads as "source
> unreachable" rather than "provision repealed." The eCFR **versioner API** is what
> distinguishes them. Practical consequence: **there is no separate internal VR&E appeal channel
> for an entitlement denial** — it runs through the same three AMA lanes as any other VA benefit
> decision.

## 7. Decision tree — veteran discharged 2013, rated 10%

```
STEP 1 — Time bar?  (§ 3103 / § 21.41)
  ├─ Discharged BEFORE 1/1/2013 → 12-year clock applies, starting on the later of
  │    discharge or first qualifying-rating notice (§ 21.42(a)); tollable (§ 21.42(b)-(c))
  │    and extendable for SEH (§ 21.44)
  └─ Discharged ON/AFTER 1/1/2013 → § 3103(g) ELIMINATES the bar entirely.
       No time-bar issue regardless of years elapsed.

STEP 2 — Basic entitlement  (statute § 3102 / regulation § 21.40 — note they word it
         differently; see §1's warning)
  ├─ Rating ≥ 20%  [§ 21.40(a)]        → statutory hook § 3102(a)(1): EMPLOYMENT HANDICAP (§ 21.51)
  ├─ Rating < 20% BUT COMPENSABLE      → statutory hook § 3102(a)(2): SERIOUS EMPLOYMENT HANDICAP
  │    [§ 21.40(b)(1) says "less than 20 percent"; § 21.42(a) requires a QUALIFYING
  │     COMPENSABLE SC disability, which is what keeps 0% out]
  │    (§ 21.52 — § 21.51's elements PLUS "significant" PLUS the nine factors at (b)(1)-(9))
  ├─ Rating 0% / non-compensable       → NOT entitled (no qualifying compensable SC disability)
  └─ Pre-discharge, in care            → § 3102(a)(1)(A)(ii) / § 21.40(c)

  ⚠ The statute says "rated at 10 percent"; the regulation says "less than 20 percent."
    Do not attribute either phrase to the other source. Neither says "combined."

STEP 3 — Feasibility  (§ 21.53)
  ├─ Reasonable doubt resolved IN FAVOR of feasibility (§ 21.53(c))
  └─ Infeasible without extended evaluation only on compelling evidence
       beyond reasonable doubt (§ 21.53(e)(2))

STEP 4 — If denied
  ├─ New application (VA Form 28-1900) after a rating increase — cleanest path
  ├─ Supplemental Claim, § 21.416(b)(1), within 1 year
  └─ HLR / Board appeal, § 21.416(b)(2)-(3), within 1 year
```

**Key structural point:** eliminating the 12-year period does **not** touch the rating
requirement. § 1025(a) amended **only § 3103**. The two gates stack; they do not substitute.
