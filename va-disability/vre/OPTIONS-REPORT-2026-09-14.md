# Three Options, Examined: Leave Tech · Retrain · Consult

**The question asked:** what can be gained through VR&E, and which of three paths — exit software
engineering, retrain for a new career, or launch an AI/LLM consulting practice — is best?

**The short answer, stated plainly up front:** the question contains a premise that the evidence does
not support, and correcting it changes the answer. All three options are framed as solutions to
_not being able to get work_. The record says you can get work — **recruiters were offering
$195–229K as recently as 2026-09-12** (corpus, prep doc §6.2). What you cannot currently do is
**sustain** work. Retraining and consulting both solve the first problem, at high cost, while
leaving the second one untouched — and consulting actively removes every legal protection that
addresses it.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Entitlement is not yet established, and probably will not be on these facts.** At 10%
   (tinnitus only), Chapter 31 runs solely through **serious employment handicap**,
   38 U.S.C. § 3102(a)(2) — and the impairment must trace **in substantial part to a
   service-connected** disability. The conditions that actually ended two jobs are not
   service-connected yet. A denial is the realistic outcome. 🟢
2. **That denial costs almost nothing.** **38 U.S.C. § 3103(g)** removes the 12-year eligibility bar
   for anyone discharged on or after 2013-01-01 — discharge was **2013-11-22**. **There is no
   deadline.** And § 21.186(c) makes ineligible status "_a bar to reconsideration… unless a material
   change in circumstances occurs_" — a grant of service connection is exactly that. 🟢
3. **VR&E is the wrong funding vehicle for a consulting practice.** The fundable core for a
   knowledge business is a laptop, certifications, license fees, and management coaching — likely
   under $25,000. **Working capital, E&O insurance, SaaS, website, and marketing are unauthorized or
   unaddressed.** And § 21.260(d) stops the subsistence allowance **exactly at the launch phase**,
   when a new practice has no revenue. 🟢
4. **Two non-VR&E programs do what VR&E is being asked to do.** **SDVOSB certification qualifies at
   10% today** — 13 C.F.R. § 128.102 sets no minimum rating. And **Texas TWC-VRS funds
   self-employment plans with no service-connection requirement**, and serves existing business
   owners, which Chapter 31's startup-only rule does not. 🟢
5. **The best-value Chapter 31 benefit is not money — but it is smaller than it first appears.**
   **38 C.F.R. § 17.37(e)** gives a VR&E participant the full § 17.38 medical benefits package
   **without enrollment**, bypassing the priority-group system, from the initial evaluation onward.
   ⚠️ **Corrected:** at 10% SC you are **Priority Group 3 with $0 outpatient and inpatient copays
   anyway**, so this is a **redundant second door, not a unique one** — file VA Form 10-10EZ and get
   care on the merits. What Chapter 31 *does* add uniquely is § 21.240 coverage beyond part 17 and
   **brief mental-health services to immediate family** (§ 21.155). 🟢
6. **Retraining fails on its own premise.** The growing sub-2-year-credential careers that pay
   ($82–105K) are hospital- and hangar-based: shift adherence, mandatory physical presence, near-zero
   tolerance for unplanned absence. That is _precisely_ the constraint you cannot currently meet. It
   buys a 50–60% permanent pay cut and does not buy sustainability. 🟢
7. **Solo consulting is the weakest option on the evidence — not because of the market, but because
   of the structure.** Year-1 utilization runs **38–46%**, putting realistic W-2-equivalent income
   **below** a mid-market W-2 AI role; going solo forfeits **ADA accommodation duty, FMLA,
   unemployment insurance, and employer disability coverage simultaneously**; and a
   26-study/3.1M-participant review links self-employment to elevated mental-illness risk through
   exactly the mechanisms consulting maximizes. 🟢/🟡
8. **The TDIU collision is the largest dollar risk in the file.** At 10% you fail the § 4.16(a)
   percentage gate entirely, so TDIU runs only through § 4.16(b) extraschedular referral. Earned
   income above the **~$16,749** one-person poverty threshold (2025; $16,320 for 2024) is presumptively
   **irrespective of hours worked** (_Faust_), and the § 1163 / § 3.343(c)(2) twelve-month protection
   **shields an existing award — it does not shield a pending claim.** 🟢
9. **The recommendation is layered, not binary:** keep the VR&E door open without committing to a
   track; build the consulting capability through the free, no-SC channels (SDVOSB, VBOC, TWC-VRS);
   take income in the near term through **agency-brokered contract engineering**, which delivers the
   autonomy and schedule control you actually want without the business-development load that the
   evidence says is most likely to fail; and **do not book revenue above the marginal-employment
   threshold until the MH rating and any TDIU decision are final.**

## 2. The gate {#2-the-gate}

Everything below is downstream of a determination that has not been made.

**The two doors** (`CHAPTER-31-LEGAL-KB.md` §1, §7):

- Rating **≥20%** → 38 U.S.C. § 3102(a)(1) / § 21.40(a): needs only an **employment handicap**.
- Rating **<20% but compensable** → § 3102(a)(2) / § 21.40(b): needs a **serious** employment
  handicap — § 21.51's elements _plus_ "significant" _plus_ the nine § 21.52(b) factors.

At **tinnitus 10%**, only the second door exists.

**Severity favors you; causation does not.** Six of the nine § 21.52(b) factors are arguable in your
favor on this corpus's own reading — an advocate's count, not a VA finding — and factor (8) names
substance abuse expressly. But § 21.51(c)(1) still requires the service-connected disability to
contribute **in substantial part**, with an "identifiable, measurable, or observable causative
effect." The honest bridge is tinnitus → chronic sleep disruption → impaired concentration and
reliability. That is a real argument and a thin one.

**Feasibility is not where you lose.** § 21.53(c) 🟢: "_Any reasonable doubt shall be resolved in
favor of a finding of feasibility._" This matters more than it appears — see §10.

**Two traps worth carrying into the room:**

- ⚠️ **§ 21.51(b)** 🟢 — an employment handicap requires that the veteran "_has **not** overcome the
  effects of the individual's impairment of employability through employment in, or qualifying for
  employment in, an occupation consistent with his or her abilities._" **Saying "I can work full
  time" can defeat entitlement outright.** The prep doc's suggested framing needs the care the
  ACTION-CARD already flags.
- ⚠️ **38 U.S.C. § 3104(a)(12)** 🟢 bars a program that would **aggravate** the service-connected
  condition. If the medical record describes work-stress intolerance, a high-stress solo
  consultancy is arguably barred _on VA's own reading of your own file_. This cuts against Option B
  from inside the program.

## 3. The reframe {#3-the-reframe}

This is the analytical core of the document.

| Framing                                                         | What it implies                                                                                     | Does the record support it?                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "I can't maintain employment because of layoffs and disability" | The problem is **access to work** → retrain, or self-employ                                         | **Partly.** Layoffs are real (§4.1), but recruiters offered **$195–229K on 2026-09-12** |
| "I can obtain work; I can't sustain it"                         | The problem is **durability under load** → change the _shape_ of the work and secure accommodations | **Yes.** Two separations in 19 months, one via PIP; eleven treatment episodes 2016–2026 |

**Why the distinction decides the case.** § 21.51(b) is built on exactly this axis — _obtaining_
versus _retaining_. The corpus already identified it as the framing that is both truthful and
consistent with the pending compensation claim (`README.md`, "Cross-claim exposure"). It is also the
axis on which the three options separate:

- **Retraining** changes _what_ the work is. It does not change the sustained-load problem — and
  the paying targets have _worse_ attendance tolerance than software.
- **Consulting** changes _who employs you_. It removes the load-management tools (ADA, FMLA,
  employer DI) and adds revenue risk and business development.
- **Changing role shape** attacks the constraint directly — lower sustained intensity, schedule
  control, bounded scope, exit ramps — while preserving earning power and legal protections.

The market data reinforces it. The 2026 recovery is **narrow and senior**: **71% of the May 2025 →
May 2026 posting growth was senior-level**; **69.3% of Q1 2026 software-dev postings were
senior-level**; MLE openings are **+59% vs Feb 2020 while general SWE is −49%**; AI is the hardest
skill to hire globally for the first time in ManpowerGroup's survey history. 🟢 You are on the
favorable side of the only recovery that exists. Retraining trades that away.

## 4. Option A — leave tech / retrain {#4-option-a}

### 4.1 What the labor market says

- Tech layoffs are **up sharply YoY but decelerating**: Challenger counts **155,126 tech cuts
  Jan–Aug 2026, +52% over 2025**, but **August's 6,103 was the year's lowest month**. 🟢
- **Hiring, not firing, is the problem.** The JOLTS hires rate hit **3.1% in Feb 2026 — matching the
  April 2020 COVID low**. **Information-sector openings were down 33% YoY**, the steepest of any
  private sector. 🟢
- BLS **cut** the software-developer outlook to **10% growth 2025–2035** (from 15% for 2024–2034);
  median pay **$135,980** (May 2025). 🟢
- Damage is concentrated on juniors: Stanford Digital Economy Lab (ADP payroll) found employment of
  **22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed quintiles fell ~11%** Nov 2022→Jun 2026 while
  least-exposed grew ~10%. Brynjolfsson's own read: "_no sign of economy-wide job destruction_" — the
  mechanism is **reduced hiring, not increased firing.** 🟢

**Implication:** the layoff narrative is true in aggregate and **does not describe your segment.**

### 4.3 The three problems with Option A

1. **A 50–60% permanent pay cut.** Against $195–229K, the $82–105K band is not a dip; it is a new
   ceiling.
2. **The stress-reduction premise is largely false.** Imaging, therapy, and hangar roles require
   **shift adherence, mandatory physical presence, continuous patient contact, and near-zero
   tolerance for unpredictable absence.** Per-diem/PRN work genuinely does offer schedule control —
   but typically requires 1–2 years of staff experience first. You would spend two years training
   and two more years accruing experience before reaching the flexibility that is the entire point.
3. **Information security analyst — $129,180, +21% growth — needs no retraining at all.** It
   captures most of the "growing, AI-resistant field" benefit with zero training years and zero
   income gap. It is still tech, and it carries on-call.

### 4.4 Whether VA would even fund it

⚠️ **Probably not, on these facts.** **38 C.F.R. § 21.47(a)(3)** 🟢 routes veterans who "(i) _Are
employable or employed in a suitable occupation_; (ii) _Have an employment handicap_; and (iii) _Need
employment services_" to **employment services** — not funded retraining. And **§ 21.72(a)(2)** 🟢
caps training at "_the level generally recognized as necessary for entry_," with only two exceptions
(§ 21.72(b)): a local labor market demanding more, or — for serious employment handicap —
training above the usual level where the veteran would be "_at a definite disadvantage in competing
with nondisabled persons._"

Holding a CS degree and ten years of experience is not a bar, but it **shifts the entire burden onto
showing that software engineering specifically is now unsuitable** for the service-connected
condition. Absent that showing, the predictable VRC output is employment services — which, under
§ 21.254(b), carries **no subsistence, no revolving-fund loan, no work-study, and no training beyond
brief licensure review courses.**

## 5. Option B — solo AI/LLM consulting {#5-option-b}

This is the stated preference. It deserves the most careful treatment, and it gets the least
favorable verdict — for structural reasons, not market reasons.

### 5.1 What is genuinely good about it

- **AI expertise commands a real premium.** Levels.fyi ML/AI SWE median TC **$248,000** (2026);
  FAANG AI/ML premium **15–25% and widening**; Dice puts the AI-skills premium at **~18%**; Robert
  Half shows AI/ML/data starting pay **+4.1%** vs +1.6% across tech. 🟢
- **Freelance AI work measurably pays more.** Upwork: freelancers doing AI work earn **34% more per
  hour**; **AI-augmented professional services grew +72% in contract starts with earnings +22%**, and
  complex AI work saw earnings **+45% in Q1 2026**. 🟢
- **Autonomy and schedule control are real, and they are legitimate needs** given a condition
  characterized by episodic incapacity. This is not a trivial preference — it is the correct
  instinct about what you need. The disagreement below is about _the vehicle_, not the goal.
- **Being employable does not bar the VR&E self-employment track.** The "only reasonably feasible
  vocational goal" language in § 21.257(b) is a **definition triggering the enhanced supply tier**,
  not an approval gate. VA deleted the employability restriction in 2010: "_Self-employment as a mode
  of employment is authorized for **all** program participants for whom it is deemed appropriate._"
  (74 FR 19164 / 75 FR 3170) 🟢 **The prior corpus framing on this was wrong and is corrected.**

### 5.2 The commoditization line, precisely drawn

Low-complexity generative-AI work is deflating: Upwork shows genAI **creative/production** work
**+90% in contract starts but per-contract earnings −13% YoY**. 🟢 Prompt-wiring,
RAG-assembly, and "add a chatbot" are pricing toward zero. Evaluation harnesses, agent reliability,
cost/latency engineering, data plumbing, and production ownership are not. **Your differentiation has
to sit on the second list.**

### 5.3 The demand picture is souring for solo operators

- **MIT Project NANDA (2025):** **95% of enterprise genAI pilots produced no measurable P&L
  impact** (52 exec interviews, 153 surveys, 300 deployments). 🟢 That is the buyer skepticism you
  would be selling through.
- **C3.ai professional-services revenue projected to fall 48%** as fewer new projects start; sales
  cycles lengthening amid enterprise AI budget reassessment (S&P Global, Jan 2026). 🟢
- **Who actually buys from a solo operator:** SMB/mid-market, seed–Series B startups, and
  **subcontract work under agencies**. Large enterprises route AI spend through vendor security
  review, MSAs, insurance, and SOC 2 — a sole proprietor generally sells **into** an agency, not
  around it. Plan on referral and subcontract revenue, not direct enterprise logos. 🟡

### 5.4 The arithmetic that decides it

Consulting-benchmark utilization for **new** independents: **100–120 billable days in year 1** out of
~260 working days — **38–46% utilization** — with 25–35 days on business development, 15–25 on
admin, 15–30 in gaps. 🟡

⚠️ **ESTIMATE:** 110 days × 8 hrs × $175/hr = **$154,000 gross**. Subtract 15.3% self-employment tax
on net, ~$9–20K family health premiums, no 401(k) match, no PTO, no severance →
**W-2-equivalent roughly $95–115K**, arriving in lumpy, unpredictable increments.

**That is below a mid-market W-2 senior AI engineering salary, in exchange for absorbing all of the
revenue risk.** Time to first client: **30–60 days with a strong warm network, 90–180 days
without.** 🟡 Five-year survival for _capitalized employer_ firms is **49.2%** (BLS Business
Employment Dynamics, 1994–2022); solo non-employer survival is measured worse and less often. 🟢

### 5.5 The structural objection — and it is the real one

**Going solo forfeits, simultaneously and permanently:**

| Protection                              | What it does                                                                           | Lost because                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ADA Title I accommodation duty**      | Flexible hours, reduced-distraction environment, intermittent leave, modified schedule | No employer → no duty                                                                                                                                                   |
| **FMLA job-protected leave**            | Up to 12 weeks protected, usable intermittently                                        | Requires a 50-employee employer + 1,250 hours                                                                                                                           |
| **Employer short/long-term disability** | Income during incapacity                                                               | No employer plan                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Unemployment insurance**              | Income floor between engagements                                                       | Self-employed are not covered                                                                                                                                           |
| **Individual disability insurance**     | The substitute for the above                                                           | ⚠️ Underwriting **routinely excludes or declines psychiatric and substance-use histories** — the one policy that would matter most is the one least likely to be issued |

**And the clinical evidence points the same direction:**

- **Systematic review, _IJERPH_ 2021 — 26 population-based studies, 3.1M participants:** evidence
  supports "_a link between self-employment and increased risk of mental illness._" Named risk
  mechanisms map directly onto consulting: **economic uncertainty, long hours, work–family conflict,
  sickness presenteeism, chronic competitive stress, thin safety nets.** 🟢
- **Freeman et al., _Small Business Economics_ (2019):** **49% of entrepreneurs reported a lifetime
  mental-health condition** vs 32% comparison — depression 30%, ADHD 29%, **substance use 12%**,
  bipolar 11%; 32% reported 2+ conditions. **Selection, not protection.** 🟢
- **The evidence-based vocational path for serious mental illness is supported _employment_, not
  self-employment.** Umbrella and systematic reviews (PLOS One 2024; Frontiers 2021) find
  high-fidelity **Individual Placement and Support (IPS)** outperforms other models on job
  attainment. There is a large RCT literature behind IPS and **essentially none** behind
  "self-employment as accommodation." Self-employment remains a small share of VR closures for people
  with psychiatric disabilities despite policy encouraging it (_J Behav Health Serv Res_ 2018). 🟢

> **The honest synthesis.** Consulting maximizes exactly the variables the literature identifies as
> risk factors for the conditions you are seeking to have service-connected — income volatility,
> chronic competitive stress, no leave protection, presenteeism pressure — while removing every legal
> tool for managing episodic incapacity. The autonomy is real. So is the exposure. **The instinct is
> right; the vehicle is wrong.**

### 5.6 And VR&E is the wrong funder for it

Even granting that VA _can_ approve self-employment for an employable veteran (§5.1), the track does
not fund what a consulting practice needs. From `CH31-RESOURCE-INVENTORY.md` Part III:

- ✅ **Fundable:** short-term training and certifications (§ 21.257(d)(1)), business-management
  training (d)(2), license fees (d)(3), necessary tools including a computer as a category (d)(4),
  placement services (d)(5).
- ❌ **Not authorized or not addressed:** **working capital, owner's salary, E&O/professional
  liability insurance, SaaS subscriptions, website, marketing.**
- ⛔ **Prohibited:** office rent or leases, vehicles (§ 21.214(f)); **non-profits categorically**
  (BVA 1506345).
- ⛔ **§ 21.260(d)** 🟢 — no subsistence allowance when VA provides "_only_… placement and
  post-placement services… or counseling." **The monthly money stops exactly at launch**, when
  revenue is zero.
- ⚠️ **Neither subsistence table has a self-employment row** — the rate turns on whether VA classifies
  the training as institutional or **training-in-the-home (½ BAH national average)**.
- ⚠️ **An already-operating business may disqualify you.** M28C scopes the track to **startups** and
  bars induction once a business has been "_established and operational for at least 12 months_"; OIG
  faulted VA for approving plans for veterans with established businesses. 🟡 **If an LLC is already
  formed and billing, resolve this before mentioning a business to anyone.**
- ⚠️ **Your runway is screened against you** — § 21.257(f)(4) requires analysis of "availability of
  financing from non-VA sources," and personal financial resources are an explicit suitability
  factor. **6–12 months of cash is an argument against a VA-funded plan.**
- **The track is ~1% of the caseload**, VA "_does not have data on the success rate_," and "_does not
  budget a specific amount_" for it (GAO-09-34; VA OIG 11-00317-37). Approval rates: ⚠️ **not
  published in any year found.**
- **Process load:** VRC suitability screen → Phase One IWRP → mandatory SBA referral → **mandatory
  Self-Employment Panel** → a **stand-alone, lender-ready business plan** satisfying seven
  § 21.257(f) elements → Phase Two concurrence → **≥12 months of case management plus a site visit**
  before closure. ⚠️ No published timeline.

**One genuinely useful door, though:** **§ 21.94(b)(2)** 🟢 lets an existing plan be amended to
self-employment on a showing that "_circumstances have changed or new information has been developed
which makes rehabilitation **more likely**._" **You do not have to prove the current goal
infeasible.** So the low-friction route is: accept a reasonable plan now, amend later.

**Verdict on Option B: pursue the goal, reject the vehicle.** Build the practice through the
no-service-connection channels in §6.3 and §9 — not by making Chapter 31 its funder, and not before
the TDIU question in §7 is resolved.

## 7. The TDIU collision {#7-the-tdiu-collision}

**This is the highest-magnitude financial risk in the file, and it constrains Option B directly.**

### 7.1 Where TDIU stands today

- **You fail § 4.16(a) entirely.** It requires one disability at **60%+**, or one at **40%+** with a
  combined **70%+**. At 10%, TDIU runs **only** through **§ 4.16(b)** — referral to the Director,
  Compensation Service, for extraschedular consideration. A materially harder path. 🟢
- Nothing has been filed as of 2026-09-14 (resolved in the corpus). The rep's plan still contemplates
  § 4.16 development.

### 7.2 The rule that matters

**§ 4.16(a)**, verbatim 🟢:

> "Marginal employment shall not be considered substantially gainful employment… marginal employment
> generally shall be deemed to exist when a veteran's earned annual income does not exceed the amount
> established by the U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of the Census, as the **poverty threshold for
> one person**. Marginal employment may also be held to exist, on a facts found basis (**includes but
> is not limited to employment in a protected environment such as a family business or sheltered
> workshop**), when earned annual income exceeds the poverty threshold."

**The number: $16,749** — Census poverty threshold, one person under 65, **2025**. 🟢 The 2024 figure
was $16,320. ⚠️ **VA adjudicators may still be citing the 2024 number** — plan against the lower one.

**_Faust v. West_, 13 Vet. App. 342 (2000)** 🟡 — substantially gainful employment means income
exceeding the poverty threshold **irrespective of hours or days actually worked.**
**"I only work ten hours a week" is not a defense if the income clears the threshold.** A
protected-environment argument is the only facts-found escape, and **a self-founded solo consultancy
is not obviously a protected environment.**

### 7.3 The protection that does not protect you yet

**38 U.S.C. § 1163(a)(1)** 🟢: a TDIU rating "_may not be reduced on the basis of the veteran having
secured and followed a substantially gainful occupation unless the veteran maintains such an
occupation for a period of 12 consecutive months._" Implemented at **38 C.F.R. § 3.343(c)(2)**.

**But § 1163(a)(2) defines "qualified veteran" as one who has already been awarded TDIU.**

> ⚠️ **The twelve-month shield protects an existing award. It does not protect a pending claim.**
> Earning income _before_ any TDIU award is simply adverse evidence on entitlement. This is the most
> commonly misunderstood point in this entire area, and getting it backwards is expensive.

### 7.4 Does VR&E participation itself hurt?

**No — and the structure argues the opposite.** 🟢 **38 C.F.R. § 3.341(c)** requires that each time a
veteran is rated totally disabled on unemployability, "_the Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E)
Service **will be notified so that an evaluation may be offered**._" § 1163(b)/(c) require VA to hand
every new TDIU awardee a Chapter 31 explainer. **VA cannot treat as disqualifying the very referral
it mandates.**

⚠️ **But documented _success_ is adverse evidence.** BVA does weigh completed and successful VR&E as
probative of employability (e.g. Citation Nr. 23014504 (2023) 🟡).

### 7.5 And VR&E records will be read

**M21-1** 🟡, quoted from an archived copy: "**When to Obtain Vocational Rehabilitation Records in IU
Claims** — _When a Veteran's claims folder indicates that he/she was seen by the Vocational
Rehabilitation and Employment Service (VR&E), obtain and evaluate any records related to this
contact. The VR&E records may document the Veteran's participation in a training program, or show
that training was not feasible or was unsuccessful._" (Citing _Murincsak v. Derwinski_, 2 Vet. App.
363 (1992) on VA's duty to obtain records it is on notice of.)

**Everything said to the VRC lands in a file the rating adjudicator is instructed to pull. It cuts
both ways.**

### 7.6 Why a "serious employment handicap" finding transfers poorly

⚠️ An SEH finding is a weaker TDIU asset than it sounds. **§ 21.50(c)(1)** lets VR&E weigh
**non**-service-connected disabilities in the impairment analysis, and § 21.51(c)(1) requires only
that the SC disability "_contribute in substantial part_." **TDIU under § 4.16 counts only
service-connected disabilities.** An SEH built partly on NSC mental-health factors **does not carry
over.** 🟢

### 7.7 The five actions that could damage the pending claim

Ranked by magnitude:

1. ⛔ **Booking self-employment income above ~$16,749** before any TDIU award. § 4.16(a) + _Faust_;
   **no § 1163 shield pre-award.** Highest-magnitude risk in the file.
2. ⛔ **Telling a VRC you can work full time** — or that you are launching a company. Risks defeating
   VR&E entitlement outright under **§ 21.51(b)** (has not "overcome the effects… through
   employment") _and_ lands in the C-file per §7.5.
3. ⛔ **Telling a C&P examiner about the business.** Occupational and social impairment **is the
   rating criterion** under the § 4.130 General Rating Formula. This damages the **percentage**, not
   merely TDIU.
4. ⚠️ **Filing VA Form 21-8940 while income is accruing.** The form demands earnings and hours;
   inconsistency between the 8940, tax returns, and VR&E notes is a credibility finding.
5. ⚠️ **Causation drift.** Building the VR&E narrative on NSC mental-health symptoms creates a record
   that cannot be carried into a § 4.16 analysis later (§7.6).

## 9. Recommendation {#9-recommendation}

**Not a single choice — four parallel moves, ordered by risk-adjusted value.**

### Move 1 — Take the health-care benefit, whatever else happens
**File VA Form 10-10EZ and get care on the merits.** At 10% SC you land in **Priority Group 3**, which
carries **$0 copay for outpatient and inpatient care** (2026 rates) — so enrollment alone delivers
nearly the whole benefit. Non-SC medications still carry Tier 1/2/3 copays of $5/$8/$11 per 30-day
supply against a **$700 calendar-year cap**.

**38 C.F.R. § 17.37(e)** — VR&E participation delivering the § 17.38 package **without enrollment** —
is a genuine but **redundant second door** here, not a unique one, precisely because PG3 already gets
you $0 copays. ⚠️ An earlier draft of this report called it "the single highest-value item in the
program"; that was overstated. See `FINANCIAL-BRIDGE-2026-09.md` §4.2 for the correction.

**What Chapter 31 medical care *does* add beyond PG3** (§ 21.240): items outside part 17 —
prosthetics, eyeglasses, corrective and assistive devices — and **brief mental-health services to
immediate family** (§ 21.155), which has **no PG3 equivalent** and is worth asking about given your
wife's caretaking role. Care can also begin during the **evaluation phase** (§ 21.240(c)(1)).

✅ **Ask about both on 09/17.** But file the 10-10EZ regardless of the VR&E outcome.

**Also free, and needing no enrollment at all:** **Vet Centers** (readjustment counseling) and the
**COMPACT Act** — free emergent suicide-crisis care at any VA or non-VA facility, up to 30 days
inpatient / 90 days outpatient, **988 → press 1**.

## 10. Sequencing: the genuine judgment call {#10-sequencing}

**Question:** force a Chapter 31 entitlement decision now at 10%, or defer and reapply after the MH
claim is granted?

**The premise that made this urgent is gone.** **38 U.S.C. § 3103(g)** 🟢, verbatim: "_Subsection (a)
shall not apply to a veteran who was discharged or released from active military, naval, or air
service on or after January 1, 2013._" Discharge **2013-11-22**. **No deadline. No reason to rush.**

> ⚠️ **Trap:** 38 C.F.R. part 21 subpart A was **never amended** for § 3103(g) — the string "January
> 1, 2013" appears nowhere in it, and §§ 21.41–21.44 still read as though the 12-year clock always
> runs. **If anyone invokes the 12-year rule, cite the statute. It controls.**
>
> Also ⚠️: **no authority was found that the 12-year period _restarts_ on a new SC rating.**
> § 21.42(a) is a **commencement** rule, not a restart rule. Any advice that "the MH grant resets the
> clock" is wrong — it is simply **moot** here under § 3103(g).

### The case for proceeding now

- **A denial is non-prejudicial.** § 21.186(c) 🟢: ineligible status "_is a bar to reconsideration of
  eligibility **unless a material change in circumstances occurs**._" A grant of service connection at
  a compensable rating is a textbook material change.
- **At ≥20%, the burden drops.** § 21.40(a) then requires only an **employment handicap** (§ 21.51),
  eliminating the serious-employment-handicap burden of § 3102(a)(2). Strictly easier.
- **Information has value** — you learn the VRC's actual read, and you get the § 17.37(e) health-care
  access immediately.
- **Reapplying and filing a Supplemental Claim are the same remedy**, not competing strategies.

### The case against — and it is serious

Because **§ 21.53(c) requires reasonable doubt to be resolved _in favor of feasibility_**, the likely
VRC output on these facts is **not** "vocational goal infeasible." It is: _"no serious employment
handicap; tinnitus does not contribute in substantial part; achievement of a vocational goal is
reasonably feasible."_

**That is a VA-authored, government-agency finding of employability**, sitting in the C-file that
M21-1 instructs the rating adjudicator to pull (§7.5) — at exactly the moment a § 4.16(b)
extraschedular referral needs the record pointing the other way.

### The resolution

⚠️ **This is a genuine judgment call, not a settled question.** It turns on a fact not resolvable from
the record: how this VRC will characterize feasibility in writing.

**The hedge that survives both branches:**

1. **Attend. Participate. Keep every statement factual and symptom-accurate** — describe your actual
   day concretely rather than reaching for either summary characterization (the ACTION-CARD's
   remaining open blocker).
2. **Ask whether the entitlement determination can be held pending the MH claim** — already the
   highest-value question on the ACTION-CARD. § 21.42 deferral is the mechanism. If she says yes,
   the dilemma dissolves.
3. **Defer business formation and revenue** until the MH rating and any TDIU decision are final.
4. **Run PCPG, DVOP, TWC-VRS, and VBOC in parallel** — none requires service connection, and **none
   generates a VA finding of employability.**

### If denied

**§ 21.416(b)** 🟢 gives three options within **one year** of the notice — and the prior corpus
characterization of the first one needs correcting:

| Option                                                                                                                                                                             | Fit here                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Supplemental Claim** (§ 3.2501, "_except that a complete application in writing on a form prescribed by the Secretary will not be required and a hearing will not be provided_") | ✅ **The right vehicle.** The MH grant is new and relevant evidence; the no-form/no-hearing relaxation makes it lowest-friction; functionally identical to reapplying under § 21.186(c) |
| **Higher-Level Review** (§ 3.2601)                                                                                                                                                 | ❌ Wrong fit — de novo review on the **same** record, **no new evidence**. Only useful for a clear misapplication of § 21.51/§ 21.52                                                    |
| **Board appeal** (part 20)                                                                                                                                                         | ⚠️ Preserves the earliest effective date; slowest lane. ⚠️ VR&E-specific timeliness data **not found**                                                                                  |

**Also available independently of § 21.416:** **§ 21.414** revision for clear and unmistakable error
(§ 3.105(a)) or **difference of opinion** (§ 3.105(b)) — the latter is the underused informal route
for a judgment-call VRC denial. 🟢

And remember: **§ 21.98 no longer exists** (removed 2019-02-19, 84 FR 138). There is no separate
internal VR&E appeal channel.

## Method and honesty note

Six parallel research lanes ran on 2026-09-14 against primary sources (eCFR renderer and versioner
APIs, uscode.house.gov, govinfo, BLS, GAO, VA OIG, BVA), with secondary and .gov-guidance sources
labeled as such. Confidence markers are carried through from the underlying research rather than

**Three things this document gets right that the prior corpus got wrong:** the entitlement-charging
section is **§ 21.79**, not § 21.74; the P911SA rate election **does not** consume Chapter 33 months
(§ 21.79(f)(2)); and the self-employment track is **not** limited to veterans for whom
self-employment is the only feasible option (75 FR 3170).

**One thing this document may get wrong:** the recommendation argues against a stated preference. The
strongest counterargument is in §5.1 and §12.3 — AI freelance rates are genuinely elevated, the
autonomy need is real and clinically sensible, and a single committed client would materially change
the math. **The argument here is about the vehicle and the timing, not about the goal.** If the
consulting practice is what you want, §9 Move 3 is how to build it without paying for it with the
disability claim.
