## 1. The five tracks

Current VA.gov terminology 🟢 (`va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/programs/`).
This supersedes older "five tracks" blog naming — some guides still merge Reemployment and Rapid
Access, which the current site treats as distinct.

| Track                                     | For                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Reemployment**                          | Returning to a former job with employer accommodation support |
| **Rapid Access to Employment**            | Job search using existing marketable skills                   |
| **Self-Employment**                       | Starting a business                                           |
| **Employment Through Long-Term Services** | Education/training for a new field                            |
| **Independent Living**                    | When employment is not currently feasible                     |

### 1.1 Self-Employment — 38 C.F.R. §§ 21.257, 21.258, 21.214 🟢

- **Approval bar.** Must flow from the § 21.50 initial evaluation and satisfy § 21.257. Where SC
  limitations are so severe that self-employment is "the only reasonably feasible vocational
  goal," § 21.257(b)/(e) triggers **enhanced** assistance under § 21.214.
- **VA funds** (§ 21.257(d)): vocational training in running the business, incidental
  business-management training, license/permit fees, necessary tools and supplies, plus standard
  employment services (§ 21.252). Enhanced tier adds minimum stocks of materials/inventory and
  essential equipment/machinery/fixtures.
- **VA does not fund** 🟡: working capital beyond minimum start-up stock, general small-business
  financing, real estate, ongoing operating expenses. _(The CFR is written affirmatively about
  what IS funded; this negative list is practitioner-guide consensus, not literal regulatory
  text.)_
- **Business plan required** (§ 21.257(f)) 🟢: feasibility analysis covering economic viability,
  cost of VA assistance, market analysis, availability of non-VA financing, **SBA coordination**,
  business-site location/costs, and the veteran's own training plan.
- **Cost approval** (§ 21.258) 🟢: under **$25,000** → VR&E Officer; **$25,000+** → Executive
  Director, VR&E Service.
  > ⚠️ **Threshold conflict.** A 2025-era practitioner guide 🟡 states current _internal_
  > delegation differs — VRC-level excludes self-employment entirely, VREO up to **$49,999**,
  > Executive Director above that. M28C delegations update more often than CFR rulemaking.
  > **Ask the VRC directly; do not rely on either figure.**
- **Timeline:** ⚠️ no published figure. Slower than the other tracks because of the feasibility
  review, SBA coordination, and possible HQ escalation. Months, not weeks.
- **"Minimum/medium/maximum" service tiers:** ⚠️ **not found in current CFR** — likely legacy
  pre-2010 terminology. The current distinction is binary: general services under § 21.257(d)
  vs. enhanced services under § 21.214.

### 1.2 Rapid Access to Employment 🟢

- For veterans with existing marketable skills seeking immediate employment rather than retraining.
- Services: job-readiness prep, résumé development, job-search and placement support. **No
  tuition/degree funding.**
- Fastest track to a finalized plan (weeks). ⚠️ No VA-published SLA.
- **What it forecloses — the important part.** Choosing Rapid Access does **not** permanently
  foreclose Long-Term Services, but the timing rule matters:
  - **Before the plan is signed**, switching tracks is an ordinary counseling conversation.
  - **After the case closes as "rehabilitated"**, reopening is governed by **§ 21.284** 🟢, which
    requires one of: SC disability worsened such that the veteran can no longer perform the
    trained occupation (§ 21.284(a)); the previous occupation is unsuitable given the employment
    handicap; or, under § 21.284(c), the original services were inadequate, employment prospects
    "should not have been expected," or **technological change** made the occupation obsolete.
  - **Bottom line: track selection is not locked until VA Form 28-8872 is signed.** "I changed my
    mind" is not a § 21.284 trigger.

### 1.3 Employment Through Long-Term Services 🟢

- **VA pays:** tuition, fees, books, supplies, tools/equipment, **plus subsistence allowance**
  (§2 below). Covers remedial/refresher coursework, post-secondary education (including graduate
  degrees where the credential is a generally-accepted hiring requirement), OJT, apprenticeships,
  and non-paid work experience 🟡.
- **48-month aggregate cap — 38 U.S.C. § 3695** 🟢: Chapter 31 combined with Ch. 30/33/35 and
  other listed programs generally may not exceed 48 months. Exceptions permitting extension:
  - Previously trained in a suitable occupation, but the SC disability has since worsened so the
    veteran can no longer perform it
  - The previously-trained occupation is now unsuitable given abilities and employment handicap
  - Additional Chapter 31 months needed to reach employability — provided **Chapter 31 alone**
    does not exceed 48 months
  - **§ 21.78** 🟢 "Approving more than 48 months of rehabilitation" is the operative mechanism
- **§ 21.79** 🟢 "Determining entitlement usage under Chapter 31" is the entitlement-charging
  provision. **Corrected 2026-09-14:** earlier drafts of this KB cited **§ 21.74** for this — that
  is a **miscitation**. § 21.74 is **"Extended evaluation."** Verified against the eCFR renderer
  API; do not cite § 21.74 for entitlement math.
  - **§ 21.79(a)** 🟢 — day-for-day charging: "a veteran who pursues a rehabilitation program for
    1 day should be charged 1 day of entitlement," at the rate of pursuit set by § 21.310.
  - **§ 21.79(b)** 🟢 — **no charge** against Chapter 31 entitlement while the veteran is (1)
    receiving employment services under an **IEAP**, (2) receiving an **employment adjustment
    allowance**, or (3) on VA-unauthorized leave. Post-training job-search support is therefore
    _free_ of entitlement.
  - **§ 21.79(c)** 🟢 — charges may be made **only** in case status Rehabilitation to the Point of
    Employability, Extended Evaluation, or Independent Living.

### 2.2 The Post-9/11 rate election (P911SA)

**Authority:** 38 U.S.C. § 3108; 38 C.F.R. §§ 21.260, 21.264 🟢. **Form: VA Form 28-0987**,
"Election for CH 31 Subsistence Allowance (CH31SA) Rate or Post-9/11 Subsistence Allowance
(P911SA) Rate."

- **Who:** a veteran with _concurrent_ Chapter 31 and Chapter 33 eligibility.
- **Amount — § 21.260(c)** 🟢: equals **BAH under 37 U.S.C. § 403 for an E-5 _with dependents_**,
  based on the **training facility's ZIP code** — and it is **not adjusted for the veteran's
  actual dependent count**; it always uses the with-dependents E-5 rate. **Distance/online-only**
  instead uses fractions of the **BAH National Average**: full-time = ½, ¾-time = ⅜,
  half-time = ¼.
- **Revocability — § 21.264** 🟢: re-election back to the Chapter 31 rate generally only **after
  completion of a term, quarter, semester, or other period of instruction**. Two exceptions:
  Chapter 33 eligibility ends before the term does, or immediate re-election is necessary to
  prevent the veteran being unable to continue. **Choose deliberately at the start of a term.**
- ✅ **RESOLVED 2026-09-14 — electing the P911SA _rate_ does NOT consume Post-9/11 _months_.**
  The answer was never in § 21.260 or § 21.264; it is in **38 C.F.R. § 21.79(f)(2)**, verified
  verbatim via the eCFR renderer API:

  > "…the entitlement usage is deducted from the veteran's chapter 31 entitlement. **No
  > entitlement charges are made against chapter 33.**" (Authority: 38 U.S.C. 3108(b))

  § 21.79(f)(1) does the same for the Chapter 30 rate election ("No entitlement charges are made
  against either chapter 30 or chapter 34"). **The election changes the dollar rate only.**
  Earlier drafts of this KB recorded this as unresolved with "primary CFR text is silent" — that
  was a **search failure, not regulatory silence.** The lesson worth keeping: the answer to a
  cross-program charging question lives in the _charging_ section, not in the _rate_ section.

- **The direction of the cap that _does_ bite** 🟢: Chapter 31 usage is **not** charged against
  Chapter 33, but Chapter 33 usage **does** consume the shared **48-month aggregate** —
  38 U.S.C. § 3695(b) (Ch. 31 is handled in (b), not the (a) list), implemented at §§ 21.78(a)
  and 21.4020(b). So GI Bill months already spent reduce the Chapter 31 months available, while
  Chapter 31 months spent leave the GI Bill balance intact. **38 U.S.C. § 3322(a)** separately
  bars _concurrent_ receipt — an election is required, not a stacking.
- **Still ask the VRC** 🟡: whether the Chapter 33 **half-of-national-average** rule for
  online-only training carries into the Chapter 31 P911SA computation. § 21.260(c) as retrieved
  does not settle it, and it halves the number if it applies.
- **Computing a real BAH figure:** DoD BAH calculator
  (`travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/BAH-Calculator/`), pay grade **E-5,
  with dependents**, ZIP of the actual training site (or the BAH National Average for
  online-only). Note BAH changes every **January 1** while VR&E rates change **October 1** — the
  cycles do not align, so confirm which rate-table year VA is applying.

## 3. VA Form 28-1902w 🟢

Retrieved and read directly: `vba.va.gov/pubs/forms/vba-28-1902w-are.pdf`
(also `va.gov/forms/28-1902w/`).

- **Official title:** "Information for Veteran Readiness and Employment Entitlement
  Determination" — ⚠️ **not** "worksheet"; that word appears nowhere on the form.
- **OMB:** 2900-0092, expires 11/30/2027 · **Edition:** NOV 2024 (supersedes JUL 2024) ·
  **Stated burden:** 45 minutes.
- ⚠️ **"Must submit" is UNVERIFIED.** VA.gov describes it as information a VRC reviews. The
  _appointment email_ requests it in advance; no primary source was found making submission a
  precondition of the evaluation. Prefer "VA requests" over "VA requires."

**Design point that changes how to use it:** by its own instructions the form is completed **with
the VRC during the evaluation** — it is the counselor's interview script. Being asked to submit it
beforehand means **pre-writing the interview**, which sets the agenda.

### 3.2 What drives the handicap finding

1. **Section II, Items 4–9** — the "do duties aggravate your SCDs" question repeated per job, and
   Item 9's checklist tying performance/relations/absenteeism explicitly back to SCDs.
2. **Section VII, Items 19–20** — the medical-impairment-to-employability link § 21.51/§ 21.52
   requires.
3. Section III matters for _vocational goal feasibility_, not for EH itself.

## 5. The assessments

**Texas Career Check Interest Profiler** (texascareercheck.com) 🟢 — 60 items across 4 pages,
5-point Strongly Dislike → Strongly Like. Output is a set of matched occupations. ~10–15 min
(⚠️ estimated from item count, not a stated figure). 🟡 Almost certainly Holland/RIASEC-based,
though the tool's own page does not label the methodology. **Measures interests, not ability.**

**The aptitude instrument** — ⚠️ **not identified.** VR&E most commonly uses **CareerScope**
(measures interests _and_ aptitudes together); **COPES** and the **O\*NET Interest Profiler** also
appear in VR&E practice. Since the appointment email attached _two separate_ documents, the
likeliest read is Texas Career Check = interests, something else (probably CareerScope) =
aptitude. **Confirm from the instructions PDF or with the VRC.**

**How results are used.** They feed the § 21.50(c) "abilities, aptitudes, and interests" analysis
and shape the vocational goal. Per M28C.IV.B.2.01 🟡, vocational exploration exists "to assist the
claimant in selecting a suitable vocational objective that is consistent with his/her abilities,
aptitudes and interests, and **does not aggravate** his/her disability(ies)."

**The honest-answering point, stated precisely.** A mismatch between _claimed limitations_ and
_assessed aptitude_ undercuts both the handicap narrative and goal feasibility. But strong
aptitude scores are **not** inconsistent with a legitimate handicap claim — and deliberately
depressing them is self-defeating, because low scores argue against § 21.53 feasibility without
helping causation. The distinction that matters: a claim that a specific limitation precludes the
work, contradicted by the instrument, is the damaging kind. A claim about _sustaining_ work is
fully compatible with high aptitude.

## 6. The evaluation and what follows

**Expected question order** tracks the 28-1902w section sequence (§3.1) — the form _is_ the
script. Realistic 30-minute shape: contact verification → employment history job-by-job →
Item 9 SCD checklist → military history → legal → substance use → education → **disability
narrative and TDIU status (most unstructured time; where entitlement is decided)** → referral
questions → next steps.

**Documents to have on hand**, implied by the form's own provided/not-provided checkboxes:
résumé, DD-214, transcripts/certifications/licenses — plus medical records and any FMLA / PIP /
severance documentation showing how the SCD drove employment disruption.

**Group orientation** is typically required before the individual evaluation 🟡; missing it twice
can cause disenrollment and require reapplication.

**After the evaluation:**

- **Entitlement decision** — written determination on EH/SEH. ⚠️ No published VA SLA found.
- **Rehabilitation Plan, VA Form 28-8872** 🟢 (also called the IWRP). Governing regs
  §§ 21.80, 21.84 🟢 (titles confirmed; full text not pulled). This is a **VBA internal form
  completed by the VRC with the veteran** — not something the veteran files. Current published
  revision is Jul 2010.
- **Signed after** the entitlement decision, once a vocational goal and track are agreed. 🟡 A
  secondary estimate of 30–90 days is a planning figure, **not** a regulatory deadline.
- ⚠️ The VRC's own determination may be captured on an internal form (secondary sourcing suggests
  **VAF 28-1902b**, distinct from 28-1902w) — **UNVERIFIED**; ask which form and how to get a copy.
