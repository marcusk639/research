# VR&E / Chapter 31 — Section Index

Veteran Readiness & Employment (38 U.S.C. ch. 31) documentation. **This is a separate benefit
track from the disability compensation claim** (ch. 11) that the rest of `va-disability/` covers —
different statute, different regulations, different decision-maker, different appeal posture. It
shares only the C-file, which is exactly where the risk lives (see §"Cross-claim exposure").

## The case in five lines

  eligibility does not apply**, per 38 U.S.C. § 3103(g).
- **Only service connection is tinnitus at 10%** — which permits entitlement _only_ through the
  **serious employment handicap** door, § 3102(a)(2). There is no second path today.
- **Severity is strong; causation is weak.** On this corpus's own reading, **six of the nine**
  § 21.52(b) factors favor him — an advocate's count, not a VA finding; factor 4 (adequacy of
  education) cuts against, and a VRC will say so. Set against roughly **a decade of residential
  and PHP treatment, 2016–2026** (ten documented episodes plus one self-reported — see prep §5.1;
  do not lead with a count). But the significant impairment must trace **in substantial part to
  tinnitus** — and the genuinely disabling conditions are not service-connected yet.
- **The pending MH supplemental claim (filed 2026-05-19) is the decisive lever.** A grant either
  reaches 20%+ (opening the easier employment-handicap door) or supplies conditions that actually
  carry SEH causation.
- **28-1900 was filed 2026-07-25.** 28-1902w + two assessments are requested before the
  appointment.

## Cross-claim exposure — the reason this section is not self-contained

VR&E statements land in the C-file and can be read by the rating board deciding the pending
compensation claim. The two programs reward opposite showings: VR&E requires that rehabilitation
be **reasonably feasible**; schedular 100% requires **total occupational _and_ social impairment**.

The framing that is simultaneously true and consistent — _if it is accurate for this veteran_ — is
**§ 21.51(b)**: a veteran "who qualifies for a suitable job, but who does not obtain or keep the
job for reasons beyond his or her control." Obtaining versus retaining. See prep doc §9.

Also live: `claim-maximization-research-2026-06.md` §D proposes VR&E to fund the **CPA-firm
transition**, while line 157 of the same file calls a staged CPA-firm transition _"the paradigm
case of VA benefits fraud."_ Do not name accounting as a vocational goal without working it
through with the rep first.

## Research method notes

- **eCFR:** the HTML UI is bot-blocked (302 to `unblock.federalregister.gov`); the **API works** —
  `/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-38?part=21&subpart=A&section=NN` for text,
  `/api/versioner/v1/versions/title-38.json?section=NN` for removal history. Use **govinfo** for
  the Federal Register and Statutes at Large.
- **A removed CFR section returns 404**, not a tombstone — indistinguishable from an unreachable
  source unless you check the versioner API. That is how § 21.98's 2019 removal went unresolved
  through two research passes.
- **M28C could not be retrieved** — KnowVA is JavaScript-rendered. Every M28C reference in this
  section is directional, not citable verbatim.
- **`personal-records/` does not exist in this checkout.** No fact here is verified against a
  primary record locally; "CONFIRMED" means a corpus document asserts it.
