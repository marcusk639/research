## 4. Entitlement Analysis

> Citation status is marked per item. A verification pass was run against primary sources
> because a prior pass on this corpus had 40 of 109 citations misstated. See §10.

### 4.1 The two doors, and which one you are at

**38 U.S.C. § 3102(a)** creates exactly two paths to Chapter 31 entitlement:

- **§ 3102(a)(1)** — SC disability **rated 20% or more**, _and_ an **employment handicap**.
- **§ 3102(a)(2)** — SC disability **rated at 10 percent**, _and_ a **serious employment
  handicap**.

You are at the second door. There is no third door, and discharge date does not create one —
that variable lives entirely in § 3103 (period of eligibility), not § 3102 (who qualifies).

VA's own appointment email states the same rule in plain language: _"If your service-connected
disability rating is less than 20%... then a serious employment handicap must be found to
establish entitlement to VR&E services."_

### 4.2 The 12-year period does not apply to you

**38 U.S.C. § 3103(g):** _"Subsection (a) shall not apply to a veteran who was discharged or
released from active military, naval, or air service on or after January 1, 2013."_

You were discharged **11/22/2013**. The 12-year bar is switched off for you entirely.

**Correction to a widely repeated citation error — this matters and the source prompt had it
wrong.** This carve-out was **not** created by "the Isakson Act, Pub. L. 115-48." It was
created by **Pub. L. 116-315, Title I, § 1025(a), 134 Stat. 4960, enacted January 5, 2021** —
the _Johnny Isakson and David P. Roe, M.D. Veterans Health Care and Benefits Improvement Act
of 2020_, § 1025 being titled "Elimination of period of eligibility for training and
rehabilitation for certain veterans with service-connected disabilities."

**Pub. L. 115-48 is a different statute entirely** — the _Harry W. Colmery Veterans
Educational Assistance Act of 2017_ ("Forever GI Bill"), enacted 08/16/2017. Its § 402 did
amend § 3103, but only subsection **(f)** (tolling for veterans recalled to active duty),
which has nothing to do with the 2013 discharge carve-out. Both laws amend § 3103, which is

### 4.3 What "serious employment handicap" actually requires

**38 U.S.C. § 3101(8)** defines SEH as _"a significant impairment, resulting in substantial
part from a service-connected disability rated at 10 percent or more, of a veteran's ability
to prepare for, obtain, or retain employment consistent with such veteran's abilities,
aptitudes, and interests."_

Compare **§ 3101(2)**, plain employment handicap: _"an impairment, resulting in substantial
part from a disability described in section 3102(1)(A)..."_ — i.e. the **20%** clause.

**Two differences, not one.** SEH adds *"significant"*, **and** it uses a different rating
predicate: EH keys to the § 3102(a)(1)(A) **20%-or-more** disability, while SEH keys to **any
service-connected disability rated 10 percent or more** — which is *broader*, not narrower. An
earlier draft of this document said "same test, minus 'significant'"; that was wrong.

So SEH adds **one word that does all the work: _significant_.** The VRC must find not merely
that your ability to get or keep suitable work is impaired, but that the impairment is
significant, and that a **service-connected** disability contributes **in substantial part** to it.

The regulations build this in three steps:

- **38 C.F.R. § 21.50(b)(1)–(3)** — the initial evaluation. The VRC determines (1) whether an
  employment handicap exists, (2) whether an individual with an EH has a _serious_ employment
  handicap, and (3) whether a vocational goal is reasonably feasible under § 21.53. The factor
  list at § 21.50(c) is separately load-bearing — § 21.51(c)(2) incorporates it by reference.
- **38 C.F.R. § 21.51** — employment handicap requires **all** of: (a) a vocational impairment;
  (b) that it has **not been overcome** through employment; and (c)(1) that the SC disability
  _"contribute in substantial part to the individual's overall vocational impairment"_ — an
  _"identifiable, measurable, or observable causative effect"_ — though it **"need not be the
  sole or primary cause."**
- **38 C.F.R. § 21.52** — SEH layers on the "significant" requirement, plus **exactly nine**
  enumerated factors at **§ 21.52(b)(1)–(9)** that VA "will develop and assess": (1) number of
  disabling conditions; (2) severity of disabling condition(s); (3) existence of
  **neuropsychiatric** condition(s); (4) adequacy of education or training for suitable
  employment; (5) number, length, and frequency of periods of unemployment **or
  underemployment**; (6) a pattern of reliance on government support programs; (7) extent and
  complexity of services needed to achieve rehabilitation; (8) negative attitudes toward
  individuals with disabilities and other restrictions on suitable employment — expressly
  including **"alcoholism or other substance abuse"** and discrimination; and (9) other factors
  relating to preparing for, obtaining, or keeping employment.

  **Read that list again — factors 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, and 8 all favor you, and factor 8 names
  substance abuse explicitly.** Your eleven documented treatment episodes, heavy psychiatric
  involvement, and unemployment history are not obstacles to this finding; they _are_ the
  finding. The severity side of § 21.52 is genuinely strong for you. Only causation is weak.

### 4.5 Feasibility — the one standard that strongly favors you

**38 C.F.R. § 21.53** governs whether a vocational goal is reasonably feasible, and it is
lopsided in your favor:

  feasibility."_ (Verified verbatim — this is the cleaner, stronger citation of the two.)
- **§ 21.53(e)(2)**: _"A finding that achievement of a vocational goal is infeasible **without a
  period of extended evaluation** requires compelling evidence which establishes infeasibility
  beyond any reasonable doubt."_

> **Correction caught in verification — do not overstate this one.** An earlier draft of this
> document stated the "compelling evidence / beyond any reasonable doubt" standard as governing
> _any_ infeasibility finding. It does not. By its terms it governs infeasibility found
> **without first conducting a period of extended evaluation**. Still protective, but the
> stretch is exactly the kind a VR&E Officer or Board attorney catches — lean on § 21.53(c).

### 4.6 The pending claim — can the VRC use it?

Sections 3102 and 21.40 speak in the present tense: a veteran who **"has"** a disability
"rated at" a percentage. There is no provision found that lets a VRC treat a _pending,
undecided_ claim as an existing rating for entitlement purposes. (The one "will likely be"
exception, § 3102(a)(1)(A)(ii), applies only to servicemembers still hospitalized pending
discharge — not you.)

**But that is about the rating percentage, not about evidence.** On this corpus's reading — an
**inference, not an express rule; no CFR or M28C provision was found on point** (see legal KB §5)
— nothing stops the VRC from
considering the medical evidence underlying that pending claim as part of the § 21.52
multi-factor severity analysis. That distinction is worth understanding precisely: _the
pending claim cannot give you a percentage today, but the records behind it can inform how
significant your impairment is._

### 4.7 If you are denied

A denial is recoverable. Three options, none of which are foreclosed by attending on Thursday:

1. **File a new 28-1900** after the MH claim is granted. Likely the cleanest path — entitlement is

2. **Supplemental Claim** under **38 C.F.R. § 21.416(b)(1)**, within **one year** of the

3. **Higher-Level Review** (§ 21.416(b)(2)–(3)) or **Board appeal** if you think the denial

### 9.1 The structural tension

The two programs reward opposite showings:

- **VR&E** requires that rehabilitation be **reasonably feasible** — that you _can_ be restored
  to suitable employment.
- **Schedular 100% / TDIU** turns on **total occupational impairment** — that you _cannot_
  maintain substantially gainful employment.

**An earlier draft of this document said declining TDIU "reduces the collision." That was sloppy
reasoning and it is now wrong twice over.** First, per `RATING-REPORT-REMEDIATION-PLAN-2026-07-16.md`
TDIU **is** being developed (§9.0). Second, even on the schedular-100% route the target is
"total occupational **and** social impairment" — **both prongs**. Declining TDIU would at most
narrow the collision to one prong; it would not reduce your exposure on the occupational prong
at all, because the occupational prong is required by the very target the corpus is pursuing.
Under _Rice v. Shinseki_ VA must consider TDIU once unemployability is raised regardless.

### 9.2 The position that is both true and consistent

severe psychiatric claim (recurrent occupational failure is evidence of impairment). It does
