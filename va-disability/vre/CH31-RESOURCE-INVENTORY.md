# Chapter 31 Resource Inventory — Everything Obtainable, and What It Is Worth

**Purpose.** `VRE-BENEFITS-KB.md` covers the five tracks, subsistence rates, and form mechanics.
This document covers **everything else** — the twenty-odd distinct benefits inside Chapter 31 that
are never listed on the VA.gov track pages, plus the parallel programs that require **no Chapter 31
entitlement at all**.

**Why it exists.** The three options under consideration (leave tech / retrain / consult) were
being compared on the wrong axis — "which track does VA approve" — when several of the
highest-value resources here are **track-independent**, and two of the most valuable require no
VR&E entitlement whatsoever. Read Part IV before Part I if you only read one section.

# PART I — The benefits nobody lists

## 1. Health care without enrollment — real, but smaller than it looks at 10%

**38 C.F.R. § 17.37(e)** 🟢, verbatim:

> "Subject to the provisions of § 21.240, a veteran participating in VA's Veteran Readiness and
> Employment (VR&E) program described in §§ 21.1 through 21.430 **will receive VA care provided for
> in the 'medical benefits package' set forth in § 17.38**"

**What this means.** It bypasses the § 17.36 enrollment requirement and the priority-group system
entirely. Chapter 31 participation is _itself_ a health-care access route.

- **38 C.F.R. § 21.240** 🟢 — Chapter 31 medical care includes everything in 38 C.F.R. part 17,
  **plus**, even if not in part 17: prosthetic appliances, eyeglasses, corrective and assistive
  devices, **services to the veteran's family**, and specialized blindness/deafness services.
- **Covered periods** — § 21.240(c): initial evaluation, extended evaluation, rehabilitation to
  employability, ILP, employment services, **and periods needed to re-enter after an interruption
  or an illness/injury discontinuance** (§ 21.240(c)(6)).
- **38 C.F.R. § 17.47(i)** 🟢 defines "participating" broadly — including a veteran "being provided
  employment assistance" — and authorizes care (**including dental**) needed to enter a program,
  achieve its goals, **prevent interruption**, hasten return from interrupted or discontinued
  status, or **secure and adjust to employment**.
- **Dental** — 38 C.F.R. § 17.161(i) 🟢: a Chapter 31 participant "**may be authorized such dental
  services as are professionally determined necessary**." Discretionary, Class V.
- **Family services** — § 21.155 🟢: consultation, homecare training, counseling, and **brief
  mental-health services to immediate family**. Hard limit: "Extended medical, psychiatric or other
  services may not be furnished to family members."
- **Limit** — § 21.242 🟢: hospital care and medical services "shall only be furnished in facilities
  over which VA has direct jurisdiction," except contract/fee basis under part 17.

> ⚠️ **Do not overrate this — correction, 2026-09-14.** At **10% SC you are Priority Group 3**, which
> already carries **$0 copay for outpatient and inpatient care** (2026 rates). So § 17.37(e) is a
> **redundant second door, not a unique one** — **file VA Form 10-10EZ and get care on the merits.**
> An earlier draft of the companion options report called this "the single highest-value item in the
> program"; that was overstated and has been corrected.
>
> **What Chapter 31 medical care genuinely adds beyond PG3:** § 21.240 reaches items outside part 17
> (prosthetics, eyeglasses, corrective and assistive devices), and **§ 21.155 brief mental-health
> services to immediate family** — which has **no PG3 equivalent** and matters where a spouse is
> performing caretaking. Care can also begin during the **evaluation phase** (§ 21.240(c)(1)), before
> any entitlement finding. **Those are the reasons to ask about it — not the copays.**
>
> **Also needing no enrollment at all:** **Vet Centers** (readjustment counseling) and the **COMPACT
> Act** — free emergent suicide-crisis care at any VA or non-VA facility, 30 days inpatient / 90 days
> outpatient, **988 → press 1**.
>
> ⚠️ **Two stale cross-references to avoid.** § 17.161(i) points to "§ 17.47(g)" for the reasons
> list, but current § 17.47(g) concerns union/insurance liability — the real list is
> **§ 17.47(i)(2)**. And § 21.240's note cites "§ 17.48(g)," but current § 17.48 is the Compensated
> Work Therapy program. **Cite § 17.37(e) and § 17.47(i)(2).**

## 6. Verified negatives — things people assume exist and do not

- **No childcare or dependent-care benefit.** Across all of part 21 subpart A, "child care" appears
  only at § 21.324, as _good cause_ for interrupting a program. 🟢
- **No relocation allowance.** See above. 🟢
- **No working capital, operating cash, or owner's salary** in the self-employment track. See
  Part III. 🟢
- **No clothing** beyond required protective articles (§ 21.219(a)). 🟢
- **Chapter 34 rate election forfeits supplies entirely** (§ 21.210(d)); **Chapter 30 rate election
  forfeits tutorial assistance** (§ 21.148(d)) and requires affirmative re-election of subsistence
  to preserve the Employment Adjustment Allowance (§ 21.268(b)). Silent forfeitures. 🟢
- ⚠️ **Do not quote the § 3108(b)(1) statutory table** ($366/$454/$535 etc.). Those are un-indexed
  1980s base rates; § 3108(b)(2)–(3) require annual CPI adjustment, so the statute's own numbers are
  obsolete on their face. Use the FY-dated VA rate tables. 🟢

## 9. Independent Living — the option not on the table, and why it matters anyway

- **Services** — § 21.160(d) 🟢: anything authorizable for a vocational program **except a course of
  education or training**, plus training in independent-living skills, attendant care, health
  maintenance, and identifying housing accommodations.
- **Four postures** — § 21.160(c) 🟢: part of rehab to employability; part of an extended
  evaluation; **incidental to employment services**; or standalone.
- **Statutory national cap** — **38 U.S.C. § 3120(e)(1)** 🟢: "**Programs of independent living
  services and assistance shall be initiated for no more than 2,700 veterans in each fiscal
  year**," first priority to veterans whose vocational infeasibility results **solely** from a
  service-connected disability. A "capacity" denial is regulated, not arbitrary.
- **Gate** — § 3120(b) 🟢: requires a **serious employment handicap** _plus_ a finding that a
  vocational goal is not currently reasonably feasible.
- **ILP can convert back to employment** — § 21.160(c)(4)(ii) and § 21.78(c)(3) 🟢 expressly
  authorize >48 months "including cases in which achievement of a vocational goal becomes feasible
  during or following a program of independent living services."

> **Why this is in a document about three employment options.** An **ILP referral is functionally an
> infeasibility finding** (§ 21.53(f): IL is considered "in each case in which a vocational
> rehabilitation program is **not** found reasonably feasible"). That makes it the single
> highest-value VR&E artifact for a later TDIU claim — and the one thing that cannot be asked for
> without contradicting an employment request. **Do not chase it.** At 10% tinnitus it is not
> realistically available, and reaching for it would wreck the credibility asymmetry that governs
> everything else here.

# PART III — The self-employment track, corrected

## 11. The premise correction that changes the answer

**Prior framing in this corpus and elsewhere:** self-employment is approved only where it is "the
only reasonably feasible vocational goal," so an employable senior engineer is effectively barred.

**That is wrong.** § 21.257(b) is a **definition that triggers the enhanced supply tier**, not an
approval gate. VA deleted the employability restriction in the 2010 rulemaking. From the preamble,
**74 FR 19164 / 75 FR 3170** 🟢:

> "Current § 21.257(a) is **overly restrictive** because it maintains that self-employment is only
> available to an individual if access to the normal channels for suitable employment is limited by
> his or her disability(ies)… **Self-employment as a mode of employment is authorized for all
> program participants for whom it is deemed appropriate for achieving rehabilitation.**"

§ 21.257(d) 🟢 grants the base services to "**any individual for whom self-employment is determined
to be a suitable vocational goal.**" The real gates are upstream (employment handicap, § 21.50
initial evaluation) and downstream (§ 21.257(f) business-plan feasibility, § 21.258 cost
concurrence).

**Being objectively employable is not a bar. Good news — and it is the only unambiguously good news
in this Part.**

## 13. The process — seven steps

From **M28C.IV.C.5, "Self-Employment Plan"** (updated 2024-12-11) 🟡 (KnowVA is JavaScript-gated;
retrieved via rendered browser, so treat as .gov guidance rather than primary law):

1. **VRC suitability screen** — aptitudes, business-planning skill, disability stability, **the
   veteran's personal financial resources**, legal considerations, commitment. Documented on
   **VAF 28-1902n**.
2. **Phase One IWRP** — feasibility and viability objectives only. VR&E Officer concurrence _not_
   required at this stage.
3. **Mandatory SBA referral** — "_The claimant must be referred to SBA or other resources_":
   **SCORE, SBDC, VBOC**, plus VA OSDBU. Governed by a VBA–SBA MOU.
4. **Self-Employment Panel (SEP) — mandatory.** A VRC or Employment Coordinator, the referring VRC,
   and an **SBA business consultant or local self-employment expert**. Produces a feasibility
   report the VRC must address.
5. **The veteran writes the business plan** — a **stand-alone, lender-ready document** satisfying all
   seven § 21.257(f) elements 🟢: economic viability; VA cost analysis; market analysis;
   **availability of non-VA financing including personal resources**; **evidence of SBA coordination
   under § 8 of the Small Business Act**; site and cost; and a training plan.
6. **Phase Two** — amend the IWRP under §§ 21.214/21.252/21.257, then obtain concurrence.
7. **Post-approval** — **≥12 months of case management** in Job Ready status and **≥1 site visit**
   (or an online business review) before closure.

**⚠️ Cost-approval threshold — a live source conflict, both halves verified:**

**Timeline: ⚠️ NOT FOUND.** No VA-published processing time exists. The "90–180 days" figure in

## 15. Money during a self-employment plan — two provisions that gut the cash case

- ⚠️ **Neither subsistence table has a "self-employment" row.** The rate depends on how VA classifies
  the plan's _training_ component — institutional vs **training-in-the-home / independent
  instructor**, and § 21.260(c) pays the latter at only **½ the BAH National Average**. Financially
  decisive and unresolved. **Ask which row applies.**
- ⛔ **§ 21.260(d), the hard stop** 🟢: "_A veteran may not receive a subsistence allowance when VA is
  providing the veteran only the following services: (1) Initial evaluation; (2) Placement and
  post-placement services under 38 U.S.C. 3105(b); or (3) Counseling._"
  **Translation: subsistence funds the training phase. It does not fund the business-launch phase.**
  Once the plan moves from training into launching and running the business, the monthly money stops
  — precisely when a new consultancy has no revenue.
- ✅ Offsetting it slightly: the **Employment Adjustment Allowance** is expressly available for a
  "Self-employment program" at the **institutional** rate (§ 21.268(c)(5)) — **2 months**, not
  charged against entitlement.

## 16. Case law and the appeal line

- ⚠️ **No CAVC precedent** on Chapter 31 self-employment or § 21.257 was found. **NOT FOUND.**
- **BVA Citation Nr. 1506345** (2015-02-11) 🟢 — the only substantive § 21.257 merits decision
  located. Holds a **non-profit categorically ineligible**: "_a non-profit organization such as the
  one proposed by the Veteran in this case cannot be supported by VR&E services, as it cannot meet
  the requirement of 38 C.F.R. § 21.257(f)(5)._" The Board added it was "_unfortunate and perhaps
  unfair that VR&E Services initially led the Veteran to believe… his non-profit business plan was a
  viable option. However, under the law, such a plan is prohibited._"
  → **A VRC's verbal encouragement creates no entitlement. Get everything in writing.**
- **BVA Citation Nr. 22062709** (2022-11-14) 🟢 — a procedural win; VR&E Service itself found the
  discontinuance "improper" for lack of administrative review.
  → **Due-process defects are the productive appeal line, not feasibility merits.**
- **Helpful:** § 21.94(b)(2) 🟢 lets an existing IWRP be amended to self-employment on a showing that
  "circumstances have changed or new information has been developed which makes rehabilitation
  **more likely**" — **you do not have to prove the current goal infeasible.** This is the
  low-friction route in: accept a plan now, amend later.

## 17. Traps, ranked by how much they would cost

1. ⛔ **An already-operating business may disqualify you.** M28C 5.02 🟡 scopes the track to
   **startups**, and bars retroactive induction once a business has been "_established and
   operational for at least 12 months._" OIG faulted VA where "_9 of 45 CER files… VR&E staff
   approved self-employment plans for veterans with an established business_," calling ~$200,000
   improperly spent 🟢. **If a consulting LLC is already formed and billing, this is the single
   biggest structural risk — resolve it before mentioning a business to anyone.**
2. ⚠️ **Personal financial resources are screened both ways** — as a § 21.257 suitability factor and
   as § 21.257(f)(4) "availability of financing from non-VA sources." **A 6–12 month cash runway is
   an argument against a VA-funded plan.** True, and it will be noticed.
3. ⚠️ **Succeeding closes the case.** § 21.283(c)(1): rehabilitated after **60 continuous days**.
   § 21.284 then restricts re-entry. (A "12-month viable business" criterion attributed to CRS
   RL34627 could not be independently read and conflicts with § 21.283's 60 days — ⚠️ unverified.)
4. ⛔ **38 U.S.C. § 3104(a)(12)** 🟢 bars a program that would **aggravate** the service-connected
   condition. If the mental-health posture describes work-stress intolerance, a
   high-stress consultancy is arguably barred **on VA's own reading of your medical record.**
5. ⚠️ **Discontinuance grounds are broad** — declining the plan, non-responsiveness, unsatisfactory
   progress, failure to complete in the allowable time (§§ 21.362, 21.364).

# PART IV — Resources requiring NO Chapter 31 entitlement

**Read this Part before deciding anything.** Several of these are available **today**, regardless of
what happens on 09/17, and two of them do what VR&E is being asked to do — without the entitlement
gate, without the causation problem, and without generating a VA finding of employability.

## 18. SDVOSB certification — available now, at 10%

**This is the most actionable finding in the entire research pass.**

- **No minimum rating percentage exists.** 13 C.F.R. § 128.102 🟢: "_Service-disabled veteran means a
  veteran who is registered in [BIRLS]… as a service-disabled veteran_," and "_Service-connected has
  the meaning given that term in 38 U.S.C. 101(16)._" **A 10% rating for tinnitus qualifies.**
- **SDVOSB** = small business, **≥51% owned and controlled** by one or more service-disabled
  veterans (13 C.F.R. § 128.200) 🟢. Certification via **SBA VetCert**
  (`veterans.certify.sba.gov`), moved from VA to SBA on 2023-01-01.
- **Self-certification is dead** 🟢: certification has been required for SDVOSB set-aside and
  sole-source awards since 2024-01-01, and for prime/subcontract goaling credit since after
  2024-12-22 (§ 128.200(c)).
- **Set-aside value**, SBA's own wording 🟢: "_At least 5% of all federal contracting dollars each
  year are set aside specifically for certified SDVOSBs_," and "_The VA sets aside at least 7% of its
  contracts each year specifically for certified VOSBs and SDVOSBs._"
  ⚠️ Note the statutory government-wide SDVOSB goal is commonly cited as **3%** — the 5% figure is
  SBA's phrasing and was not independently reconciled.
- **8(a) is not a veteran program** 🟢 — it is socially/economically-disadvantaged based. Veteran
  status alone does not qualify.
- **7(a) fee relief** 🟢 exists for qualified veterans under the VET Act of 2015; the "Veterans
  Advantage" brand no longer appears on current pages and ⚠️ FY2026 fee specifics were not retrieved.

> **Reality check, unsourced analysis:** SDVOSB eligibility is **not a pipeline**. Winning federal
> work still requires SAM registration, NAICS codes (541511 / 541512 / 541690), past performance,
> and for DoD work a security posture. But sole-source authority plus VA's 7% goal is a real,
> legally durable advantage that a non-veteran competitor cannot replicate — and it costs a
> certification application, not a VR&E approval.

## 19. Louisiana Rehabilitation Services (LRS) — the state VR route, revised 2026-09-14

> 📍 **Superseded.** This section previously covered **Texas TWC-VRS**. The veteran now resides in
> **Lake Charles, Louisiana**, so the Texas agency is unavailable. The Louisiana equivalent is **LRS**,
> under the Louisiana Workforce Commission — and it **does** fund self-employment. 🟢

**Eligibility — no service connection, no VA rating, no DD-214** (LRS Policy Manual §109;
LAC 67:VII.109.A.1) 🟢: a physical or mental impairment that is a **substantial impediment to
employment**, a need for VR services, and an ability to benefit — **presumed** unless LRS rebuts it by
"clear and convincing evidence."

- **Self-employment is funded:** **$20,000** start-up cap; **20% client cash match**, cash only, waived
  for SSI/SSDI recipients; **business training and disability-related assistive technology sit OUTSIDE
  the cap**; occupational tools $4,000 / computer $2,000 (**uncapped if it qualifies as AT**). 🟢
- **Mandatory SBDC business-plan review**, with a submit → evaluate → revise → **resubmit** loop, and a
  letter from the SBDC to the counselor. The plan needs revenue/expense and profit estimates, local
  market analysis, insurance quotes, and a **6-month cash-flow projection**. 🟢
- ⛔ **Will not fund:** real estate, land, construction, **nonprofits**, **hobbies**, speculative
  ventures, **vehicles**, refinancing or existing debt, cash advances. 🟢
- **Office:** LRS **Region V**, 3616 Kirkman Street, Lake Charles, LA 70607 — **(337) 475-8038**;
  serves **Calcasieu**, Allen, Beauregard, Cameron, Jefferson Davis. 🟢
- ⭐ **The required SBDC is at McNeese State University** — Henning SEED Center, 4310 Ryan St Ste 162,
  **(337) 475-5529** — serving the identical five parishes. Free consulting; mandatory step = easy step. 🟢
- **Order of Selection: open, no waiting list** since 2022-08-15. ⚠️ Source is LRS-authored but `.org`-
  hosted — re-confirm by phone. ⚠️ A tinnitus-only profile likely lands in **Group III**; the
  **non-service-connected mental-health conditions** are what would move him to Group I/II.
- ⚠️ **Timeline:** eligibility within 60 days (statutory), IPE within 90 days of eligibility; realistic
  first-call-to-funded-plan **5–7 months**. ⛔ **Nothing is paid retroactively.**

> **Why this still matters strategically.** Every structural objection to the Chapter 31
> self-employment route — the 10% causation problem, the tinnitus-only SEH thin reed, the ~1% caseload
> share, the § 21.260(d) no-subsistence-at-launch stop — **does not apply to LRS.** Different agency,
> different statute, and **the qualifying disability need not be service-connected.**

⚠️ **Three caveats, all weaker than the Texas program it replaces:** (1) **no dedicated business
consultant** — TWC had certified BTACs; LRS outsources expertise to the SBDC and leaves decisions with
a generalist counselor (verified negative). (2) **Whether LRS serves an EXISTING business owner is NOT
FOUND** — policy is written entirely for *establishment*, the same trap as Chapter 31's startup-only
bar. (3) **VR&E coordination is undocumented** — LRS names "Veterans Administration" as a comparable
benefit to exhaust and is payer of last resort for higher education, but "Chapter 31," "Chapter 33,"
and "GI Bill" appear **nowhere** in its policy. **Get it in writing.**


## 20. Chapter 36 / Personalized Career Planning and Guidance (PCPG)

- ⚠️ **The form is VA Form 27-8832**, not 28-8832 (the 28- number is legacy VR&E-side) 🟢.
- **Eligibility** 🟢: qualifying for educational assistance under a VA education program **or**
  separated under other-than-dishonorable conditions within the last year **or** discharging within
  6 months. **No service connection required.** At 13 years post-discharge, the live hook is
  **remaining Chapter 33 eligibility** — so confirming the GI Bill balance matters twice.
- **Provides:** career, educational, academic and adjustment counseling; résumé support; goal
  planning. **Funds no training, no tuition, no subsistence.**
- **Value:** free, fast, and it generates **no adverse employability finding** beyond counseling
  notes. A weak substitute for Chapter 31 but a genuine no-downside parallel step.

## 21. DOL VETS — DVOP and LVER at American Job Centers

🟢 (dol.gov) — Jobs for Veterans State Grants. Eligibility: a veteran with an employment barrier;
**no service connection required.** DVOP specialists **prioritize veterans with significant barriers
to employment** — a list that expressly includes substance-use issues, mental or physical problems,
and low income. Provides individualized career services and case management; LVERs do employer
outreach. Active, with FY2026 funding expanding DVOP-servable populations.

## 22. SBA entrepreneurship ecosystem — free, and no SC requirement

🟢 (sba.gov)

- **VBOC** (Veterans Business Outreach Center) — "_business plan workshops, concept assessments,
  mentorship, and training_"; available to service members, veterans, Guard/Reserve, and spouses.
  VBOCs "_conduct on-site visits with clients to ensure adherence to their business plans_" and
  review monthly financials. **This is also the § 21.257(f)(5) SBA-coordination evidence** a Chapter
  31 business plan must show — so engaging a VBOC serves both paths at once.
- **Boots to Business Reboot** — open to "_veterans of all eras, National Guard and Reserve members,
  and military spouses_," online or local. **No SC requirement.**
- **SCORE** and **SBDC** — free mentoring and plan review; named in M28C as acceptable referral
  channels.
