# § 4.130 General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders + Controlling Case Law

Scope: the verbatim rating criteria at every level of 38 CFR § 4.130, and the
Federal Circuit / CAVC case law that controls how those criteria are actually
applied by an RVSR or reviewed by the Board. This is the file the harness checks
a proposed rating percentage against.

---

## 1. Verbatim criteria, 38 CFR § 4.130

Fetched from Cornell Legal Information Institute (LII), which mirrors the current
eCFR text: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.130

> **100%** — "Total occupational and social impairment, due to such symptoms as:
> gross impairment in thought processes or communication; persistent delusions or
> hallucinations; grossly inappropriate behavior; persistent danger of hurting
> self or others; intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living
> (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene); disorientation to time or
> place; memory loss for names of close relatives, own occupation, or own name."

> **70%** — "Occupational and social impairment, with deficiencies in most areas,
> such as work, school, family relations, judgment, thinking, or mood, due to
> such symptoms as: suicidal ideation; obsessional rituals which interfere with
> routine activities; speech intermittently illogical, obscure, or irrelevant;
> near-continuous panic or depression affecting the ability to function
> independently, appropriately and effectively; impaired impulse control (such as
> unprovoked irritability with periods of violence); spatial disorientation;
> neglect of personal appearance and hygiene; difficulty in adapting to stressful
> circumstances (including work or a worklike setting); inability to establish
> and maintain effective relationships."

> **50%** — "Occupational and social impairment with reduced reliability and
> productivity due to such symptoms as: flattened affect; circumstantial,
> circumlocutory, or stereotyped speech; panic attacks more than once a week;
> difficulty in understanding complex commands; impairment of short- and
> long-term memory (e.g., retention of only highly learned material, forgetting
> to complete tasks); impaired judgment; impaired abstract thinking;
> disturbances of motivation and mood; difficulty in establishing and
> maintaining effective work and social relationships."

> **30%** — "Occupational and social impairment with occasional decrease in work
> efficiency and intermittent periods of inability to perform occupational tasks
> (although generally functioning satisfactorily, with routine behavior,
> self-care, and conversation normal), due to such symptoms as: depressed mood,
> anxiety, suspiciousness, panic attacks (weekly or less often), chronic sleep
> impairment, mild memory loss (such as forgetting names, directions, recent
> events)."

> **10%** — "Occupational and social impairment due to mild or transient
> symptoms which decrease work efficiency and ability to perform occupational
> tasks only during periods of significant stress, or symptoms controlled by
> continuous medication."

> **0%** — "A mental condition has been formally diagnosed, but symptoms are not
> severe enough either to interfere with occupational and social functioning or
> to require continuous medication."

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.130 (mirroring current eCFR
text) — CONFIRMED verbatim (verified by citation-verifier 2026-07-16, cross-checked

## 2. What separates 70% from 100%

The operative word at 70% is **"most"** — deficiencies must be present across a
majority of the listed functional domains (work, school, family relations,
judgment, thinking, mood), not just one or two. The operative word at 100% is
**"total"** — impairment must be pervasive across _both_ the occupational and
the social sphere simultaneously. A veteran who is severely impaired
occupationally but retains some functional social relationships (or vice versa)
does not meet the 100% bar on that basis alone; the case law in § 3 below governs
how "most" and "total" are actually assessed against a real symptom picture.

## 3. Controlling case law

### 3.1 Mauerhan v. Principi, 16 Vet.App. 436 (2002) — the symptom list is non-exhaustive

The CAVC held that the "such as" language preceding each symptom list in
§ 4.130 means the enumerated symptoms are illustrative, not a required
checklist. VA is not required to find any specific number — or even any — of the
listed symptoms present to assign a given rating; the listed symptoms are
examples of the _type and degree_ of impairment that justify a rating level, and
a rater must consider all symptoms of record bearing on occupational and social
impairment, not only the ones named in the regulation:

> "The use of the term 'such as' in [38 C.F.R. § 4.130] demonstrates that the
> symptoms after that phrase are not intended to constitute an exhaustive list,
> but rather are to serve as examples of the type and degree of symptoms, or
> their effects, that would justify a particular rating."

Secondary sources reporting this holding (opinion itself was not independently
re-pulled for this file — see Verification Ledger):
https://veteranclaims.wordpress.com/2011/08/26/single-judge-application-mauerhan-v-principi-16-vet-app-at-442-ptsd/
and https://ratemyvso.net/dc/mental-health-rating-formula, which frames the
practical effect as: "A symptom not explicitly named in the criteria can still
support a higher rating if it contributes to overall impairment at that level."

### 3.2 Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki, 713 F.3d 112 (Fed. Cir. 2013) — severity, frequency, and duration, not a symptom count

The Federal Circuit held that a veteran qualifies for a given rating level not by
matching the exact listed symptoms, but by demonstrating symptoms of "similar
severity, frequency, and duration" to the ones listed, that in turn _cause_
occupational and social impairment in the functional domains the regulation
names:

> "[A veteran] may only qualify for a given disability rating under the
> regulation by demonstrating the particular symptoms associated with that
> percentage, or others of similar severity, frequency, and duration."

The court also emphasized that § 4.130 is "symptom driven" — symptoms are the
fact-finder's primary focus — but that the ultimate question is still the
resulting functional impairment level ("most areas" at 70%, "total" at 100%),
not a raw symptom tally.

Source: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/summary/opinion/us-federal-circuit/2013/04/08/263984.html
(case summary, fetched directly). Full opinion located at
https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/12-7114.opinion.4-3-2013.1.pdf but
could not be extracted by automated fetch (PDF returned as undecoded binary
stream) — flagged for the citation-verifier pass.

**Read together, Mauerhan and Vazquez-Claudio set the operative test:** the
symptom list is a set of examples, not a checklist (Mauerhan) — but a veteran
still has to show symptoms of comparable severity/frequency/duration that
actually _produce_ the described level of occupational and social impairment
(Vazquez-Claudio). Neither case licenses assigning a rating on symptom
vocabulary alone; both require tying symptoms to functional consequence.

### 3.3 Bankhead v. Shulkin, 29 Vet.App. 10 (2017) — suicidal ideation may support (not automatically compel) 70%

The CAVC's holding is **permissive, not mandatory**: the presence of suicidal
ideation — including passive ideation (e.g., a wish to be dead, without a plan or
intent) — "may cause occupational and social impairment with deficiencies in most
areas," and VA may not require evidence of an actual plan, intent, or attempt as a
precondition to considering it:

> "may cause occupational and social impairment with deficiencies in most areas"
> — Bankhead v. Shulkin, 29 Vet.App. 10 (2017), the Court's own language (not a
> secondary-source paraphrase).

Note: the phrase "suicidal ideation is the hallmark of the 70-percent rating for
PTSD," previously block-quoted here, does **not** appear in the Bankhead opinion —
it is a law firm's characterization of the holding (attigcurransteel.com), not the
Court's language. It has been removed as a direct quote; the sentence above
replaces it with the opinion's actual language.

Source (secondary characterization, not the opinion itself):
https://attigcurransteel.com/cases/veterans-court-suicidal-ideation-increase-rating-for-ptsd-70-percent/
(fetched directly; discusses a CAVC remand where the Board had improperly rated
at 50% despite documented years of chronic suicidal ideation).

**Practical rule for the harness:** documented suicidal ideation (passive or
active) must be considered and may not be discounted for lack of a plan, intent,
or attempt. SI **can support** a 70% evaluation but does **not automatically
compel** one — the holistic severity/frequency/duration analysis under
Vazquez-Claudio (§ 3.2 above) still governs whether the overall symptom picture
reaches "deficiencies in most areas." Whether the record additionally supports
100% still turns on the "total" occupational-and-social-impairment standard in
§ 2 above, which Bankhead does not relax.
