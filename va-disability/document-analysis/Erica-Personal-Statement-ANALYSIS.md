## Score: 3/10 (accuracy · completeness-for-purpose · consistency · credibility — NOT persuasiveness)

The substantive factual content checks out well against `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` (see Consistency Check below) — accuracy of the visible claims is not what's driving this score down. Completeness-for-purpose is also reasonable: the document touches most of the domains `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.1 says a genuine Erica statement doesn't need "engineered phrasing" to cover (isolation, marital strain, addiction cycle, occupational collapse). The score is driven almost entirely by **internal consistency** and **credibility risk**, which fail badly and are inseparable from each other here: the file itself, as saved at this path, still contains an appended "Notes" section (lines 35–41) written in the voice of a third-party drafter instructing "whoever finalizes this" that "Erica should read it and rewrite anything that doesn't sound like her, in her own words," that it is "a first draft built for tone and structure," and that certain figures were left out because they were "only in the 'coached' version." A document cannot simultaneously be a final, independently-authored statement and contain, unedited, editorial instructions addressed to a future editor about how to make it sound like the declarant's own words. That is a first-order internal-consistency failure in the artifact itself, independent of anything in the provenance log or the blog-draft comparison. Combined with the fact that this document's own governing risk-assessment document — `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md`, one day older — names this exact document/topic ("Erica's statement was directively coached," §9 item 3; "the single biggest credibility risk in the entire file set," §0.2) as the top unresolved threat to the whole claim, and that no visible edit distinguishes this "confirmed" file from the acknowledged AI-drafted version, the credibility-risk axis alone caps this well below a passing score regardless of how accurate the underlying facts turn out to be.

A document that is simultaneously (a) logged as Erica's confirmed, fully original final statement and (b) internally self-described, in its own unedited text, as someone else's unfinished draft awaiting her rewrite, is internally inconsistent on its face — this is anchored entirely within the document itself, not dependent on any cross-corpus comparison.

Nothing in this file's visible content demonstrates that step occurred — the body text is unchanged from the acknowledged AI-extracted draft, and the file even retains that draft's own editorial notes.
- **No differentiating edit is visible.** If Erica had, in fact, independently rewritten this in her own words after reading the AI-drafted version (a legitimate path per the corpus's own guidance — starting from a blank page, or even reviewing a "framing rules" explainer, is fine per `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.1, so long as the final wording is hers), one would expect at least some textual divergence from `BLOG-POST-DRAFT-OUR-STORY.md` and the removal of the "Notes" meta-commentary addressed to a third-party editor. - **Adversarial-exposure specifically:** if this exact file (including its Notes section) were ever produced — in a records request, an OIG referral, or any adversarial review — the Notes section alone would read as documentary proof that a "confirmed independently authored" witness statement began life as, and remains textually identical to, an AI-generated draft explicitly built from a coached email chain (per `BLOG-POST-DRAFT-OUR-STORY.md`'s own header and `EDIT-DECISIONS-BLOG-DRAFT.md`'s edit log). - This is a **credibility risk finding about the document as currently saved**, not a finding that the underlying observations in it are false — the underlying content (isolation, job losses, addiction cycle) is independently corroborated elsewhere in the corpus per the Consistency Check below.
