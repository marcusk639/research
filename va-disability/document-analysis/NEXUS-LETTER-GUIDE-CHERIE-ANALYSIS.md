## Summary

This document is a briefing memo, not evidence — its stated purpose (line 4) is to be "hand[ed] to
Cherie directly, or use[d] to brief her, so her letter addresses everything VA needs — in her own
clinical judgment, not scripted language." It lists five things a legally sufficient nexus letter
needs to cover (diagnoses, nexus-opinion standard/phrasing, dual occupational-and-social impairment,
frequency/severity/duration, OCD-specific content), lists materials she can review, flags two things
she should know about before being surprised by them, and closes with an explicit "what NOT to ask her
to do" section. It supersedes four earlier guide documents and states, up front, two corrections it
makes to what those earlier versions got wrong (missing OCD; a since-abandoned "iatrogenic SUD"
theory).

## Score: 8/10 (accuracy · completeness-for-purpose · consistency · credibility — NOT persuasiveness)

Accuracy and internal consistency are both strong — every specific factual claim in this document
that could be checked against `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` and `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` matched
(see Consistency Check below); no internal contradictions were found. Credibility risk is low: this
document is structurally the _opposite_ of the Erica-email-chain coaching pattern this corpus treats
as its central risk — it never proposes wording for Cherie to adopt, repeatedly conditions every
substantive ask on "if her clinical judgment supports it," and dedicates an entire closing section to
telling the veteran what not to ask her for. The one point costing this document a full point off a
9-10 is completeness-for-purpose: its own §7 is explicitly designed to prevent Cherie (and, by
extension, a C&P examiner) from being surprised by something in the record, but it omits the
suicidal-ideation frequency discrepancy that `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.2 and Part 3 item 6 rank as an
open item needing reconciliation "before it reaches a C&P examiner cold" — exactly the kind of thing
§7's stated purpose implies it should cover. This is a completeness gap, not an accuracy or
credibility problem.

## Strengths

- **Explicit non-coaching design, applied consistently, not just as a preamble disclaimer.** The
  opening paragraph ("Important, up front," line 7) states the guide should not be read as "telling
  Cherie what to conclude," and every substantive ask in §§1-5 is hedged with language like "if her
  clinical judgment supports it" (lines 20, 26, 32, 36, 40, 42, 48). This is then reinforced by a
  dedicated closing section, "What NOT to ask her to do" (lines 60-62), which names specific things
  not to request (a rating percentage, the word "total," omitting anything true).
- **Correctly reflects the corpus's most current diagnosis-and-theory conclusions, not stale ones.**
  The "What changed" section (lines 9-14) accurately states OCD is a confirmed diagnosis missing from
  two of four prior guides, that the iatrogenic-SUD theory has been superseded by self-medication, and
  that PTSD has never been formally diagnosed — all three match `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md`
  §§0.4, 0.5 and the Diagnosis Ledger (§3) exactly.
- **Accurately scopes what NOT to raise with Cherie.** The document does not ask Cherie to address the
  unexplained F15.20 Amphetamine-type SUD diagnosis or the stray "Bipolar 2" ROS notation — both of
  which `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` (§0.6, §9 item 12) and `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` (§0.2 table)
  treat as separate issues to resolve directly with the originating facility, not items for a treating
  LCSW's nexus letter. This is a correct scoping decision, not an oversight (see Gaps section for the
  one comparable omission that _is_ a gap).
- **Correctly treats materials-to-review as still partly unresolved rather than assuming completion.**
  §6 (line 52) lists Goff's statement as "once obtained" and flags the WHODAS 2.0 file as "a broken
  export" needing regeneration — both match open items in `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §0.7 and
  Open Tasks §10 items 4 and 6, rather than presenting them as already resolved.

## Weaknesses

- **§4's suicidal-ideation guidance is generic where the corpus record calls for something more
  specific.** Line 44 asks generally "whether suicidal ideation is passive or active and how
  frequently it occurs," citing _Bankhead v. Shulkin_ — but does not surface that the record already
  contains three data points in tension with each other (FMLA eval 5/14/26: "denies current SI...
  history of passive SI"; personal statement 6/17/26: SI "hits every day"; chart note ~6/25/26:
  "denies SI"), which `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.2 and Part 3 item 6 explicitly flag as needing
  reconciliation before a C&P exam. See Gaps below — this is the document's most concrete
  completeness shortfall relative to its own stated purpose in §7.

## Gaps

- **§7 ("Two things to flag for her awareness, not surprise") should arguably be three things.** The
  document's own framing purpose — surfacing record content that could otherwise surprise Cherie or
  an examiner — covers the trauma-denial pattern and the "gainfully employed" checkbox, but omits the
  SI-frequency discrepancy that `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` ranks as action item #6 ("Reconcile the SI
  discrepancy... before it reaches a C&P examiner cold"). Since §4 of this same document already
  raises SI frequency as a nexus-letter content item, the gap is specifically the missing flag that
  the record itself is inconsistent on this point — the same treatment given to the trauma-disclosure
  pattern in §7's first bullet would be the natural place for it.
- **No explicit note on citation-verification status.** The document states legal standards and cites
  case law/CFR sections as settled fact for Cherie's benefit (see Citations section below), but nothing
  in the document indicates whether these specific citations have been run through `citation-verifier`
  — relevant given `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §8 records that a prior pass found 40 of 109
  citations in this corpus misstated. Not a defect in this document's content, but a completeness gap
  against the corpus's own citation-verification norm (`../CLAUDE.md`).

## Credibility Risk Assessment

- **Low direct risk — this document is a structural counter-example to the corpus's central
  credibility problem, not an instance of it.** `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.2 (and `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-
2026-07.md` §0.2) identify the Erica email chain as the single biggest credibility threat in the
  entire corpus specifically because it _scripted_ language and _coordinated_ the narrative across
  independently-sourced evidence ("keep her statement consistent with what he's separately telling
  therapist Cherie Mills and telling military friends"). This document does the opposite: it never
  supplies wording, repeatedly defers to Cherie's independent clinical judgment, and explicitly warns
  against asking for a rating number or the word "total" (lines 60-62). On its own terms, this is the
  model the Erica statement should have followed.
- **One second-order risk worth naming, not because this document does anything wrong, but because of
  how it could read if produced alongside the Erica email chain in an adversarial review.** This
  document's own stated purpose — "hand this to Cherie directly, **or use it to brief her**" (line 4)
  — is, in category, a document prepared to brief an evidence source before she produces her account,
  the same general category as the Erica coaching thread (albeit executed correctly here, without
  scripted content). If a rater or opposing party ever obtained both documents, the argument they'd
  likely make is "a pattern of prepping every evidence source" rather than isolating the Erica thread
  as an aberration. This is not a reason to change this document's content — the hedged, non-scripting
  approach here is exactly right — but it is a reason the veteran/VSO should be prepared to explain the
  distinction (guidance on what legal standards require vs. supplied wording) if it ever comes up,
  consistent with `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` Part 0's framing that credibility, not evidentiary
  insufficiency, is this claim's real ceiling.
- **Downstream risk this document appropriately does not attempt to resolve.** The trauma-denial
  pattern (§7, first bullet) and the SI-frequency discrepancy (see Gaps) both remain live credibility
  exposures in the underlying record regardless of what this guide says — this document correctly
  flags the first but should flag the second too; neither is something this guide could or should
  resolve on its own (that is Cherie's clinical judgment to exercise, and the veteran's job to prepare
  for at a C&P exam per `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` Part 3 item 14).
