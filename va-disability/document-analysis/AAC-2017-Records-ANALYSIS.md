## Impact on service-connection theories

### 1. SUD secondary to PTSD/MDD/GAD (_Allen_ theory) — slightly favorable / neutral

- **Supports:** heroin documented as daily "since about 2015," i.e., post-separation (11/2013) and consistent with `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md`'s escalation at age 25 (2014). This is a 2017 contemporaneous clinician note — the earliest record in the corpus — and it supports the 2026-07-16 resolution in `RATING-REPORT-REMEDIATION-PLAN` that heroin/IV use is post-service. **Correction to that plan:** it dates the heroin/IV use "Victory 2018 onward"; AAC shows daily use from ~2015 and daily IV use by early 2017. AAC does **not** date *first* heroin use and so neither confirms nor refutes the Victory "first opiate/heroin use at age 18" self-report.
- **Cuts against the kratom-only pre-service account:** the psychosocial records first alcohol/drug use at 18 as "just curious, experimentation" (recreational, not prescribed) and gives an undated lifetime list — alcohol, opioids, benzos, ecstasy, stimulants, LSD, mushrooms, cocaine, marijuana, heroin — with **no kratom**. Because the list is undated it does not prove pre-service polysubstance use, but it is a second veteran-signed contemporaneous record (with Victory 2018) a rater can set against the kratom-only / prescribed-medication framing in `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md`, `MASTER-STRATEGY.md`, and the `PRE-POST-SERVICE-SEVERITY-CONTRAST` suggested language, and against the signed personal statement's "no recollection of using opioids before active duty" (§6/§9.2(c), resolved with the VSO 2026-07-06 per `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS` §0.8). The REP should know this record exists before relying on that resolution. The "experimental pre-service vs. severe post-service" contrast itself still holds.
- **Supports (weakly):** depression (Zoloft), anxiety, and insomnia were treated alongside the OUD in 2017 — co-occurring psychiatric symptoms during the SUD course.
- **Cuts against:** AAC treated chemical dependency as the primary disorder and never linked it to trauma or a psychiatric condition. The record documents a **multi-generational family SUD history** (father, grandfather, brother) — a competing etiology a C&P examiner can cite. Cherie Mills's addendum (Remediation Task 6) should acknowledge and weigh it, not leave it to be discovered.

### 2. MDD/GAD — direct onset vs. pre-existence — **the main new risk**

- **Supports continuity:** Zoloft prescribed for depression in May–June 2017 fills the gap between the 2015 VA visit (negative PHQ-2) and the 2018 Victory records.
- **New pre-service evidence:** the 2017 psychiatrist recorded outpatient treatment for depression/anxiety at **~age 19 with an antidepressant for several months**, and the psychosocial places it in **Lake Charles**. Born 5/30/1989, the veteran was 19 from 5/2008 to 5/2009; he entered active duty 11/23/2009. This is now the **earliest** of three records (AAC 2017, Victory 2018, Starlite 2022/2023) placing depression/anxiety treatment before service, and the first written by a physician with a medication, a duration, and a location. `RATING-REPORT-REMEDIATION-PLAN` Task 8 describes the pre-service flag as "a lone self-reported intake line ~15 years later" (already inconsistent with `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS` §1 and `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md`, which list multiple records) and offers secondary defense (ii) — "low-reliability self-report — if VET was not actually formally diagnosed at 18, he clarifies." A physician note recording treatment with a medication substantially weakens defense (ii): any corrective 21-4138 saying he was *not treated* pre-service would now conflict with a medical record. Whatever he writes must be accurate to what actually happened.
- **Why it isn't fatal:** under the presumption of soundness (38 U.S.C. § 1111; 38 CFR 3.304(b)), a condition not noted on the entrance examination is presumed absent at entry, and VA can rebut that only with clear and unmistakable evidence that the condition **both** pre-existed service **and** was not aggravated by it (_Wagner v. Principi_, 370 F.3d 1089, 1096 (Fed. Cir. 2004)). A history of pre-service existence recorded at the entrance exam does not by itself count as "noted," though it is still weighed with the other evidence on when the condition began (38 CFR 3.304(b)(1)) — so the AAC history remains relevant evidence even if the presumption holds. If VA cannot rebut soundness, the claim proceeds as **direct** service connection (_Wagner_ at 1096); the aggravation framework (38 CFR 3.306) governs only if the condition is found noted at entry — which is why aggravation is pled in the alternative, not alongside. PTSD is unaffected — it rests on the in-service stressor, not on prior depression.
- **What it changes in practice:** (a) actual pre-service treatment records (Lake Charles provider, ~2008–09) may exist, and VA could request them if identified; (b) the **entrance exam and Report of Medical History (DD Form 2807-1)** in the STRs (Remediation Task 2) become load-bearing — both for whether anything was noted and for how the veteran answered any depression/anxiety question at enlistment; (c) the veteran and REP should decide how to address the history before the C&P exam rather than have the examiner surface it.
- Minor drift: "~19" here vs. "age 18" in Victory/Starlite. Both are pre-service; no reconciliation needed beyond awareness.

### 3. PTSD (MST) — slightly unfavorable, but an existing, explicable pattern

- **Adds a 2017 non-disclosure data point:** psychosocial Q69 (rape/molestation/exploitation/abuse) answered "No" on a form the veteran signed. The provider-record chronology becomes: in-service lay disclosures to Reyes and Poquette (2013) → 2015 VA explicit denial → **2017 AAC "No"** → 2018 Victory explicit denials (8/25/18, 10/1/18 forms) → 2022 Starlite LEC-5 "unwanted sexual experience" endorsed (page-image-verified) → Jan 2023 Starlite "None Reported" / Jul 2023 "DENIES" → 2024–25 Starlite entries → MST raised in treatment with Cherie Mills. `MASTER-STRATEGY.md` item 12 and `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` list the denials as 2015/2018/2023–25 — add 2017.
- **The legal cover is narrower than it may look.** 38 CFR 3.304(f)(5) lets post-assault behavior changes (e.g., substance abuse, deterioration in work performance, episodes of depression or anxiety without identifiable cause) and non-service-record evidence corroborate a personal-assault stressor — and this record documents several of those markers post-service — but the regulation does not itself list delayed disclosure as a marker; that point needs clinical support (Cherie's addendum / the delayed-male-MST-disclosure literature). _AZ v. Shinseki_, 731 F.3d 1303 (Fed. Cir. 2013), holds that the absence of a service record of an unreported in-service sexual assault, and failure to report it to military authorities, are not pertinent evidence it did not occur. **AZ does not reach a post-service treatment-record "No"** — that is a prior statement VA may weigh for credibility, so the 2017 entry must be explained on the record (clinical addendum, veteran's own account), not neutralized by citing AZ.
- **Rater-usable lines:** most memorable feeling about service "The friends I made"; career plan "Join the military again as an officer." Neither is inconsistent with avoidance-based non-disclosure, but neither is helpful.
- **Mixed:** "Had a great childhood," no physical abuse, no family abuse narrows the room for a pre-service trauma alternative etiology — but it adds to existing drift against a "neglected most of childhood" self-report already flagged in `RATING-REPORT-REMEDIATION-PLAN` Task 11. It also sits against the 2022 LEC-5, which endorses physical assault "happened to me" (undated, unnarrated). Be ready to answer cold at the C&P.
