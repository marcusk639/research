## Summary

This is a private, non-VA-facing preparation document intended to help the veteran perform well
and truthfully at his upcoming C&P mental-disorders exam — it is not evidence submitted to VA and
will never be read by a rater. Its stated purpose is threefold: (1) frame the exam around VA's
100%-tier "total occupational AND social impairment" standard rather than an occupational-only
narrative, (2) walk through a symptom checklist (including OCD, which the document says the prior
version it supersedes omitted) with repeated instruction to "describe honestly, don't hide, don't
overstate," and (3) rehearse likely adversarial questions (trauma non-disclosure, sustained
high-level employment despite claimed severity, current daily functioning) with honest, non-
defensive framing. It explicitly closes by telling the veteran not to claim symptoms he doesn't
have and not to cite law to the examiner.

## Score: 7/10 (accuracy · completeness-for-purpose · consistency · credibility — NOT persuasiveness)

**Accuracy** is high: every specific factual claim in the document (the 6/17/26 personal
statement's daily-SI language, the 6/25/26 "denies SI" chart note, the framing of substance use as
connected to psychiatric symptoms, the "work until I burn out, then relapse" employment pattern,
the instruction not to claim ADL/disorientation/memory-loss symptoms) matches specific, named facts
in `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` and `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md`. **Completeness-for-purpose** is
the axis that most limits the score: the document's own stated goal is "be ready for these
specific questions" and "don't be caught by surprise," but it rehearses the _category_ of the
disclosure-denial problem generically rather than the specific, named landmines the ground-truth
documents flag as most likely to surface (the explicit 2015 MST denial + negative PC-PTSD screen,
the two 2018 Victory denials, the repeated 2022–2024 "None Reported" trauma checkboxes, the
unexplained F15.20 diagnosis, and the pre-service age-18 psychiatric/substance-use self-reports) —
see Gaps below. **Internal consistency** is good, with one minor asymmetry noted in Weaknesses.
**Credibility risk** from the document itself is low — it repeatedly conditions its advice on
genuineness ("if genuinely present," "if genuinely true") rather than telling the veteran what to
say, which is a meaningfully different posture than the coached-witness-statement problem
documented elsewhere in this corpus (`CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.1). The 7 reflects solid accuracy and
low self-generated credibility risk, held back by completeness gaps relative to its own stated
purpose, not by any inaccuracy or inconsistency found.

## Credibility Risk Assessment

- The document's dominant posture — condition every piece of advice on genuineness ("describe
  honestly," "if genuinely present," "don't claim symptoms you don't have") — is a materially
  different and lower-risk posture than the coached-witness-statement problem this corpus has
  already identified as its single biggest credibility threat (`CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.1,
  `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §0.2). Nothing in this document scripts specific content for
  the veteran to say, unlike the Erica email chain's directive rewrites.
- One line sits closer to the line than the rest and is worth flagging even though it does not
  cross it: line 29 tells the veteran to "frame [substance use], if genuinely true, as connected to
  managing the psychiatric symptoms rather than a separate, unrelated problem — this is the theory
  the actual treatment record supports." This is legally and factually accurate (matches
  `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §1.4's recommendation and the veteran's own contemporaneously-documented
  statements, e.g., "I use opioids to cope with work stress"), and it is explicitly conditioned on
  "if genuinely true" — but it is nonetheless advising the veteran on which of several possible true
  framings is legally advantageous, which is a fundamentally different (and here, permissible)
  activity than telling him what to say, per the same distinction `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.1 itself
  draws ("a 'framing rules' guide is useful to explain _why_ certain details matter... that's fine —
  but the final statement needs to be [the declarant's], not a template filled in to spec"). No
  action needed, but this is the single line in the document where that distinction is doing the
  most work and would reward a second look if this document is revised further.
- No content in this document appears coached, internally inconsistent, or contradicted by the
  clinical record. The gaps identified above are omissions of preparation, not misstatements — they
  raise the risk of the veteran being caught off guard mid-exam, not the risk of the document itself
  containing something false or fabricated.
