## Summary

This is a lay/witness statement from Zachary S. Poquette, a fellow USAF service member who served with Marcus Klein at Seymour Johnson AFB (4th Equipment Maintenance Squadron), submitted in support of the MST/PTSD claim. Its evidentiary purpose under 38 CFR 3.304(f)(5) is to independently corroborate behavioral-change markers and an in-service disclosure of the claimed 2012 sexual assault. The document as read is a prepared-template version of the statement (placeholders for mailing address/phone still marked "[Poquette to fill in]," signature/date fields blank) rather than the final executed form.

## Score: 6/10 (accuracy · completeness-for-purpose · consistency · credibility — NOT persuasiveness)

Driven primarily by **consistency and credibility-risk factors**, not by missing severity or weak content. The narrative content itself is accurate on the facts it covers (correct unit designation, correct veteran/witness relationship, plausible non-dramatic tone) and independently valuable as corroboration. It loses points because: (1) the document contains an internal timeline tension within its own four paragraphs; (2) it conflicts with the Reyes buddy statement on the disclosure year, a fact category (in-service vs. post-service timing) that is directly outcome-determinative under 3.304(f)(5); and (3) — the most significant finding of this review — the Section IV text in **this specific file** does not match the Section IV text in the **actual signed VA Form 21-10210** that Poquette executed on 06/29/2026 (see Consistency Check below). As a stand-alone artifact it is also incomplete (no signature, no witness contact info), though that is expected of a prep draft and is cured in the executed version.

## Strengths

- Correctly states the unit as "4th Equipment Maintenance Squadron (4 EMS), Maintenance Flight" (Section IV, para. 1) — this matches the DD-214 ground truth exactly (`COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §1: "4th Equipment Maintenance Squadron (ACC)"), unlike the still-uncorrected Reyes statement, which COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS §9a item 3 flags as still reading "4th Maintenance Squadron."
- Provides specific, low-drama corroborating detail independent of the assault itself — letting Marcus stay in his apartment during a move, "gave me tips on how to be healthy" (Section IV, para. 1) — texture consistent with a genuine 16-year friendship account rather than a narrative built solely around the claim.
- Corroborates a concrete, non-clinical behavioral marker: an unexplained request for money about a year after separation, described as "not like Marcus" (Section IV, para. 3) — this is the specific detail `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §7 and §4 calls out as the most evidentially valuable part of this statement.
- Balanced tone — closes by noting Marcus "has done a lot to better his life" and crediting his recovery work (Section IV, final para.) rather than presenting only catastrophizing content, which reads as more credible than a one-sidedly severe account.
- Relationship-to-veteran field is specific and verifiable ("Fellow service member (USAF); served together at Seymour Johnson AFB," Section III) rather than vague.

## Weaknesses

- **Internal timeline tension within the statement itself.** Section IV states plainly: "In 2012, I noticed a change in him... He told me he had been sexually assaulted that summer... This was while we were both still in service at Seymour Johnson." Later in the same statement: "it wasn't until a couple of years later that the truth came out." Read together, it is not clear from the text alone whether Poquette is describing one in-service 2012 disclosure, or a partial 2012 acknowledgment followed by a fuller post-separation disclosure — the document does not distinguish these for the reader.
- **Direct conflict with Reyes's statement on disclosure year.** `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §9a item 2 already flags this: Reyes's signed statement dates in-service disclosure to "late 2013, while we were both still serving," while this document dates it to "2012... while we were both still in service." Two independently-sourced corroborating witnesses disagreeing on the year of a legally load-bearing event weakens the corroborative value of having two witnesses in the first place.
- Section III's "Relationship to Veteran" field bundles descriptive prose ("Fellow service member (USAF); served together at Seymour Johnson AFB") rather than using the checkbox categories on the actual VA Form 21-10210 (SERVED WITH / FAMILY-FRIEND / COWORKER-SUPERVISOR / OTHER) — a cosmetic mismatch with the real form, not a substantive problem, but confirms this file is a pre-form drafting aid rather than the filing artifact itself.

## Credibility Risk Assessment

This section is the most consequential part of this review, consistent with `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.2/§0.8 treating credibility — not insufficient severity — as the actual ceiling on this claim.

- **The internal 2012-vs-"couple of years later" tension** (Weaknesses, above) is exactly the kind of ambiguity an adversarial rater or C&P examiner could probe. It is not evidence of fabrication, but it is unresolved on the face of the document.
- **The Reyes/Poquette disclosure-year conflict** (2012 vs. late 2013) is a known, already-logged open item (`COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §9a item 2, Open Task #14), and this review confirms the exact quoted language attributed to Poquette there is present verbatim in this file.
- **Material finding: this file's Section IV narrative does not match the Section IV narrative in the actual signed/executed VA Form 21-10210.** This review located `personal-records/supporting-docs/PocketBuddyLetterexportedtextfilled.pdf` — the official VA Form 21-10210 (OMB 2900-0881, Jul 2024 revision), filled with Poquette's real contact information (phone, email) and bearing an actual signature dated 06/29/2026, matching the "Poquette buddy statement signed 06/29/2026" entry in `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §2. That executed document's Section 17 narrative is nearly identical to this file's Section IV **except that it removes both legally load-bearing timing claims present in this file**:
  - This file: "**In 2012**, I noticed a change in him..." → Executed form: "After about **8-9 months** of seeing him day in and day out, I noticed a change..." (no calendar-year anchor).
  - This file: "...he had been sexually assaulted **that summer**..." → Executed form: "He vocalized that he was sexually assaulted..." (no season/date reference).
  - This file: "**This was while we were both still in service at Seymour Johnson.**" → **This sentence does not appear anywhere in the executed form.**

  This is a documented, anchored divergence between the file this review was asked to analyze and the document that was actually filed with VA under penalty of perjury. Three non-exclusive explanations are possible: (a) Poquette himself revised his own account for accuracy between drafts, consistent with the provenance log's confirmation that the narrative is his own words throughout; (b) the "(timeline-corrected)" label applied to the Poquette row in `1_Claim_Evaluation_and_Rating_Outlook.pdf`'s evidence table reflects exactly this edit cycle; or (c) the specific dates/in-service framing were adjusted between drafts for reasons not documented anywhere in this corpus. Given that `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §0.2 already documents an instruction, in a different context, to keep buddy-letter content "consistent with what he's separately telling therapist Cherie Mills and telling military friends for their buddy letters," the existence of an undocumented date-revision cycle on this specific witness's statement is worth resolving affirmatively rather than leaving unexplained — not because there is direct evidence Poquette was coached (the provenance log affirmatively found the opposite), but because an unexplained multi-draft history on the one legally decisive fact (in-service vs. post-service disclosure timing) is precisely what an adversarial reviewer would ask about if this file set were ever produced.
  - **Practical consequence:** `COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §9a item 2 appears to have synthesized its "Poquette" quote from this draft file rather than from the actual executed form, meaning the corpus's own ground-truth document may currently be citing superseded language. The executed form's version (no explicit "in service" claim) is arguably weaker, not stronger, for 3.304(f)(5) purposes than the strategy documents assume, since the sentence most directly establishing in-service disclosure timing was dropped before filing.
