# VA Disability Ratings

> Status: active · Started: 2026-06-06 · Last updated: 2026-06-12

## Research Question

How should this veteran (USAF 2009–2013, F-15 Crew Chief, current SC tinnitus 10%)
build and sequence a multi-condition VA disability claim — PTSD/MST, secondary
mental-health and SUD, the in-service ankle injury, F-15 chemical-exposure
conditions, TDIU/sheltered-employment, 100% P&T, and SMC — to maximize **realistic,
truthfully-documented** outcomes?

## Key Findings

Full synthesis: **[`claim-maximization-research-2026-06.md`](./claim-maximization-research-2026-06.md)**
— 10 research tracks, web-sourced from primary materials, each citation
adversarially re-verified (68 confirmed / 41 flagged; see the Verification Ledger
at the top of that file).

Headline conclusions:

- **Do not file TDIU while earning ~$200K.** Current senior-engineer income is the
  dominant obstacle to TDIU/100% and a credibility risk across all contentions
  (_Faust v. West_, 13 Vet. App. 342). The clean near-term target is a **70–90%
  combined schedular** rating built on a single, well-documented mental-health
  evaluation under 38 C.F.R. § 4.130.
- **PACT Act presumptions do not apply** (non-deployed, stateside). The F-15
  chemical-exposure conditions are **direct-nexus** claims requiring exposure proof
  - diagnosis + medical opinion.
- **MST is workable** under the relaxed marker standard of 38 C.F.R. § 3.304(f)(5)
  and _AZ v. Shinseki_; the non-disclosure across prior SUD stays is a manageable
  credibility vulnerability that a private nexus opinion must address directly.
- **SUD secondary claims** add little schedular value (§ 4.14 collapses MH into one
  rating) — their worth is nexus/treatment-history, not stacked percentages.
- **Bright line throughout:** truthful documentation of genuine limitations is
  legitimate and supported; manufacturing evidence or staging sham employment is
  fraud (38 U.S.C. § 6103; 18 U.S.C. § 1001) and is flagged as such.
