## 2. Licensing: who can evaluate you, and from where

**Louisiana psychologists.**

- A Louisiana-licensed psychologist doing telehealth must verify that "the originating site of the client" is within Louisiana [S3].
- Louisiana enacted the PSYPACT compact as Act 302 of 2026, but "This Act shall become effective on January 1, 2028" [S1].
- The PSYPACT Commission's map still lists Louisiana as a non-member state [S2].

**Texas.** Texas treats a provider as practicing in Texas whenever the patient is physically in Texas:

- for psychologists [S5];
- for physicians [S6].

Texas has been a PSYPACT state since 7/1/2020 [S2].

**Louisiana physicians.** Louisiana likewise treats medicine as practiced "at the location of the patient" [S4]. The statute that authorized that rule may have been repealed in 2024, so the current form of the rule is unverified (see Limitations).

| Provider's license                | You're in Louisiana                 | You're in Texas |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Louisiana only                    | Yes                                 | No              |
| Texas, or PSYPACT telehealth only | **No** (until 2028 at the earliest) | Yes             |
| Both states (e.g., Chafetz)       | Yes                                 | Yes             |

**Practical rule:** book the evaluation for a time when you'll be physically in the provider's licensed state. An in-person visit to a Louisiana office also works.

## 4. Free and low-cost routes in Louisiana

- **LSU Law's Brookshire Veterans Law Clinic** handles "service-connected disability compensation appeal[s]" [S16]. It's the only verified law-school option. It won't pay for an IMO, but it's useful if you appeal. (225) 578-5292.
- **Southeast Louisiana Legal Services:** its veterans work is listed as "pension, overpayments, and debts" [S17], not compensation claims.
- **VA clinicians:** VA providers "are often not well suited to assess causality" [S20], so this is a weak route for your case.
- **No free Louisiana program that supplies doctoral IMOs was found.**
- **Louisiana's PLUS Act**, which regulated paid claims consultants, was enjoined in February 2026 [S18]. So no Louisiana law currently limits what an evaluator can charge. The flat-fee, never-a-percentage-of-back-pay rule from the Texas doc is your own protection.
