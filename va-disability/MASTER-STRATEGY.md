## The core strategic shift from earlier research

The June 2026 research assumed high income (~$150–200K) as the main obstacle and treated 70–90% combined as the ceiling, with 100% only reachable via TDIU. That's now outdated on two fronts: you're unemployed (which is legitimate evidence of occupational impairment, not something to hide), and you've explicitly ruled out TDIU. The updated plan targets **schedular 100% under 38 CFR 4.130**, which requires proving **"total occupational AND social impairment"** — both prongs, not just inability to work. That distinction is what separates a schedular-100% case from a TDIU case, and it's the frame to build the C&P exam and every piece of supporting evidence around.

The SUD nexus theory has also changed (see `CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` §1.4): drop the iatrogenic in-service-opioid framing — your own clinical record across five Starlite admissions contradicts it — and lead with **SUD as self-medication for the psychiatric conditions**, which your own repeated statements to treating clinicians directly support.

**Framing for the C&P exam and nexus letter:** the target is the examiner selecting **"total occupational and social impairment"** on the mental disorders DBQ, or — realistically — building the strongest possible case for **"deficiencies in most areas"** (70%) with enough severity/frequency/duration evidence (per _Vazquez-Claudio_) that the examiner is pushed toward the top of that band or into 100% if the evidence genuinely supports it (per _Mauerhan_ and _Bankhead_ — you don't need every listed 100%-symptom, but whatever symptoms exist must actually produce total impairment). Describe worst/typical days, not best days. Don't hide suicidal ideation, but be consistent about its frequency across documents.

**SUD framing:** secondary to the psychiatric conditions (self-medication), which _Mittleider v. West_ supports — if symptoms of the SUD and the psychiatric conditions can't be medically separated, VA must attribute the whole picture to the service-connected condition.

### 2. Ankle — parallel, lower-priority track

Direct service connection is strong, but not as clean as it first looks: the injury date conflicts across your own records (personal statement says May 2011; your 2015 VA intake says 2012) — confirm the actual date against STRs or hospital records before relying on either one. Also still needed: the X-ray already recommended in the existing rating-strategy memo, and a witness who can corroborate the in-service injury/hospital trip (Poquette's statement doesn't cover it — get a different lay statement or confirm it's otherwise in your STRs). Coordinate timing with your VSO on whether to file this now or after the mental-health claim resolves.

### 3. Secondary conditions — the path to 100% combined if MH lands below schedular-100

Per the combined-ratings math in `100-PERCENT-SCHEDULAR-PATHWAY.md` §7: from a 70% MH anchor, reaching 100% combined needs roughly 80%+ worth of other conditions — a heavy stack. From a **90%** MH anchor, a single **50%** secondary clears 100%. That makes obstructive sleep apnea (secondary to PTSD, rated 50% if CPAP-required) the single highest-leverage move if the MH rating lands at 90%. Get a sleep study scheduled now rather than waiting.

Other secondaries worth pursuing in parallel, roughly in order of value: sleep apnea (50%, highest leverage) → gait-related knee/hip/back secondary to the ankle → GERD/migraines/hypertension secondary to PTSD, if genuinely present → hearing loss (additive to tinnitus, JP-8 ototoxicity nexus).

### 4. TDIU — not pursued, but can't be fully suppressed, and that's fine

Per _Rice v. Shinseki_, VA must consider TDIU once unemployability is reasonably raised by the record — you can decline to pursue it as a claim, but you can't force VA to ignore the evidence of unemployment. In practice this isn't a real risk: worst case, VA grants TDIU as an automatic fallback floor (paying at the 100% rate) while you keep arguing for schedular 100% by consistently framing the case around **both** occupational and social impairment, not "can't work" alone.
