## 0. The honest headline (read this first)

A multi-agent deep-research pass (107 agents, adversarial 3-vote verification) confirmed the **legal/fee framework and the free-resource landscape with high confidence**, but **could not independently verify any firm's "best/strongest track record" reputation claims** — those are marketing (firm websites, PR), not verifiable fact. So this KB gives you (a) the **rules**, which are solid, and (b) a list of **candidates to vet yourself**, not a ranked "best firms" leaderboard. That distinction is the point: nobody should pick a VA lawyer off a self-reported win rate.

## 1. Decision guide — VSO vs. attorney vs. claims agent, for YOUR stage

VA's Office of General Counsel accredits exactly **three** types of representative ([va.gov FAQ](https://www.va.gov/resources/va-accredited-representative-faqs/); 38 C.F.R. § 14.629):

| Type                                                                                    | Cost                                                             | Can charge?                | Best for                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **VSO representative** (DAV, VFW, American Legion, Louisiana Dept. of Veterans Affairs) | **Always free, every stage**                                     | Never                      | The baseline — correct representation for an initial/pre-decision claim, which is likely where you are. You already work with a VSO. |
| **VA-accredited attorney**                                                              | Free consult; contingency fee **only after** an initial decision | Only post-initial-decision | An _appeal_ (HLR / Board / CAVC) or a post-decision Supplemental Claim, especially if the MH rating comes back low.                  |
| **VA-accredited claims agent**                                                          | Same fee timing as attorneys                                     | Only post-initial-decision | Same as attorneys but non-lawyer; passed OGC exam. Less common for complex appeals.                                                  |

**The rule that decides whether an attorney can charge you _right now_ (38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(1)):** no attorney or agent may charge a fee for work done **before VA gives notice of its initial decision** on the claim. VSOs are free at every stage.

**★ The synthesis insight that matters for you:** whether an attorney can take your _current_ claim on contingency turns on the **same unknown** as the effective-date question in `CLAIM-COMPLETION-PLAN-2026-07-08.md` §2.5 / Tier 0 — **has this mental-health issue ever been formally decided before?**

- If **yes** (this Supplemental Claim reopens a prior decision) → there's already been an "initial decision," so an **accredited attorney can represent you now on contingency** for the supplemental/appeal.
- If **no** (this is the first adjudication of the MH cluster) → **no attorney may charge yet**; a **free VSO is the correct representation for this stage**, and an attorney only becomes a paid option if the decision is unfavorable and you appeal.
- **Action:** the VSO answer to the effective-date/prior-decision question also tells you which representation path is available today. Ask it once; it resolves both.

## 2. Free / low-cost resources, ranked for THIS fact pattern

1. **Accredited VSO (free, every stage) — your baseline, use it now.** DAV, VFW, American Legion, and the **Louisiana Department of Veterans Affairs** all provide accredited reps at no cost; VSO services on a benefit claim are _always free_ ([benefits.va.gov/vso](https://www.benefits.va.gov/vso/)). Correct for a pre-decision claim where attorneys can't charge anyway. _Best for:_ everything you're doing right now.
2. **VA OGC accreditation search — ogc.va.gov — use before trusting anyone.** Free lookup to confirm a person is actually VA-accredited ([find a rep](https://www.va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative/find-rep/)). _Best for:_ vetting any attorney/agent/consultant before signing.
3. **Law-school veterans clinics (free) — for a complex appeal.** **Stetson** (Veterans Advocacy Clinic, ~250 free hrs/client/yr, focuses on the _most complicated_ denials — but **Tampa Bay area**, not nationwide) ([stetson.edu](https://www.stetson.edu/law/veterans/legal-help-for-veterans.php)); **Yale VLSC** (notable for **MST/PTSD** rulemaking litigation against VA — but selective, clinic-capacity-limited) ([law.yale.edu/clinics/vlsc](https://law.yale.edu/clinics/vlsc)). _Best for:_ a hard MST/PTSD appeal if you qualify and can work with their geography/capacity limits.
4. **NVLSP (free, nationwide) — appellate level, with a key limit.** Does no-cost CAVC-level VA-disability appellate work; **BUT its Lawyers Serving Warriors pro bono track explicitly does NOT handle general schedular rating claims** (only discharge upgrades, medical retirement, CRSC, TSGLI) ([nvlsp.org](https://nvlsp.org/)). _Best for:_ a court-level appeal, not the current rating claim.
5. **Stateside Legal / pro bono programs** — referenced as a resource but **not independently verified in this research pass**; treat as a lead to check, not a confirmed fit.

## 4. Fee-rule primer (citation-verified — see §7)

- **Timing bar (§ 5904(c)(1)):** no attorney/agent fee for work **before notice of the initial decision**. VSOs free always.
- **Fee caps (§ 5904(a)(5), (d)(1); 38 C.F.R. § 14.636(f)(1)):** a fee **≤ 20%** of past-due benefits is **presumed reasonable** (this presumption requires that the representation _continued through the date of the award decision_); a fee **> 33⅓%** is **presumed unreasonable** (rebuttable). The 20% figure is also the cap for **VA direct-pay out of past-due benefits**.
- **Correction of a common myth (this was refuted 0-3 in verification):** it is **NOT** true that both 20% and 33⅓% are "presumptively reasonable." Only ≤ 20% is presumed reasonable; > 33⅓% is presumed *un*reasonable.
- **Direct pay from past-due benefits (§ 14.636(h)):** VA pays the attorney directly out of your back-pay only if the fee is **contingent**, **≤ 20%**, and the representative is **accredited on the fee-allocation-notice date** (per the Dec. 21, 2023 Federal Register rule).
- **EAJA (Equal Access to Justice Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2412):** at the **CAVC** (court) level, a veteran who prevails can have the _government_ pay attorney fees — separate from the 20% contingency, and it can reduce your out-of-pocket. Relevant only if the claim reaches court.

## 5. How to run a free consult (and red flags)

**Bring:** the claim status (Supplemental Claim, acknowledged 29 May 2026), the condition list (PTSD/MST + MDD/GAD/OCD/SUD as one MH rating), and the honest note that a C&P hasn't been scheduled. **Ask:** how many MST/PTSD _schedular_ appeals they've handled; whether they'd wait for the decision or act now; their contingency %; whether they use EAJA at the CAVC level; who actually works your file.

**Red flags:**

- **Not accredited** — if they're not in the ogc.va.gov search, walk away.
- **Asking for money up front on an initial claim** — illegal under § 5904(c)(1).
- **"Claim sharks" / unaccredited paid consultants** — note that **Louisiana's 2024 PLUS Act, which regulated paid veteran-benefits consultants, was struck down in Feb 2026** (_Military-Veterans Advocacy, Inc. v. Landry_, M.D. La. — a **district-court** ruling the Louisiana AG has said she will appeal, so not yet final), so Louisiana currently has _less_ state regulation of these consultants — buyer beware; stick to VA-accredited people.
- **Guaranteed outcomes / advertised win rates** — no one can guarantee a rating; the numbers are marketing.

## 6. How this feeds the strategy

Representation is a **Route-1-and-Route-2 amplifier, not a substitute** for the evidence work. The single highest-value legal-representation move is holding an **accredited attorney in reserve for a post-decision appeal** that argues the rater ignored the treating clinician under 38 C.F.R. § 4.126(a)/§ 4.2 and _Nieves-Rodriguez_ (all still **pending `citation-verifier`**). For the current pre-decision evidence-gathering, the **free VSO is the correct and sufficient representation** — spend money on an attorney only if/when there's an unfavorable decision to appeal.
