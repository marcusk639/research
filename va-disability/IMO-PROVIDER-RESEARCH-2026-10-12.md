## 1. What makes VA credit a private opinion

**How VA weighs it**

- Probative value comes from "factually accurate, fully articulated, sound reasoning"; "most of the probative value of a medical opinion comes from its reasoning" (_Nieves-Rodriguez_, 22 Vet. App. at 304, CONFIRMED). Claims-file review isn't required, and the Board can't prefer one opinion over another just because one examiner reviewed the file (id. at 303–04, CONFIRMED). The opinion needs "a clear conclusion with supporting data, and a reasoned medical explanation connecting the two" (id. at 301, CONFIRMED).
- "A mere conclusion by a medical doctor is insufficient"; the opinion must give an analysis the Board can weigh against contrary opinions (_Stefl v. Nicholson_, 21 Vet. App. 120, 124 (2007), CONFIRMED). A well-reasoned private opinion can therefore outweigh a conclusory C&P exam.
- The rater evaluates on "all the evidence of record … rather than solely on the examiner's assessment" (38 CFR 4.126(a), CONFIRMED).
- **No rule protects an opinion because it's private, and none penalizes it because it was paid for.** The Board may favor one competent opinion over another if it explains why (_Owens v. Brown_, 7 Vet. App. 429, 433 (1995), confirmed by secondary sources only). That cuts both ways, so the reasoning has to be better than the examiner's.
- **Credentials:** no regulation requires a psychiatrist or psychologist for a private opinion. 38 CFR 3.304(f) and 4.125(a) require only a DSM-5-conforming diagnosis (CONFIRMED). Credentials affect weight: a factor is the physician's "knowledge and skill in analyzing the data" (_Guerrieri v. Brown_, 4 Vet. App. 467, 470–71 (1993), confirmed by secondary sources only).
- A strong letter states the author's credentials, lists the records reviewed, gives a coded diagnosis, uses the "at least as likely as not" standard, and **addresses the negative evidence** in the file ([Pinyerd Law](https://pinyerdlaw.com/va-nexus-letter/); [After Service](https://afterservice.com/va-nexus-letters/)).

**Who can write it**

- For mental health, the natural authors are psychiatrists and doctoral psychologists ([PTSD NC](https://ptsdnc.com/blog/how-to-pick-the-correct-doctor-to-write-a-veterans-nexus-letter)).
- The Initial PTSD DBQ itself limits **C&P** exams to board-certified or board-eligible psychiatrists, licensed doctoral psychologists, and supervised trainees (`planning-docs/VA-DBQ-for-PTSD-Initial-DSM-V.pdf`, p. 1).
- Your current nexus author, Cherie, is an LCSW. The 10/8 reassessment counts that as a weakness against service connection.

**Red flags in a provider** ([PTSD NC](https://ptsdnc.com/blog/how-to-pick-the-correct-doctor-to-write-a-veterans-nexus-letter); [VMHA](https://www.vmhaforvets.com/what-is-a-nexus-letter/); [Dr. Foster](https://www.psyconops.com/va-disability-second-opinion)):

- guaranteed approval language;
- no review of your records;
- template or cookie-cutter letters;
- very low prices with no individual analysis;
- fees charged as a percentage of your back pay;
- not knowing what credential the author holds until after you've paid.

One provider says VA is "cracking down" on high-volume "form mills" whose reports carry little weight. That's a single-source claim and unverified.

Cal., 2025-11-14](https://www.justice.gov/index%2ephp/usao-sdca/pr/former-va-employee-charged-running-criminal-scheme-obtain-va-benefits-others)).

An honest evaluation protects you as well as the claim.

## 2. Free option: a VA clinician

- **What the policy says:** VHA Directive 1134 requires VA providers, when asked, to give medical statements. Any causality opinion must include "clear and specific rationale citing evidence to support the conclusion reached" ([VHA Directive 1134](https://www.va.gov/vhapublications/ViewPublication.asp?pub_ID=4300) para 5.d.(1)(b)1, CONFIRMED). The official PDF is stamped "AMENDED July 8, 2025"; that amendment didn't touch these provisions (CONFIRMED).
- **The limit for mental health:** for the mental-health DBQ, VA recommends "the Veteran's treating provider not complete the DBQ to maintain the integrity of the patient-provider relationship" (para 5.d.(1)(c)3, CONFIRMED).
- **Why it's a weak option for you:** you aren't in ongoing VA mental-health care, and VA providers "often do not have access to military medical records."

## 4. How to screen and verify

**Verify before paying:**

- Psychologists: the Texas license on the [BHEC license lookup](https://bhec.texas.gov/), and PSYPACT telehealth authority in the [PSYPACT directory](https://bhec.texas.gov/texas-state-board-of-examiners-of-psychologists/psypact/). Texas adopted PSYPACT in 2019 (H.B. 1501).
- Physicians: the Texas Medical Board license and ABPN board certification.

**Questions to ask each one:**

1. Will you interview me, and will you read **all** my records, including the unfavorable ones (Starlite and Victory intakes, the August hospital records, the trauma denials)?
2. Do you use symptom-validity testing?
3. Will you address the in-service markers, the delayed disclosure, soundness at entry, and substance use as secondary to PTSD?
4. If the evidence doesn't support service connection, will you tell me so rather than write a weak letter?
5. Is the fee flat? (It should never be a percentage of back pay.) Are addendums included?
6. How long does the report take?

**What to send them:** the full record, not a curated one. That means service records, Cherie's letter and notes, the Reyes/Poquette/Erica statements, every treatment admission (including Starlite and Victory), the August hospital records, and your 21-0781 and 21-4138s. An opinion built on selected records is the kind VA discounts, and it misrepresents the case.

The C&P exam happened on **10/13**, so the "get it in before the examiner reviews it" argument from the 10/12 version no longer applies. Two realistic paths:

- **Path A, commission it now.** It goes into the file while the claim is still pending, and the rater weighs it under § 4.126(a) alongside the exam. It costs about $500 even if you'd have won without it. It can't respond to the examiner's reasoning unless you get the exam report first.
- **Path B, wait for the decision (the existing plan).** If service connection is granted, you skip the cost. If it's denied, the opinion becomes the "new and relevant evidence" for a supplemental claim (§ 3.2501), or supports an HLR argument. And it can rebut the examiner's actual reasoning point by point (`REASSESSMENT-2026-10-08-CP-EXAM.md` §3; `RECORDS-TO-GET-AND-PATHS-TO-100-2026-10-08.md` #17).
- **Middle step either way:** ask James to pull the exam report (the DBQ Dr. Howard completed) as soon as it's in the file. If it's unfavorable or thin, Path A becomes much stronger, because the private opinion can answer it before the decision issues.

3. **Screen with the §4 questions.** Get the fee, what's included (addendums), and turnaround **in writing**.
4. **Send the full record, unfavorable parts included** (§4 list). Add Dr. Howard's DBQ once you have it.
5. **Do the evaluation honestly.** Answer the way you did at the C&P. Don't rehearse or exaggerate. Validity testing works in your favor if you're straightforward.
6. **Review the draft for factual accuracy only:** dates, names, events. Don't ask for changes to the conclusion or the wording of the opinion. Correcting a wrong date is fine; asking for a stronger conclusion damages credibility.
7. **Have James submit it**, with a cover note pointing the rater to it. Ask that the 21-4138 or submission describe it as an independent medical opinion.

**What the finished letter should contain**, so VA takes it seriously. Check it against this list:

- the author's credentials and license number;
- a list of every record reviewed;
- that they interviewed you, on what date, and how long it lasted;
- any testing used, and validity results;
- a DSM-5 diagnosis (§ 4.125(a));
- the opinion in VA's standard, "at least as likely as not (50% or greater)";
- a reasoned explanation tying the markers (§ 3.304(f)(5)) to the in-service assault;
- explicit treatment of the five prior denials, the delayed disclosure, the pre-service history, and substance use as secondary;
- the author's signature and date.

A letter missing the reasoning or the negative evidence is the kind _Stefl_ says is insufficient.
