## 1. Bottom line up front

**The realistic default outcome is 70–90%. A 100% schedular rating is a genuine, earned possibility — not the expected result — and it hinges on two hard contingencies that are not yet resolved.**

**Honest odds — REASONED ESTIMATES, not statistically derived** (verbatim from `PATH-TO-100-PERCENT-2026-07-07.md` §9; the honesty anchor — nothing here drifts above them):

- **70% mental health ≈ 90%+**
- **90% mental health ≈ 60–70%**
- **100% schedular, MH-alone (Route 1) ≈ 40–50%**
- **100% schedular, combined (Route 2) ≈ 50–60%**

**The two hard contingencies that pin these down — read these first:**

1. **Route 1's married-veteran / total-social-impairment problem (the doctrinal linchpin).** 100% on the mental-health cluster alone requires "total occupational **AND** social impairment" (38 CFR 4.130 **[confirmed — `PATH-TO-100` §11]**), and you are married to a spouse who helps with your care, which a rater can weigh against a finding of _total social_ impairment. The question is genuinely fact-specific and unsettled. As an _illustrative, non-representative_ sample, the corpus has found four non-precedential Board decisions on the married-veteran question — three cutting against, one favorable (BVA 1513531, 22009295, 22015499 adverse; 1648197 favorable; all **[pending citation-verifier]**). Four cherry-picked BVA cases are **not** a measure of "the weight of authority" (the Board issues thousands); they show only that the issue can go either way and **turns entirely on the factual record of the veteran's actual social functioning.** This is the single biggest reason Route 1's odds do not go higher.

2. **Route 2's not-yet-obtained sleep study.** The combined route depends on a 50% sleep-apnea secondary, and **no polysomnography-confirmed OSA diagnosis exists.** Booking the test is not the same as having the diagnosis: **if the study is negative, Route 2 is foreclosed** and the ceiling returns to ~90% via Route 1's evidence alone. Until a positive study exists, Route 2 is a possibility, not a filable claim.

**Operational state (confirmed 2026-07-07):** the claim on the mental-health cluster was acknowledged by VA **29 May 2026**, **no C&P exam is scheduled yet**, and the VSO is submitting records as they are sent — **the evidence window is open.** Everything below that must "land before the exam" still can. The moment a C&P is scheduled is the hard cutoff. (On the "Supplemental Claim" label and what it implies, see I-16 — it matters for both back-pay and representation.)

## 2. The two convergent schedular routes

Both routes share ~90% of the same evidence work, so they run in parallel rather than as a bet on one. **The center of gravity of both is identical: maximize the single mental-health rating.** From a 70% MH anchor the realistic combined ceiling is ~90% even with a full secondary stack; from a 90% MH anchor, one 50% sleep-apnea rating cleanly reaches 100%. Raising MH from 70→90 is worth more than any pile of marginal secondaries.

### Route 1 — Schedular 100% on the mental-health cluster (the ideal)

The rater finds **total occupational AND social impairment** under 38 CFR 4.130, built from genuine symptoms across all five diagnoses that feed the single MH score (PTSD/MST + MDD + GAD + OCD + SUD, rated as one under anti-pyramiding, 38 CFR 4.14 **[pending citation-verifier]**). Note that Cherie's signed **2 July 2026 letter already supplies the actual clinical PTSD diagnosis** (plus MDD/GAD/OCD/SUD) — resolving what had long been the corpus's single biggest open item (`COMPREHENSIVE-FINDINGS-2026-07.md` §0.13); the service-connection foundation for a rating therefore exists, and the work now is severity/impairment documentation.

- **Two potential 100%-tier anchors** — both genuine, both **not yet in any treatment record**, which is exactly the documentation work to be done:
  - **ADL / personal-hygiene dependence** (spouse-assisted) — corresponds to the 100%-tier "intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene)." This would be the first 100%-tier-language symptom anywhere in the corpus. The WHODAS 2.0 self-care domain (62.5%) is a **self-report data point consistent with** meaningful self-care impairment — it is _not_ objective evidence and a moderate score does **not** by itself establish the 100%-tier "inability"; it supports, it does not prove.
  - **Two persistent delusions (persecutory + grandiose)** — correspond to the explicit 100%-tier "persistent delusions" language. The grandiose one is already within Cherie's clinical assessment; neither is yet in her signed letter or any record. Whether this is "PTSD with psychotic features" or a separate psychotic-spectrum picture is **Cherie's clinical call**; it is rated under the same 4.130 formula either way.
- **The mandatory social prong** requires an accurate picture of the veteran's _actual_ social functioning. To the extent he is genuinely isolated (whether he has friends, voluntary social contact, community ties, or is confined to home), that is what the record needs to reflect — from his own account and from whatever those close to him independently choose to describe **in their own words**. The marriage's actual character, including any care Erica provides, is a fact for Erica and Cherie to describe truthfully, **not a conclusion anyone is directed to reach.** WHODAS social domains (getting-along 70%, participation 75%, "making new friends = cannot do") are self-report data supporting _severe_ — not, by themselves, _total_ — social impairment. (Why the social prong is doctrinally pivotal is analysis for the veteran/VSO/attorney; it is never conveyed to a witness as instruction — see I-3/I-6.)
- **A dense 70%-tier floor** proves pervasiveness: near-continuous depression/anxiety (DASS-21 all three subscales "Extremely severe," 98–100th %ile — self-report), OCD obsessional rituals (a textual 70%-tier symptom), daily passive SI described honestly at the 70%-tier line (not dressed up as the 100%-tier "persistent danger").
- **Legal leverage that de-risks a lowball examiner:** the rater weighs the whole record, not just the exam snapshot (38 CFR 4.126(a) **[confirmed — `PATH-TO-100` §11]**); a treating clinician's opinion/DBQ endorsing "total impairment" can outweigh a skeptical C&P examiner. Cherie's signed letter already states, in her own words, "total impairment in occupational and social realms."

### Route 2 — Schedular 100% combined (the insurance, and co-equal target)

**90% MH + 50% sleep apnea = 95 → rounds to 100%** (38 CFR 4.25 whole-person math **[confirmed — `PATH-TO-100` §11]**):

- **The theory is sound but not yet filable, and inherits the full 90%-MH burden.** Route 2 still requires (a) a not-yet-existent OSA diagnosis, (b) a secondary-nexus grant on 38 CFR 3.310, **and** (c) the MH rating actually reaching 90% — which itself needs nearly all the same hard MH evidence as Route 1. Its anchor (50–60%) is only _modestly_ higher than Route 1's (40–50%), and only _if the study is positive_ — it is **not** a safe fallback, just a somewhat higher-probability parallel path. The strongest causal pathway is PTSD hyperarousal lowering the arousal threshold, with two non-precedential BVA grants on this fact pattern that succeeded on **treating-provider** opinions citing peer-reviewed literature (BVA 1648003, A21018009 — **[confirmed — `SLEEP-APNEA-SECONDARY-CLAIM.md` log]**).
- **The 50% CPAP tier is the current tier but is not settled:** 38 CFR 4.97 DC 6847 **[confirmed — `SLEEP-APNEA-SECONDARY-CLAIM.md` log]** currently grants 50% for CPAP use, but a pending NPRM would restructure DC 6847 to 0/10/50/100 tiers, under which full CPAP control could fall to 0–10%. Still proposed, not final; the current 50% tier remains in effect; existing ratings expected to be grandfathered. **Implication: file promptly once diagnosed** so a 50% grant lands and grandfathers before any rule change.
- **If the sleep study is negative:** Route 2 is foreclosed — no OSA, no secondary, no 100% via this path. All weight returns to Route 1, and the realistic ceiling is ~90%. Plan for this branch; do not treat the diagnosis as a formality.
- **Only three secondaries genuinely move the math:** sleep apnea (50%, load-bearing), migraines (30%, only if genuinely prostrating), ankle (10%). ED is worth claiming for SMC-K money but adds 0% to the percentage; GERD/hypertension/hearing-loss are low-value — do not build on them.

**Why both at once:** arguing for 100% (Route 1) does not forfeit 90% (Route 2's anchor). Build the record to genuinely support "total," present it that way, and let 90%+sleep-apnea be the floor you land on if the examiner stops short — _provided_ the study is positive.

### TDIU — the labeled, rejectable fallback (not pursued)

TDIU is **explicitly declined** — you want to retain earning capacity and reach 100% through the schedular criteria. It cannot be fully suppressed: under _Rice v. Shinseki_ **[pending citation-verifier]** VA must consider TDIU once unemployability is raised by the record, but this is low-risk — worst case VA grants it as an automatic fallback floor (paying at the 100% rate) while the schedular-100% argument continues. To keep schedular-100% alive, frame everything as **total occupational AND social impairment**, never "can't work" alone.

## 3. Do this next, in order (the highest-leverage spine)

Items 1–3 are the whole game and must land **before the C&P exam is scheduled.** **None of these involves coaching a witness or reaching for a symptom** — they are timing and completeness for impairment that is already real.

1. **The sleep study — single highest-leverage action.** Book a private home sleep apnea test (faster, ~1–3 weeks) or a VA PCP referral, reporting the symptoms you genuinely have (snoring, witnessed apneas, daytime fatigue). This is the one action that _can_ convert a 90% MH rating into a 100% combined rating — **if it comes back positive** (if negative, see Route 2's foreclosure branch).
2. **Get the two genuine 100%-tier symptoms into Cherie's treatment record — as a care matter first.** Raise the ADL/hygiene dependence and both delusions with Cherie now, so she can assess, chart, and decide the diagnostic characterization in her own clinical judgment. Then ask whether she will provide an **updated letter + a Mental Disorders DBQ (VA Form 21-0960P-2)** — the legally correct form, since PTSD is not yet service-connected so she cannot complete the initial-PTSD DBQ (21-0960P-4, VHA/contract-examiner only) — with a **short narrative addendum tying each checked symptom to its 4.130 impairment tier.** Do not tell her what to conclude.
3. **Erica's statement — entirely her own words, from her own memory.** Her existing (confirmed self-authored) statement predates the facts now most relevant; the move is for her to **revise/expand it herself**, describing daily life and any assistance she genuinely provides. **Hard constraint (law + this corpus's #1 rule): no one drafts, scripts, suggests wording, or tells her what topics or conclusions help the claim — open-ended memory prompts only.** A coached statement is the single biggest credibility risk in this claim. Log the update in `DOCUMENT-PROVENANCE.md` once she has revised it.
4. **Establish and attend the new VA mental-health provider** so current symptoms are contemporaneously charted by a second source before the exam.
5. **Send the objective instruments now, honestly framed.** DASS-21 and WHODAS 2.0 to the VSO with a cover note stating plainly they are self-administered, vendor-auto-scored instruments — real evidence of severity/function, not a stand-alone rating basis. The honest framing is a credibility asset.
6. **Ask the VSO the effective-date / prior-decision question this week** (see I-16) — it also determines whether an attorney can charge now (see §4). Free to ask; materially changes the back-pay stakes.
7. **If the record's silence on withheld symptoms comes up, the veteran explains it in his own words.** The records may understate severity because of how he engaged with past treatment; if a rater or examiner raises the gap, the explanation should be his own genuine account, not a supplied rationale. Same for the SI-fluctuation and trauma-disclosure patterns — his words, kept truthful and consistent.
8. **Confirm the DC 6847 NPRM's current status** before relying on the 50% CPAP tier, and **ask the VSO to flag the instant a C&P is scheduled.**

**At the C&P exam** (work from `CP-EXAM-PREP-MENTAL-HEALTH.md`): argue **both** prongs, never occupation-only; describe worst/typical days, not best days; cover the ADL/hygiene dependence and the delusions (already charted via step 2, so not surfacing cold); SI honestly at the 70%-tier line; describe your actual social functioning truthfully. Expect an in-person/telehealth exam regardless of how strong the private evidence is; if the examiner is dismissive of Cherie's findings, that is not decisive — the rater weighs the whole record.

**After the decision:** if MH lands at 90% and the sleep study is positive → pursue the sleep-apnea secondary to reach 100% combined. If the decision fails to engage with Cherie's opinion → challenge in writing on exam-adequacy / whole-record grounds before the one-year deadline (38 CFR 3.2500 **[confirmed — `CLAIM-COMPLETION-PLAN` §2.5]**), calendared the moment any decision issues.

## 4. Representation — when, who, and free vs. paid

Full detail and the vetting checklist: [`ATTORNEY-AND-LEGAL-RESOURCES-KB.md`](./ATTORNEY-AND-LEGAL-RESOURCES-KB.md). The essentials:

- **Right now, the free accredited VSO is the correct representation.** VA accredits three representative types (VSO reps, attorneys, claims agents; 38 CFR 14.629 **[confirmed]**). VSO services are **always free**; attorneys/agents **cannot legally charge a fee for work before VA's initial decision** on a claim (38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(1) **[confirmed]**).
- **The synthesis insight:** whether an accredited **attorney can take this claim on contingency now** turns on the _same fact_ as the effective-date question (I-16) — the procedural posture of this claim. Because it is filed as a **Supplemental Claim** (which by definition follows a prior decision — the record notes a 2015 MST denial), a prior decision very likely exists, which would mean **an attorney can be engaged on contingency now** for the supplemental/appeal. Confirm at the eFolder pull (I-16); one lookup settles both the effective-date bucket and representation timing.
- **Where an attorney adds the most leverage:** hold an accredited attorney **in reserve for a post-decision HLR/Board appeal** arguing the rater failed to weigh Cherie's treating-clinician opinion under 38 CFR 4.126(a)/4.2 and _Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake_ **[pending citation-verifier]**. Run a free consult now if useful (bring only claim status + condition list; verify accreditation at **ogc.va.gov**; ask about MST/PTSD _schedular_ appeal experience and EAJA). **Never pay upfront on an initial claim (illegal).**
- **Fee rules (citation-verified 2026-07-09):** ≤20% of past-due benefits is presumed reasonable; >33⅓% presumed unreasonable; VA direct-pays an attorney from back-pay only if the fee is contingent, ≤20%, and the attorney is accredited on the fee-allocation-notice date. EAJA (28 U.S.C. § 2412) can shift fees to the government at the CAVC level.
- **Firms are candidates to vet, not a ranked "best" list** — the deep-research verification could not certify any firm's reputation claims (they're marketing). Vet accreditation yourself; judge fit in the free consult, not by advertised win rates.
