## 2. How the process actually works, end-to-end (new this pass, primary-sourced)

This section exists because the corpus's legal research (Mauerhan/Vazquez-Claudio/Bankhead/4.130) was already strong, but the _mechanics_ of exams, DBQs, and post-decision options hadn't been independently verified against primary sources. Today's research fills that in and, in two places, adds real leverage the existing plan didn't have.

### 2.1 The pipeline, step by step

1. **Exam assignment.** VA schedules the exam with either an in-house VHA clinician or one of four contracted vendors — **Loyal Source Government Services, OptumServe, Leidos QTC, or Veterans Evaluation Services (VES)**. You cannot request a specific vendor; assignment is by location/availability. [va.gov/resources/va-claim-exam](https://www.va.gov/resources/va-claim-exam/)
2. **Acceptable Clinical Evidence (ACE) — almost certainly does not apply here.** ACE lets a VBA-assigned clinician rate the claim from existing records alone, without an in-person exam. It is **examiner/VBA-controlled, not something you or your VSO can formally request** — a VSO cover letter arguing "the file is already sufficient" is advocacy, not a procedural trigger. [news.va.gov/9048](https://news.va.gov/9048/ace-eliminates-need-for-some-in-person-disability-exams/) Sources disagree on whether mental-health claims are _categorically_ excluded from ACE or just disfavored, but they agree it's rare for exactly this fact pattern (a new/re-evaluated diagnosis, not a stable re-confirmation). **Plan around an in-person or telehealth exam happening — do not expect the strong private evidence to eliminate it.**
3. **The exam itself.** A board-certified/eligible psychiatrist or licensed doctorate-level psychologist (or closely-supervised trainee) conducts a structured interview, typically with the **PCL-5** (20-item self-report) and often the **CAPS-5** structured interview, running 30–90 minutes, and completes the appropriate DBQ (see §2.2). Known, well-documented failure modes: **"good day" bias** (you under-describe severity by defaulting to a typical day, not your worst), the **single-encounter snapshot problem**, and **incomplete file review** by the examiner. [cck-law.com/blog/compensation-pension-exam-ptsd](https://cck-law.com/blog/compensation-pension-exam-ptsd/)
4. **The rater, not the examiner, decides the rating** — and is legally required to look past the exam snapshot (see §2.3). A **VSR** reviews the exam report against the whole file; if complete, an **RVSR** issues the rating decision. There's no "proposed rating" step for a new grant — that mechanism is for _reductions_ of existing ratings, not initial adjudications.
5. **Decision letter issues**, stating service connection and percentage.
6. **If unsatisfying**, three review lanes exist, each with a one-year deadline (see §2.4).

### 2.2 The DBQ system — which form, and who can fill it out (this matters for Cherie's paperwork)

VA runs a family of forms, not one "PTSD DBQ":

| Form                                                                 | Covers                                                   | Who can complete it                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **21-0960P-2** — Mental Disorders (other than PTSD/eating disorders) | MDD, GAD, OCD, SUD, etc.                                 | **Public** — a private treating provider may complete and submit it directly.                                                  |
| **21-0960P-3** — PTSD _Review_                                       | Re-evaluating an _already service-connected_ PTSD rating | **Public** — private provider can complete it.                                                                                 |
| **21-0960P-4** — PTSD _Initial_                                      | First-time PTSD service connection                       | **Restricted** — VHA staff or a VA-contract examiner only. A private psychiatrist legally cannot complete and submit this one. |

Source: the live form itself explicitly states "If the Veteran has a diagnosis of PTSD, the Initial PTSD Questionnaire must be completed by a VHA staff or contract examiner in lieu of this questionnaire." [benefits.va.gov/compensation/docs/mental_disorders.pdf](https://www.benefits.va.gov/compensation/docs/mental_disorders.pdf)

**Why this matters concretely:** since PTSD has never been service-connected, Cherie legally cannot complete a "PTSD DBQ" for you — the corpus's existing plan already avoided this mistake by pointing her at **21-0960P-2** (correct, since MDD/GAD/OCD are her diagnoses too), but her letter needs to carry the PTSD-specific narrative separately, since the form she's allowed to use doesn't have a PTSD-specific section. **Keep doing exactly what Tier 1 #3/Tier 2 #6 of the existing checklist already says — this just confirms it's the legally correct form and explains why.**

**Section map of 21-0960P-2** (confirmed from the live PDF): diagnosis (§1), the single-select occupational/social-impairment checkbox (§1C — the box the rater leans on hardest), clinical history (§2A–F), a symptom checklist mirroring every § 4.130 tier (§3), behavioral observations (§4), other symptoms (§5), competency (§6), and **§7 "Remarks (including any testing results)"** — explicitly optional, which is exactly where DASS-21/WHODAS scores would go if Cherie chooses to reference them.

**A real weakness to close, not previously flagged in this corpus:** DBQ forms don't require the completing clinician to explain _why_ a box is checked — [CCK Law notes](https://cck-law.com/blog/disability-benefits-questionnaires-dbqs-evidence-for-your-va-disability-claim/) VA adjudicators often discount bare DBQs for exactly this reason. **New action item:** ask Cherie to attach a short narrative addendum to the DBQ (or fold it into her updated letter) that ties each checked symptom to the § 4.130 impairment tier and to concrete functional consequences — this is exactly what Vazquez-Claudio requires anyway (symptoms must be shown to _cause_ the impairment, not just be present), so it strengthens the DBQ's legal sufficiency and its practical persuasiveness at the same time.

**Confirmed verbatim, 70%/100% checkbox text (38 CFR § 4.130, cross-checked against Cornell LII):**

- **70%:** "Occupational and social impairment, with deficiencies in most areas, such as work, school, family relations, judgment, thinking, or mood, due to such symptoms as: suicidal ideation; obsessional rituals which interfere with routine activities; speech intermittently illogical, obscure, or irrelevant; near-continuous panic or depression affecting the ability to function independently, appropriately and effectively; impaired impulse control...; spatial disorientation; neglect of personal appearance and hygiene; difficulty in adapting to stressful circumstances...; inability to establish and maintain effective relationships."
- **100%:** "Total occupational and social impairment, due to such symptoms as: gross impairment in thought processes or communication; persistent delusions or hallucinations; grossly inappropriate behavior; persistent danger of hurting self or others; intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene); disorientation to time or place; memory loss for names of close relatives, own occupation, or own name."

This matches the text already used in `PATH-TO-100-PERCENT-2026-07-07.md` — no correction needed, just independent confirmation.

### 2.3 The strongest new legal finding this pass: your treating clinician's opinion has _binding_ case-law support, not just persuasive BVA templates

The existing plan's §2.1 leans on **BVA A25038905** (non-precedential, persuasive only) for the proposition that a treating clinician's DBQ can outweigh a lowball C&P examiner. Today's research found the actual **binding CAVC precedent** this rests on:

- **38 CFR § 4.126(a)** (verbatim, verified again today): "The rating agency shall assign an evaluation based on **all the evidence of record** that bears on occupational and social impairment **rather than solely on the examiner's assessment of the level of disability at the moment of the examination.**" [ecfr.gov/4.126](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-4/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRfa64377db09ae97/section-4.126)
- **38 CFR § 4.2** (verified, not previously cited in this corpus): "It is the responsibility of the rating specialist to interpret reports of examination in the light of the whole recorded history... If a diagnosis is not supported by the findings on the examination report or if the report does not contain sufficient detail, it is incumbent upon the rating board to return the report as inadequate for evaluation purposes." [law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.2)
- **Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake, 22 Vet.App. 295 (2008)** — a **precedential CAVC decision** (not previously in this corpus): the Board cannot simply credit a C&P exam over a private opinion _because VA ordered it_; both are "nothing more or less than expert witnesses," and probative value turns on "factually accurate, fully articulated, sound reasoning" — not who wrote it or whether they had claims-file access. [CAVC opinion](https://www.uscourts.cavc.gov/documents/Nieves-Rodriguez_06-0312_Filing_Version.pdf)

**Why this is worth adding to the plan, flagged for citation-verifier before filing:** A25038905 is a single non-precedential BVA grant — useful as a template, but it binds no one. Nieves-Rodriguez is **binding CAVC precedent** that stands for the same proposition with real legal weight. If the C&P examiner lowballs the mental-health rating, the strongest procedural argument isn't just "here's a similar case that came out our way" — it's "the Board is legally required to explain, with sound reasoning, why it's crediting the examiner over Dr. Mills, and cannot default to the examiner simply because VA ordered the exam." **Action:** have this ready for the VSO/attorney as the legal anchor for any post-decision challenge (Tier 5), not just A25038905.

### 2.4 Post-decision options, concretely mapped to your two scenarios

| Lane                                          | Reviews                                     | New evidence?                             | Typical time (secondary-sourced, treat as directional) | Use when                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Higher-Level Review (HLR)**                 | Existing record only, for clear error       | No                                        | VA goal 125 days; real-world ~3–6 months               | The exam/decision failed to engage with Dr. Mills's opinion (a 4.126(a)/4.2/Nieves-Rodriguez argument) — no new facts, just "the rater got this wrong." |
| **Supplemental Claim**                        | New adjudication                            | Yes, required, and VA must help gather it | ~60–75 days typical, longer if a new exam is ordered   | You have genuinely new evidence (e.g., a rebuttal letter from Cherie addressing the exam's specific findings).                                          |
| **Board of Veterans' Appeals, Direct Review** | Existing record, a judge (fastest BVA lane) | No                                        | ~7 months (VA-published figure)                        | Same as HLR but you want a Board-level (not RO-level) reviewer.                                                                                         |

[CCK Law — HLR vs. Supplemental Claim](https://cck-law.com/veterans-law/what-is-the-difference-between-the-higher-level-review-lane-and-the-supplemental-claim-lane/) · [Board decision wait times](https://department.va.gov/board-of-veterans-appeals/decision-wait-times/veteran-choices-for-type-of-board-appeal-influences-wait-times/)

**Practical best move if the exam findings are weak but you have a rebuttal letter ready:** submit the rebuttal letter _before_ the rating decision issues, not after — VA's own guidance and practitioner consensus agree this is far more effective than any post-decision lane, since the rater is required to weigh it either way but is more likely to do so proactively if the conflict is flagged before the decision is made.

### 2.5 A genuinely new open question this pass surfaced — confirm this week

**38 CFR § 3.2500(h)** governs effective dates for Supplemental Claims:

- **(h)(1):** if filed within **one year** of a prior unfavorable decision on the _same issue_, and the review chain stays unbroken, the effective date can go back to **the original claim date** — potentially significant retroactive back-pay.
- **(h)(2):** if filed **more than one year** after that prior decision, the effective date is capped at **the date this Supplemental Claim was received (29 May 2026)** — no matter how far back the evidence shows the condition existed.

**This corpus has never established which bucket applies, because it's never confirmed the date and outcome of the prior decision this Supplemental Claim is responding to.** [ecfr.gov/3.2500](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/chapter-I/part-3/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR429f47d98271c40/section-3.2500) There's also a more basic question worth confirming with the VSO: **was the mental-health cluster ever previously and formally denied**, making this a genuine "Supplemental Claim" in the AMA sense — or is this actually the _first_ time PTSD/MST has been adjudicated, in which case ordinary original-claim effective-date rules (generally the date the claim was received) might govern instead of §3.2500 at all? **This is a real gap, not a hypothetical one — the answer changes what's financially at stake and doesn't cost anything to find out. Ask the VSO this week (folded into Tier 0 below).**
