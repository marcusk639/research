## Substance use: how it fits the rating

**One rating, not added together.** VA rates all mental disorders together (PTSD, MDD, GAD, OCD, and SUD if service connected) as **one** evaluation, based on total impairment. Adding SUD to the list doesn't add a percentage. What matters is **whether its impairment counts.**

- **If SUD is service connected as secondary to PTSD, its effects count.** That includes relapses, residential stays, work leaves and the strain on the marriage. Drug-abuse disability caused by a service-connected condition is compensable (*Allen v. Principi*, 237 F.3d 1368; 38 CFR 3.301(c)(3), both verified 2026-10-08). Much of your strongest work evidence, the treatment stays that interrupted every job, is SUD treatment. If it counts, it supports 70% and TDIU.
- **If VA treats SUD as primary, its impairment is excluded.** The examiner would then try to separate what's due to PTSD, depression, anxiety and OCD from what's due to substance use, and rate only the first. That could pull the rating down.
- **If the examiner can't separate them,** all the symptoms are attributed to the service-connected condition (*Mittleider v. West*, 11 Vet. App. 181, verified in the July pass).

**Why the pre-service question matters here.** *Allen* requires "clear medical evidence" that the substance problem was caused by the service-connected condition. Two intake records listing opioid use at 18 (Victory 2018, Starlite Jan 2023) are what an examiner could use to call the SUD primary or pre-existing. Your consistent answer on pre-service history protects your credibility, and it also protects whether roughly half the occupational evidence counts.

**What supports secondary connection:**
- Cherie's letter: SUD "at least as likely as not" caused or aggravated by PTSD.
- Reyes: your drinking increased from summer 2012.
- Your personal statement: weekend drinking became nightly after the assault.
- Your 21-4138: first opioid exposure was the May 2011 ankle-fracture prescriptions.

**At the exam:**

- Be honest about **current** use: what, how much, how often, last use, treatment.
