## THE BRIGHT LINE — read before anything else (this is also the #1 strategic point)

You said two things that need a direct, honest response, because getting them wrong destroys the whole claim:

1. **"I can get buddy letters for whatever I need."** Buddy letters are powerful **only when the writer attests to what they actually witnessed** — a behavioral change they saw, a duty you performed, an exposure they observed. That is legitimate and valuable. **Soliciting anyone to attest to things they did not witness is fraud** (18 U.S.C. § 1001 — up to 5 years/count) and it is _especially_ self-destructive here: the MST/PTSD claim rests almost entirely on **your credibility**. One buddy letter that an adjudicator catches as coached can collapse the entire mental-health cluster. Get many letters — but only true ones, describing only what each person genuinely saw.

2. **TDIU / income.** Do **not** file TDIU or claim unemployability while earning ~$200K, and do **not** stage an income reduction to the family CPA firm to manufacture eligibility. That is the paradigm VA-OIG fraud case (_Faust v. West_; _LaBruzza v. McDonough_) and it would poison every other contention. A **70–90% combined schedular rating is achievable on honest facts today** without touching employment.

Everything below stays on the legitimate side of that line. The honest path is also the _strong_ path.

Order worst-first; each new rating applies to _remaining efficiency_. e.g. 70% leaves 30%; 50% of that 30% = 15; 70+15 = 85 → **rounds to 90%**.
