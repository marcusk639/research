Part B — The Mock Examination

Real exam order. Each block: the examiner's question and likely follow-ups → what is being assessed → what your file already shows → how to answer truthfully and completely.

How to rehearse with this

Have Erica read the bold question and the italic follow-ups. Answer out loud, in your own words, from memory. Then read the guidance together and ask: Did I give a number? A duration? A consequence? A real example from the last month? Did I leave anything true out because it was embarrassing? Do not memorize phrasing — examiners recognize rehearsed language, and it costs credibility.

Key: Assessing = what the examiner is measuring · Your file = what they may already have read · Guidance = how to answer well · Trap = the specific way this question goes wrong. HIGH = among the Ten Things.

B0 · Opening (≈5 minutes)

B0.1"Can you confirm your name, date of birth, and where you are right now? Is anyone with you?"

Assessing Identity, location (for emergencies), privacy, and orientation (the first item of the mental-status exam).

Guidance DOB 05/30/1989. Give your Austin address. Introduce Erica and ask whether she may stay (A6). If any document the examiner reads shows "30 May 1998" or "Kline," that's a clerical error in Cherie's first letter — just correct it.

B0.2"How are you doing today?" HIGH

"How did you sleep last night? Did you have any trouble getting here / setting up?"

Assessing Self-reported mood, which is often charted verbatim ("Veteran reports mood is 'okay'").

Guidance Answer the real question. If you're anxious, exhausted or barely slept, say so. If Erica had to get you up, showered or set up for the call, say that now — it heads off a "well-groomed, cooperative, punctual" impression.

Trap Reflexive "good, thanks." That word ends up in the report.

B0.3"Have you used any alcohol, drugs, or medications today that could affect this interview? When did you last use opioids?" HIGH

Assessing Validity of the exam itself, and whether what they observe is substance-induced (intoxication or withdrawal).

Your file Relapse documented June 2026 (opioid pain pills and kratom, Suboxone discontinued); Harmony Junction April–May 2026. You report current opioid use.

Guidance Tell the truth, including the last time you used and whether you are in withdrawal. Add, briefly and truthfully, that you are trying to get back into treatment and what steps you've taken (calls, referrals, waitlists).

Trap Denying use that the examiner then sees on camera, or that conflicts with records — one false answer can discredit every true one.

B0.4"Do you understand the purpose of this exam? I'm not your treating provider, and what you tell me goes into a report for VA."

Guidance Yes. You can also say if you'd prefer not to go into detail about the assault itself. The C&P Clinicians' Guide discourages examiners from pressing for a detailed account of MST, but they must still establish what happened at a basic level (B4).

B2 · Pre-military history

Why this section matters more than it looks: the examiner is establishing your baseline and looking for alternative causes. Your records contain an age-18 self-report of anxiety/depression treatment, kratom use from 18, a DUI at 18 (later expunged), a paraphernalia arrest at 20, both "pretty good childhood" and "neglected most of childhood," and self-reported autism. Answer every one honestly. Hiding a pre-service fact that is sitting in the Starlite record is far more damaging than the fact itself. Being sound at entry is presumed unless something was noted on your entrance exam (38 U.S.C. § 1111), and aggravation is a separate route — those are legal questions for the rater, not things you need to argue.

B2.1"Tell me about growing up — family, home life, school."

"Any abuse or neglect? How did you get along with your parents? Any learning problems, special education, fights, suspensions? Friends growing up?"

Assessing Developmental baseline, childhood trauma (a competing cause), and social functioning before service.

Your file Starlite has both "'pretty good' childhood" (2022) and "neglected most of childhood" (2023), plus "on the spectrum" since childhood. The 2023 Starlite biopsychosocial attributes distress to childhood neglect, autism and work stress, with no MST mention. Paternal criticism appears once as a relapse trigger.

Guidance Give your honest, considered account. If both descriptions feel partly true (a stable surface, emotional neglect underneath), say exactly that. Then describe how you actually functioned before service: friends, school, work, sports. The examiner needs a baseline to measure the 2012 change against.

Trap Defensiveness ("my childhood was perfect"). Records already say otherwise, and an examiner can read that as unreliability.

B2.2"Did you have any mental-health problems or treatment before you joined?" HIGH

"Your records say you were diagnosed with anxiety and depression around 18 — tell me about that. Any medications? Counseling? How long?"

Your file 2018 Victory (age-19 self-report) and 2022/Jan 2023 Starlite (age-18 self-report) of anxiety/depression treatment before enlisting. Your entrance exam (in the STRs) is the controlling record — check whether it noted anything if you have a copy.

Guidance Say exactly what happened — who, what, how long, how severe — and how you functioned then (graduated, worked, passed MEPS, enlisted, BMT Honor Graduate). If it was brief or mild, describe it accurately. Then, if true, describe what changed after 2012 in kind and severity: nightmares, hypervigilance, shame, panic, SI, substance escalation. That contrast is the core of an aggravation-or-new-onset finding. The examiner decides which it is; your job is accurate facts.

Trap Contradicting the record ("I never had any problems before"). The Starlite entry is in your file.

B4 · The stressor (MST)

For Criterion A the examiner needs to know that a qualifying event occurred, roughly when, and how you responded — not a graphic account. You can say "I'd rather not go into the details, but I can tell you what happened in general." If you choose to say more, everything must match what you've filed: summer / mid-to-late 2012, a coworker's wife, at a party, while you were incapacitated; preceded by harassment from early 2012.

B4.1"What is the event or events you believe caused your symptoms?" HIGH

"Approximately when? Where? Who was involved — another service member? Were you drinking? Did you feel your life or safety was threatened? What did you feel right after?"

Your file 21-0781 Item 9 says "same female service member." Reyes ("wife of a coworker"), Poquette ("one of our friend's wife"), your personal statement and the CPT worksheet all describe a coworker's wife. Cherie: "an incapacitated assault … male survivors of female-perpetrator assault."

Guidance State it in your own words at the level of detail you can tolerate.
