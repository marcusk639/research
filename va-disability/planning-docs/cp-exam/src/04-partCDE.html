Part C — The Questionnaires

What each instrument measures and how to take it honestly. Item wording is paraphrased; you'll see the real forms on Tuesday.

One rule for every questionnaire: read each item, think about the time window it asks about (usually "past month" or "past two weeks"), and answer that item truthfully. Don't answer by pattern ("all 4s"), don't skim, and don't soften because answering feels dramatic. Psychometric scores never stand alone as a diagnosis or rating — they corroborate the interview — but an invalid profile can be used to discount everything.

Logistics first, then self-scoring, then the post-exam playbook.

E1. Before Tuesday

  • ☐ Confirm the appointment time and video link with VES (877-637-8387); confirm your phone number on file.
  • ☐ Test camera, microphone and internet in the actual room; charger plugged in; second device ready.
  • ☐ Photo ID; appointment letter; medication list (names, doses, prescriber).
  • ☐ Fill in the frequency worksheet (notes doc) with Erica.
  • ☐ Re-read your own signed statements once (personal statement, 21-0781), plus Cherie's and Erica's letters, for consistency.
  • ☐ Decide with Erica: she sits in only if the examiner agrees; she doesn't answer for you; she can add collateral at the end if invited.
  • ☐ Plan for safety: naloxone accessible; 988 → press 1.
  • ☐ Plan to be neither impaired nor in unmanaged withdrawal; tell the examiner honestly at the start if either applies.
  • ☐ Ask your VSO (today or Monday): is everything submitted actually in the claims file — Cherie's updated letter, Erica's 7/12 statement, Reyes, Poquette, DASS-21/WHODAS? Was the 21-0781 perpetrator correction filed?
  • ☐ Sleep apnea: confirm whether an Intent to File (21-0966) was ever filed. If not, file one now — it costs nothing and locks an effective date.

E2. During the exam

  • Log in 10–15 minutes early. Quiet, private room, door closed.
  • Answer "How are you?" truthfully.
  • Give numbers, durations and consequences — a real example from the last month for each.
  • Typical day and bad day — and how many bad days.
  • Correct errors calmly (perpetrator status; "service member").
  • If you don't know or don't remember, say so. Don't fill gaps.
  • If the examiner asks Erica to leave, agree.
  • If you need a break, ask for one. Don't push through a panic attack silently — tell the examiner it's happening.
  • Use the closing question (B13.5) to add anything missed.

E3. Red flags to note afterward

  • Exam under ~30 minutes with no testing.
  • No questions about ADLs, social life outside the marriage, SI, or OCD.
  • Examiner says or implies non-reporting means it didn't happen.
  • No discussion of the records or of Cherie's opinion.
  • Examiner refuses to consider symptoms because of substance use without analysis.

If you realize you misstated something, tell your VSO. A short, truthful clarifying statement (VA Form 21-4138) is far better than leaving an error in the record.

E6. After the exam

  1. Get the DBQ. Exam results usually appear in your VA.gov claim file or C-file over the following days or weeks. Ask your VSO to pull it, or request your claims file.
  2. Read it against the rubric. If it is inaccurate or incomplete (missing ADLs, social, OCD or SI; treating non-reporting as negative evidence; dismissing symptoms as substance-induced with no analysis; ignoring Cherie's opinion), your VSO can submit a written response before the decision, pointing to Barr v. Nicholson (an exam VA provides must be adequate), AZ v. Shinseki (missing report ≠ negative evidence), 38 CFR 4.126(a) (rate on the whole record), and Mittleider (inseparable effects go to the service-connected condition). A treating-provider DBQ from Cherie or a doctoral-level clinician can counterweight a thin exam.
  3. At the decision: calendar the one-year deadline immediately (Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Board appeal — your VSO picks the lane). Under Rice, VA may also consider TDIU because unemployment is in the record. You don't have to request it, and it wouldn't stop the schedular argument.
  4. Treatment. Whatever happens with the claim, getting into SUD and mental-health treatment is the priority, and it also documents the condition going forward.
  5. Sleep apnea. Still unfiled and no study yet. ITF now; study when you can; file inside the ITF year.
The whole exam in one sentence

Tell the truth at its real size: how often, how long, how bad, what it stops you doing, and who has to do it for you — and fix the few known contradictions calmly rather than letting the examiner find them.
