## Track 2 — Secondary conditions (the path to 100% combined if MH lands below schedular-100)

Per `100-PERCENT-SCHEDULAR-PATHWAY.md` §7, run against the current, real state (not a hypothetical):

- **From a 70% MH anchor:** reaching 100% combined needs roughly 80%+ worth of other conditions — a heavy stack (sleep apnea 50 + ankle 20 + tinnitus 10 + GERD 10 ≈ 90%, not quite 100%; a heavier stack — add migraines 30 — gets to ≈95%, which rounds to 100%).
- **From a 90% MH anchor:** a single 50% secondary (sleep apnea, if CPAP-required and established as secondary to PTSD) combines to ≈95%, which **rounds to 100%.** This is the efficient path, and it's why the sleep study is Track 1 item 1, not a Track 2 afterthought.
- Other secondaries worth pursuing in parallel, roughly in order of value: sleep apnea (highest leverage) → gait-related knee/hip/back secondary to the ankle → GERD/migraines/hypertension secondary to PTSD, if genuinely present → hearing loss (additive to tinnitus, JP-8 ototoxicity nexus).

**Sleep apnea specifics** (`SLEEP-APNEA-SECONDARY-CLAIM.md`): the legal theory is sound and matches two real, non-precedential BVA grants on this exact fact pattern (Citation Nr. 1648003, A21018009), both won on treating-provider opinions, not paid IMOs. Once a diagnosis exists, get the treating psychiatrist or a sleep specialist to write a nexus opinion addressing the hyperarousal/arousal-threshold mechanism directly — not just "obesity from medications," which is the weaker of the two available theories.

## Track 4 — TDIU (not pursued, and that's fine)

Per _Rice v. Shinseki_, VA must consider TDIU once unemployability is reasonably raised by the record — you can decline to pursue it as a claim, but can't force VA to ignore the evidence. This isn't a real risk in practice: worst case, TDIU becomes an automatic fallback floor (paying at the 100% rate) while you keep arguing for schedular 100% by framing the case around **both** occupational and social impairment consistently, not "can't work" alone.
