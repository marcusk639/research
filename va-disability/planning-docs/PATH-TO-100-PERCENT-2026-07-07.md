## 2. The two legal insights this plan is built on (both new this pass, both verified)

Everything downstream follows from these two corrections to how the claim has been understood.

### 2.1 The C&P examiner does not decide your rating — the rater does, on the _whole record_ (38 CFR 4.126(a))

The corpus has treated the C&P examiner's DBQ selection as "the single most outcome-determinative item." That is a fair _practical_ observation but **wrong as a legal proposition**, and the difference is strategically decisive. **38 CFR 4.126(a)** (verified verbatim, law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/4.126):

> "The rating agency shall assign an evaluation based on **all the evidence of record** that bears on occupational and social impairment **rather than solely on the examiner's assessment of the level of disability at the moment of the examination.**"

**Why this changes the strategy:** the C&P examiner's "total occupational and social impairment" checkbox is the _most influential_ single piece of evidence, but it is **evidence the rater weighs, not a verdict the rater must adopt.** This cuts in your favor:

- A **treating clinician's** opinion or DBQ selecting "total occupational and social impairment" — from someone with a multi-year treatment relationship — **can outweigh a skeptical C&P examiner who lowballs.** This is not theory. **Verified BVA grant A25038905 (2025)** did exactly this: a treating psychiatrist's DBQ endorsing persistent delusions and total impairment was found "the more highly probative opinion" while the VA examiner's opinion was given "low probative value," and the veteran was granted **100%.** (Non-precedential/persuasive — but a real, on-point template.) [va.gov/vetapp25/Files4/A25038905.txt]
- **Cherie Mills's signed letter already states, in her own words, "total impairment in occupational and social realms."** That is her genuine clinical judgment, already on the record. A **Mental Disorders DBQ** (VA Form 21-0960P-2) completed by her, consistent with that letter — selecting the "total occupational and social impairment" box (item C1) she has already concluded applies — becomes competing, corroborating evidence to whatever the VA C&P examiner finds. This is legitimate precisely because it reflects a conclusion she already reached on her own, not one suggested to her.

> **Recommended corpus edit (tracked in §11):** soften the "single most outcome-determinative item" phrasing in `CP-EXAM-PREP-MENTAL-HEALTH.md` / `100-PERCENT-SCHEDULAR-PATHWAY.md` §3 to "the most influential single item in practice, though legally the rater weighs the whole record under 38 CFR 4.126(a)." The examiner still matters enormously — but the case is not lost if that examiner lowballs, _provided the treating-clinician record is strong._

### 2.2 The 70→100 gap is the "total _social_ impairment" prong — and it is your biggest vulnerability (38 CFR 4.130 + 4.126(b))

The 70% and 100% tiers differ in one structurally critical way (both verified verbatim, 38 CFR 4.130):

- **70%** names "**inability to establish and maintain effective relationships**" — relationships may exist but are dysfunctional; _some_ social capacity remains.
- **100%** requires "**total** occupational **AND** social impairment" — social capacity essentially extinguished: near-total isolation, no meaningful relationships.

And **38 CFR 4.126(b)** (verified verbatim): the rater "**shall not assign an evaluation solely on the basis of social impairment.**" So the 100% case needs **both** prongs — the mirror image of the existing warning against an occupation-only ("I can't work") frame. You cannot reach 100% on isolation alone, and you cannot reach it on inability-to-work alone. You need both, documented.

**How verified 100% grants prove the social prong** — concrete, observable near-total isolation, not adjectives:

- BVA **1451194**: "no friends, unable to keep a job." [va.gov/vetapp14/Files6/1451194.txt]
- BVA **1623106**: "Most of the time, the Veteran is inactive and isolated in his house." [va.gov/vetapp16/Files3/1623106.txt]

**Objective corroboration now in hand (2026-07-07):** the WHODAS 2.0 of 11 Jun 2026 (§11) scores **"Getting along with people" 70%** and **"Participation in society" 75%** ("high functional limitations"), and rates **"making new friends" at the ceiling ("extreme / cannot do")** — the first objective, instrument-based evidence of _severe_ social impairment. It supports the social prong strongly; as self-report data (caveats, §11) it evidences _severe_ impairment, not by itself the _total_ impairment the 100% tier requires.

**Your specific vulnerability, stated plainly:** you are **married, and your spouse actively helps with your care.** A rater can argue a maintained marriage cuts against _total_ social impairment. The honest way to hold both truths — without overstating anything — is the one the facts actually support: **the marriage has become a caregiving dependency, not a reciprocal social relationship.** Erica helping you with hygiene and reminding you of things is _evidence of severity_ (a 100%-tier ADL marker), not evidence of preserved social function; and you are otherwise isolated from friends, extended family, and community. **This framing is genuine and it is the linchpin of Route 1.** (Caveat — the plan's biggest doctrinal vulnerability, now researched by `citation-verifier`: persuasive Board authority cuts **both ways**. On an _illustrative, non-representative_ sample the corpus found **3 adverse (BVA 1513531, 22009295, 22015499) vs 1 favorable (1648197)** — not a measure of the weight of authority; 1513531 and 22009295 confirmed 2026-07-09. **BVA 1648197 supports** the framing — it granted 100% total impairment to a _married_ veteran, reasoning that a _conflict-ridden_ marriage plus broad social isolation is not the same as _effective_ social functioning. But **BVA 22015499 is directly adverse** — it found no total social impairment _because_ the veteran kept "a good relationship with his wife." No decision adopts the specific "caregiving-dependency" wording. So this is **supportable but not settled** — it turns entirely on the factual characterization of the marriage. Cite 1648197 as persuasive support; be ready to distinguish 22015499. Both are non-precedential. See §11-V1.)

| Step                     | Combine                         | Result   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- | -------- |
| Mental health            | 90                              | 90       |

### 5.2 The NPRM risk on the sleep-apnea 50% tier — why timing matters

A pending proposed rule (**87 FR 8474**, Feb. 15, 2022; with a supplemental NPRM at **89 FR 74162**, Sept. 12, 2024) would restructure DC 6847 so the automatic 50%-for-CPAP tier disappears. **Corrected per `citation-verifier`:** the proposed tiers are **0/10/50/100 based on treatment responsiveness — there is no 30% tier**, and a veteran whose OSA is fully controlled on CPAP would likely fall to **0% or 10%**, not 30%. As of this writing the rule is **still proposed — no final rule, no effective date, and the current 50% CPAP tier remains in effect**; existing CPAP-tier ratings are expected to be grandfathered if it finalizes. **Implication:** get the sleep study done and the sleep-apnea claim _filed_ sooner rather than later, so a 50% grant lands (and is grandfathered) before any rule change. ## 6. Why pursue both routes simultaneously (and the one place they interact)

The two routes share ~90% of their evidence work — a stronger mental-health record serves both. The **only** divergence is emphasis at the C&P exam and in the argument to the rater:

- **For Route 1**, you argue the mental-health picture reaches _total_ impairment (aim for the 100% box).
- **For Route 2**, you argue it reaches at least _deficiencies in most areas → 90%_ (a lower, safer bar), and let sleep apnea finish.

These are **not in tension** — arguing for 100% does not forfeit 90%; the rater assigns whatever the evidence supports. The correct posture: **build the record to genuinely support "total," present it that way, and treat the 90%+sleep-apnea combined path as the floor you land on if the examiner/rater stops short.** The sleep study is what makes that floor a 100% floor instead of a 90% one. **Do not let the sleep study slip — it is the difference between "we tried for 100 and got 90" and "we got 100 either way."**

### I-2 — Severe symptoms surfacing first at the C&P exam are credibility-fragile

**What:** a genuine symptom that appears for the first time at the comp exam, absent from treatment records, is _legally cognizable but credibility-vulnerable._ Verified law: **Buchanan v. Nicholson** — absence from records "may be a fact the Board can consider and weigh," but "does not, in and of itself, render lay evidence not credible"; **Caluza v. Brown** — consistency with the contemporaneous record is a permissible credibility factor; **Kahana v. Shinseki** — the Board may _not_ reject lay evidence merely because the symptom "should have been documented."
**Why it matters:** the delusions and ADL-dependence are exactly this risk. A skeptical examiner/rater can lawfully weight their record-absence against them (just not reject them solely on that basis).
**Resolution options:**

- **(a) The fix is documentation timing, not legal argument** — see I-1(a)/(b). Get them charted before the exam.
- **(b) Frame as genuine worsening/recent disclosure**, which is truthful (the delusions and the ADL pattern are recent/worsening) and legally converts "why now?" into "the condition deteriorated," triggering the worsening-exam duty.
- **(c) Corroboration from a second source** (Erica's statement + treating-clinician documentation) — two independent genuine accounts of the same symptom are far harder to discount than the veteran's report alone.
- **(d) The veteran's own affirmative explanation for the record's silence (his account, 2026-07-07) — the strongest single answer to the "why isn't it documented?" challenge.** Across his ~9 addiction-treatment episodes he **deliberately minimized and withheld his psychiatric symptoms**: to **avoid involuntary psychiatric holds**, to "fly under the radar" and just get through detox and off drugs, and because he **wasn't ready to talk and didn't trust the intake staff.** This is genuine, coherent, and a clinically-recognized pattern, and it means the contemporaneous records **understate** his psychiatric severity (minimized _downward_, not accurate) — which directly explains the absence of the delusions, the ADL dependence, the SI (I-7), and the MST disclosures (I-9) alike. Legally this is exactly the Buchanan/Kahana situation: absence from records does not render genuine lay evidence not credible, _especially_ where there is a credible reason for the absence. **Best handling:** he states it himself, in his own words, honestly and consistently (at the C&P and/or in a supplemental statement), and **Cherie can corroborate the clinical pattern** — trauma survivors and addiction patients commonly under-disclose to avoid holds and before they trust a provider, and she is the provider he _did_ come to trust (since Feb 2023). **Honest caveat to prepare with the VSO/attorney:** this does acknowledge he was not fully forthcoming with prior providers, so the wording must center the genuine reasons (hold-avoidance, trauma, not-ready, distrust) and stay consistent — framed that way it converts the record's silence into an explained pattern; framed carelessly it could read as general unreliability. The net is strongly favorable, because it explains _downward_ underreporting, which is consistent with a current presentation more severe than the thin record shows. (Supporting clinical literature on under-disclosure in addiction/trauma treatment could be added via a future research/`citation-verifier` pass; not asserted here without verification.)
