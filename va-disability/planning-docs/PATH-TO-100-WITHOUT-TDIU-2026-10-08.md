## 3. Routes

### Route 1: Schedular 100% for mental health

**The law.**

- 4.130's 100% level is "total occupational and social impairment, due to such symptoms as..." The listed symptoms include "persistent danger of hurting self or others" and "intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene)."
- The symptoms are examples, not a checklist (_Mauerhan v. Principi_, 16 Vet. App. 436, 442).
- The symptoms must cause the level of impairment (_Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki_, 713 F.3d 112, 117–18).
- _Bankhead v. Shulkin_, 29 Vet. App. 10, has two holdings that matter here:
  - suicidal ideation alone "may" support 70%, and passive ideation counts;
  - ideation is a 70%-level symptom; risk of self-harm "is referenced in the criteria for a 100% evaluation as 'persistent danger of hurting self'," and treating the two as one is error (slip op. at 12).
- The whole record governs, not only the exam day (38 CFR 4.126(a)).

### Route 3: Temporary 100% for hospital stays over 21 days

**The law.**

- 38 CFR 4.29 gives 100% when a service-connected disability "required hospital treatment in a Department of Veterans Affairs or an approved hospital for a period in excess of 21 days."
- Under 4.29(b), service connection established after admission can attach "if otherwise in order."
- M21-1 VIII.iv.8.B, read on KnowVA and last updated 8/24/2026:
  - "approved" is not defined in general;
  - a military treatment facility qualifies;
  - non-VA stays approved on or before admission take the admission date;
  - domiciliary or residential status alone does not qualify;
  - the test is "treatment consistent with hospital care."

**What the record shows now.**

- **Harmony, 4/17–5/11/2026 (about 24 days), cuts against.**
  - It predates the claim's likely effective date (5/19/2026).
  - It was medically monitored detox followed by residential care (discharge summary: "Detox, Residential"). The detox phase alone was well under 21 days, and the Board will not add short stays together (A25017339).
  - A25033069 denied a private residential stay, VA-referred, at a comparable program (Victory Addiction Center / Clearview). That is not established to be the same facility as your 2018 Victory Addiction Recovery Center in Lafayette; the reasoning is what matters. The Board's grounds were that the stay was not physician-directed, not confined, and rehabilitative.
- **The August overdose events (one overnight admission, one same-day release) cut against.** They are far under 21 days, there is no special rule for overdoses, and they postdate the claim.
- **Effective-date interaction is neutral.** If an earlier effective date were established (see the lever below), Harmony's timing problem would change, but its type of care would still be doubtful.

**Odds:** **~0% for past stays** (REASONED ESTIMATE). **Future stays:** this applies if a genuine stay of more than 21 days of hospital-level care occurs after service connection.

**What not to do:** schedule or extend treatment to reach 21 days. Treatment length is a clinical decision.

### Route 4: Surgery or cast (38 CFR 4.30)

**Closed.** It requires surgery with at least a month of convalescence, severe postoperative residuals, or cast immobilization of a major joint. Psychiatric and substance-use treatment can't meet it.

### Route 5: Contesting a 70% award

**The law (38 CFR 3.2500, 3.2501, 3.2601, 20.202, 20.302).** You have one year from the decision notice to choose a review option:

- **Higher-Level Review (HLR):** a new look at the same record. Duty-to-assist errors are sent back for correction (3.2601(g)).
- **Supplemental claim:** new and relevant evidence; it can be filed at any time.
- **Board appeal**, on one of three dockets:
  - direct (no new evidence);
  - evidence (new evidence with the notice of disagreement and within 90 days);
  - hearing (testimony, plus evidence within 90 days after the hearing).

Pursuing these continuously preserves the original effective date (3.2500(h)(1)).

**Which lane fits which error:**

| Situation                                                                                 | Lane                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| The examiner ignored the August records, the Reyes markers, or the 2022 disclosure        | **HLR** (duty to assist)            |
| You have a new treating-provider DBQ, an independent evaluation, or records not yet filed | **Supplemental claim**              |
| A credibility judgment went against you and your own testimony matters                    | **Board hearing docket**            |
| Pure legal error on the existing record                                                   | **HLR**, or the Board direct docket |

**Board statistics:** the latest annual report's grant and remand rates and days-to-decision by docket were **not retrieved**; the research lane stalled. Check bva.va.gov before choosing a docket. Board appeals commonly take a year or more.

**Risks:** an appeal reopens the whole record, including the pre-service and SI inconsistencies. A review can't lower a rating that has already been granted, but it focuses attention.

### The effective-date lever (cuts across all routes)

**The law.**

- **If** this supplemental claim was received more than a year after the 2024 decision notice (the 2024 decision date isn't in the files reviewed; confirm with James), it gets an effective date **no earlier than the date it was received** (3.2500(h)(2)). Here that is 5/19/2026.
- **There is an exception, 3.156(c).** If VA associates relevant **official service department records** that existed and weren't in the file when it first decided the claim, it reconsiders. An award based on those records is effective from the date entitlement arose or the date of the **original claim**, whichever is later.
- 3.156(c) doesn't apply to records VA couldn't have obtained because the claimant didn't identify them.

**What it means here.**

- For an Air Force separation in 2013, STRs are held by the **VA Records Management Center**. They may or may not have been associated before the 2024 decision.
- If the STRs or personnel file (for example, the 2012 disciplinary action, any change in EPRs, though your personal statement says duty performance "stayed strong," or the in-service Ambien treatment, which is currently unverified) are newly associated and the award rests on them, the effective date could reach back to the **2024 claim**.
- At $1,961.45/month for 70%, roughly 1.5–2 years of extra back pay would be about $35,000–$47,000. That is an arithmetic estimate, not a projection of what VA will award.

**What to do:** ask James to check the C-file for whether and when STRs and the personnel file were associated. Then request the OMPF (SF-180). This is the single highest-value records step after the August records.

## 5. What not to do, and why

- **Don't present the August overdoses as suicide attempts, or as accidents, beyond what the records and your memory support.** The Board weighs intent and course. A mischaracterization found later damages everything else.
- **Don't time or extend treatment** to reach 21 days (Route 3), and don't seek hospitalization to build a record. It is wrong, and records created by strategy rather than by care are discoverable.
- **Don't claim conditions you don't have** to make the Route 2 arithmetic work.
- **Don't adopt the strongest document's version.** That includes "estranged from family," "FMLA at every job," "feeds me," "delusions," and "every day for years." Your accurate account protects the credibility that Route 1 depends on.
- **Don't coach or shape anyone's statement or opinion,** whether Erica, Cherie, or an independent examiner. Ask for their honest, independent judgment.
- **Don't let an appeal become an attempt to prove severity by volume.** Board grants turn on documented course and examiner findings, not on the number of documents.
