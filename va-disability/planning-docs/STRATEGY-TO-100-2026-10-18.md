## 1. The correction that changes the plan

**38 CFR 4.130 has no 90% mental-health rating.** The General Rating Formula's levels are 0 / 10 / 30 / 50 / 70 / 100.
Every corpus document built on "90% MH" — including "Route 2: 90% MH + 50% sleep apnea = 100%" — rests on a rating VA
cannot assign. Mental health jumps from 70 to 100.

Combined ratings (38 CFR 4.25: combine highest-first against remaining efficiency, round intermediate values to whole
numbers, round the final value to the nearest 10 with 5s rounding up), with the **current 20% (ankle 10 + tinnitus 10)**:

| Scenario | Combined value | Rated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MH 50 + ankle 10 + tinnitus 10 | 60 | **60%** |
| MH 70 + ankle 10 + tinnitus 10 — *the likely default* | 76 | **80%** |
| MH 70 + sleep apnea 50 + existing 20 | 88 | **90%** |
| MH 70 + sleep apnea 50 + migraines 30 + existing 20 | 92 | **90%** |
| MH 70 + sleep apnea 50 + migraines 30 + ankle ↑20 + tinnitus 10 | 93 | **90%** |
| MH 70 + sleep apnea 50 + migraines **50** + existing 20 | 95 | **100%** — knife-edge: needs 50% migraines ("very frequent completely prostrating and prolonged attacks productive of severe economic inadaptability") *and* every intermediate rounding to break upward |
| **MH 100** (+ anything) | 100 | **100%** |

**What follows:** the only realistic schedular path to 100% is **a 100% mental-health rating**. Secondary conditions
raise the floor (80 → 90) and are worth filing if genuine, but they are not a route to 100% on a 70% mental-health
rating. The old "Route 2" is withdrawn; the old "Route 1" is now the strategy.

## 2. The strategy, in order

### Step 1 — The C&P exam, Wed 21 Oct 2026 (the largest single lever)

100% requires **total occupational AND total social impairment** (38 CFR 4.130; a rating may not rest on social
impairment alone, 4.126(b)). Unemployment and the documented job-loss sequence supply the occupational prong. **The
social prong decides 100% vs. 70%:** the marriage, AA attendance and the weekly outing are on the record and will be read
as preserved social function unless described exactly as they are. The ADL/hygiene dependence on Erica is the
strongest 100%-tier fact in the file and must reach the examiner. Prep: `planning-docs/cp-exam/` (mock exam PDF +
exam-day notes).

### Step 2 — Complete the record before the decision

The rater must weigh all evidence of record, not only the exam (38 CFR 4.126(a)).

- **Treating-provider DBQ.** Ask Cherie whether she will complete VA Form 21-0960P-2 (Mental Disorders DBQ) with a short
  narrative, selecting the impairment level her own clinical judgment supports. Raters weigh the DBQ's single checkbox
  heavily; a bare DBQ without rationale is often discounted. Never ask her to reach a particular level.
- **Treatment.** Get into VA mental-health and opioid-use-disorder treatment now. Health first; it also creates
  contemporaneous records from a second clinician and answers the "symptoms are substance-induced" argument (Allen /
  Mittleider framework).
- **Confirm the file.** VSO verifies that Cherie's updated letter, Erica's 7/12/2026 statement, the Reyes and Poquette
  statements, and the DASS-21/WHODAS are associated with the claim, and whether the 21-0781 perpetrator correction
  (worklist B11) was filed.
- **Effective date.** VSO pulls the eFolder to settle whether a prior decision on the mental-health issue exists (the 2024
  denial) and which effective-date rule applies (issue I-16) — this also determines whether an attorney can charge now.

### Step 3 — If the decision is 70%: challenge it, don't accept it

Calendar the one-year deadline the day the decision arrives (38 CFR 3.2500). VSO/attorney picks the lane:

- **Higher-Level Review** if the exam was inadequate — it omitted ADLs, social functioning outside the marriage, OCD or
  SI; treated the absence of an in-service report as negative evidence (*AZ v. Shinseki*); dismissed symptoms as
  substance-induced without analysis (*Mittleider*); or ignored Cherie's opinion. An exam VA provides must be adequate
  (*Barr v. Nicholson*).
- **Supplemental Claim** with new evidence: Cherie's DBQ and, ideally, an independent opinion from a psychologist or
  psychiatrist (a doctoral-level opinion carries more weight than an LCSW's).
- This is where a **VA-accredited attorney** adds the most leverage (contingency, post-decision).

Insomnia, SUD, OCD, MDD and GAD are **not** separate claims — they fold into the single mental-health rating (4.130,
anti-pyramiding).

### Step 5 — TDIU (the veteran has declined it; recorded here honestly)

TDIU (38 CFR 4.16(a)) pays at the 100% rate and requires inability to secure or follow substantially gainful employment,
**not** total social impairment. A 70% mental-health rating alone meets the schedular threshold. Given current
unemployment, it is the most reliable route to 100%-level pay. Trade-off: no substantially gainful work while receiving
it (marginal employment, including some protected/family-business settings found marginal on the facts, is allowed).
VA may raise it on its own because unemployment is in the record (*Rice v. Shinseki*); accepting it does not end the
schedular-100% argument. **Decision point: after the rating decision, with the VSO.**

### Also worth asking the VSO once a total rating exists

- **SMC aid and attendance** (38 U.S.C. § 1114(l); 38 CFR 3.352(a)) if the veteran needs another person's regular
  help with daily living because of the service-connected mental condition — the Erica caretaking facts.
- **SMC housebound (§ 1114(s))** — whether a 100% mental-health rating plus additional independently ratable
  disabilities (e.g., a future sleep-apnea 50% + ankle + tinnitus) would qualify. Do not assume; have the VSO run it.
- **Permanent & total** designation once the condition is judged static, for the related benefits.

## 3. Honest expectations

Reasoned judgment, not statistics:

- **Most likely:** 70% mental health → **80% combined**.
- **Real downside risk:** 50% mental health (→ 60%) if the exam is thin, frequencies and consequences aren't elicited,
  or symptoms are attributed to active opioid use. The July mock adjudication's adversarial reviewer argued for 50%.
- **100% schedular:** a genuine possibility, not the default. It turns almost entirely on a finding of **total social
  impairment** despite the marriage, AA and weekly outing. July's ~40–50% estimate for 100% MH assumed the ADL and
  belief-content facts would be documented before the exam; Cherie's updated letter now covers much of that, but the
  social prong (≈3 adverse vs. 1 favorable non-precedential Board decisions on married veterans) remains the main
  counterweight. The old Route 2 estimate (~50–60%) is void (§1).
- **The two levers that move these most:** an accurate, complete exam on Wednesday, and a treating-clinician DBQ in the
  file before the decision.

## 5. Guardrails (unchanged)

Truthful documentation of genuine impairment only. No coaching of witness statements; no directing a provider's
conclusions; no symptom-reaching; no manufactured or staged employment changes (38 U.S.C. § 6103; 18 U.S.C. § 1001).
Internal strategy documents are never submitted to VA.
