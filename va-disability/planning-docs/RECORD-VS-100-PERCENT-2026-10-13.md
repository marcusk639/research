## 2. What 100% actually requires

**The regulation.** 38 CFR 4.130 sets six levels for mental disorders: 0, 10, 30, 50, 70 and 100%. **There is no 90% level** (`rater-kb/criteria-4130-caselaw.md` §1). All of my mental diagnoses (PTSD, MDD, GAD, OCD, and SUD if it's found secondary) get one combined rating. Any plan built on a "90% mental health" step, including the stale `REPORT.md` "Start here" route, doesn't work.

The 100% level reads: "Total occupational and social impairment, due to such symptoms as: gross impairment in thought processes or communication; persistent delusions or hallucinations; grossly inappropriate behavior; persistent danger of hurting self or others; intermittent inability to perform activities of daily living (including maintenance of minimal personal hygiene); disorientation to time or place; memory loss for names of close relatives, own occupation, or own name" (38 CFR 4.130; `criteria-4130-caselaw.md` §1).

The 70% level reads "deficiencies in most areas, such as work, school, family relations, judgment, thinking, or mood." Its examples include suicidal ideation, obsessional rituals, near-continuous panic or depression, neglect of personal appearance and hygiene, difficulty adapting to stress, and inability to establish and maintain effective relationships (same source).

**In plain language:**

- **"Most" versus "total."** At 70%, problems span most areas of life. At 100%, impairment is total in **both** work **and** social life at the same time. Being severely impaired at work while keeping some working relationships doesn't meet 100% on that basis alone (`criteria-4130-caselaw.md` §2). VA also may not rate on social impairment alone (38 CFR 4.126(b)).
- **The symptom list is examples, not a checklist.** _Mauerhan v. Principi_, 16 Vet. App. 436 (2002), holds that "such as" makes the listed symptoms illustrations of the type and degree of impairment. A symptom that isn't named can still count.
- **But symptoms must cause the level.** _Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki_, 713 F.3d 112 (Fed. Cir. 2013), requires the listed symptoms "or others of similar severity, frequency, and duration," and they must produce the impairment. Read together, the two cases mean neither the words on the list nor a count of symptoms decides the rating. Function does.
- **Suicidal ideation.** _Bankhead v. Shulkin_, 29 Vet. App. 10 (2017), holds that SI, including passive SI with no plan or intent, "may" support 70%. It doesn't automatically compel 70%, and it doesn't relax the "total" standard for 100% (`criteria-4130-caselaw.md` §3.3). The 10/8 path document reports that Bankhead also treats ideation (a 70% symptom) as distinct from "persistent danger of hurting self" (a 100% symptom), and that treating them as one is error. That was citation-verified against the slip opinion (`PATH-TO-100-WITHOUT-TDIU-2026-10-08.md` §3, §7).
- **The whole record counts, not just exam day.** VA weighs "the frequency, severity, and duration of psychiatric symptoms, the length of remissions, and the veteran's capacity for adjustment during periods of remission" (38 CFR 4.126(a)). That cuts both ways for me: bad stretches count, and so do the stretches between them.
- **How the examiner records it.** The DBQ has one "check only one" box summarizing impairment across all mental diagnoses. The top box is "Total occupational and social impairment" (`rater-kb/dbq-mental-disorders.md` §1.1). If symptoms can't be separated between diagnoses, impairment is treated as one whole (§1.2).

### 6.2 TDIU, 38 CFR 4.16(a)

**Requirements** (verified 2026-10-13 against 38 CFR 4.16 via Cornell LII):

- Unable to secure or follow a substantially gainful occupation because of service-connected disabilities.
- One disability ratable at 60% or more, or, with two or more, one at 40% or more and a combined rating of 70% or more.
- "Marginal employment shall not be considered substantially gainful employment." Marginal employment generally exists when earned annual income doesn't exceed the Census poverty threshold for one person.
- _Faust v. West_, 13 Vet. App. 342 (2000), treats income above that threshold as substantially gainful regardless of hours worked. It defined that in the context of reducing a total rating (38 CFR 3.343(c)), and the Board applies the same definition to TDIU. It isn't absolute: under 4.16(a), above-threshold income can still be "marginal employment" "on a facts found basis," including "employment in a protected environment such as a family business or sheltered workshop." The same paragraph says "consideration shall be given in all claims to the nature of the employment and the reason for termination." That bears directly on the family CPA-firm plan.
- TDIU is part of the initial rating when the record reasonably raises it (_Rice v. Shinseki_, 22 Vet. App. 447 (2009); verified in the corpus 10/8, `REASSESSMENT` evening update; `vre/` lists it as unverified). My 10/7 21-4138 already raises it.

- **For:** unemployed since 6/2026; an involuntary termination after FMLA leave; the Metal attendance record; treatment interruptions at every documented job.
- **Against:**
  - two layoffs;
  - high-wage skills and current offers;
  - consulting plans;
  - possible unemployment certifications and VR&E statements of capacity;
