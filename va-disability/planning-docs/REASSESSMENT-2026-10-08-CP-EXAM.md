## 1. Correction to earlier planning documents (most important)

**38 CFR 4.130 has no 90% level.** The General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders uses 0, 10, 30, 50, 70 and 100%. All of the veteran's mental diagnoses (PTSD, MDD, GAD, OCD, SUD) receive **one** combined evaluation.

The following documents build "Route 2" on a **90% mental-health rating** plus 50% sleep apnea reaching 100%:

- `PATH-TO-100-PERCENT-2026-07-07.md` (lines 17, 19, 110–118, 322, 351, 364)
- `100-PERCENT-SCHEDULAR-PATHWAY.md` Scenario C (lines 96–97, 120)
- `MASTER-STRATEGY.md` line 65

That level does not exist, so Route 2 does not work as written.

Combined-rating math under 38 CFR 4.25, verified in the 2026-07-07 pass:

| Mental health | Other conditions                     | Combined calculation | Combined rating |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------- | --------------- |
| 70            | tinnitus 10                          | 73                   | **70**          |
| 70            | sleep apnea 50 + tinnitus 10         | 85, then 86.5        | **90**          |
| 70            | full secondary stack (PATH line 120) | about 94             | **90**          |
| 100           | any                                  |                      | **100**         |

**There are only two ways to total compensation:**

1. **100% schedular** on mental health, or
2. **TDIU** under § 4.16(a): a single disability rated 60% or more qualifies, so a 70% mental-health rating clears the threshold.

Sleep apnea is still worth pursuing for its own value, but it cannot reach 100% combined from a 70% mental-health rating.

**The pre-service history is a credibility issue more than a legal bar.** Under 38 U.S.C. § 1111 (CONFIRMED), a veteran is presumed sound unless clear and unmistakable evidence shows **both** that the condition preexisted service **and** that service did not aggravate it. *Wagner v. Principi*, 370 F.3d 1089, 1096 (CONFIRMED) holds that the government must prove both.

A recorded history of preservice existence is not a "notation." It is still "considered together with all other material evidence" (38 CFR **3.304(b)(1)**, CONFIRMED; corrected from "(b)").

A 2–3-month outpatient course for mild anxiety/depression at about 19 is weak evidence against in-service aggravation, given the 2012 markers. **SUD.** Drug abuse arising **secondarily** from a service-connected disability is compensable (*Allen v. Principi*, 237 F.3d 1368, holding CONFIRMED; pincites 1376/1381 unverified). Drug use resulting from a service-connected disability is "not... of misconduct origin" (38 CFR **3.301(c)(3)**, CONFIRMED). Mental disorders receive one combined rating, so this adds no percentage. It does let SUD-driven impairment count toward the PTSD rating if service connection is granted.
