## A few areas that would help the reviewer — _only if they match your assessment_

Think of these as "the reviewer can't see what you see unless you spell it out."

### 1. "Total" vs. "severe" social impairment

The VA treats **"total"** social impairment as a very high bar. When a reviewer sees the
word "total" next to the facts that Marcus is still married and attends AA meetings, they
may discount the word — even though your underlying point is sound.

- **Question for you:** Is your clinical picture that his _only_ remaining human contact is
  a caregiving dependency (his wife) plus you as his therapist, with **no reciprocal
  relationships**? If so, describing it that precisely is both accurate and more convincing
  than the single word "total." If "total" slightly overstates it, the precise description
  is stronger. Use whichever is true.

### 2. The phrase "paranoid delusions"

The letter twice describes his job-loss fears as "paranoid delusions." In VA terms,
"delusions" implies a fixed, false, psychotic-level belief.

- **Question for you:** Is that your clinical assessment — genuine delusions/psychosis? Or
  is it better captured as intense, irrational, evidence-resistant fear and paranoia that
  is _not_ psychotic? Please use whichever term reflects your actual finding. (Both are
  serious; the goal is only that the term matches your assessment, since a VA examiner may
  probe "delusions" specifically.)

### 3. The earlier records point to other causes

Some of Marcus's earlier treatment records (around 2023) attribute his distress to
childhood or work-related factors, without mentioning the military assault.

- **Question for you:** In your clinical judgment, are those earlier factors _part of_ the
  same trauma-driven picture (i.e., what was really underneath the "work stress" you were
  treating), or genuinely separate? A reviewer will likely notice that earlier records tell
  a different story. If, in your view, the earlier presentation was the same condition
  before the assault was disclosed, saying that directly closes the biggest gap in the file.

### 4. Naming the _why_ behind the connection

Your conclusion that his symptoms are "directly tied" to the assault is correct. The VA
gives the most weight to opinions that also explain the _reasoning_ — the "why."

- **Question for you:** In a sentence or two, what is the clinical reasoning that links the
  assault to these conditions? (For example, the continuity of symptoms over time, the
  self-medication pattern, the trauma presentation.) You already reference these; stating
  them as your _basis for the opinion_ makes the opinion carry more weight.

### 5. You have objective test scores — consider citing them

Marcus completed two validated measures that strongly support what you describe:

- **DASS-21:** Depression, Anxiety, and Stress all scored **"Extremely severe"**
  (98th–100th percentile).
- **WHODAS 2.0:** overall disability score of **~71%**.

- **Question for you:** If these results are consistent with your own assessment, citing
  them by name gives the reviewer objective numbers to go with your clinical narrative.

### 6. Naming the daily-living impact for what it is

You describe that Marcus cannot reliably maintain his own hygiene and depends on his wife
to do it. In VA rating terms, an **"inability to perform activities of daily living,
including maintaining minimal personal hygiene"** is one of the specific examples the VA
lists at its highest severity level.

- **Question for you:** If that is accurate, noting that the times he _appears_ well-groomed
  reflect his wife's help (not his own ability) makes the point unmistakable to a reviewer.
