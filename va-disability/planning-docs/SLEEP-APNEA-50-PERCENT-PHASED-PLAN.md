## Read this before Phase 0 — one correction to the framing

You described 50% as "appropriate given my symptoms based on doctor feedback along with my wife's feedback." Two things worth separating, because they change what the work actually is:

**Your symptoms don't set the tier — a prescription does.** Under DC 6847 the 50% tier is not a severity judgment. It turns on one word: whether you **"require use of a breathing assistance device."** Prescribed CPAP = 50%, automatically. No device = 30% at most, 0% if daytime sleepiness isn't documented. So **50% is not something to argue for — it's something that follows** once the clinical facts are established. The entire job is accurate documentation, in the right order. There is no version of this where advocacy substitutes for a prescription.

**Erica's observations are real evidence, and they matter most right now.** Witnessed pauses, gasping, and choking are the highest-value diagnostic symptoms, and you cannot report them yourself — you're asleep. Her account is competent lay evidence (_Layno v. Brown_; 38 CFR § 3.159(a)(2)) and is the single most useful thing for getting the study **ordered** in Phase 1. Note the distinction this corpus enforces: describing **what she genuinely witnessed** is exactly right; being told what to say is not. Nobody drafts her wording.

**Honest ceiling:** 50% is the realistic maximum. The 100% tier requires chronic respiratory failure, cor pulmonale, or a tracheostomy — not reachable, and chasing it costs credibility on the tier you can win. See `SLEEP-APNEA-RATING-MAXIMIZATION.md` §1.

## Phase 3 — The 50% hinge ⭐ _(the highest-leverage phase in this plan)_

**Objective:** secure a prescribed, medically-required CPAP. **This single step is the difference between 50% and 30% — a 20-point swing.**

- [ ] **3.1 — Ask directly: "Is CPAP medically necessary for me?"**
- [ ] **3.2 — Confirm the record says _required / medically necessary_** — not "offered," "trialed," or "discussed as an option." The regulatory word is **"requires."**
- [ ] **3.3 — Actually start using it.**
- [ ] **3.4 — Preserve the compliance data.** The machine logs nightly usage; that data objectively corroborates the "requires" element and is hard for a rater to discount.
- [ ] **3.5 — If a dental appliance or positional therapy is prescribed _instead_ of CPAP** — flag it to the VSO before filing. The regulation says "such as" CPAP, so other breathing-assistance devices can qualify, but it's a weaker argument needing attention.

> **🚫 Anti-pattern guards**
>
> - **Do not let anyone frame successful treatment as "resolved."** Well-controlled **on** CPAP still rates 50% under the current rule — the criterion is that you _require_ the device, not that you're still symptomatic while using it.
> - **Do not** skip Phase 3 to rush to filing. Filing without the prescription invites a 30% grant that then requires an increase claim to fix.

**✅ GATE 3:** CPAP prescribed, documented as required, in use, compliance data accumulating.

## Phase 4 — The nexus opinion

**Objective:** a _reasoned_ etiology opinion from a competent provider. Runs in parallel with Phase 3.

- [ ] **4.1 — Ask the sleep-medicine physician / pulmonologist** — not Cherie. An LCSW opining on an upper-airway mechanism is directly challengeable on competency.
- [ ] **4.2 — Make the request open-ended.** Use the wording in `SLEEP-APNEA-NEXUS-OPINION-GUIDE.md` §6: give the facts, ask the question, **do not supply the conclusion.**
- [ ] **4.3 — Hand them the guide** (`SLEEP-APNEA-NEXUS-OPINION-GUIDE.md`) — it's written for the provider, listing the four required elements and the literature.
- [ ] **4.4 — Confirm the opinion contains:** the **"at least as likely as not"** standard · **causation AND aggravation** in the alternative · and a **stated rationale**.
- [ ] **4.5 — If they decline** — normal outcome. Fall back to the prescribing psychiatrist on the medication/weight pathway, or an IMO. Note: **both verified BVA grants succeeded on treating-provider opinions, not paid IMO firms.**

> **🚫 Anti-pattern guards**
>
> - **Do not** ask a provider to "note that my meds and weight gain caused this." That hands them your conclusion — the exact pattern that has already caused credibility problems in this claim (`CLAIM-ASSESSMENT.md` Part 0).
> - **Do not** accept a bare chart note as the deliverable. An opinion with only data and a conclusion, and no reasoning connecting them, is entitled to **no weight** (_Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake_, 22 Vet.App. 295, 304 (2008)).
> - **Do not** lead with weight gain. Lead with **PTSD hyperarousal lowering the arousal threshold** — the mechanism that won both BVA cases, and the one that doesn't depend on your weight history.
> - **Do not** state a specific weight figure. **Resolved 2026-07-31:** the tracked records contain **one documented weight — 189.8 lbs at 6'0" (2018), BMI ≈ 25.7** — and no current weight. Even accepting a 20-lb gain, that's **BMI ≈ 28.5: overweight, not obese**, while the VAOPGCPREC 1-2017 doctrine runs through _obesity_. **The weight pathway is weak here — treat it as optional support, never the primary theory** (`SLEEP-APNEA-APPOINTMENT-PREP.md` §4).

**✅ GATE 4:** written opinion in hand with all four elements. _(Note: this gate does **not** block Phase 5 — see the override.)_

## Phase 5 — File

**Objective:** get the 21-526EZ in, inside the ITF window.

- [ ] **5.1 — File VA Form 21-526EZ** through the VSO: **"obstructive sleep apnea, secondary to service-connected PTSD"** under 38 CFR § 3.310.
- [ ] **5.2 — Attach:** sleep study (with AHI/RDI) · CPAP prescription · compliance data · nexus opinion · Erica's statement if she's written one.
- [ ] **5.3 — Plead causation AND aggravation** — § 3.310(a) and **(b)**. _Allen v. Brown_ makes aggravation independently sufficient; pleading only causation discards a second path to a grant for free.
- [ ] **5.4 — Pre-empt the pyramiding objection in the filing itself.** Have the VSO address it affirmatively — one paragraph now is far cheaper than a Higher-Level Review later. The argument, the controlling test, and the adverse authority to distinguish are all in `SLEEP-APNEA-RATING-MAXIMIZATION.md` §5.
- [ ] **5.5 — Re-confirm the DC 6847 rule status** (NPRM 87 FR 8474) immediately before filing.
- [ ] **5.6 — Do NOT file Form 21-8940 (TDIU).**

> **⏰ HARD OVERRIDE — the rule that governs this whole plan**
> **At `ITF + 10 months`, file — regardless of which gates are still open.** A diagnosis alone is enough to file; the nexus opinion can follow as additional evidence.
> **Do not wait for the PTSD claim to be decided.** If it's denied and appealed, that's _years_ — and the ITF expires in **365 days** with no cure. There is no legal bar to filing a secondary while the primary is pending; VA simply defers. **Deferral costs nothing. A blown window costs everything between the two dates.**

**✅ GATE 5:** claim filed, confirmation received, inside the window.
