# 08 — Implementation Roadmap

> As of 2026-10-19. Labels: see [REPORT.md](REPORT.md#labels-used).

**Summary.** The roadmap runs in four horizons (90 days, 6 months, 12 months, 36 months), each with a gate that must be met before moving on. The first 12 weeks set up the company, build the product with TWK as a paying reference client, and open the industrial track. Hires wait for both triggers, and deployment grows from client-owned appliances to a shared rack, and only then to an own facility.

## Phase 0: Foundation (weeks 1–6; under $10K combined)

- Form the LLC (or finalize an existing one), get an EIN, and register with Calcasieu Parish Sales and Use Tax and the City of Lake Charles. The entity decision is pending; see [11-related-workstreams.md](11-related-workstreams.md#company-formation-and-brand).
- Open a bank account and credit line; set up bookkeeping with TWK.
- Bind insurance.
- Apply for SBA VetCert and register in SAM.gov (UEI). Status: SAM.gov and/or VetCert applications are already submitted and pending (Oct 2026).
- Draft the MSA, SOW and DPA.
- Engage a tax attorney for the §7216 opinion.
- File the SBA application.
- Get BOP and TWIC.
- Buy the demo/lab appliance (~$25–35K).
- Set up the company WISP, MFA and a password manager.

The "under $10K combined" budget can't include the ~$25–35K demo appliance. See [12-open-questions.md](12-open-questions.md#contradictions-found).

### What the founder stops doing, in order

1. Installs (months 9–12).
2. First-line support (month 12).
3. Workflow configuration (Year 2).
4. Delivery entirely (Year 3).

He keeps sales, product and key accounts.

### How the founder's time is used

- Year 1 split: about 40% selling, 40% delivery and build, 20% support and admin.
- Bookkeeping, legal and insurance are outsourced.

## Runbooks

## Support standards

- Business hours.
- P1 (system down): 4-hour response. P2: next business day.
- Best-effort uptime on client-owned hardware, with a spare-parts plan.
- A monthly usage and health report to each client.

## Deployment stages

The "local data center" idea is the last stage of the same business.

| Stage | When                  | What                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Cost / funding                                    |
| ----- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Now                   | Appliances in clients' buildings, plus the industrial services                                                                                                                                       | Clients buy hardware                              |
| 2     | Years 2–3             | A shared rack in existing colocation (DartPoints in Baton Rouge or Shreveport, or Houston) for clients who want private AI without owning a server: contractors, small firms, and hurricane failover | About $50–100K of hardware, fundable from revenue |
| 3     | Only if Stage 2 fills | An own facility near Lake Charles, ideally with a partner that already owns power and space, such as the proposed Azimuth project in Moss Bluff                                                      | Separate legal entity                             |

**Rules:**

- One business, one brand ("private AI, local").
- Keep plant data and CPA tax data on-prem; offer hosting only to clients whose compliance story allows it.
- The current raise funds Stage 1 only.
- **Decision:** training an own base model is rejected.
- Louisiana data-center incentives don't fit, because they need 50+ jobs and $200M of investment.
