# 04 — Market and Clients

> As of 2026-10-19. Labels: see [REPORT.md](REPORT.md#labels-used).

**Summary.** The market is SWLA CPA firms, law firms, and the plants and contractors of the Lake Charles corridor, with Lafayette and Baton Rouge as the Year 2 market. The demand signal is unsanctioned "shadow AI" with no written policy, not a lack of interest. The real competitor is bundled cloud AI such as Microsoft 365 Copilot; local MSPs are channel partners, not rivals.

## Geography

- Start in Southwest Louisiana (SWLA).
- Expand along I-10 to Lafayette and Baton Rouge.
- Lake Charles alone supports roughly 15–25 good-fit clients at maturity [Estimate]. The I-10 corridor is therefore the Year 2 market, not a stretch goal.

## Other market facts

- **Calcasieu Parish:** 4,705 employer establishments and 75,412 employees (2023), with employment up 6.4% year over year (Census QuickFacts).
- **CPA consolidation:** firm counts are shrinking, from 1,980 in 2019 to about 1,724–1,797 now. That means consolidation into the larger firms this business targets.
- **IT spend anchor:** a 20-person professional firm already spends about $2,200–3,700/month on managed IT, at $110–170 per user.

**Clio 2026** (solo and small law firms):

- 71% of solos and 75% of small firms use AI.
- But 55–57% have no AI policy.
- 43–48% rely on generic ChatGPT/Copilot.
- Fewer than 33% report revenue gains.

**Dissenting view:** The Legal Stack estimates regular, workflow-integrated use at firms under 20 lawyers at only 28–35% (Q3 2026).

**What this means:**

- The pain is unsanctioned "shadow AI" plus no AI policy, not lack of interest.
- The sale is "replace the ChatGPT your staff already use with a governed system and a written policy."
- Firms judge it on time saved, so hours recovered must be measured and reported.

## Buyer roles

**At a plant**, buy as "services" on a purchase order. Department managers can often approve up to ~$30K, and general managers up to ~$50K [Estimate; confirm per plant].

| Role                                                | Pain                                                         | First offer                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Reliability engineer / reliability manager          | Repeat failures, no time to mine work-order history          | Bad-actor study                |
| Maintenance manager / superintendent                | Losing experienced mechanics, slow troubleshooting           | Knowledge capture for one unit |
| PSM coordinator / process safety lead               | Audits due, procedures out of date, MOC backlog              | PSM documentation support      |
| Turnaround manager                                  | Tracking thousands of contractors and permits                | Turnaround dashboards          |
| Training coordinator / operations readiness manager | New hires replacing retirees; new terminals hiring from zero | Training modules               |
| HSE manager                                         | Contractor paperwork and compliance gaps                     | Contractor onboarding tracker  |

**At a contractor**, the owner, general manager or operations manager decides alone.

**Avoid early:**

- corporate IT;
- procurement;
- anything touching control systems or the OT network.

Plant managers are sponsors, not buyers.
