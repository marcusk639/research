# Louisiana Treatment Venture — Capital Raising Plan

**Prepared:** 2026-10-06 · Companion to the capital-requirements plan of the same date.

**The requirement, from the companion document:** $150–255K (recovery residences), **$400–700K
(licensed ASAM 2.1 IOP)**, $900K–2.0M (ASAM 3.5 residential). Roughly 60% of the IOP figure is
pre-revenue payroll through licensure and credentialing.

---

## 0. Executive summary

### 0.1 Reliability comes from sequencing and stack design, not from finding the right source

There is no single source that funds this. The financeable structure is a **stack**: personal cash

- SBA 7(a) + a full-standby seller note + a small accredited friends-and-family raise, with
  **non-dilutive public money entering as revenue, not capital**, and a Louisiana state program used
  specifically to keep a lien off your house.

### 0.2 The asymmetry that should drive the whole plan

SBA loan-level default data, FY2020–2025 `[FOIA]`:

| Loan purpose                          | Default rate |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Change of ownership (acquisition)** | **0.71%**    |
| Existing business, >2 years           | 1.20%        |
| Startup, funds will open business     | 1.43%        |
| **New business / ≤2 years**           | **1.99%**    |

Acquisitions default roughly **3× less** than new-business loans, and lenders price and approve
accordingly. **A de novo IOP is the single hardest thing in this plan to debt-finance**, and a rule
change five days ago made it harder: **SOP 50 10 8.1, effective 10/1/2026**, moved acquisition
underwriting to a new Appendix 15 requiring **1.25× DSCR — with projections no longer counting
toward the floor** `[SOP]`. A pre-revenue IOP has nothing _but_ projections. Portfolio-wide 7(a)
defaults hit **4.8% in March 2026**, the highest since 2013; lenders are tightening.

**Plan to fund a de novo IOP mostly with equity, or buy instead of build.**

### 0.3 The one number that governs your personal downside

**$500,000.** Above that, an SBA loan must be fully collateralized where possible — and if business
collateral is insufficient, **the lender must take a lien on personal real estate, including a
second mortgage on a home with available equity.** The personal guarantee itself is **uncapped**,
payable on demand, and the lender need not exhaust business collateral first.

A bare guarantee produces a judgment that may not reach a protected homestead. **A recorded
mortgage is foreclosable.** Louisiana's homestead exemption does not protect against a mortgage you
consensually granted. After foreclosure the deficiency survives, SBA acquires it, and it can
garnish wages. **Chapter 7 discharges personal liability but does not strip a validly recorded
lien.**

**The specific mitigation:** Louisiana LED's **Collateral Support** program pledges **cash up to
$250,000** to secure loans up to $1M `[LED]`. That is the cleanest available lever for keeping a
mortgage off your home, and it should be arranged _before_ a lender structures collateral, not
after.

### 0.4 Three things the individual research threads each missed

**(a) Rural siting unlocks three separate doors at once.** LDH's **Rural Health Transformation
Program** names "rural behavioral health or substance use providers" as explicitly eligible;
**USDA B&I guarantees 85%** on loans under $5M and is commonly used for rural healthcare
facilities, with wide Louisiana eligibility; and **LED Quality Jobs**, which otherwise excludes
medical professionals, has a qualifying path for siting in a parish in the **lowest 25% by per
capita income**. Site selection is therefore a financing decision, not just a market decision.

**(b) Opioid settlement money fixes a payer-mix problem, not a capital problem.** Parishes must
prioritize people **not covered by Medicaid or private insurance** — exactly the population a
Medicaid-only pro forma excludes, and exactly the concentration risk the companion plan flags as
High. It arrives as a service contract, so it diversifies revenue rather than funding build-out.

**(c) Louisiana's corporate-practice posture is softer than the companion plan implied.** The
LSBME's **December 2024 Statement of Position** holds that physician employment by a
non-professional corporation is **not _per se_ unlawful**; the operative test is whether the
arrangement impairs independent medical judgment. For a non-clinician owner this materially
simplifies structuring versus strict-CPOM states.

## 1. Debt

### 1.1 SBA 7(a) — current terms

| Item                   | Current                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Maximum                | $5M, **plus** $5M of 504 (decoupled since July 2026)                                                                            |
| Guarantee              | 75% above $150K                                                                                                                 |
| Rate                   | Variable cap base **+3.0%** over $350K; well-qualified borrowers ~**8.5–9.25%**; FY2025 Q4 acquisition loans averaged **8.86%** |
| Guarantee fee (FY2026) | 3% on $150–700K; 3.5% to $1M; 3.75% above                                                                                       |
| Amortization           | **10 years** business portion; 25 years with real estate                                                                        |
| Equity injection       | **10% minimum**; acquisitions require **≥5% cash**; lenders routinely want 15–20%, **20%+ for true startups**                   |

Approval rates run ~67% for 7(a) versus ~43% conventional `[vendor]`.

**Correction to my earlier note:** I wrote that seller notes counted toward equity injection
require 24 months of deferral. **That rule is obsolete.** Under SOP 50 10 8 (eff. 6/1/2025) and
carried into 8.1, a seller note counts toward the injection **only on full standby — no principal
and no interest — for the entire life of the SBA loan**, and may not exceed 50% of the injection.

### 1.2 The credentialing gap cannot be factored

This is the most important negative finding in the research. Medical receivables factoring advances
against **existing billable claims**. During licensure and credentialing you have no claims at all —
there is nothing to factor. Factoring addresses the 30–90 day claims-aging lag, not the 6–12 month
pre-revenue window.

Even post-credentialing it is unattractive to a first-time biller: expect **3–5% per 30 days at
60–75% advance**, not advertised rates; most factors require **6–12 months of operating history and
$10–20K/month volume**; and **Medicaid receivables cannot be directly assigned**, requiring
sweep-account structures, with Medicaid advances at 65–80% versus commercial 75–90% `[vendor]`.

**Therefore: the $250–400K credentialing reserve must be equity or 7(a) working capital funded at
close.** Do not plan to draw it later.

### 1.3 SBA 504 and other debt

**504** finances owner-occupied real estate at roughly 50% bank / 40% CDC debenture / **10% borrower
down** — but **15% for single-purpose property**, which residential treatment may be classified as.
It **cannot fund working capital**; pair with 7(a), which must be approved first.

**Louisiana and federal programs worth using:**

- **LED Collateral Support** — pledged cash to $250K securing loans to $1M. _The home-lien lever._
- **LED Small Business Loan Guaranty** — up to 80% or $1.5M, 15% minimum equity, fee 0–2%. New rule
  dated 5/20/26; pull current text.
- **USDA B&I** — 85% guarantee under $5M, commonly used for rural healthcare.
- **SSBCI** — up to $113M deployed through LEDC via lenders, not direct.
- **CDFIs serving Louisiana** — DreamSpring ($1K–350K), LiftFund ($500–$1M), TruFund ($500–$1M),
  LISC.

### 1.4 Lender selection matters more than borrowers assume

**No SBA lender markets a behavioral health vertical.** Live Oak is #1 by dollar volume ($2.68B
CY2025, average $1.25M) with dedicated healthcare, veterinary and dental teams — but behavioral
health is **not** among them, and vertical expertise is precisely Live Oak's edge. Huntington is #1
by loan count (6,071 loans, average $307K, 10% down, fee waivers for underserved borrowers).
Portfolio default rates vary widely by lender — **1.90% (Huntington) to 6.28% (First Internet
Bank)** — which is a proxy for underwriting posture and workout behavior.

**Screen for PLP status.** A non-PLP lender adds 2–4 weeks of SBA review, which can expire an LOI's
exclusivity window.

On sector stigma: **no formal SBA industry exclusion exists** — substance abuse treatment centers
are explicitly permitted. Reported lender reluctance (regulatory complexity, reimbursement
uncertainty, staffing costs) comes **entirely from lender and broker marketing content**; no primary
bank policy document was found. Treat as unverified.

## 2. Non-dilutive public money — revenue, not capital

**Bottom line: no non-dilutive source will fund your build-out as a for-profit startup.** Every
meaningful pool is nonprofit-restricted, state-intermediated, too large-scale, or structured as a
service contract. Three are still worth real effort.

### 2.1 Parish opioid settlement funds — the best target

Louisiana's State-Local MOU created the **Opioid Abatement Task Force**, with administration
delegated to the **Opioid Abatement Administration Corporation**. The split is **80% local
governments / 20% sheriffs**, and **parishes and sheriffs each decide autonomously** — there is no
statewide application.

**The money:** ~**$600M to parishes and sheriffs across CY2022–2038**. As of October 2024
approximately **$98.5M had been distributed but only ~$8.6M actually spent** by 20 parishes and 24
sheriffs. Those unspent balances are your leverage.

**The published model — Calcasieu Parish:** 30-day prequalification screen → full application; **up
to $500,000 in year one**; annual payouts projected $600–800K; 12 entities funded in round one;
**reimbursement-based**.

**For-profit eligibility:** not categorically barred — Calcasieu's language covers "organizations,
agencies, and service providers." Two constraints bind: parishes must **prioritize treatment for
people not covered by Medicaid or private insurance**, and the practical vehicle is **contracting
to deliver services or buy beds**, not receiving startup capital.

**Verdict: accessible as a revenue contract.** Approach individual parishes. Reimbursement-based
means you must be able to float the cost first — which is itself a working-capital requirement.

### 2.2 State Opioid Response — a named slot if you offer MOUD

LDH's **Office of Behavioral Health** is the grantee at **$18,001,460 (FY2025)**, flowing to **10
Local Governing Entities** covering all 64 parishes. Louisiana runs a hub-and-spoke model: **10 OTP
hubs plus 50 office-based opioid treatment (OBOT) spokes — five per LGE — with financial support
provided to participating OBOT providers**, plus a Spoke Care Team. Volume expectation is 20 new
patients in year one, 10 in year two.

**Verdict: plausibly accessible as a subrecipient** if your IOP includes medication for opioid use
disorder. **Contact your LGE, not LDH.** This is the most concrete named slot in the state for a new
clinical provider — and adding MOUD is a program-design decision you can make now.

### 2.3 Rural Health Transformation Program — if you site rurally

LDH's **Rural Collaborative Provider Models Program** explicitly names "rural behavioral health or
substance use providers" as eligible. A separate **Capital Improvement** round ran **$100,000–$10M
with a 10% cash/in-kind match** (applications closed 7/10/2026; Year-1 funds must be spent by
9/30/2027). Note the Collaborative Models notice **excludes capital improvements, equipment,
telehealth infrastructure, and EHR modernization**.

**Verdict: conditionally accessible — and it should influence site selection.** Monitor the RHTP
funding-opportunity page for new windows.

### 2.4 Ruled out

- **NMTC** — most transactions require $2–5M minimum; projects under $7M are unlikely to secure
  allocation; average healthcare NMTC project is **$11.2M**. Your project is too small.
- **CCBHC** — Louisiana joined the Section 223 demonstration **5/28/2026** with staggered start
  dates through 7/1/2027, and cost-based PPS economics materially beat Medicaid FFS. But it
  requires **nine services with at least three delivered directly**, and eligible entities are
  typically CMHCs, FQHCs, state-designated providers and nonprofits. **Not reachable by a new
  single-service IOP.** Track as a 5-year aspiration or partnership target.
- **HUD Recovery Housing Program** — nonprofit acquisition/rehab only, administered through
  OCD-LGA. Not accessible to a for-profit.
- **LED Enterprise Zone** — sunset 6/30/2025.
- **Direct SAMHSA grants** — SOR and SABG flow to the state, which subawards; many programs are
  nonprofit-restricted.

**Correction to the companion plan:** I wrote that the FY26 budget proposed dissolving SAMHSA with
~$1B in cuts. **Congress rejected it** — appropriators refused the plan (an Appropriations
Committee vote went **26–3 against, with funding added**) and approved a **$7.1B SAMHSA topline**;
the Administration for a Healthy America has never been authorized or funded. However, HHS has
proceeded administratively via staffing cuts and transferred duties, and the **FY27 request renews
the proposal.** Net: the agency survives on paper and is weakened in practice.

## 3. Equity, seller financing, and partnership

### 3.1 Self-funded search is the only open equity architecture

Traditional funded search targets **$2–10M EBITDA**; **self-funded search targets $500K–$3M** —
your range. Self-funded searchers retain **50–80%** and draw cash from year one, with a typical
stack of roughly **75% SBA / 15% seller note / 10% investor equity** at ~20% common plus a 10%
preferred return.

**Existence proof that ETA touches this sector:** ETA Equity's portfolio includes **Windmill
Wellness Ranch**, a co-occurring inpatient/outpatient SUD treatment center acquired in 2024.

**Closed at your scale:** traditional funded search funds, institutional PE, family offices, and
health-system joint ventures. The binding reason is not capital scarcity — it is that **Louisiana's
Medicaid-weighted payer mix caps entry _and_ exit multiples at 3–5×**, which makes targets cheaper
to buy and simultaneously makes outside equity uninterested in funding the purchase.

### 3.2 Seller financing — smaller and harder than LOI chatter implies

Broker survey data puts seller financing at **4–14% of deal value**, earnouts at 1–6%, and cash at
close at **75–89%** `[vendor]`.

**The catch that kills most full-standby notes:** a note on full standby for the life of the loan
costs the seller roughly **61% of face value in present-value terms**, versus ~5% for a normal
seller note. Sellers with competing offers routinely refuse. Negotiate standby treatment **before**
the LOI binds.

**Equity rollover is largely incompatible with SBA.** If the seller retains **≥20%**, they must
provide a **full unconditional personal guaranty for the life of the SBA loan**. Below 20% it is a
limited guaranty. Also new under SOP 8.1: **seller earnouts are prohibited**, a quality-of-earnings
report is required at purchase prices ≥$3M, and total debt is capped at appraised business value.

**A Louisiana-specific leverage point** `[infer]`: because a BHSP license does not transfer and you
must re-license via CHOW, you carry genuine post-close regulatory risk that the seller controls.
That is a legitimate basis to condition a portion of consideration — a seller note or holdback — on
**LDH issuing the license to you**. Earnouts being prohibited under SOP 8.1 pushes this toward a
standby note or escrow structure. This is a deal-structure question for counsel.

### 3.3 Partnering with a clinician — structure it as two separate things

Louisiana's softer CPOM posture (§0.4c) helps, but the anti-kickback constraint does not relax.
Medical director compensation must be **fair market value, fixed and predictable, and decoupled
from referral volume or value**. DOJ settled for **$5.3M in October 2024** over medical director
fees paid without genuine services rendered.

**Avoid "equity instead of salary."** The clean structure is two separable items: (a) an hourly or
fixed FMV stipend for **defined, logged duties** — build and keep the supervisory ledger; and (b)
equity, if any, as a **separate arm's-length investment at FMV with real capital contributed** and
proportional returns. Commercial reasonableness also constrains size: a thin-revenue startup cannot
defend a large stipend.

**Exposure note:** Stark and AKS bite hardest on federal program business. A Medicaid-heavy
Louisiana program is fully exposed; a cash-pay or commercial-only program materially less so,
though state fee-splitting and CPOM rules still apply.

### 3.4 Friends and family — the right instrument

For an operating LLC with no priced round coming, **sell membership units under Reg D Rule 506(b)
to accredited investors only**: PPM, subscription agreement, accreditation questionnaire, Form D.
506(b) preempts state blue-sky registration.

Two traps: **convertible notes are the wrong instrument** here — they exist to defer valuation to a
future priced round that will never happen; and **admitting non-accredited investors** triggers
registered-offering-grade disclosure that makes a small raise uneconomic, and a non-accredited
convertible holder can destroy a later exemption. Note also that **raising search capital is itself
a securities offering** subject to the same compliance.

## 4. The recommended capital stack

### 4.1 If acquiring a licensed outpatient program (the financeable path)

| Layer                                  | Share                 | Notes                                               |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal cash                          | **5–10%**             | ≥5% must be genuine cash `[SOP]`                    |
| SBA 7(a)                               | ~70–80%               | 10-yr amortization, ~8.5–9.25%                      |
| Seller note, full standby              | up to 5% of injection | Costs seller ~61% of face in PV — expect resistance |
| Accredited F&F, Reg D 506(b) LLC units | balance               | Holdco/MSO level                                    |
| LED Collateral Support                 | up to $250K pledged   | **Keeps the lien off your house**                   |
| Opioid settlement service contract     | —                     | Revenue, post-close                                 |

### 4.2 If building a de novo IOP

Expect to fund **most of $400–700K with equity**, because projections no longer satisfy DSCR and a
pre-revenue licensed facility has no cash flow to underwrite. Realistic composition: personal cash

- accredited F&F units + a modest 7(a) working-capital piece if a lender will take it. **This is
  the expensive path, and the research says so plainly.**

### 4.3 Structuring to stay under the $500K lien threshold

A first deal sized so that **SBA debt stays below $500,000** avoids the mandatory
full-collateralization rule that reaches personal real estate. Combined with LED Collateral
Support, this is the difference between "lose the invested capital" and "lose the capital, owe an
uncapped guarantee, and face foreclosure on your home." **Size the first deal to the downside you
can survive, not to the upside you want.**

## 5. The sequenced plan

### Phase 0 — Months 0–6: become financeable (costs nothing but time)

1. **Take the paid industry role** recommended in the companion plan. It is _also_ the
   financing-optimal move: SBA dropped "experience and depth of management" as a regulatory
   criterion in 2023 `[CFR 120.150, 88 FR 21085]`, but **lenders retained it as credit policy**, and
   in licensed healthcare they commonly require relevant management experience plus evidence
   licensure will be reissued.
2. **Clean up personal credit and document everything.** Lenders assess credit, cashflow, and
   collateral — the three remaining §120.150 criteria.
3. **Get LDH's CHOW position in writing** before a lender asks. Lenders want evidence licensure
   will be reissued; Louisiana's rule is that it does not transfer. Resolve this on paper early.
4. **Open the parish conversation.** Identify your parish's opioid abatement committee and
   application cycle. Calcasieu's is published; others vary.
5. **Contact your LGE** about the OBOT spoke program if MOUD is in your program design.

### Phase 1 — Months 6–12: assemble the package

6. **Engage a Louisiana healthcare-regulatory attorney and a securities attorney.** Different
   people. The first handles CHOW, CPOM, AKS/EKRA and medical-director structure; the second
   handles the Reg D offering.
7. **Build the lender package.** SBA Form 1919 (all owners ≥20%), Form 413 personal financial
   statement, Form 912 if required; 2–3 years business returns, YTD P&L and balance sheet, AR/AP
   aging, full debt schedule, 6 months bank statements, formation documents, licenses; per owner,
   2 years personal returns, PFS, **résumé**, ID; business plan with detailed use of funds and
   3-year projections. Acquisitions add LOI, purchase agreement, seller's 3-year financials, and a
   **certified valuation**.
8. **Screen lenders for PLP status and healthcare appetite.** Ask directly whether they have
   underwritten behavioral health, and what they require on licensure.
9. **Apply for LED Collateral Support** before collateral is structured.

### Phase 2 — Months 12–18: execute

10. **LOI with an explicit financing contingency** and enough runway — 7(a) runs **60–90 days
    application to funding**, LOI to close **60–90 days** (60–120 for search-style deals).
    Full search-to-close commonly runs **6–12 months**; BizBuySell's 2025 median time-to-close was
    **170 days**.
11. **Negotiate seller-note standby treatment before the LOI binds.**
12. **Confirm 10+ years of remaining lease term** — SBA requires it, and a landlord who will not
    extend kills the deal.
13. **Condition closing on LDH CHOW approval**, or escrow against it.
14. **Fund the credentialing reserve at close.** It cannot be factored later.

## 6. Deal-killers

| Trap                                              | Why it kills                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Licensing transfer at close**                   | Named as a top source of SBA closing delay generally — and in Louisiana the license does not transfer at all |
| **<10 years lease term**                          | SBA requirement; landlord refusal ends the deal                                                              |
| **Seller refuses full standby**                   | Costs them ~61% of face value in PV; most refuse when they have options                                      |
| **Valuation below negotiated price**              | Required above $250K; the gap must be closed in buyer cash                                                   |
| **Non-PLP lender**                                | Adds 2–4 weeks; can expire LOI exclusivity                                                                   |
| **Seller retains ≥20%**                           | Triggers full unconditional personal guaranty from the seller for the life of the loan                       |
| **Target under revocation/provisional licensure** | **Cannot undergo a CHOW at all** — the distressed bargains are often legally unsellable                      |
| **Non-accredited F&F investor**                   | Destroys the Reg D exemption; makes the raise uneconomic                                                     |
| **Planning to factor the credentialing gap**      | Nothing to factor — no claims exist yet                                                                      |

## 9. Bottom line

**The capital is raisable, but the structure matters more than the amount.**

1. **Buy rather than build if you can.** 0.71% versus 1.99% default rates is why lenders say yes,
   and SOP 8.1's removal of projections from DSCR math makes a de novo IOP substantially an
   equity-funded proposition.
2. **Keep SBA debt under $500,000 on the first deal, and use LED Collateral Support.** This is the
   difference between a recoverable failure and foreclosure on your home.
3. **Treat opioid settlement contracts and the SOR OBOT spoke as revenue strategy, not fundraising.**
   They solve the Medicaid-concentration risk the companion plan rates High — and the settlement
   money targets precisely the uninsured population a Medicaid pro forma ignores.
4. **Let site selection carry financing weight.** Rural siting opens RHTP, USDA B&I, and the LED
   Quality Jobs low-income-parish path simultaneously.
5. **The paid industry role is the cheapest financing move available.** It builds the management
   record lenders require, at positive income rather than negative.

The honest frame carried from the companion plan still holds: against a ~$145K baseline, this path
spends years and real capital to reach comparable income, with an uncapped personal guarantee added.
The financing research does not change that — it only shows which version of the bet is survivable
if it goes wrong.
