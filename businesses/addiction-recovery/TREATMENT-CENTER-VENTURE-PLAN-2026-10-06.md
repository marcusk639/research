**The dominant line is not build-out — it is the pre-revenue window.** For any licensed level you
are fully staffed, fully compliant, and collecting nothing for 6–12 months through licensure and
payer credentialing. In the IOP model that reserve is **$250–400K of the $400–700K.** Ring-fence
it.

**Minimum to begin credibly: $150–255K.** **To reach a licensed clinical business: $400–700K** for
a de novo IOP — the cheapest licensed entry and the one Louisiana's rates reward best.

### 0.1 Three corrections to what I told you in revision 2

1. **"Louisiana requires no Certificate of Need" — wrong as stated.** Louisiana has no traditional
   CON, but its **Facility Need Review (FNR)** program gates BHSP licensure for **PSR, CPST, and
   OTP** services: "no BHSP or applicants seeking to provide psychosocial rehabilitation (PSR)
   and/or community psychiatric support and treatment (CPST) services shall be eligible to apply
   for licensure … unless the FNR committee has granted FNR approval" `[LAC §12525(A)]`. **SUD
   residential (3.1/3.3/3.5/3.7) and IOP are not FNR-gated** — so your core plan is clear. But if
   you add CPST/PSR (the high-volume Medicaid outpatient lines) or opioid treatment/MAT, FNR
   approval is a precondition to _applying_.
2. **"Accreditation is a licensing condition for residential" — wrong.** LAC Ch. 56 contains **no
   accreditation prerequisite for licensure of any service type.** Accreditation appears only as
   optional _Deemed Status_ (accepted "in lieu of on-site licensing surveys") and as a revocation
   ground "if accreditation is a federal or state requirement for participation in the program"
   `[LAC Ch.56]`. The actual requirement is **Medicaid enrollment**: facilities "must be accredited
   prior to enrollment" by CARF, COA, or TJC `[LA Medicaid BHS Provider Manual]`. **A
   commercial/private-pay-only residential program may be licensable without accreditation.**
3. **My "accreditation catch-22" was overstated — there is a defined pathway.** CARF requires six
   months of _conformance/service records by the survey date_, but new programs not yet serving
   clients can obtain **Preliminary Accreditation** by presenting proposed treatment and
   operational procedures, with a full survey roughly six months after services begin. The real
   sequence is: license → open → Preliminary Accreditation → operate 6 months → full survey →
   Medicaid enrollment. Not circular. Confirm specifics with CARF and LDH.

## 1. The Louisiana numbers that actually drive the model

### 1.1 State licensing fees are trivial; the cost is everything around them

`[LDH HSS fee schedule]` BHSP initial license **$600**; renewal $600; $5/unit; offsite/branch $300;
**CHOW $600**; change of address $600. A statutory escalator (HB 414, 2017 RS) trends fees toward a
$1,000 cap.

National guides quoting "$25,000–$100,000 for licensing and legal" `[vendor]` are describing
**attorneys, policy development, licensing consultants, and accreditation prep — not state fees.**
Budget that work honestly; do not mistake the $600 for the cost of getting licensed.

**Correction to revision 2:** I used $364.24 for ASAM 3.5 — that is the _prenatal/postpartum_

**⚠️ Room and board is MCO-discretionary.** `[IB]` states R&B rates "have been removed from the
SBHS fee schedule, as these are not State Plan services and are payable at the discretion of the
managed care organization." Plan on **service-only** rates; treat R&B as upside to be confirmed
MCO-by-MCO.

**Three consequences:**

**(a) Staff credential mix is a revenue decision.** IOP group pays **$180/day at Master's level
versus $120 at less-than-Bachelor's** — a 50% revenue swing on identical service. Cheaper staff can
cut revenue faster than payroll.

**(b) Louisiana residential is more viable than revision 2 implied.** At 24 beds, 80% occupancy,
service-only: **$303.67 × 24 × 0.80 × 365 ≈ $2.13M/yr** (≈$2.35M if R&B is paid). Revision 2's
"$150–300 Medicaid" range bracketed neither end — 3.1/3.3 fall below it, and 3.5/3.7 (the levels an
actual residential program bills) fall well above. The commercial-vs-Medicaid spread is roughly
**2.4–4.5×**, not the 4–5× I stated. **This weakens the case for leaving Louisiana.**

**(c) IOP remains the best capital efficiency**, but on throughput, not per-diem comparison —
see §2.2.

### 1.3 ASAM 4th Edition: rulemaking is complete

`[LDH HSS memo 2/4/2026]` Notice of Intent published 9/20/2025; **final rule published in the
Louisiana Register 1/20/2026**; "all BHSPs shall be required to be in compliance with the updated
regulations no later than **July 1, 2026**." This answers revision 2's open question — the rules
are final, not pending.

Terminology follows: under 4th Edition, former "PHP / Level 2.5" is **High-Intensity Outpatient**,
Level 1 becomes **1.5**, and the 3.x/WM levels are restructured. Build to 4th Edition labels.

### 1.4 Incumbent operators are publicly warning

Leaders from Odyssey House Louisiana and O'Brien House testified that rate levels and eligibility
gaps together threaten provider capacity `[trade]`. The 2025 legislature did fund an increase
(implemented as the higher of new actuarial rates or the existing temporary 25% bump) `[IB]`.
Weigh accordingly: do not enter Medicaid-only at thin margins with no commercial or private-pay mix.

### 2.4 Acquisition — corrected rationale

**You do not buy the license.** `[LAC Ch.56]` on CHOW: the new owner must submit "**all documents
required for a new license**" plus the fee, and "once all application requirements are completed
and approved … **a new license may be issued to the new owner**." The license is valid up to one
year and for one geographic service area only.

**And the distressed-asset angle is largely closed:** "**A BHS provider that is under license
revocation, provisional licensure or denial of license renewal may not undergo a CHOW**"
`[LAC Ch.56]`; outstanding fees or fines suspend a CHOW until paid. The struggling sellers a
bargain-hunter would target are frequently the ones legally barred from selling.

**What an acquisition does buy:** physical plant already built and surveyed to standard; clinical
leadership and staff in post; existing census and referral relationships; **accreditation**, which
attaches to the program; policies that have passed survey; documented operating history; and a
seller available for transition. Those are real. **Budget $150–300K transition reserve** for CHOW
re-licensure, re-credentialing, and census disruption. Payer credentialing generally attaches to
entity/TIN — assume Medicaid re-enrollment and check each commercial contract for
assignment/novation clauses rather than assuming they travel.

### 2.5 Financing — and the downside

- **SBA 7(a)** finances startups and acquisitions to $5M. **Correction to a common belief:**
  **13 CFR § 120.150**, as amended at 88 FR 21085 (Apr. 10, 2023) `[CFR]`, lists only credit
  history, earnings/cashflow, and equity/collateral — the former "experience and depth of
  management" criterion **was removed.** Industry experience is now _lender credit policy_, not an
  SBA eligibility rule. In licensed healthcare, lenders commonly still require relevant management
  experience plus evidence licensure will be reissued. **The binding constraint on a Louisiana
  acquisition is the state CHOW, not SBA eligibility.**
- **The downside, stated with the same specificity as the upside:** 7(a) loans are personally
  guaranteed by owners of 20%+, commonly secured against home equity. A $1–3M acquisition means a
  **$1–3M personal guarantee.** Combined with the undercapitalization risk, the realistic bad
  outcome is not "lose the invested capital" — it is **lose the capital, owe the guarantee, and
  carry it personally after the entity fails.**
- SBA's **active-ownership rule** bars passive investors: an "own it, hire an administrator, stay
  employed in tech" structure is not 7(a)-financeable.

## 3. Sequencing — revised after review

Revision 2 recommended _sober living → IOP → residential_. The review substantially damaged the
first leg, and I now think it was wrong.

**The case against sober-living-first, which I accept:**

1. **It does not build the track record that matters.** What a BHSP applicant and a payer need is
   _clinical_: ASAM level-of-care fidelity, LMHP supervision structure, treatment planning and
   documentation, utilization review, survey response, incident reporting, 42 CFR Part 2 handling.
   A recovery residence produces none of these — it is a leased-property business with a house
   manager.
2. **It manufactures your largest criminal exposure.** An owned sober-living → owned-treatment
   referral path is the paradigm EKRA "body brokering" fact pattern. Sequences that put IOP first
   simply do not create the structure. This is a _cost of the inversion_, not a neutral feature.
3. **Its own economics are weak** — $1,200–17,500/month across 3–5 houses for 18 months of work.

**What survives from revision 2:** the _ordering_ of difficulty. Entering at residential or detox
first — no clinical credential, no operating history, no payer contracts, no survey record — remains
the worst available entry. That conclusion does not depend on any contested number.

## 4. Non-negotiable architecture

### 4.1 You are not a clinician

A Louisiana BHSP license is **institutional** (held by the entity), so non-clinician ownership is
legal and common — unlike a dental or medical practice, where the license is personal. You must
install a **Medical Director** (physician, typically part-time stipend) and a **Clinical Director**
(independently-licensed LMHP). For SUD, Louisiana requires an identified **Addictionologist**
(§5693) and sufficient independently-licensed LMHPs `[LAC]`. **The Clinical Director is your single
most important hire.**

### 4.2 EKRA

`[18 U.S.C. § 220]` — "shall be fined not more than **$200,000**, imprisoned not more than **10
years**, or both, **for each occurrence**." Covers recovery homes, clinical treatment facilities,
and laboratories, and is not limited to federal programs.

_United States v. Schena_ (9th Cir. 2025): **the court affirmed the EKRA conviction.** Its narrow
holding is that percentage-based compensation is not _per se_ unlawful absent undue influence over
the flow of referrals. Read it as a conviction affirmed with a narrow carve-out — not as reassuring.
It binds only the Ninth Circuit.

**Build in from day one:** no admissions, marketing, or outreach compensation varying with
headcount, census, or revenue — **salary only**, with contemporaneous documentation. **42 CFR
Part 2** governs SUD records more strictly than HIPAA and reaches your software.

### 4.3 Insurance — availability is the gate, not price

Break out and quote early: **professional liability**, general liability, **abuse/molestation**
(frequently sublimited or excluded in behavioral health), D&O, and **cyber / Part 2 breach**.
Carriers underwrite on operator experience, level of care, and claims history; residential and
withdrawal management are materially harder and dearer than outpatient. **A declination is a stop
signal**, and operating history is an underwriting input — another argument for Option B.

### 4.4 Wind-down and continuity of care

Absent from revision 2 entirely, in a sector with accelerating closures. `[LAC Ch.56]`: a license
"shall be immediately null and void if a BHS provider ceases to operate," and "during the period of
inactivation, the department prohibits CHOW" — **an operator in trouble cannot simply sell out of
it.** Clinical staff carry individual licensure duties that survive the LLC's cash.

Gate each stage on: transfer agreements with at least two competitor programs; a ring-fenced
closure reserve covering 30–60 days of care continuity; a named clinical lead accountable for
orderly transfer.

## 7a. Updates from the capital-raising research

Three items were refined by the companion `CAPITAL-RAISING-PLAN-2026-10-06`:

1. **SAMHSA was not dissolved.** Congress rejected the FY26 proposal (Appropriations vote 26-3
   against, with funding added) and approved a $7.1B topline; the Administration for a Healthy
   America was never authorized or funded. HHS has nonetheless proceeded administratively via
   staffing cuts and transferred duties, and the FY27 request renews the proposal.
2. **Louisiana's corporate-practice posture is softer than Section 4.1 implies.** The LSBME's
   December 2024 Statement of Position holds that physician employment by a non-professional
   corporation is **not per se unlawful**; the operative test is whether the arrangement impairs
   independent medical judgment. This materially simplifies structuring for a non-clinician owner
   relative to strict-CPOM states such as California and New York.
3. **Financing independently reinforces the Option B sequencing recommendation.** SBA removed
   "experience and depth of management" as a regulatory criterion in 2023 (13 CFR 120.150, 88 FR
   21085), but lenders retained it as credit policy and in licensed healthcare commonly require
   relevant management experience plus evidence licensure will be reissued. A paid industry role
   therefore improves financeability on its own terms, independent of the operating-skill
   rationale.

Also newly identified there: an SBA-financed Louisiana acquisition has a **closing collision** -
lenders fund at close and cite licensing transfers as a top delay source, while a Louisiana BHSP
license does not transfer and must be reissued by LDH to the new owner. Condition closing on CHOW
approval or escrow against it.
