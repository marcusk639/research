## The single most important reframing

**You are comparing two different _kinds_ of money, and the headline numbers are not comparable as stated.**

- The CPA firm's **~$2.5M/yr revenue is your brother's firm**, not your income. On the documented arrangement you are an **employee + eventual external consultant** — you earn a **salary ($50K start) + a small revenue-share + later external consulting fees**. You are selling **labor**, not building **equity** you own. Your realistic personal ceiling on this path is **~$150K–$220K combined income by Year 3–5**, and it is capped because you are not a partner/owner of the firm. `[Verified in-repo: viability-analysis.md, venture-worthiness-assessment.md]`
- The **addiction-recovery SDVOSB venture is a business you would own 100%.** Its output is an **owned, scalable asset** (SaaS ARR + a government-contract vehicle protected by an SDVOSB moat) with real enterprise value you could grow and potentially sell. Its ceiling is far higher — and its floor is far lower (no salary, slow first dollar, high execution risk). `[Inference from REPORT.md + procurement-validation.md]`

So the real question is **not "which earns more"** — it is **"do you want a high-probability, capped, near-term _income_ (a job), or a lower-probability, uncapped, owned _asset_ (a business)?"** They optimize for different things, and — importantly — they are **not mutually exclusive**.

## Venture 1 — CPA-Consulting (the income engine)

**What it actually is:** an embedded internal AI/automation role at your brother's ~$2.5M, ~20-person firm (TWK), building a documented case study that _later_ seeds an external workflow-consulting practice for other CPA firms. SaaS is a low-probability Year 3–5 tail. `[Verified in-repo]`

**Why it's strong:**

- **It neutralizes the #1 and #2 failure modes of consulting transitions** (no clients, no pipeline): you start with a guaranteed client, validated pain points, internal access, and a warm referral network. ~80% of consulting startups fail in 2 years; this structure removes the usual cause. `[Verified in-repo]`
- **First dollar is immediate** — a salary lands in month 1. Lowest-variance income of the two paths.
- **The skills transfer directly** to the recovery venture (AI back-office automation is the same muscle).

**The real upside levers (corrected — this is more than consulting):** the planned deal adds, on top of salary, **(1) a 20% revenue share on the _net-new_ advisory/automation/recurring revenue you create** (recurring; a cut of the growth you drive, not just hours), and **(2) retained IP ownership + the right to resell your tooling to other CPA firms** (the S3 arm, ~$50–150K incremental — an _owned, resellable asset_, not just labor). The firm gets only a perpetual internal-use license. `[Verified: revenue-share-term-sheet.md, horizon-plan.md]` **Highest-leverage open item:** whether that 20% covers _net-new recurring revenue you enable_ (e.g., 9 new CAS retainers → ~$43K/yr to you, recurring) or only narrow "advisory" (which the model shows is ~$3.8K/yr — negligible). That single definition is worth tens of thousands/yr — paper it before signing.

**Why it's still capped / risky:**

- **It's a share of growth, not equity in the firm.** The 20% applies only to the _incremental_ line you create — the existing ~$2.5M tax/bookkeeping/audit/CAS book is excluded. You share in growth you cause, not the base. The external practice still scales with your hours; the _IP arm_ is the one genuinely sellable piece, and it's heavily gated (WTP validation, ~100-day cycles, kill switches). `[Verified in-repo]`
- **The base case is a 3–5 year path to mere _income recovery_** (back to your prior ~$145K corporate-engineer comp), not wealth creation. The 3-year cumulative income gap vs. staying in corporate tech is ~$150K–$190K. `[Verified in-repo]`
- **Two structural dependencies you don't control:** (1) the brother must actively _sell_ recurring services for the ROI to materialize — the model collapses to break-even if he treats it as "let Marcus build tools"; (2) **family-relationship risk** is rated High-impact throughout. `[Verified in-repo]`
- **Karbon is commoditizing the work** — AI adoption in CPA firms jumped 9%→41% in one year; the "ahead of the curve" window is closing. `[Verified in-repo]`

## Venture 2 — Addiction-Recovery SDVOSB AI venture (the owned asset)

**What it actually is:** a solo-owned company selling **AI back-office/operational tooling** to **private sober-living/SUD operators** (fast, HIPAA-only, first revenue) and into the **VA via the SDVOSB "Veterans First" procurement moat** (slow, gated, but structurally defensible), reusing the existing `recovery-platform` codebase (RATS/regroup, recovery-api). `[Verified in-repo: REPORT.md, procurement-validation.md]`

**Why it's strong:**

- **A durable, founder-specific moat almost no competitor can copy:** your service-connected-disabled-veteran status unlocks the **mandatory** SDVOSB preference (Kingdomware Rule of Two, sole-source up to $5M). VA demand is real (FY2024: $10.2B to SDVOSBs; a ~$5M sole-source SDVOSB _mental-health-software_ award exists). `[Verified]`
- **It's owned, scalable equity** — SaaS ARR + a government-contract channel compound and have enterprise/sale value; the CPA path does not.
- **Three of your assets stack here uniquely:** AI/LLM specialty (the product), disabled-vet status (the channel), lived recovery experience (credibility with operators). Few people on earth hold all three.
- **A VA disability check is a tax-free income floor** under this path, materially lowering the runway risk that normally kills high-variance solo bets. `[Inference]`

**Why it's risky:**

- **Slow first dollar** — months of build + VetCert + first pilots before meaningful revenue. **No salary floor** from the venture itself.
- **The VA channel is gated and slow** (~12–18+ months behind VetCert → ATO; FedRAMP not cleanly avoidable), and **lumpy** (certification buys eligibility, not the award). `[Verified]`
- **EKRA is a criminal wire** — every structure must be flat-fee software, never referral-tied. `[Verified]`
- **Solo execution + market-entry risk** is real; a meaningful share of solo ventures stall at lifestyle-business scale. I weight a **~30–40% probability this stalls under ~$100K/yr.** `[Inference — do not over-optimize]`

## Verdict — the optimal path

**These are complementary, and the financially-rational play is to use one to fund the other — not to choose blindly between them.**

1. **The CPA path is the better _risk-adjusted near-term income_ and the rational runway + skills engine.** It pays immediately, it's high-probability, and it builds the _exact_ AI-automation skillset the recovery venture needs. Treat it as the **income floor**, not the destination. `[Inference, high]`
2. **The recovery SDVOSB venture is the better _long-term wealth_ bet** — it's the only one of the two that produces _owned, scalable equity_ behind a moat competitors structurally cannot match. Its expected value is dragged down by execution/market risk, but its ceiling and its asset-ownership are categorically superior. `[Inference, med-high]`

**Decisive recommendation (sequenced, not fence-sitting):**

- **Take the CPA role as the floor** (or a scoped, part-time version of it) for income + runway + transferable AI skills. Negotiate the written milestones, the revenue-share, and explicitly **the right to do non-competing outside work** (the recovery venture) — already recommended in the CPA viability docs. `[Verified in-repo]`
- **In parallel, start the recovery venture's _zero-cost, long-pole_ steps now**: file **SBA VetCert** (it's mostly passive waiting — start the clock today), stand up the entity for 51% veteran ownership _and control_, and land **1–2 design-partner sober-living operators** via your warm recovery network. Your **VA disability income is the safety net** that makes carrying this variance sane.
- **Set an explicit 12–18 month traction gate on the recovery venture:** if by then you have _paying operators + an SBIR award or a credible VA/sub path_, **shift weight to it** as the primary wealth vehicle. If it's still near $0 with no signal, the CPA consulting practice is the proven fallback that's already working. This converts a binary bet into a staged, de-risked one.

**One-line answer:** _Don't pick one — run the CPA path as the near-guaranteed income engine and runway, and bet the owned-equity upside on the recovery SDVOSB venture behind a 12–18 month traction gate; the CPA job is how you can afford to take the recovery swing without betting the household on it._

## Honest caveats

- The CPA figures are **grounded in sourced in-repo models** (re-baselined to TWK's ~$2.5M actual); the recovery figures are **estimates with much wider error bars** — the asymmetry in certainty is itself a finding.
- The **hybrid has real execution tension**: the CPA internal role is near-full-time, so the recovery venture realistically runs as a nights/weekends validation bet until its traction gate clears — not a true 50/50 split. Be honest that you cannot give both 100%.
- **Family-relationship risk** (CPA) and **a year near $0** (recovery) are the two catastrophic tails; both are mitigated by the hybrid (the salary cushions the recovery downside; the recovery venture gives you a non-family identity that reduces over-dependence on the brother).
