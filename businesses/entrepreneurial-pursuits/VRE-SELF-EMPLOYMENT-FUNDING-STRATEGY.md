# VR&E (Chapter 31) Self-Employment Track — Optimal Strategy to Fund Startup Costs for a Service-Disabled Veteran's Software/AI Consulting Firm

> **Updated:** 2026-06-26 · **Method:** adversarial multi-source verification — 17 claims (core funding strategy) + 25 claims (SDVOSB stacking follow-up) surviving 3-vote review. Primary sources are the governing VA policy manuals (M28C, which supersedes the older M28R) and the controlling federal regulations (38 CFR Part 21). Where a benefits.va.gov manual URL now 302-redirects to the KnowVA knowledge base, the underlying CFR has been used as the stronger primary authority.

## Executive Summary

A service-disabled veteran can pursue the VR&E (Chapter 31) **Self-Employment track** to fund the startup of a software/AI consulting firm, but the funding flows through a specific, conditional process rather than as an open checkbook. Eligibility requires a service-connected disability, an employment barrier/handicap, and enrollment in VR&E; regulation (38 CFR 21.257(a)) lets VR&E approve self-employment as a goal for _any_ eligible veteran whenever VA deems it "a suitable vocational goal" — the "most severe disability" language is a distinct higher tier (Category I), not a gate for all self-employment cases (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). For a standard (Category II) case, the furnishable startup assistance is limited to vocational/business-management training, license and fees, and "necessary tools and supplies for the occupation" — which is the regulatory category a high-end development laptop and software must be justified under (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). VA explicitly lists a full **computer package** (laptop, memory, monitor, software, printers, scanners, extended warranties) as a fundable supply, but only when the assigned **VRC determines it is necessary** for the rehabilitation/employment goal, after checking whether the veteran already owns a computer, and documented on **VA Form 28-1905m** (sources: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_V/pt05_ch04_secA.pdf, https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000150413/M28CVA3-Services-Supplies-and-Equipment). There are **no per-category dollar caps** on equipment in the manual — the only dollar thresholds are approval-authority tiers (who signs off), not spending ceilings (source: https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000143938/M28C.IV.C.5--Self-Employment-Plan). The winning strategy is therefore evidentiary: get the VRC to write the specific AI-development hardware into the approved self-employment plan as a documented "necessary tool of the occupation," rather than arguing for a generic high-end machine.

## Findings

### Eligibility and program structure for the Self-Employment track

**Confidence: high.** VR&E (Chapter 31), formerly called Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment, serves veterans whose service-connected disability "limits your ability to work or prevents you from working" (source: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/). Self-Employment is **one of five** distinct support tracks under the program (the others being Reemployment, Rapid Access to Employment, Employment Through Long-Term Services, and Independent Living) (source: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/). Eligibility for the Self-Employment track specifically requires three things: a **service-connected disability**, an **employment barrier/handicap**, and **enrollment in VR&E** (source: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/programs/self-employment/). (Note: high-level eligibility also presumes the general Chapter 31 thresholds — an other-than-dishonorable discharge and a service-connected rating, typically at least 10% — layered underneath these three.)

### Category I vs. Category II: which track the veteran is on, and why it matters

**Confidence: high.** VR&E self-employment cases are assigned to one of two categories. **Category I** is for the most-severely disabled and requires _both_ VR&E Officer (VREO) and Central Office (CO) approval, plus a prior Independent Living/IEEP plan. **Category II** is the standard track for veterans who want to start a business and do not meet Category I criteria; critically, **Category II assignments do NOT require Central Office approval and do NOT require an IEEP** (source: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_IV/M28R.IV.C.8.pdf). The manual states verbatim: "Category II assignment is appropriate for Veterans who request assistance to start a business during the rehabilitation process and do not meet Category I criteria. Category II assignments do not require CO approval and do not require an IEEP." A software/AI consultant who is not in the most-severe-disability tier will almost certainly be a Category II case — meaning a faster, lower-bureaucracy path where the VRC can begin self-employment plan development upon VRC approval.

### Self-employment is NOT gated behind "most severe disability" — it is available to any eligible veteran when suitable

**Confidence: high.** This is the single most important strategic clarification. 38 CFR 21.257(a) states: "A program of vocational rehabilitation benefits and services may include self-employment for an individual if VA determines that such an objective is a suitable vocational goal" — with **no** severe-disability gate (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). The "most severe service-connected disability" language appears in a _separate_ paragraph as a definition, and only the _more extensive_ services are reserved for that subset. The structure is graduated/two-tier, not an absolute gate. Practical caveat: some VRCs operationally route wage-employable veterans toward other (cheaper) tracks, so a strong "self-employment is the suitable goal" narrative still matters — but the regulation does not bar a less-than-most-severely-disabled veteran from the track.

### What VR&E services the track provides (beyond money)

**Confidence: high.** The Self-Employment track furnishes: coordination services and help **developing a proposed business plan**; **analysis of the business concept**; **training in small-business operations, marketing, and finances**; and **guidance on getting the right resources to implement the plan** (source: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/programs/self-employment/). After the veteran develops the business plan, "we'll review it and evaluate whether self-employment and the proposed business are viable options" — i.e., a **viability review is a precondition to approval/funding** (source: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/programs/self-employment/). The M28R/M28C requires the VRC to complete a business-plan analysis "clearly identifying the viability of the business plan."

### What startup costs VR&E can fund — and the exact regulatory category hardware falls into

**Confidence: high.** For an **ordinary (Category II / non-most-severe) self-employment case**, 38 CFR 21.257(d) limits furnishable startup assistance to: (1) **vocational training**, (2) **incidental training in business management**, (3) **"License or other fees required for self-employment,"** and (4) **"Necessary tools and supplies for the occupation."** The broader package — minimum stocks of inventory/saleable merchandise plus "Essential equipment, including machinery, occupational fixtures, accessories, and appliances" under 38 CFR 21.214 — is reserved for the **most-severe** tier under 21.257(e) (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). **Strategic consequence:** for a standard case, a development laptop and software must be justified specifically as a **"necessary tool/supply of the software-consulting occupation"** under 21.257(d)(4), not as "essential equipment/machinery." This category is broad enough to legitimately include a development laptop — the burden is to document necessity, not to fit it into a different bucket.

For self-employment more generally, VA-provided supplies are "generally limited to the following supplies necessary to begin operations": minimum stocks of materials, **essential equipment/machinery/occupational fixtures/accessories/appliances**, and **other related assistance such as business license fees**; and **if the business requires more than VA may authorize, the veteran must self-fund the remainder** (source: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_V/pt05_ch04_secA.pdf).

### The "computer package" is an explicitly named, fundable supply

**Confidence: medium-high** (2-1 verification vote; corroborated by mirror of the primary PDF). The M28R supplies-and-equipment chapter lists, verbatim, a fundable "**Computer package** (e.g., desk top or lap top computer, power source, memory, monitor, disk drives, video and sound cards, speakers, **software**, printers, scanners, fax/modems, surge protectors and **extended warranties/service plans**)" (source: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_V/pt05_ch04_secA.pdf). This means the laptop, the RAM, the software, and even an AppleCare-style extended warranty are all within the named, fundable scope. **Important conditionality:** funding is not automatic. The VRC must approve all supplies in advance, the item must be "deemed necessary to assist the Veteran in completing the objectives of a rehabilitation plan," and the VRC must check whether the veteran already owns a computer. For a self-employment startup specifically, the hardware flows through the approved self-employment plan's toolkit (M28R Part IV, Section C, Ch. 8 / M28C.IV.C.5) rather than the general training-supplies chapter — but the chapter still establishes that computer packages are fundable for employment goals.

### No dollar caps on equipment — only approval-authority tiers

**Confidence: high.** The manual provides **NO per-category dollar caps** for equipment or supplies (source: https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000143938/M28C.IV.C.5--Self-Employment-Plan). The fundable categories — "Essential equipment, and tools, including machinery, occupational fixtures, accessories, and appliances," minimum stocks of materials/supplies, and required licenses/fees — match 38 CFR 21.214(e) verbatim. The **only** dollar threshold is an approval-authority escalation: 38 CFR 21.258 sets a **$25,000** line where plans under $25K are approved by the VR&E Officer with jurisdiction, and plans of $25K or more must be approved by the Executive Director, VR&E Service. This is **who signs off, not a spending ceiling** — larger plans can be funded with the higher-level approval. (Note: several specific tiered-dollar-threshold formulations — e.g., a "$49,999/$50,000" split, or "Category II cases over $25,000 must go to VR&E Service" — were tested and **refuted** in verification; rely on the $25,000 approval-authority line from 38 CFR 21.258 rather than these.)

### The decision standard a hardware request must meet: "necessary for the rehabilitation goal"

**Confidence: high.** VR&E defines "supplies" as "books, tools, and other supplies and equipment which VA determines are necessary for the Veteran's rehabilitation program," and "the case manager is responsible for ensuring that any purchase of supplies is required or necessary for the Veteran to pursue his or her rehabilitation goal" (source: https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000150413/M28CVA3-Services-Supplies-and-Equipment). The underlying regulation (38 CFR 21.212) actually **narrows** this further: supplies must be what a non-disabled person in the same training/employment situation would use, **or** must mitigate the veteran's disability. **Evidence/justification implication for high-end AI hardware:** the strongest case is _occupational necessity_ — that a comparable non-disabled AI/software developer would require a machine of that specification (e.g., 48GB+ RAM to run local LLMs/containers/IDEs/build toolchains for AI-heavy development) to perform the work and deliver to clients. Frame the spec to the _job_, not to preference.

### Who decides and how it is documented: the VRC, VA Form 28-1905m, and 28-1905d

**Confidence: high.** The **VRC is solely responsible** for determining the need for supplies; for self-employment, the VRC "must determine what supplies and equipment the Veteran needs for self-employment **while developing the self-employment plan** and secure all necessary approvals before authorizing supplies," documenting the VA-provided supplies on **VA Form 28-1905m** (the "Request for and Receipt of Supplies" form), with provision based on the veteran's **category assignment** (sources: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_V/pt05_ch04_secA.pdf, https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000150413/M28CVA3-Services-Supplies-and-Equipment). The VRC must also document the determination of need on **VA Form 28-1905d** (or in CWINRS notes), authorize a computer package only **after analyzing the veteran's situation**, and **consider whether the veteran already owns a computer** before authorizing the purchase (source: https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin28/M28R/Part_V/pt05_ch04_secA.pdf). **Strategic implication:** the leverage point is the self-employment plan-development phase with the VRC — get the specific hardware itemized into the approved plan up front, and pre-empt the "do you already own one?" question (an old/underpowered personal machine is not adequate for AI-heavy development).

### Timing gate: case status and a signed plan before equipment is authorized

**Confidence: medium-high** (2-1 vote; a minor supplies→equipment generalization was noted). A veteran's case must be in one of **four case statuses** to be eligible to receive supplies — **Rehabilitation to the Point of Employability (RTE), Extended Evaluation (EE), Independent Living (IL), or Job Ready (JR)** (the M28C "JR" label corresponds to the older M28R "Employment Services" stage) — and **supplies/equipment generally cannot be authorized until the veteran signs the rehabilitation plan and is accepted to start training** (source: https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000150413/M28CVA3-Services-Supplies-and-Equipment). **Timeline implication:** hardware money does not arrive at intake. The sequence is: enroll in VR&E → evaluation → develop and **sign** the self-employment plan (with the hardware itemized) → reach the right case status → authorization on Form 28-1905m. Expect this to span the evaluation and planning phases, not to be a same-week purchase.

### Explicitly prohibited startup costs (do not request these)

**Confidence: high.** VR&E self-employment funding **cannot** be authorized for: (1) **purchase of, or partial payment for, land or buildings**; (2) **full or partial lease/rental payments**; (3) **purchase or rental of trucks, cars, or other means of transportation**; and (4) **stocking a farm for animal husbandry** (sources: https://knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000143938/M28CIVC5-Self-Employment-Plan, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.214). This is confirmed by binding federal regulation (38 CFR 21.214(f)). For a software/AI consulting firm this is mostly favorable — no office lease or vehicle is fundable, so the plan should concentrate on the items that _are_ fundable: hardware/software as "necessary tools," certifications/training, and license/registration fees.

### How this stacks with SDVOSB certification and government subcontracting

> **Verified in a dedicated follow-up research pass (2026-06-26):** 5 findings, 25 claims surviving 3-vote adversarial verification, 0 killed. The earlier "low confidence / framing only" caveat on this topic is now superseded by the primary-source findings below (38 CFR 21.257, 13 CFR 128, FAR Subpart 19.14, 89 FR 48114).

**Bottom line:** VR&E Self-Employment and SDVOSB certification are **mutually reinforcing, with no regulatory conflict**. VR&E funds the _startup capability_ (training, certifications, dev hardware/software, license fees); SDVOSB certification opens the _demand side_ (set-aside and sole-source federal contracting). The clean narrative for the VR&E feasibility review is that self-employment is a suitable, viable goal **because** the SDVOSB channel is a concrete, regulation-backed revenue path.

#### The feasibility review explicitly contemplates SBA coordination

**Confidence: high** (3-0). 38 CFR 21.257(a) permits self-employment only where VA determines it "a suitable vocational goal," and 21.257(f) requires a **comprehensive pre-approval feasibility review** that _must include_: (1) economic viability, (2) cost analysis, (3) **market analysis** for the proposed services, (4) availability of non-VA financing, and (5) **"Evidence of coordination with the Small Business Administration to secure special consideration under section 8 of the Small Business Act"** (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). A credible SDVOSB federal-contracting demand path feeds directly into factors (3) and (5) — so building the SDVOSB story into the business plan _strengthens the exact determination that gates plan approval_. (Approval-authority nuance: the **VR&E Officer** approves plans under $25K and the **Executive Director, VR&E Service** approves $25K+; the VRC develops and recommends but does not unilaterally approve.)

#### No conflict between VR&E benefits and SDVOSB certification / federal bidding

**Confidence: medium** (derived from 3-0 claims; based on the affirmative SBA-coordination factor plus absence of any contrary authority in 38 CFR 21.257, 13 CFR 128, FAR Part 19). No prohibition, offset, or disqualification was found for simultaneously holding VR&E self-employment benefits and SDVOSB certification or bidding on federal contracts (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257). If anything, VR&E law treats SBA/small-business engagement as supportive. **Open risk to verify** (see Open Questions): whether VR&E grant funds or equipment could be read as outside investment/affiliation affecting SDVOSB ownership-and-control analysis under 13 CFR 128.202–128.203.

#### Sequencing: entity + SAM registration must precede VetCert

**Confidence: high** (3-0). Under SBA's post-2023 **VetCert** framework (13 CFR part 128), an SDVOSB must already be an operating small business: **registered in SAM.gov**, small under the size standard for a NAICS code in its SAM profile, and **at least 51% unconditionally and directly owned and controlled by service-disabled veteran(s)** (13 CFR 128.200(b), 128.202, 128.203) — the veteran must hold the highest officer position and run daily operations, with ownership held directly (not through another entity/trust, narrow revocable-living-trust exception) (sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/13/128.200, https://veterans.certify.sba.gov/). Because certification presupposes an existing SAM-registered entity, the **natural order is: form the entity + register in SAM → develop and sign the VR&E self-employment plan → obtain VetCert.** VR&E feasibility analysis logically happens _around_ entity formation, so the two timelines interleave rather than block each other.

#### The revenue path is concrete and regulation-backed

**Confidence: high** (3-0). FAR **Subpart 19.14** authorizes the SDVOSB revenue channel that substantiates "viable": contracting officers may **set aside** contracts for SDVOSBs under the **rule of two** (FAR 19.1405 — reasonable expectation of ≥2 SDVOSB offers at fair market price) and may make **sole-source** SDVOSB awards up to **$8.5M (manufacturing) / $5M (other)** when ≥2 offers aren't expected (FAR 19.1406) (sources: https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-19.14, https://www.acquisition.gov/far/19.1406). Effective **January 1, 2024, only SBA-certified SDVOSBs** (status determined by SBA under 13 CFR 128, designated in SAM) are eligible for those awards — **self-certification ended** (SBA final rule 89 FR 48114; self-certification was _never_ permitted for VA opportunities) (sources: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/06/06/2024-12252/eliminating-self-certification-for-service-disabled-veteran-owned-small-businesses, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/13/128.200). There is also a **3% governmentwide SDVOSB contracting goal**, giving agencies a standing incentive to award SDVOSBs. _Practical present-state rule (2026): the self-certification sunset deadlines have passed — SBA VetCert certification is simply mandatory._

#### VR&E assistance does not jeopardize SDVOSB ownership/control

**Confidence: medium-high** — grounded in direct, verbatim primary-source regulatory text; the formal 3-vote adversarial pass on this question was **inconclusive due to transient API rate-limiting (votes errored out, not substantive refutations)**, so this rests on the regulation's plain text and an on-point 2025 OHA decision rather than a completed vote. The reading is clear and one-directional across every source examined:

- **It's an in-kind VA benefit, not equity.** VR&E self-employment assistance is "a program of vocational rehabilitation benefits and services" — enumerated in-kind items (vocational/business-management training, license/fees, and "necessary tools and supplies for the occupation"), **not** a cash grant, loan, or equity contribution to the LLC. 38 CFR 21.257 contains **no provision creating any VA ownership, title, or control interest** in the business and imposes no condition on how the veteran structures or runs it (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257).
- **Ownership (13 CFR 128.202) is untouched.** SDVOSB status requires the veteran's **51% direct, unconditional** ownership. "Unconditional" targets arrangements that route the _benefits of ownership_ to another party (voting trusts, restrictions, executory agreements) — an in-kind government benefit is not equity and does not dilute or condition that 51% (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/13/128.202).
- **Control (13 CFR 128.203) is untouched.** The negative-control and financing-dependency prohibitions are framed against a **non-qualifying veteran / person / concern** gaining influence — e.g., critical financing or a license that lets _that party_ significantly influence decisions, or control "through loan arrangements" (commercially reasonable loan guaranties are expressly allowed). A federal benefit program (VA) is not such a party, so VR&E funds/equipment do not by their terms create a 128.203 control defect (source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/13/128.203).
- **No affiliation (13 CFR 121.103).** Affiliation turns on control or power to control by another **concern**; the VA is not a "concern" (not a business organized for profit, 13 CFR 121.105). Government grants/benefits/in-kind equipment are **not listed** as affiliation triggers (source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-13/chapter-I/part-121/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFRd133f03f6d8398b/section-121.103).
- **OHA backs the "protective vs. operational" line.** SBA's Office of Hearings and Appeals (e.g., _VSBC Appeal of Blue Skye Foods, LLC_, No. VSBC-442-A, Sept. 4, 2025) holds that even a minority _investor's_ veto rights are impermissible only when they reach ordinary operations rather than narrow protective/extraordinary actions — a far more invasive fact pattern than receiving a VA benefit, which involves no investor and no governance rights at all (source: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbas-oha-further-defines-extraordinary-action-in-sdvosb-appeal).

**Practical structuring takeaways:** keep the **veteran/LLC as the owner and operator** with no governance/voting rights ceded to anyone; characterize VA assistance as a **rehabilitation benefit**, not third-party investment; and since 38 CFR 21.257 is silent on equipment title, **confirm with your VRC who holds title** to VA-purchased hardware and ensure nothing in that arrangement gives a non-veteran party control. On these facts, holding VetCert while receiving VR&E assistance is not a conflict.

## Action Plan — Securing up to $25,000 for Equipment & Startup Costs

> **Your starting position (stated):** registered LLC, products already built, and you can document business existence, productivity, and a path to revenue. That is a strong evidentiary base — it directly feeds the 38 CFR 21.257(f) feasibility factors (economic viability, market analysis). The work below is about (a) getting/confirming VR&E entitlement, (b) getting a signed self-employment plan with the equipment itemized, and (c) keeping the ask **under the $25,000 approval-authority line** so it's approved at the VR&E Officer level without Executive Director escalation.

> **One reframe to internalize first:** VR&E is a _vocational rehabilitation_ program, not a small-business grant. Funding is approved because self-employment is your **suitable vocational goal** and the equipment is a **necessary tool of that occupation** — not because the business "deserves" capital. Every document you submit should answer "why is this necessary for the veteran to achieve/maintain suitable employment given the service-connected disability?" An already-built business is evidence of _viability_; pair it with a clear disability-related barrier to traditional employment so the program fit is obvious.

### Eligibility gate (confirm before anything else)

You must clear all of these or the plan can't be funded (see Findings: Eligibility; Timing gate):

- [ ] **Service-connected disability** + other-than-dishonorable discharge.
- [ ] **An employment barrier/handicap** tied to the disability (the rehab rationale).
- [ ] **VR&E (Chapter 31) entitlement established** — you must be _in the program_, not just eligible.
- [ ] Case reaches a **qualifying status** (Rehabilitation to the Point of Employability / Extended Evaluation / Independent Living / Job-Ready) before equipment is authorized.
- [ ] A **signed self-employment plan** exists — equipment generally cannot be authorized until the plan is signed and you're accepted to start.

### Step-by-step

**Step 1 — Apply / re-engage VR&E and request the Self-Employment track.**
Apply via VA Form 28-1900 (or through VA.gov / your eBenefits successor portal). In the application and intake, state explicitly that you are pursuing **self-employment** as your vocational goal and already operate a registered LLC. Outcome: an **Initial Evaluation appointment** with a Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor (VRC).

**Step 2 — Establish entitlement and case status at the Initial Evaluation.**
The VRC confirms a service-connected disability creating an employment handicap and that VR&E is the right vehicle. Bring the disability-barrier narrative. Outcome: entitlement determination and assignment toward a self-employment plan (most software/AI consultants are **Category II** — no Central Office approval, no IEEP; see Findings).

**Step 3 — Develop the self-employment plan _with the VRC_ (this is the leverage point).**
The VRC must "determine what supplies and equipment the Veteran needs for self-employment **while developing the self-employment plan** and secure all necessary approvals before authorizing supplies." So: get every piece of equipment **itemized into the plan up front**. Hand the VRC a clean, costed equipment list (Step 6 template) framed as "necessary tools of the occupation" under 38 CFR 21.257(d)(4). Outcome: a draft plan listing the goal, the business, and the itemized toolkit.

**Step 4 — Assemble and submit the feasibility/viability evidence packet.**
38 CFR 21.257(f) forces a feasibility review covering economic viability, cost analysis, **market analysis**, non-VA financing availability, and **SBA coordination**. Your existing assets map straight onto these — package them (see Evidence Packet checklist below). This is where you load the **SDVOSB demand path** (set-asides/sole-source + 3% goal) as the market-analysis + SBA-coordination evidence. Outcome: a feasibility file that makes "approve" the easy decision.

**Step 5 — Justify each hardware item as occupational necessity, and pre-empt the "do you already own one?" test.**
The standard is what a comparable **non-disabled** worker in the same occupation would need (38 CFR 21.212), plus any disability-mitigation. For the MacBook Pro M5 / 48GB RAM: tie the spec to the _work_ — running local LLMs/containers/build toolchains/multiple IDEs for AI-heavy development and client delivery. Explicitly address why any machine you currently own is inadequate (underpowered/old/insufficient RAM), because the VRC must check existing ownership before authorizing. Outcome: a one-paragraph necessity justification per line item.

**Step 6 — Keep the total under $25,000 and sign the plan.**
There are **no per-category dollar caps**, but a plan **≥ $25,000 escalates** approval to the Executive Director, VR&E Service (38 CFR 21.258); under $25K stays with your **VR&E Officer** — faster, fewer gates. Build the budget to land comfortably below $25K. Then **sign the plan** — signing + qualifying case status is the precondition to any authorization. Outcome: an approved, signed self-employment plan.

**Step 7 — Get equipment authorized and purchased on VA Form 28-1905m.**
Once the plan is approved, the VRC authorizes supplies/equipment on **VA Form 28-1905m** (need determination documented on **28-1905d** / case notes). VA typically procures or reimburses per its process — confirm with your VRC whether VA buys directly or authorizes you to purchase and reimburse, and **who holds title** to VA-purchased equipment. Outcome: hardware/software in hand.

### Evidence packet checklist (maps to the 21.257(f) feasibility factors)

- [ ] **Business existence** — LLC formation docs, EIN, operating agreement showing **51% direct, unconditional veteran ownership/control** (also your SDVOSB foundation).
- [ ] **Productivity / traction** — the built products (demos, repos, screenshots), any users/clients, LOIs, contracts, invoices, pipeline.
- [ ] **Path to revenue** — a concise business plan with **market analysis** and financial projections (economic viability + cost analysis factors).
- [ ] **SDVOSB demand evidence** — explanation of the SDVOSB set-aside/sole-source channel (FAR 19.14) and the 3% governmentwide goal as your market; note intent to obtain **SBA VetCert** (the "SBA coordination" factor in 21.257(f)(5)).
- [ ] **Non-VA financing picture** — what you can/can't self-fund (the regulation asks; honest constraints support the need for VA assistance).
- [ ] **Itemized equipment list** with per-item **occupational-necessity justification** and the "current equipment is inadequate" note.
- [ ] **Disability-barrier narrative** — why traditional employment is impaired and self-employment is the suitable goal.

### Pitfalls to avoid

- **Don't pitch it as a business grant.** Frame everything as vocational necessity. (See reframe above.)
- **Don't request prohibited items.** No land/buildings, no lease/rent, no vehicles, no farm-animal stocking (38 CFR 21.214(f)) — wasted asks erode credibility.
- **Don't let the "already own a computer" test ambush you.** Address it proactively.
- **Don't blow past $25K** unless the extra is truly necessary — the escalation adds time and a higher approval bar with no upside if you don't need it.
- **Don't assume the VRC approves alone** — they develop/recommend; the **VR&E Officer** (<$25K) signs. Build the file to make their approval defensible.
- **SDVOSB control risk — resolved (low):** VR&E in-kind assistance does **not** jeopardize SDVOSB ownership/control or create affiliation (see "VR&E assistance does not jeopardize SDVOSB ownership/control" above). Still, structure cleanly: keep the **veteran/LLC as sole owner-operator** with no governance rights ceded to any non-veteran, characterize VA help as a **rehabilitation benefit (not equity)**, and **confirm with your VRC who holds title** to VA-purchased equipment (38 CFR 21.257 is silent on title).

## Caveats & Limitations

- **Manual migration / URL staleness.** The governing manual transitioned from **M28R** to **M28C** (~2022–2023). Many benefits.va.gov M28R PDF URLs now **302-redirect** to the KnowVA knowledge base, and KnowVA pages are JavaScript-gated (hard to fetch directly). Where possible, the controlling **38 CFR Part 21** regulation was used as the stronger, stable primary source. The substantive policy (VRC-determined, necessity-based, no dollar caps, four prohibited items) is carried forward from M28R to M28C, but the _exact_ M28C item wording (e.g., "extended warranties") should be confirmed against the live M28C before relying on it for a present-day approval.
- **Computer-package claim** survived on a 2-1 vote and rests partly on a third-party mirror of the M28R PDF; treat the verbatim list as M28R-era and confirm the M28C equivalent (M28C.V.A.3).
- **Several dollar-threshold formulations were refuted.** Do not rely on "$49,999/$50,000" tiers or "Category II over $25,000 must go to VR&E Service." The verified threshold is the **$25,000 approval-authority line** in 38 CFR 21.258 (who approves), not a spending cap.
- **SDVOSB stacking is now verified** (follow-up pass, 25 claims, 0 killed) — see that section. Residual cautions from that pass: the self-cert sunset dates (Dec 31 2023 / Dec 22 2024) are now expired, so the live rule is simply "VetCert is mandatory"; FAR sole-source dollar thresholds ($8.5M/$5M) are periodically inflation-adjusted (verify before relying); and the "no conflict" finding is medium-confidence (rests on the affirmative SBA-coordination factor + absence of contrary authority, not a single source expressly blessing the combination).
- **Operational vs. regulatory reality.** Regulation permits self-employment for any suitable case, but individual VRCs have discretion and may steer wage-employable veterans elsewhere; outcomes vary by counselor and region.
- **"Already own a computer" test is real.** A capable existing personal machine can undercut a hardware request; the necessity argument must address why current equipment is inadequate for AI-heavy development.

## Open Questions

1. **VR&E funds vs. SDVOSB ownership/control — RESOLVED (medium-high):** VR&E in-kind assistance does not affect SDVOSB ownership (128.202), control (128.203), or affiliation (121.103) — see the dedicated finding above. _Caveat: the formal adversarial vote on this was rate-limited rather than completed, so the conclusion rests on plain regulatory text + a 2025 OHA decision; a clean re-run when the API isn't throttled would upgrade it to high confidence._
1. **M28R/M28C operational sequencing:** Does VA's VR&E procedures manual (M28R/M28C Part IV.C.8) impose any practical sequencing requirement on entity formation, SAM registration, or VetCert relative to the signed self-employment plan — and does it set funding/equipment rules that interact with using VR&E funds to stand up an SDVOSB?
1. **VetCert timeline + NAICS/subcontracting:** What is the typical 2026 SBA VetCert processing time (and resulting gap before a vet can first bid on set-asides), and for a software/AI firm which NAICS codes (e.g., 541511/541512/541519/541715) and size standards belong in the SAM profile — including how limitations-on-subcontracting rules (13 CFR 125.6 / FAR 52.219-14) constrain bidding as prime vs. subcontracting to a larger prime?
1. **M28C exact equipment/computer language:** What is the precise, current M28C.V.A.3 / M28C.IV.C.5 wording for fundable computer packages, including whether "extended warranties/service plans" survived the M28R→M28C migration?
1. **Real-world dollar magnitudes:** What is the typical _approved_ total dollar value for a Category II software/consulting self-employment plan, given there is no category cap but a $25K approval-escalation line — i.e., where do most plans actually land?
1. **Hardware-spec justification evidence:** What documentation (job analyses, BLS/occupational-standard references, client/contract requirements) most reliably persuades a VRC that a 48GB+ RAM AI-development machine is a "necessary tool of the occupation" rather than a preference?
