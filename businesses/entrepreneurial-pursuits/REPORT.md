## 3. Treatment-Industry Opportunity

**The single best wedge: an AI behavioral-health denial-prevention + concurrent utilization-review copilot, layered on top of existing EHRs (not a replacement).**

It is the only wedge that maximizes all three filters (AI gap × weak incumbent × buildable solo):

- **Acute, quantified, dollar-denominated pain.** BH claims are denied ~85% more often than medical (12–20% vs 5–10%); **82–85% of denials are preventable**; **~60% are never resubmitted** (permanent revenue loss); yet **81.7% of *appealed* denials are overturned** ([BlueBrix](https://bluebrix.health/blogs/the-top-reasons-behavioral-health-claims-denial)). The money is real, recoverable, and lost to a *labor* bottleneck — exactly what an AI agent replaces. Behavioral-health orgs lose 20–30% of earned revenue to preventable billing errors ([PIMSY](https://pimsyehr.com/addiction-treatment-revenue-cycle-management/)).
- **Weak incumbent coverage.** Every AI prior-auth/denial vendor found is general-medical or payer-side (Cohere, Innovaccer); the well-funded BH-AI players (Eleos, Commure) sit in *documentation* — already a lost category — **not RCM/UR**. No BH-native pre-bill scrubber tuned to BH's idiosyncratic denial drivers (ASAM level-of-care continued-stay UR, MHPAEA parity, 42 CFR Part 2, psychotherapy CPT-time mismatches) surfaced ([Cohere](https://coherehealth.com/blog/five-emerging-trends-in-prior-auth/), [Innovaccer](https://innovaccer.com/resources/blogs/top-5-ai-vendors-for-prior-authorization-2025)).
- **Buildable solo, AI-native.** It is a text/document problem (clinical notes → medical-necessity justification, CPT/time/parity checks, ASAM mapping, draft appeal letters) — pure LLM territory, no proprietary dataset or device. It sits *on top of* the EHR, avoiding the unwinnable rip-and-replace fight, and is **ROI-priced** (% of recovered/avoided denials), trivially justifiable when 60% of denials are otherwise abandoned.

**The advantage needed to displace incumbents:** none at the EHR core — that fight is refuted for a solo founder (switching costs, ONC/EPCS certification, HITRUST 6–24 months, $35M-funded AI-native competitors). The defensible posture is to **attack the connective tissue and adjacencies the EHRs structurally under-serve**, where the moat to *acquire* is (a) BH-specific denial/UR domain depth and (b) distribution via the brother's CPA book of treatment-center clients — the same buyers, warm.

**Call: CAUTIOUS-GO, validation-gated.** It is the best treatment wedge found, materially better-positioned than regroup or homegroups, and complements (does not compete with) regroup's operator focus. But two hard gates before any build: (1) **42 CFR Part 2 + HIPAA architecture** — feeding SUD records to LLMs requires BAA-covered models / de-identification [VERIFY viable BAA path]; (2) **willingness-to-pay validation** with 3–5 actual treatment-center clients from the CPA book before writing code. Do not build on the 14,620-facility headline — real SAM is a few thousand private IOP/PHP/residential operators ([SAMHSA 2023 N-SUMHSS](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt53012/2023-nsumhss-annual-report.pdf)). This is later-sequence than the income engine.

## 4. The Recovery-App → Treatment-Center Bridge

**The EKRA-safe model, in one paragraph:** Sell the treatment center a **continuity-of-care software suite it operates itself**, for a **flat fee fixed in advance at fair market value**, strictly decoupled from any patient flow — homegroups licensed as the center's *own* alumni/outcomes tool (flat Institutional tier, not per-enrolled-alumnus), regroup licensed to sober-living *operators* (operator-pays is itself EKRA-aligned because the payor isn't buying referrals) and optionally to the center as a neutral discharge-planning view, and Next Step Recovery kept **D2C (families pay)** or as **fixed-scope B2B consulting** under a 42 CFR § 1001.952(d)-compliant personal-services contract with navigators on salary; the software must be bidirectional/neutral (never preferentially route patients toward paying facilities), no money may flow in the referral direction, and all patient data must ride a single 42 CFR Part 2-compliant TPO consent under a signed BAA with each customer — because EKRA is **intent-based**, a flat fee is necessary but *not sufficient*: the moment the package actually moves patients toward a paying center, "flat" stops protecting you, and a per-bed/per-admission price dressed as "SaaS metering" is the classic disguised kickback.

**ATTORNEY-GATED LINE (load-bearing, do not launch without it):** Do not collect a single dollar from a treatment center, and do not move Part 2 data between any two parties, until a licensed healthcare-regulatory attorney (EKRA + AKS + 42 CFR Part 2 + **Louisiana** patient-brokering competent) has, in writing, blessed the flat-fee license + FMV memo, the §1001.952(d) consulting contract, the single TPO consent + counseling-notes consent + BAA templates, and confirmed no routing logic steers patients to payers. This matters acutely because **Louisiana sits in the 5th Circuit, which has *not* adopted the defense-friendly *Schena* gloss** ([Health Law Diagnosis](https://www.healthlawdiagnosis.com/2026/02/eliminating-kickbacks-in-recovery-act-2025-updates-and-looking-to-2026/)) — relying on the 9th Circuit's pro-defendant reading in Marcus's home circuit is unproven, and the penalty is **$200,000 + 10 years per occurrence** ([18 U.S.C. § 220](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:18%20section:220%20edition:prelim))).
