## The slate

Priors use the five-scenario frame loosely; real figures come from research.
Confidence = my confidence in the _prior_, not in success.

### A. CPA-firm automation — productization & resale (anchor extension)

- **Thesis:** Use the brother's firm as proving ground, then resell packaged
  workflow/AI automation to other CPA firms (and adjacent SMBs).
- **Fit:** Highest — already in motion, warm channel, validated pain.
- **Riskiest assumption:** External firms pay for this before Karbon/vendors absorb the
  gap; resale ≠ internal success.
- **Prior:** Base $90–130k by Yr2–3 (salary + a few external clients); aggressive needs
  productized packages selling without his hands. Likelihood (reach conservative): High.
- **Deep research resolves:** real willingness-to-pay & price points for non-family CPA
  firms; how fast vendors close the gap; productized-service vs. SaaS economics. _(Most
  of the internal-role economics already done; research focuses on the EXTERNAL resale
  and productization paths.)_
- **Prior confidence:** Medium-High.

### B. Vertical AI-automation agency (beyond CPA)

- **Thesis:** Same playbook (audit → integrate → adopt) applied to other professional
  services — law firms, medical/dental billing, insurance, agencies.
- **Fit:** High — transferable skill; less warm-channel advantage than CPA.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Without a brother-equivalent referral channel in each
  vertical, client acquisition reverts to the #1 consulting failure mode (no pipeline).
- **Prior:** Base $80–150k once a niche is chosen; high variance on ramp. Likelihood: Moderate.
- **Deep research resolves:** which vertical has the best pain × payer × low-vendor-
  saturation profile; realistic CAC and ramp without a warm network.
- **Prior confidence:** Medium.

### C. Fractional / high-end freelance AI engineering (income-replacement baseline)

- **Thesis:** Sell the elite resume directly — fractional staff/lead AI engineer or
  contract LLM/agent work at $120–250/hr, remote.
- **Fit:** Very high — no new domain, no product risk, fastest to cash.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Utilization (billable %), not rate, is the binding
  constraint; feast-or-famine and self-marketing discipline.
- **Prior:** Base $120–180k at moderate utilization; this is the realistic _floor that
  meets the goal_ and the benchmark every other venture must beat. Likelihood: High.
- **Deep research resolves:** real fill rates/utilization for solo fractional AI
  engineers in 2026; platform vs. network sourcing; rate realism; demand durability.
- **Prior confidence:** Medium-High. **(Recommended as the comparison baseline.)**

### D. VA-disability / veteran-services tech

- **Thesis:** Productize the VA-claims corpus + RAG into a tool for veterans or for
  accredited VSOs/claims agents.
- **Fit:** High domain fit (veteran + existing corpus), but heavy regulation.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Legal/regulatory — VA accreditation rules (38 CFR), the
  for-profit "claim shark" crackdown (GUARD Act / state laws), and fee restrictions can
  make the most lucrative model (B2C fees) illegal or reputationally toxic.
- **Prior:** Base highly bimodal — either a constrained B2B/info tool ($40–90k) or
  legally fraught B2C. Likelihood (of a _clean_ model clearing the goal): Low-Moderate.
- **Deep research resolves:** what's legally permissible for a non-accredited builder;
  viable B2B (VSO/agent) models; payer and TAM; reputational landscape.
- **Prior confidence:** Low-Medium (regulatory uncertainty is the whole ballgame).

### E. Agentic-engineering products & education (audience-led)

- **Thesis:** Monetize Claude Code / agentic-engineering expertise via paid content,
  courses, templates/skills, sponsorships — and use the audience as a consulting funnel.
- **Fit:** Strong skill fit; weak fit with stated personality (audience-building is
  self-promotion-heavy; "no-BS" can be an asset or a throttle).
- **Riskiest assumption:** Audience monetization is winner-take-most and slow;
  base-case creator income is low and the field is crowding fast.
- **Prior:** Unfavorable/Conservative $0–30k; base $30–70k; the value may be as a
  _funnel_ to C, not a standalone business. Likelihood (standalone goal): Low.
- **Deep research resolves:** realistic dev-creator/course economics in 2026; whether
  it's better treated as marketing for consulting than as revenue.
- **Prior confidence:** Medium.

### F. Bootstrapped micro-SaaS (solo product)

- **Thesis:** Build and sell a focused B2B SaaS solo, leveraging engineering speed.
- **Fit:** Skill fit high; _temperament/time_ fit poor while doing the CPA role.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Distribution, not building, kills solo SaaS; multi-year ramp;
  the prior CPA analysis explicitly rated SaaS "Low / 3–5 years out — do not plan around."
- **Prior:** Unfavorable $0 (most likely for years); base modest MRR; aggressive is the
  only exciting case and it's low-probability. Likelihood (clearing goal <3yr): Low.
- **Deep research resolves:** bootstrapped B2B SaaS base rates, time-to-ramen-profit,
  whether any wedge from A/B/D is SaaS-shaped enough to justify it.
- **Prior confidence:** Medium-High that it's a Year-3+ option, not a now option.

## My recommendation (pre-research)

Research **C (fractional baseline)**, **A (CPA resale/productization)**, and **D
(VA-services tech)** first:

- **C** establishes the realistic income floor that every other option must beat — and
  it directly satisfies the stated goal with the lowest risk. Without it, the whole
  comparison floats.
- **A** is already in motion and has the strongest structural advantages; research
  should target only the unresolved external/resale economics.
- **D** is the highest _personal-meaning_ fit (veteran helping veterans) but the
  regulatory question could kill it — worth resolving early and cheaply.

**B, E, F** are second-wave: B if a specific vertical looks unusually good, E mainly as
a funnel for C, F as a Year-3+ option contingent on a wedge from A/B/D.

## Open questions before deep research

1. Financial runway (months of expenses covered) — gates how much income-gap risk is
   tolerable and changes the verdict.
2. Is the corporate-tech door being closed (full commitment) or kept open (hedge)?
3. Any ventures here you want dropped, or any I've missed?
