# Comparable Creators — Case Studies

> Supporting file for [`REPORT.md`](./REPORT.md) §Competition. **Figures verified**
> — all three case studies' claims were confirmed (3/3 supporting votes each) in the
> low-concurrency re-verification pass (`wf_e2573e34-85d`). Cited to origin URLs.

The set is chosen to span the **full outcome distribution** — one breakout, one
mid/cautionary, one long-tail grinder — so survivorship bias is visible rather than
hidden. The recurring lesson: the _content model_ replicates; the _breakout
outcomes_ track pre-existing platforms.

## 1. Holly Whitaker — _Recovering_ (breakout, hard to replicate)

- **Platform:** Substack newsletter, **~37,000 subscribers**; examines culture
  through the lens of addiction, recovery, and alcohol.
- **Book:** _Quit Like a Woman_ — NYT bestseller, reportedly **~600,000 copies**
  sold, translated into six languages.
- **Replicable?** The voice-and-topic model, yes. The outcome, less so: Whitaker
  had a book deal and media presence feeding the newsletter (and vice versa). This
  is the **tail**, not the base case.
- Source: https://newsletter.hollywhitaker.com/

## 2. A.J. Daulerio — _The Small Bow_ (mid / cautionary)

- **Starting point:** Former Gawker editor-in-chief — i.e., started with a media
  career and name recognition.
- **Platform:** Recovery newsletter launched 2018; **~7,000 subscribers** several
  years in, twice-weekly.
- **Monetization:** Memberships at **$5/mo or $45/yr**, plus merch (**$150–400/mo**)
  and reader donations (**~$1,000/mo** when new readers arrive).
- **Outcome:** Despite ~7,000 subs and a real media résumé, revenue is **not enough
  to support his family.** The clearest cautionary data point in the set.
- Source: https://inboxcollective.com/a-surprising-gift-how-a-j-daulerio-turned-the-worst-time-in-his-life-into-something-beautiful/

## 3. Benya Clark — _Exploring Sobriety_ (long-tail grinder / viability proof)

- **Platform:** Weekly long-form sobriety essay newsletter on Substack;
  **1,000+ subscribers.**
- **Read:** Proof that the **content model is viable** at small scale and that a
  consistent weekly confessional-essay cadence sustains a real (if small) audience —
  not proof of a living wage.
- Source: https://exploringsobriety.substack.com/

## Cross-case extraction

| Creator        | Subs    | Pre-existing platform? | Living wage from it?    |
| -------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Holly Whitaker | ~37,000 | Yes (book + media)     | Plausibly (book-driven) |
| A.J. Daulerio  | ~7,000  | Yes (Gawker EIC)       | **No**                  |
| Benya Clark    | 1,000+  | No (apparent)          | No                      |

**Replicable for Marcus:** weekly long-form confessional cadence; recovery-topic
affinity; building an owned list. **Not freely replicable:** the platform/book
head-start that the two larger cases relied on — Marcus must manufacture that
through seeding and consistency.

**Implication for the wedge:** none of these three occupies the **sardonic,
elite-tech, veteran** register. The existing recovery-writing field skews earnest
and un-ironic — which is precisely the gap the founder's voice can take. That is the
strongest replicable-advantage argument in the set, but it is a positioning bet, not
a measured outcome.

## Survivorship-bias note

Public case studies over-represent winners. For every _Recovering_ there are
thousands of abandoned sober Substacks with <100 subscribers that leave no
trace — consistent with the platform-wide data in [`REPORT.md`](./REPORT.md)
§Business Model (only ~10,000 publications have ever crossed 1,000 paid subs). Read
these three as the **upper envelope** of plausible outcomes, not the median.
