# Deep-Research Prompt — Writing Monetization & Reach

**Purpose:** Drive the `deep-research` skill to (1) judge whether an
addiction-centered memoir/essay venture is worthwhile and analyze that verdict,
and (2) regardless of the verdict, deliver a tactical playbook for getting the
founder's voice in front of as many people as possible — falling back to an
adjacent venture if the memoir framing doesn't hold up.

**How to run:** `/deep-research <paste the prompt block below>`

**Status:** Not yet run. Output should land in `../REPORT.md`.

---

## Prompt

```
RESEARCH QUESTION
Two linked questions: (1) Is building a large, monetized audience by writing
long-form, brutally honest personal essays and memoir centered on addiction a
worthwhile venture for the writer profiled below — yes/no, with the reasoning
behind that verdict made explicit? (2) Regardless of that verdict, what is the
highest-probability tactical playbook to get this writer's voice in front of as
many people as possible and convert that reach into a sustainable income?

The writer WILL proceed with getting his voice heard either way — so the report
must deliver an actionable playbook even if the verdict on the addiction-memoir
framing is negative. If that specific framing is judged not worthwhile, identify
the most worthwhile ADJACENT venture that still maximizes reach for this voice,
and write the playbook around that instead.

WRITER PROFILE (the asset being evaluated — treat as ground truth, do not flatter)
- Voice: long-form, grounded, unpretentious, sharply sardonic; gritty realism
  and unflinching vulnerability. Explicitly counter-positioned against today's
  short-form, attention-grabbing, surface-level content.
- Validated signal so far: lifelong unsolicited praise for writing quality from
  habitually critical people; unanimously positive feedback on writing-focused
  subreddits. No published track record, no existing audience or email list yet.
- Raw material / story: USAF veteran; 10-year software-engineering career at
  top-tier firms (e.g., Meta), reaching Staff level and earning well above the
  senior median, while concealing active addiction; earned a CS degree and
  landed his first engineering job while a full-time heroin user; decades of
  poly-substance addiction (heroin and most opioids, benzodiazepines,
  psychedelics, MDMA, cocaine, and more); 3 years clean during which he met his
  wife in Narcotics Anonymous; chronic relapse cycles running in parallel with
  career ascent; recently left tech to start a consulting business.
- Central theme: addiction as the lens on the human condition — its nature,
  meaning, causes, and connection to simply being human and coping with life.
  Career success and love are subplots, not the point. The aim is to expose, not
  boast, and let readers judge — and want more.

WHAT THE REPORT MUST COVER (organize under these headings)

1. Demand & market size
   - Appetite for long-form addiction/recovery/memoir writing today. Quantify the
     addiction-affected population (US and global), recovery community size, and
     the memoir/personal-essay reading market. Cite figures and show derivation.
   - Is long-form, voice-driven writing actually gaining or losing ground vs.
     short-form? Evidence for the "newsletter/Substack long-form renaissance"
     claim, with hard numbers, not vibes.
   - The role of addiction stigma: does it suppress audience, or is destigmatized
     candor itself a draw? Find evidence both ways.

2. Comparable creators (case studies — the most important section)
   - Profile 6-12 writers who built audiences from personal-narrative,
     addiction, recovery, or confessional long-form writing. For each: starting
     point, platform(s), how they grew, time-to-traction, audience size, and —
     where disclosed or credibly estimable — revenue and revenue mix.
   - Include at least 2-3 who did NOT break out, to expose survivorship bias.
   - Explicitly extract what was replicable vs. luck/timing/pre-existing platform.

3. Channels & platforms
   - Compare the realistic options for THIS writer: paid newsletter (Substack,
     beehiiv, Ghost), Medium, Reddit-to-newsletter funnels, Kindle/self-publishing,
     traditional memoir publishing, literary magazines/essays as on-ramps,
     podcasting, and personal blog. For each: audience-acquisition mechanics,
     discovery/algorithm dynamics, monetization ceiling, ownership of audience,
     and effort profile.
   - Which channel(s) best fit a sardonic, long-form, no-existing-following start?

4. Monetization models & realistic economics
   - For each viable model (paid subscriptions, book advances/royalties for
     memoir, sponsorships/ads, Patreon-style support, speaking, affiliate,
     coaching), give realistic benchmarks: e.g., free-to-paid conversion rates,
     typical paid-subscriber counts and pricing, memoir advance ranges for
     debut authors, churn, and what "large fanbase" must translate to in dollars.
   - Model unfavorable / conservative / base / optimistic income scenarios with
     stated assumptions. Be honest about how few writers earn a living this way.

5. Verdict & its analysis (worthwhile or not)
   - A clear go / no-go / needs-more-validation call on the addiction-memoir
     framing specifically, with the reasoning laid out: what evidence drives it,
     how confident you are, what would change the call, and the single assumption
     it most depends on.
   - Steelman BOTH sides before landing — the strongest case for pursuing it and
     the strongest case against — so the verdict is defensible, not asserted.

6. Adjacent-venture fallback (only develop if the verdict in §5 is negative or
   marginal)
   - Identify the most worthwhile alternative venture that still maximizes reach
     for THIS voice and story. Candidates to evaluate, not limited to: a broader
     "veteran + elite-tech-career + recovery" personal-brand newsletter where
     addiction is one thread among several; tech/career writing with addiction as
     subtext; narrative nonfiction beyond memoir; audio/podcast-first; a
     book-first push with platform built around it; or a hybrid.
   - Briefly justify the pick against the others on reach potential, monetization,
     and fit with his voice. This becomes the subject of the playbook in §7 if
     §5 is negative.

7. Tactical playbook (ALWAYS deliver — this is the core output)
   - Built around the addiction-memoir framing if §5 is go; otherwise around the
     §6 fallback venture. State at the top which subject the playbook targets.
   - Zero-to-first-1,000-true-fans plan: platform choice and why, first content
     to publish, posting cadence, exactly where to seed (which subreddits and
     communities, with named examples), how to convert Reddit/forum praise into
     owned email subscribers, serialization vs. standalone essays, and the hooks
     that work for confessional long-form.
   - A sequenced 6-12 month roadmap with the metric that proves or kills the
     thesis at each stage, and the earliest realistic monetization trigger.
   - The first three concrete actions to take this week.

8. Risks, ethics & unknowns
   - Privacy and consent: writing about a living spouse, family, and people met
     in recovery; tension with NA's anonymity tradition (12th Tradition) and
     recovery-community norms. Real cases of backlash.
   - Reputational/legal exposure of publicly detailing illegal drug use and
     behavior tied to named former employers; pseudonym vs. real-name tradeoffs.
   - Emotional/sustainability cost of writing from active or relapse-prone
     addiction; saturation of the recovery-memoir space.

METHOD & QUALITY BARS
- Cite every market figure, conversion rate, audience number, and revenue claim
  inline so it is traceable; keep a full source list. Prefer primary/recent
  (last ~3 years) sources; flag anything older or shaky.
- Distinguish hard data from anecdote and from your own inference. Where evidence
  is thin or contested, say so explicitly rather than smoothing it over.
- Adversarially verify the load-bearing claims — especially "long-form is
  growing," typical creator earnings, and memoir-market demand.
- Do not flatter the premise. If the most likely outcome is a small audience and
  negligible income, say that plainly and quantify it — then still give the best
  available playbook for maximizing reach.
- Audience: a smart, skeptical person who has already decided to put real work
  into getting his voice heard and needs the most effective path, not pep talk.
```

---

## Design notes

- **Two linked questions up front** — the worthwhile/not verdict _and_ an
  always-on playbook — with an explicit clause that the founder proceeds
  regardless, so the report can never bail at "no."
- **§5 verdict + analysis** requires a steelman of both sides before the call,
  making the verdict defensible rather than asserted.
- **§6 fallback** answers "if not worthwhile, what venture still gets my voice
  heard by as many as possible," naming candidate pivots and forcing a justified
  pick.
- **§7 playbook is mandatory and conditional** — it states whether it targets the
  memoir framing or the §6 fallback, so the output is actionable either way.
- **Honesty bars** keep the skepticism but append "then still give the best
  available playbook," so candor doesn't kill the deliverable.
