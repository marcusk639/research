## 5. Texas UI Mechanics (verified — high confidence)

- **Characterization is determinative.** Layoff for lack of work preserves
  eligibility; voluntary quit/resignation generally forfeits it (absent narrow
  good-cause); discharge disqualifies only if for misconduct ([TWC eligibility](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/eligibility-benefit-amounts);
  [TWC EFTE eligibility](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/ui_law_eligibility_issues.html)).
- **Settlement cash is UI-neutral.** "Incentive money…paid to obtain a release or
  waiver of liability or to settle a claim" is **not** disqualifying severance;
  **unilateral/policy severance and wages-in-lieu-of-notice ARE** disqualifying for
  the covered weeks ([TWC EFTE severance](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/final_pay_severance_benefits.html);
  [TWC how other money affects benefits](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/how-money-other-sources-can-affect-your-benefits)).
  _(One overbroad claim — that any severance disqualifies "any benefit period" — was
  **refuted 0-3**; the nuanced rule above is correct.)_
- **A no-FILE waiver is void; a no-CONTEST promise is valid.** An employee's promise
  not to file a UI claim is void and unenforceable (Tex. Labor Code §§207.072,
  207.074); an **employer's promise not to contest** is permissible and useful
  ([TWC EFTE release/waiver](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/release_waiver_agreements.html)).
- **Requalification is easily met.** A new claim (filed in/after April 2026) draws a
  fresh CY2025-dominated base period; the 6× weekly-benefit-amount re-earning
  threshold (max ~$3,630; WBA caps at $605 as of Oct 2025) is cleared **~58×** by a
  year of ~$210k W-2 wages ([TWC eligibility](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/eligibility-benefit-amounts)).
  The prior claim ~14–18 mo ago does not impede a fresh, fully-qualifying claim.
- **Dollar value of preserved UI: up to ~$15,730.** WBA caps at the Texas max
  **$605/week** (eff. Oct 5 2025); max total benefit is the lesser of 26 × WBA or 27%
  of base-period wages → for this earner, the full **$15,730 over the benefit year (up
  to 26 weeks)** ([TWC eligibility](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/eligibility-benefit-amounts)).
  Per the claimant (2026-06-23), a genuine eligible unemployed window exists, so the
  **full ceiling is realistic** — making the layoff/won't-contest characterization a
  **~$15.7k term that costs the employer ≈nothing.**
  - **Eligibility caveat:** full UI requires being unemployed/underemployed, able and
    available, actively searching, and **reporting any work/earnings each certified
    week**; weeks with family-business work/income must be reported and reduce that
    week's benefit. Texas cross-matches new-hire and quarterly-wage data (see
    `../unemployment/REPORT.md`); collect only what's genuinely owed for truly eligible
    weeks.

## 6. Return-to-Work Option (instead of / alongside settlement)

> Added 2026-06-23 from a targeted Exa pass (102 sources, 2 subagents). **Not legal
> advice** — verify against actual leave paperwork with counsel.

**Returning is legitimate, and may not require dropping claims.** Two things are
separable: (a) the _right to return_, and (b) _releasing claims_.

- **You likely already hold an FMLA reinstatement right** at the end of certified
  leave (~Jul 28): restoration to the **same or an equivalent position** (virtually
  identical pay, benefits, authority), independent of any settlement ([29 CFR
  §825.214–.215](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/825.215)). The employer may
  deny it only via narrow exceptions **it must prove** — "would have been laid off
  anyway" or "key employee" (highest-paid 10%, requires written notice at leave
  start, else forfeited) ([§825.216](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/825.216)).
- **Dropping claims is therefore NOT a legal precondition to returning** — it would be
  a _negotiated trade_ (reinstatement-for-release), which is legitimate and common;
  employers may condition re-employment on releasing **past** claims ([Employer Law
  Report](https://www.employerlawreport.com/2019/03/articles/labor-relations/employers-good-faith-offer-to-reinstate-employee-as-part-of-settlement-negotiations-in-exchange-for-dismissing-a-lawsuit-is-not-considered-retaliation/)).
  You **cannot prospectively waive future FMLA rights**, so a fresh retaliation after
  return is its own new claim ([§825.220(d)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/415/364/524797/)).
- **The catch — it trades away your best leverage.** After reinstatement you remain
  **at-will** and can be lawfully re-terminated for a genuine independent reason; the
  5th Cir. permits it absent pretext ([5th Cir.](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/22/22-10746.0.pdf)).
  Returning under the same manager who retaliated is the core risk, and a bare
  release-to-return spends your clean one-day-proximity FMLA claim for a job you can
  be fired from.
- **If pursuing return, negotiate protections — don't return bare:** (1) **new
  manager/team**; (2) **written performance reset** (rescind the 2.5/PIP, clean
  slate); (3) **severance backstop** — guaranteed payout if terminated without cause
  within ~12–24 months (deters a manufactured re-termination); (4) **release scoped to
  past claims only**, future rights preserved ([management-side guidance](https://themanagementparadox.com/negotiating-your-comeback-after-layoffs-key-strategies/)).
- **Tradeoff vs. cash exit:** returning moots the UI-characterization issue (you keep
  job + income) and the "wants him back" signal suggests the lane is live — but weigh
  it against a clean cash exit + replacement income late July, and the wellbeing cost
  of re-entering the same environment during SUD/MH recovery.
- **No-LTD update (2026-06-24):** because there is **no LTD**, "return before Jul 27"
  would only be to resume _salary_ after STD ends ~Jul 9 — i.e., back to the hostile
  manager during recovery. The absence of an STD→LTD bridge **further favors the clean
  cash exit**: settle + separate as layoff/not-for-cause around early July, collect
  severance + UI, bridge to the family business late July.

## 7. Reading the Cadence (Timeline Anxiety)

> Added 2026-06-23 from a targeted Exa pass (48 sources). Addresses the ~1-day silence
> after the 15-week counter vs. the prior sub-24h cadence.

- **One business day of silence is not a signal.** Practitioner norm: a **1–2 day lag
  after a counter is routine**; the concern threshold doesn't begin until **~5–7
  business days** of _unexplained_ silence ([Crain Brogdon](https://www.crainbrogdon.com/how-long-after-a-demand-letter-does-a-settlement-take/);
  [Super Lawyers](https://www.superlawyers.com/resources/employment-litigation/employment-litigation-settlement-negotiation-what-to-expect/)).
- **Expected counter window: this week (~Jun 24–27).** The 15-wk counter went out ~Jun
  22; with client authorization as the gate, the most-likely response is **2–3 business
  days (≈ Jun 24–25)**, plausibly to **end of week (Jun 26–27)** for public-company
  HR+legal sign-off. Only **>5–7 business days** of unexplained silence would be a first
  flag.
- **The cadence change is explained by content, not cooling.** Earlier employer
  replies sat **at/near the policy floor** (3–4 wk) — reflexive, pre-authorized, fast.
  The 15-week counter is the **first number crossing the floor**, so it now requires
  genuine **client authorization** (HR/legal/exec), a cycle that typically runs
  **24–72 hours** ([Nisar Law](https://www.nisarlaw.com/blog/2025/september/termination-negotiations/)).
  Counsel's stated "awaiting client authorization" makes the pause the _predicted_
  behavior — a sign you've moved them off auto-pilot, i.e. engagement.
- **Best practice: do not follow up at day one.** Immediate follow-up when the ball is
  in their court reads as softness and erodes leverage ([Kogan & DiSalvo](https://www.kogan-disalvo.com/firm-news/when-the-defense-smells-softness-litigation-readiness-as-a-settlement-multiplier/)).
  If a nudge is wanted, the trigger is **~5–7 business days** — a single professional
  status request from counsel, on a specific date — his call.
- **Leverage holds with the claimant.** Not financially forced, replacement income
  late July, clean FMLA claim as fallback → silence costs the employer (open clean
  liability) more than the claimant. The real accelerant is **credible readiness to
  file** (demand letter / EEOC-TWC charge), held in reserve, not a day-one follow-up.
- **Low-signal caution:** cadence is weak evidence this early; the dominant variable
  remains the **internal authorization ceiling**, invisible from outside. A pause tells
  you they're deciding, not what they'll decide.

## 8. Concession Dynamics / Handling a Lowball

> Added 2026-06-23 from a targeted Exa pass (50 sources). Addresses the worry that
> the drop to 15 weeks invites a stingy counter (~4.5 wk) that drags out the timeline.

- **The 15-wk move was small — it did not signal weakness.** The claimant's ladder is
  5 mo → 4 mo (≈17.3 wk) → **15 wk**, i.e. a final concession of only **~2 weeks**.
  Shrinking concessions signal you are **near your floor**, which is the message you
  want to send ([Harvard PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/four-strategies-for-making-concessions/)).
- **A ~4.5-wk counter barely moves the landing zone.** Midpoint of their 4 vs your 15
  is **~9.5 wk**; a 4.5-wk counter shifts it to **~9.75 wk** — a quarter-week. A stingy
  counter **costs a round, not the deal**, and by concession-rate signaling it
  telegraphs **their** limited flexibility, not bad faith ([PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/business-negotiations/business-skills-make-concessions-strategically-in-negotiation/)).
- **A lowball lengthens the timeline only if you chase it.** Compress instead:
  (1) **don't reciprocate with another big cut** — match their move size with a tiny
  decreasing step (e.g., 15 → 13–14); (2) **make them justify** the number ("walk us
  through the basis"), forcing budget-vs-principle into the open ([PON](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/dealing-with-difficult-people-daily/dealing-with-difficult-people-coping-with-an-insulting-offer/));
  (3) **trade cash for low-cost terms** via contingent concession — "your cash number
  only paired with layoff/not-for-cause + won't-contest UI + 4 mo COBRA + neutral
  reference"; (4) **set a "best and final" + ~7-day deadline**, then the demand-letter
  / EEOC-TWC charge held in reserve — a deadline **forces** decisions, it doesn't
  extend them ([Ruggles](https://ruggleslawfirm.com/boost-your-executive-severance-pay-demand-letter-tactics-that-work/)).
- **The claimant outlasts a lowball.** Not financially forced, replacement income late
  July, clean FMLA claim as fallback → the employer's open clean-liability exposure
  makes **delay their problem**. A 4.5-wk counter is an opening posture to outlast, not
  a trap.
- **Caveat:** the midpoint rule is a heuristic; the real ceiling is MNTN's internal
  **authorization**, invisible from outside. If authorization genuinely caps near the
  policy floor, no tactic conjures more — that is where the demand-letter/charge fork
  and a credible willingness to walk do the work.

## 10. Methodology & Limits

- **Probabilities are structural estimates, not calibrated odds.** They derive from
  the negotiation's observable shape (the 4↔15 wk gap, fast cadence, immaterial
  dollar stakes), the verified counsel/employer profiles, and general practitioner
  knowledge — **not** from verified settlement base-rate data.
- **Verified (cited, high confidence):** Beckstead/BTD profile; MNTN financials and
  no-material-litigation disclosure; the full set of Texas UI characterization and
  requalification mechanics.
- **NOT verified in this run (treat scenario dollar/probability figures as
  estimates):** FMLA-retaliation settlement/verdict ranges; 5th-Cir. plaintiff
  success and summary-judgment dismissal rates; liquidated-damages frequency;
  employer initial-to-final offer ratios; 5th-Cir. one-day temporal-proximity case
  law; litigation-cost-asymmetry figures; recently-IPO'd-employer settlement base
  rates; comparable FMLA fact-pattern settlements. These are the open questions below.
- **Dominant hidden variable:** MNTN's internal authorization ceiling — capacity to
  pay is proven, willingness is not knowable from public data.
- **Unverified leverage signals:** "manager disciplined" and "wants him back" are
  counsel's FRE-408 representations, not corroborated facts; they drive the upper-edge
  scenario only.
- **Source-quality note:** attorney-profile claims lean partly on Super Lawyers (a
  paid/peer-nominated service, used only to confirm selections occurred) but are
  corroborated by the primary State Bar directory and the firm's own bio. MNTN
  financials are as of Q3 2025; TWC max WBA is the Oct 2025 figure.
