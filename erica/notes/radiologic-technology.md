# Radiologic Technology and Fast Healthcare Income Options for Erica

_Research notes, 2026-10-13. Web sources were fetched on that date and treated as untrusted data. Lines marked **[Inference]** are my reasoning, not something a source states. Lines marked **[single-source]** rest on one source._

**Question:** Is radiologic technology (radiography) a realistic way for Erica to "make a little money" while she builds toward a full-time creative income in about 2 years? If not, what is faster?

**Short answer:** No. Radiography is a 2-year, full-time, selective career retraining. It is not a side income. Without biology coursework, Erica would also need about 1–2 semesters of prerequisites before she could even apply. That puts the first rad-tech paycheck roughly 2.5–3.5 years away, which is after her creative deadline. The details follow.

---

## 1. What it takes to become a radiographer in the US

### Credential and the degree question

- **ARRT degree rule:** To use the primary pathway, a candidate must earn an associate degree or higher. ["The degree doesn't have to be in the radiologic sciences"](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/initial-requirements/primary-requirements/education-requirements-primary). It can be earned before or after the radiography program, but it must be finished before the exam. The degree also has to come from an institution accredited by an agency ARRT recognizes ([ARRT](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/initial-requirements/primary-requirements/education-requirements-primary)).
  - **For Erica:** Her associate's in business **should satisfy the ARRT degree requirement**, provided her school was accredited by an ARRT-recognized (regionally or nationally recognized) accreditor. Some hospital programs set a stricter bar. For example, WellSpan "will only accept regionally accredited college degrees and credits" ([WellSpan catalog 2025–27](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/269a6006063e-wellspanxmcef9f-production4406-c241/media/Project/Wellspan-Site-Tenant/wellspan-site/data/media/files/WellSpan-Health-Catalog-2025-2027.pdf)). **She should check her school's accreditor.**
- **ARRT also requires** completing an ARRT-recognized radiography program (in practice, JRCERT-accredited), meeting an ethics requirement, and passing the exam. Candidates have a three-year window after finishing the program ([ARRT](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/initial-requirements/primary-requirements/education-requirements-primary)).
- **State licensure:** BLS says "most states require radiologic technologists to be licensed or certified," and employers typically require or prefer ARRT certification ([BLS OOH, modified Aug 27 2026](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
  - A handful of states have no licensure standards. Secondary sources disagree on which ones: lists include Alabama, Idaho, Missouri, Nevada, North Carolina, South Dakota and DC ([Diagnostic Imaging](https://www.diagnosticimaging.com/view/radiologic-technologist-requirements-vary-state); [AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/industry-news/regulatory/article/15555411/technologists-travel-to-washington-dc-in-bid-to-spur-on-licensing-bill)). **The lists conflict, so verify with the ASRT or the state board.**
  - **[Inference]** Employers want ARRT certification even in states without licensure, so this barely changes the path.

### Typical prerequisites (Erica currently has none of the science ones)

- **Ascension St. Vincent's** requires College Algebra, A&P I and II, Medical Terminology, English Comp I, Physics with lab, Intro to IT, plus gen-eds ([handbook](https://medicaleducation.ascension.org/florida/radiologic-technology/-/media/project/microsites/fl-st-vincent-radiologic-technology/jax-radiography-program/2024-2026-handbook.pdf)).
- **Mary Washington** requires English Comp, Digital Literacy, Quantitative Reasoning or higher math, Medical Terminology, and A&P I and II with lab ([MWH](https://www.marywashingtonhealthcare.com/education/school-of-radiologic-technology/program-admission/)).
- **WellSpan** requires College Algebra or higher, a communication course, 6 credits of natural science (A&P required), and Medical Terminology ([WellSpan](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/269a6006063e-wellspanxmcef9f-production4406-c241/media/Project/Wellspan-Site-Tenant/wellspan-site/data/media/files/WellSpan-Health-Catalog-2025-2027.pdf)).
- Many programs also require an entrance exam (TEAS or HESI), observation hours, an essay and an interview ([MWH](https://www.marywashingtonhealthcare.com/education/school-of-radiologic-technology/program-admission/); [CFCC](https://cfcc.edu/radiography/)).
- **[Inference]** A&P I and II are usually taken in sequence, so Erica would need about 2 semesters of prerequisites (plus math and physics if her business degree didn't include them) before she could apply. Most programs take one cohort a year, so missing a deadline costs a full year.

### How competitive admission is (real programs)

- **Portland CC (OR):** "accepts up to 38 out of 350+ applicants" (about 11%). The 2026 average prerequisite GPA was 3.62, and 500+ hours of healthcare experience is one criterion for the interview phase ([PCC](https://www.pcc.edu/programs/medical-imaging/apply/)).
- **Texas College (TCL) acceptance data:** 46 applications for 17 seats in Fall 2025 (37%), and 46 for 16 in Fall 2024 ([TCL PDF](https://www.tcl.edu/wp-content/uploads/RAD-Program-Acceptance-Data-2024-2026-v2.pdf)).
- **UW–La Crosse:** 23 applicants for 11 places in 2026 ([UWL](https://www.uwlax.edu/academics/department/health-professions/academic-programs/radiologic-technology/)).
- Most programs use point-based ranking. Pennsylvania College of Technology caps the class at 30 ([PCT](https://www.pct.edu/catalog/selective-admission-criteria-process/radiography)). Northern Kentucky takes about 24 a year and keeps no waitlist from one year to the next ([NKU](https://nku.catalog.acalog.com/preview_program.php?catoid=29&hl=%22rad%22&poid=4702)).
- **[Inference]** Acceptance runs from about 1 in 10 at large urban community colleges to about 1 in 2 at smaller programs. A career-changer with no science grades yet is not competitive until she has strong A&P grades.

### Cost (wide range)

- **Ascension St. Vincent's:** $7,500 total tuition plus about $1,300 in fees and books. It does not take FAFSA; students use private loans ([handbook](https://medicaleducation.ascension.org/florida/radiologic-technology/-/media/project/microsites/fl-st-vincent-radiologic-technology/jax-radiography-program/2024-2026-handbook.pdf)).
- **WellSpan:** $21,000 total plus about $1,500 in books ([WellSpan](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/269a6006063e-wellspanxmcef9f-production4406-c241/media/Project/Wellspan-Site-Tenant/wellspan-site/data/media/files/WellSpan-Health-Catalog-2025-2027.pdf)).
- **ARRT exam fee:** about $225 ([Ascension handbook](https://medicaleducation.ascension.org/florida/radiologic-technology/-/media/project/microsites/fl-st-vincent-radiologic-technology/jax-radiography-program/2024-2026-handbook.pdf)).
- **[Inference]** Prerequisite coursework at a community college adds roughly $1–3k. The bigger cost is about two years of very limited earnings.

### Can she do it part time, and can she earn during it?

- **Part time:** Rare. Bunker Hill and Sacred Heart have part-time tracks, but both still require daytime clinicals ([BHCC](https://catalog.bhcc.edu/preview_program.php?catoid=13&poid=1400&print=); [SHU](https://www.sacredheart.edu/majors--programs/radiography---as/)). Most programs, such as Cape Fear, are full-time day programs only ([CFCC](https://cfcc.edu/radiography/)).
- **Earning during the program:** Full-time programs run around 40 hours/week of class plus clinical ([Emory](https://med.emory.edu/departments/radiology/education/radiologic-technology-certificate-program/index.html)).
  - Students cannot be paid to take X-rays before they are credentialed. In some states, such as Maine, they "may not be employed to administer ionizing radiation" until certified and licensed (program handbook, surfaced via search; **[single-source]**).
  - Students also may not replace regular staff during clinicals ([UNMC](https://catalog.unmc.edu/allied-health-professions/radiography/radiography-student-employment)).
  - **[Inference]** Realistic income during the program is a 10–15 hour/week evening or weekend job. That is not "making money."

## 2. Pay, schedule and working conditions

### Pay and outlook (BLS, May 2025)

- **Radiologic technologists:** median **$80,110** a year. The middle half earn $64,810–$98,750; the 10th percentile earns $55,980 and the 90th earns $118,660 ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)). That works out to $38.52/hr ([Wagedex, which republishes OEWS May 2025](https://wagedex.com/jobs/radiologic-technologists-and-technicians)).
- **MRI technologists:** median $95,480 ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
- **Outlook:** Rad and MRI tech employment (278,100 jobs) is projected to grow 5% from 2025 to 2035, with about 15,300 openings a year ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
- **Shortage:** The ASRT 2025 staffing survey found a radiography vacancy rate of **15.6%**, down from 18.1% in 2023 but "at or near all-time highs." CT reached a record 19.4% ([ASRT, Jul 2025](https://www.asrt.org/news/article/2025/07/24/asrt-staffing-and-workplace-survey-shows-vacancy-rate-increases-near-record-highs-aligning-with-overall-health-care-profession-trends)).

### Schedules

- Most rad techs work full time. Because imaging is needed around the clock, some work evenings, weekends or overnight ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
- Job postings show a range of arrangements:
  - **Mayo Clinic (Eau Claire) PRN:** "part-time and not benefits eligible," $30.12–$45.19/hr, with weekend and holiday rotation ([Mayo job](https://jobs.mayoclinic.org/job/eau-claire/radiologic-technologist-prn/33647/67892791872)).
  - **Catholic Health (Buffalo):** a 0.6 FTE part-time role that is benefits eligible, $39.35–$49.64/hr ([search summary of posting](https://careers.aah.org/job/21315776); **[single-source]**, the posting itself was not fetched).
  - **Prisma Health urgent care:** 3×12 shifts, 8am–8pm, with benefits at 36+ hours/week ([via Indeed listing summary](https://www.indeed.com/q-Radiology-Tech-jobs.html); **[single-source]**).
- **Part-time benefits:** Hospitals commonly set benefits eligibility at about 20–30 scheduled hours/week. For example, Nantucket Cottage Hospital offers benefits to regular part-time staff scheduled 20+ hours ([NCH](https://nantuckethospital.org/?p=8220)). PRN/per diem roles generally carry no benefits.
- **[Inference]** For Erica, the attractive version would be a 0.5–0.6 FTE benefited role (for example, two or three 12-hour shifts a week), leaving 4–5 days for creative work. That is a strong setup, but only after she is credentialed.

### Physical, radiation and emotional load

- **Physical:** The job means long periods on her feet, lifting and turning patients ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
- **Radiation:** Exposure is "minimized by the use of protective lead aprons, gloves, and other shielding devices" ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
- **Patient-facing all day:** Every exam involves positioning and talking to a patient. **[Inference]** It is not solitary work, though most exams are one tech with one patient, sometimes with a second tech.
- **Trauma exposure:**
  - Research on radiographers who image trauma patients found "professional emotional saturation, desensitisation and vicarious traumatisation" ([Univ. of Johannesburg study](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/diagnostic-radiographers-lived-experiences-of-trauma-imaging/)).
  - Emergency-department radiographers scored significantly higher on emotional exhaustion and depersonalisation than non-ED colleagues (search summary; [JSEM listing](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/jsem/24/1/_contents/-char/en), **[single-source; not fetched]**).
  - **[Inference]** Outpatient, mammography or MRI-center roles reduce trauma exposure. Hospital ED and night shifts increase it.
- **Burnout:** Incredible Health's 2025 report (vendor survey, sample size not given) says rad techs report lower burnout than other technician groups, and only 50% feel underpaid versus 74% of all techs ([Applied Radiology summary](https://appliedradiology.com/articles/radiologic-technologists-navigate-ai-integration-and-workforce-challenges-with-optimism)). **[single-source, vendor survey]**
  - The same article claims "nearly 40% of rad techs are 65+." That is implausible for an active workforce, so I disregarded it.
  - I found no rigorous US burnout prevalence figure for technologists.
- **[Inference, Erica-specific]**
  - Clinical training means constant real-time evaluation by technologists and instructors, competency check-offs, and strict protocols. That is the kind of prescriptive, critical-feedback environment the profile says stresses her.
  - Repeated images are documented and dose matters, so mistakes are visible. Students get corrected all the time.
  - This could trigger the critical-parent dynamic. Once she is working, the work becomes routine and protocol-driven, which some anxious people find calming. Training is the hard part.

## 3. Career ladders that use her analytical side, and the art connection

- **CT and MRI after radiography (postprimary pathway):** These require ARRT registration first, 16 hours of structured education, and documented clinical repetitions. CT needs 125 procedures across 6 categories ([ARRT postprimary](https://arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/initial-requirements/postprimary-requirements/education-requirements-postprimary)).
  - MRI can also be entered directly through an MRI primary-pathway program ([ARRT MRI](https://arrt.org/pages/resources/earn-additional-credentials/earn-additional-credentials-primary/magnetic-resonance-imaging)). MRI median pay is $95,480 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)).
  - CT has the highest vacancy rate, 19.4% ([ASRT](https://www.asrt.org/news/article/2025/07/24/asrt-staffing-and-workplace-survey-shows-vacancy-rate-increases-near-record-highs-aligning-with-overall-health-care-profession-trends)).
  - **[Inference]** CT, MRI and 3D-reconstruction work are the most "technical and visual" parts of imaging.
- **Mammography and interventional** are also postprimary credentials (same ARRT postprimary page).
- **Sonography is a separate path:**
  - It needs its own program (associate or certificate).
  - Median pay is $96,590, and growth is projected at 14% for 2025–35 ([BLS sonographers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/diagnostic-medical-sonographers.htm)).
  - It involves no ionizing radiation, but it is intensely hands-on with patients and done in dim rooms.
  - **[Inference]** It is the most "artful" imaging modality, since the operator composes the image in real time. It is also just as long a commitment.
- **Imaging informatics / PACS:** The CIIP credential uses a point system based on experience, education and credentials, and requires more than 2 years of work experience ([O*NET CIIP](https://www.onetonline.org/link/certinfo/9624-C)).
  - **[Inference]** This is the most solitary, computer-based branch and could eventually be partly remote. It is a 3–5 year, post-radiography ladder, not an entry point.
- **Medical illustration:**
  - Most practitioners hold a master's from an accredited 2-year graduate program.
  - Applicants need a bachelor's degree, premedical science (A&P or comparative anatomy with dissection, general chemistry, upper-level biology) and an art portfolio ([Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_illustration); [UW–Madison prehealth](https://prehealth.wisc.edu/explore-healthcare/medical-illustration)).
  - **[Inference]** This is the true "science + art" career. Radiography does not lead to it. She would need a bachelor's plus science prerequisites. Rad-tech A&P coursework would overlap slightly.

### AI disruption risk: radiologist vs technologist

- **Radiologists (physicians):** Hinton's 2016 prediction that AI would replace radiologists has not come true. Radiologist pay and demand rose, with average compensation around $571k in 2025 per Medscape, as reported by Fortune ([Fortune, May 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/04/godfather-of-ai-geoffrey-hinton-radiologists-future-of-work-tech-ai-job-anxiety/)).
- **Technologists:** AI tools such as CT auto-positioning, scan-range planning and deep-learning reconstruction speed up the workflow. A 2025 European study concluded "the role of CT radiographers remains indispensable" for running and monitoring these tools ([ESR EuroSafe 2025 poster](https://epos.myesr.org/poster/esr/eurosafeimaging2025/ESI-14296)). 38% of rad techs report using AI clinically ([Applied Radiology](https://appliedradiology.com/articles/radiologic-technologists-navigate-ai-integration-and-workforce-challenges-with-optimism)).
- **[Inference]** Rad tech is among the more AI-resistant healthcare jobs, because the core task (physically positioning a person and operating the machine) is manual and patient-facing. The AI risk sits with interpretation (radiologists) and documentation jobs, not acquisition.

## 4. Genuinely fast "make a little money" alternatives

Figures are BLS May 2025 medians unless noted.

- **Phlebotomy**
  - **Pay:** $45,230 a year ($21.75/hr) ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm)).
  - **Training:** A certificate program usually takes less than a year, and some employers train on the job ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm)). Community-college programs run 13–16 weeks and cost about $1.2k–$4.7k (examples: Delaware Tech 13–16 weeks, $3,295; Waubonsee $1,154) ([search summary of state ETP listings](https://joblink.delaware.gov/etp/public/institution_programs/1685)).
  - **Outlook:** +7%.
  - **Nature of work:** Very patient-facing, often anxious patients, and hospital roles include nights and weekends ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm)).
- **EKG technician**
  - **Training:** Courses run 50–80 hours (about 6–8 weeks) and cost about $950–$2,265 (Calhoun CC $999; Metropolitan CC $2,265, six weeks) ([Calhoun](https://calhoun.edu/programs-training/career-training/healthcare-training/ekg-technician/); [MCC](https://www.mccneb.edu/programs/ekg-technician)).
  - **Pay caveat:** BLS's combined "cardiovascular technologists and technicians" median of $74,310 includes echo and invasive techs (via [tradecareerpath summary](https://tradecareerpath.com/trade-school/best-ekg-technician-programs/), **[secondary]**). **[Inference]** EKG-only techs earn far less, likely in the high teens to low $20s/hr. I did not verify this.
  - **Nature of work:** Patient-facing but brief encounters.
- **Sterile processing technician**
  - **Pay:** OEWS medical equipment preparers median $22.93/hr, $47,700 a year, May 2025 ([Wagedex, republishing OEWS](https://wagedex.com/jobs/medical-equipment-preparers)).
  - **Training:** Programs run about 14–16 weeks and cost about $2–4k (BMCC 16 weeks, $1,995). Full CRCST certification requires 400 hands-on hours ([BMCC](https://www.bmcc.cuny.edu/csp/); search summary of HSPA requirement).
  - **Nature of work:** **Not patient-facing.** It is back-of-house work in small teams, decontaminating and assembling surgical instrument trays. It is precise and protocol-driven, and usually comes with hospital benefits and evening or night shifts. **[Inference]** This is the closest match to her "alone or with one other person" preference among healthcare jobs, though the protocols are very prescriptive.
- **Pharmacy technician**
  - **Pay:** $45,750 a year ($22/hr) ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/pharmacy-technicians.htm)).
  - **Training:** A high school diploma plus on-the-job training or a short program. State rules vary.
  - **Outlook:** +6%.
  - **Nature of work:** Retail roles are customer-facing and high pressure. **[Inference]** Hospital pharmacy roles (compounding, filling) are more back-of-house.
- **Medical scribe**
  - **Pay:** About $14–$20/hr ([Lindy summary](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/what-is-a-healthcare-scribe), **[secondary]**).
  - **AI risk:** **High.** Ambient AI scribes are displacing the work, and the largest employers (ScribeAmerica, Augmedix) are moving to AI platforms ([JobZoneRisk](https://jobzonerisk.com/roles/medical-scribe), **[secondary]**). Not recommended.
- **Patient access / registration**
  - **Pay:** 2026 postings show $16–$31/hr depending on market (for example, Catholic Health Port Jefferson $21.94–$35.57; Shriners full time with day-one medical) ([Shriners posting](https://careers-shriners.icims.com/jobs/8742/patient-access-representative---full-time/job); search summary).
  - **Training:** None. **[Inference]** Her business associate's degree and any customer-service history make her immediately hireable. Time to first paycheck is about 2–6 weeks (the hiring process only).
  - **Nature of work:** Front-desk, customer-facing, and involves insurance verification and conflict.
- **Medical transcription:** $40,410, **projected −4%**, explicitly because of speech recognition and NLP ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-transcriptionists.htm)). It can be done from home, but it is a declining field. Not recommended.
- **Medical records / coding**
  - **Pay:** Medical records specialists earn $51,140, outlook +8% ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-records-and-health-information-technicians.htm)).
  - **[Inference]** Coding is the most remote-capable and solitary option. But entry-level coders struggle to land a first job without experience, and AI-assisted coding is advancing. It takes about 4–9 months to get certified (CPC/CCA). The AI risk is moderate and rising.
- **Non-healthcare:** I found nothing clearly better for her constraints that is quick and W-2 with benefits. **[Inference]** Large employers that offer benefits to part-timers (for example, some grocery chains, UPS, Starbucks) were out of scope and not researched here.

## 5. Opportunity cost against a 2-year creative ramp

- **Timeline [Inference]:**
  - Prerequisites (A&P I/II, math, physics): 2 semesters, roughly Jan–Dec 2027.
  - Apply in early 2028 for a fall 2028 cohort, if accepted on the first try.
  - Program: 24 months, ending around summer 2030.
  - ARRT exam, licensure and first rad-tech paycheck: around fall 2030.
  - **That is about 4 years out, and about 3 years even in the best case.** The creative goal is about 2 years out.
- **Can she build a creative portfolio during the program? [Inference]** Barely. A full-time program means about 40 hours/week of didactic and clinical time ([Emory](https://med.emory.edu/departments/radiology/education/radiologic-technology-certificate-program/index.html)), plus study and commuting. Clinical sites can be up to 60 miles away ([SHU](https://www.sacredheart.edu/majors--programs/radiography---as/)).
  - Most programs advise limiting outside work (secondary sources suggest 10–12 hours/week; **[single-source]**).
  - For someone whose fear shows up as procrastination, a high-stakes, externally graded program will claim the scarce discretionary energy first. Creative work would likely stall for 2 years.
- **[Inference]** The only way rad tech fits is in sequence, not in parallel. If the creative ramp stalls after 2 years and she wants a stable, well-paid, part-time-friendly fallback with benefits, then radiography (or sonography) is an excellent second career. A 0.5–0.6 FTE benefited role at about $38/hr, roughly $40k/yr part time, could fund creative work indefinitely.

## Bottom line

**Radiologic technology is a career pivot, not a "little money" option.**

- She would need about a year of science prerequisites she doesn't have, a competitive application (roughly 1 in 2 to 1 in 10 acceptance), then 2 years of full-time, unpaid, intensely supervised clinical training.
- The first rad-tech paycheck would arrive around 2030, after her 2-year creative deadline.
- The training would likely consume the energy her creative ramp needs.
- The clinical-evaluation culture runs straight into her sensitivity to criticism.

**When it would make sense [Inference]:**

1. **As Plan B after the creative ramp**, if by about late 2028 creative income isn't on track and she wants a durable, well-paid, AI-resistant job that supports a 2–3-shift week with benefits. At that point she could start A&P while still creating, with no prerequisite time lost.
2. **If she decides imaging itself is the destination.** In that case, CT, MRI or sonography are the more technical and visual endpoints. Imaging informatics is the most solitary.

**For income now:**

- **Patient access/registration** is the fastest benefited job (weeks, $0 training, uses her degree).
- **Sterile processing** is the best temperament fit in healthcare (about 4 months, about $2–4k, no patients, hospital benefits).
- Either one also gets her inside a hospital, where she could later use tuition assistance or observe imaging before committing to rad tech.
