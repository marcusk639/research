# Landscape: Voice, Audio, and Music Careers for Erica

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Scope:** Voice acting (commercial, character, games, e-learning, IVR), audiobook narration, podcast editing and production, audio post-production and dialogue editing, sound design, music for media (libraries, sync, game/film scoring), mixing and mastering, and science-adjacent audio (sonification, game audio implementation, audio description).
**Method:** Web search plus full reads of 9 primary or near-primary sources: BLS OOH, GameSoundCon 2025 survey, GameSoundCon 2026 jobs analysis, CISAC/PMP AI study, Publishers Weekly on Audible AI, the ElevenLabs earnings post, TechCrunch on Deezer, ppc.land's summary of the APA 2026 survey, and Wikipedia on the SAG-AFTRA strike.

**Conventions**

- **[Fact]** means the claim is cited to a source.
- **[Inference]** is my own judgment. Every fit judgment about Erica is an inference.
- **(single-source / unverified)** marks a claim I could only find in one place, or only on a low-authority aggregator.
- Rates from vendor blogs and rate guides describe what the market asks for. They are not measured earnings.

## 0. Cross-cutting context

**Government baseline [Fact].**

- BLS has updated to May 2025 wages and 2025–35 projections.
- Median pay for broadcast, sound, and video technicians overall is **$60,150**. Sound engineering technicians earn **$73,130**, and audio/video technicians earn **$58,100** ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm)).
- Employment is projected to grow **1%** from 2025 to 2035, which is slower than average. There are about **9,800 openings a year**, mostly to replace people who leave. Only **11%** of these workers are self-employed ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm)).
- Sound engineering is a small specialty: about 15,000 jobs, against 87,600 for audio/video techs ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm)).
- Music directors and composers earned a median of **$73,710** in May 2025 ($63,670 in May 2024), with 1% projected growth ([BLS OOH – Music Directors & Composers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/entertainment-and-sports/music-directors-and-composers.htm)).
- Caveat: BLS mostly counts employees. Freelance voice and music income is poorly captured.

**AI is the biggest variable. It hits each segment differently [Fact].**

- **Music:** a CISAC/PMP Strategy study (Dec 2024) projects that generative-AI music will make up about **60% of music-library revenues** and about 20% of streaming revenues by 2028. It puts **24% of music creators' revenues** and 21% of audiovisual creators' revenues at risk ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/cisacpmp-strategy-ai-study)). This is a forecast commissioned by a rights-holder body, so it is not neutral.
- **Music uploads:** fully AI-generated tracks rose from 10% of Deezer's daily uploads (Jan 2025) to 44% (about 75,000 a day, Apr 2026) ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/04/20/deezer-says-44-of-songs-uploaded-to-its-platform-daily-are-ai-generated/)), then passed 50% by June 2026 ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/ai-generated-music-surpasses-50-of-daily-uploads-deezer)). But AI music is only **1–3% of streams**, and 85% of those streams are flagged as fraudulent. In Deezer's survey, 97% of listeners could not tell AI music from human music ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/04/20/deezer-says-44-of-songs-uploaded-to-its-platform-daily-are-ai-generated/)).
- **Labels and AI companies have moved from lawsuits to licensing deals.** UMG settled with Udio in Oct 2025 and is partnering on a licensed platform ([Euronews](https://euronews.com/culture/2025/10/31/universal-music-group-and-ai-music-firm-udio-settle-lawsuit-and-announce-new-music-platfor)). Warner settled with Suno in Nov 2025 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/warner-music-settles-lawsuit-with-ai-music-startup-suno)).
  - [Inference] AI music generation is becoming legal and mainstream, not going away.
- **Audiobooks:**
  - Audible offers 100+ AI voices to publishers, plus AI translation in beta ([Publishers Weekly, May 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250519/97756-audible-expands-catalog-with-ai-narration-and-translation-services.html)).
  - Findaway/Spotify partnered with ElevenLabs so authors can self-narrate with a voice clone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/audible-is-expanding-its-ai-narrated-audiobook-library/)).
  - Yet APA data shows AI titles earned **0.03% of 2025 US audiobook revenue**. Only 16% of listeners have heard an AI title, and willingness to try AI narration **fell from 70% to 61%** in 2026 ([ppc.land summarizing APA/Edison, Jun 2026](https://ppc.land/audiobook-sales-hit-2-43-billion-in-2025-as-157-million-americans-tune-in/)).
  - The overall market grew 9% to **$2.43B** ([Infodocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/)).
- **Voice buyers:** Voices' 2025 Audio Trends Report found that only **26%** of buyers had tried an AI voice, and 70% of those used it on fewer than a quarter of projects ([Voices/Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/view/content/over-half-of-brands-need-multilingual-voice-over-voices-annual-voice-22479778)). Voices is a marketplace that sells human talent, so it has an interest in this result.
- **Union protections:**
  - The SAG-AFTRA video-game strike ran from 26 Jul 2024 to 9 Jul 2025. It ended with a contract ratified by 95%. The contract requires consent and disclosure for digital replicas, lets performers suspend consent during strikes, raises pay about 15% immediately, and adds 3% a year after that ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/video-games-strike-ends-after-11-months-as-sag-aftra-ratifies-new-contract)).
  - Performances recorded for real-time AI generation, such as AI chatbot characters, are paid at **at least 7.5× scale** ([Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di)).
  - The 2025 Commercials contracts bar AI training on a performer's work without consent and set minimum payments when a replica is used ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-board-approves-commercials-contracts-prevent-ai/)).
  - Critics said the long strike pushed work to non-union and non-US talent ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike)).
  - [Inference] Union rules protect union work. Most entry-level work Erica could reach is non-union, and it has no such protection.
- **A new income stream: licensing your own AI voice.** ElevenLabs says voice creators have earned **more than $22M** in total, across 10,400+ creators, as of Sep 2026 ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/22-million-earned-by-voice-creators-on-elevenlabs)).
  - [Inference] That averages about $2,100 per creator over the platform's life. The site shows standout cases only, not the median, so for most people this is pocket money, not a living.

## 1. Commercial and character voice acting (games, animation, ads, e-learning, IVR/corporate)

**What the work is.** Recording auditions and booked jobs in a home booth, often with a client directing remotely. Volume work is mostly corporate, explainer, and e-learning scripts. Character work (games and animation) is more performative but rarer.

**Pay [Fact].** The GVAA non-union guide, as reported second-hand, gives these ranges ([VoiceCrafters, citing GVAA](https://www.voicecrafters.com/?p=140657)):

- e-learning: **$30–55 per finished minute** (or $0.20–0.35 per word), with a $350–450 minimum
- IVR/on-hold: **$50–200 per finished minute**, with a $100–200 minimum

I did not verify these against the GVAA PDF itself. Game composers and voice are not covered by BLS.

**Demand and outlook [Fact].** Brands report a growing need for multilingual voice-over ([Voices](https://www.newswire.com/view/content/over-half-of-brands-need-multilingual-voice-over-voices-annual-voice-22479778)).

- Corporate, e-learning, and IVR narration is widely described as the segment AI is displacing fastest ([Outspoken Voices](https://www.outspokenvoices.com/blog/the-state-of-the-voice-over-industry-in-2025-trends-and-insights); [JobZoneRisk](https://jobzonerisk.com/roles/voice-actor)). These are blog and aggregator sources, so treat this as directionally plausible but **unverified** as a statistic.
- One figure says "65% can't tell AI from human in e-learning." It traces back to a Voices report via secondary blogs, so it is **single-source / unverified**.

**How work is obtained without self-promotion [Fact].**

- **Agents** submit talent to jobs for a 10% commission, but they generally want an established demo and track record.
- **Pay-to-play sites** cost about $395 a year and offer 10–20 auditions a week. Booking rates are **about 1 in 100**, with jobs worth $75–$2,500 ([Gravy for the Brain](https://www.gravyforthebrain.com/?p=1397)). This is an older, vendor-authored source.
- Auditioning on posted jobs is not cold calling. But it is relentless, high-rejection pitching.

**AI risk [Inference from the facts above].**

- e-learning, IVR, and explainer work: **high**
- commercial: **medium**
- union game and animation work: **medium-low**, but slow to break into

**Entry and time to competence [Inference].** A competitive commercial or character demo plus a treated booth typically takes 6–18 months. Steady full-time booking usually takes years.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** Her acting talent and equipment fit well. But the business model is high-volume auditioning, and with a booking rate around 1%, she would face constant rejection. Clients deliver tight, prescriptive direction ("read it exactly like this, by tomorrow"). The most profitable route, agent rosters and union work, rewards networking and visibility. Both the rejection and the direction style press on her stated stressors. **Moderate–low** as a primary path. **Good** as a supplementary stream, especially character work.

## 2. Audiobook narration (ACX/Audible, publishers, Findaway/Spotify)

**What the work is.** Solo, long-form performance: prepping the manuscript, voicing characters, recording in a home booth. The narrator often edits and masters too, unless the publisher handles post-production.

**Pay [Fact].**

- ACX non-union jobs average about **$165 per finished hour (PFH)**. Union jobs pay **$250–450 PFH**, and the SAG-AFTRA minimum on ACX is about $250 ([Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-much-do-audiobook-narrators-make-76704/); [Backstage PFH](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/pfh-audiobook-rates-explained-76681/)).
- One finished hour takes **about 6–10 hours** of total work ([Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-much-do-audiobook-narrators-make-76704/)).
- ACX royalty-share deals split royalties 50/50 with no upfront pay ([Backstage PFH](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/pfh-audiobook-rates-explained-76681/)).
- [Inference] At $165 PFH and 8 hours per finished hour, effective pay is about $20/hr early on. Using a separate editor, or moving to publisher work at $250+ PFH, roughly doubles that.

**Demand and outlook [Fact].** The US market reached $2.43B in 2025, up 9%, with 750,000+ active titles ([ppc.land/APA](https://ppc.land/audiobook-sales-hit-2-43-billion-in-2025-as-157-million-americans-tune-in/)). Only about 2–5% of all books exist in audio ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250519/97756-audible-expands-catalog-with-ai-narration-and-translation-services.html)).

**AI risk.**

- [Fact] On the supply side the threat is real: 100+ Audible AI voices, AI translation, and ElevenLabs/Spotify voice cloning ([PW](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250519/97756-audible-expands-catalog-with-ai-narration-and-translation-services.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/audible-is-expanding-its-ai-narrated-audiobook-library/)).
- [Fact] On the demand side AI is negligible: 0.03% of revenue, with declining listener willingness ([APA via ppc.land](https://ppc.land/audiobook-sales-hit-2-43-billion-in-2025-as-157-million-americans-tune-in/)).
- [Inference] Expect AI to take low-budget indie, backlist, and nonfiction titles. It looks likely to keep human narrators for fiction, especially multi-character genre fiction, for the 2-year horizon. That makes the risk **medium**.

**How work is obtained without self-promotion [Fact and inference].**

- ACX lets narrators audition on posted titles, and authors and publishers can browse narrator profiles (Backstage, above).
- Publishers keep narrator rosters and cast from demos. [Inference] Once she is on a roster, work comes to her without marketing. Genre fiction (romance, fantasy) has heavy, repeat demand.

**Entry and time to competence [Inference].** 3–9 months to a credible sample and a booth that meets ACX specs. One to two years of ACX credits before publisher rosters are realistic.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** **Strong.**

- Combines her voice and acting talents
- Works alone, with nobody watching, using the gear she already owns
- The brief is a book, not a prescriptive script, so she has interpretive freedom
- Long deadlines (weeks) rather than "by Friday"

Risks:

- Income ramps slowly
- Procrastination on long solo projects needs a structure: daily finished-minute targets
- Full-time within 2 years probably requires publisher roster work, or narrating plus editing for other narrators

## 3. Podcast editing and production

**What the work is.** Cleaning dialogue (noise, filler words, pacing), editing for story, mixing and mastering, and sometimes show notes and producing.

**Pay [Fact].**

- Market rates run about **$50–100 per episode** at the budget end, **$150–350** mid-tier, and **$350–600** for senior or specialist editors ([The Podcast Consultant](https://thepodcastconsultant.com/blog/podcast-editing-services); vendor source).
- AIR publishes annual benchmarks for independent audio producers and editors ([Inside Radio on AIR's guide](https://www.insideradio.com/free/how-much-should-freelancers-earn-air-s-new-guide-offers-benchmarks-for-creators/article_40a3f642-f27c-11ef-a6af-2f5b339bc069.html)). I could not retrieve the full guide (HTTP 403).

**AI risk [Fact and inference].** Tools like Descript, Adobe Podcast Enhance, and Auphonic now handle noise reduction, leveling, and filler-word removal in seconds. One estimate says editing time drops from 3–4 hours per recorded hour to 30–45 minutes ([MillionPodcasts](https://www.millionpodcasts.com/blog/?p=5206)); this is a blog source, so **unverified**.

- [Inference] Mechanical cleanup is being commoditized, so budget per-episode work is shrinking. Story editing and narrative producing for shows with real budgets is less exposed. Overall risk: **medium-high** for cleanup-only work, **medium** for narrative editing.

**How work is obtained without self-promotion [Inference].**

- Hiring for networks and production companies (Pushkin-style houses, agencies) and contractor rosters
- Freelance platforms where clients post jobs
- Repeat clients: one podcast typically means weekly recurring work

**Entry and time to competence [Inference].** 3–6 months to basic competence. Narrative editing takes 1–2 years.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** **Good.** It is analytical and craft-based, behind the scenes, and solitary or one-on-one with a host. Weekly recurring deadlines are predictable, not arbitrary. The downsides are a lower ceiling and AI pressure at the low end.

## 4. Audio post-production and dialogue editing (audiobook post, film/TV, games)

**What the work is.** Editing, proofing, and mastering recorded voice. This includes audiobook QC, which means checking audio against the text. In film and TV it includes dialogue editing and ADR prep.

**Pay [Fact].** In audiobook post-production, editing and mastering pay about **$90–150 PFH**, and proofing pays **$30–75 PFH** ([Travsonic](https://www.travsonic.com/the-complete-guide-to-audiobook-production-budgeting/); vendor). ACX guidance splits a finished hour into roughly $200 for narration and $200 for post-production (same source, **unverified**). Publishers post freelance roles, for example an [IPG job board posting for a Freelance Audiobook Editing/Mastering Engineer](https://independentpublishersguild.com/IPG/Jobs_Board/Job-posts/Freelance-Audiobook-Editing---Mastering-Engineer.aspx). Sound engineering technicians have a BLS median of $73,130 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm)).

**AI risk [Inference].** **Medium.** AI cleanup speeds the work up, but someone must still check the audio against the text and meet specs, and AI-narrated books still need QC. Human post-production also rises with human narration volume, which is growing (see Section 2).

**How work is obtained without self-promotion [Fact and inference].** Production houses and publishers recruit freelance editors and proofers onto rosters through posted listings (IPG example above). [Inference] This is applying to openings, not pitching. Once on a roster, assignments flow in.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** **Strong.** It is detail-oriented and analytical, invisible, solitary, and remote. Low barrier to entry: a DAW, her existing equipment, and an ACX-spec test. The pressure comes from spec compliance, not taste-policing. It also pairs naturally with narration (Section 2), which raises income per book.

## 5. Sound design and game audio implementation (Wwise/FMOD)

**What the work is.** Creating sound effects and ambiences, then **implementing** them in the game engine. Implementation means setting up Wwise or FMOD events, parameters, and scripting. This is where the "analytical + science" side comes in.

**Pay [Fact].**

- GameSoundCon 2025 (n=631): US/Canada salaried sound designers average **$124,277** (median $100,621). Recent hires (0–2 years) average **$63,457**. US freelancers have a median of **$58,800** ([GameSoundCon 2025](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/gamesoundcon-game-audio-industry-survey-2025)).
- 2026 postings: mid-level sound designer about **$114K**, senior about $140K. These skew to California and Washington, where salary disclosure is required ([GameSoundCon 2026 jobs](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/game-audio-jobs-and-skills-2026-what-employers-are-looking-for)).

**Demand and outlook [Fact].**

- In 142 postings (Apr–Jul 2026), **Wwise appeared in 58%** and implementation in 57%.
- Only **6.3%** of roles were entry-level, and **68%** were on-site. Most required 3–5 years of experience. Collaboration was listed in 77% ([GameSoundCon 2026](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/game-audio-jobs-and-skills-2026-what-employers-are-looking-for)).
- 16% of respondents were laid off in the past year. On average it takes **1.2 years** to land a first job after graduating ([GameSoundCon 2025](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/gamesoundcon-game-audio-industry-survey-2025)).
- Audio outsourcing companies now employ **14.7%** of game audio workers, up from 5% in 2016 (same source). [Inference] These firms are a more remote-friendly route in.

**AI risk [Fact].** Only **8%** of game-audio professionals use generative AI, mostly for placeholder dialogue ([GameSoundCon 2025](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/gamesoundcon-game-audio-industry-survey-2025)). [Inference] For implementation work the risk is **low-medium**: engine integration is hard to automate.

**How work is obtained [Inference].** Mostly through employers and outsourcing studios that hire via job postings and portfolios. Some networking is expected, though not social-media self-promotion.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** **High on interest, low on the 2-year timeline.**

- It suits creative-plus-technical thinkers and pays best.
- But 80% of practitioners have a bachelor's degree or higher ([GameSoundCon 2025](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/gamesoundcon-game-audio-industry-survey-2025)).
- Entry-level openings are scarce, most roles are on-site and team-based, and sprint deadlines are tight.
- Consider it a 3–5 year stretch track, entered through an outsourcing studio.

## 6. Music for media: production libraries, sync, scoring for games, film, and ads

**What the work is.** Writing and producing tracks to a genre brief (libraries), or custom scoring to picture or game.

**Pay [Fact].**

- Library deals: exclusive deals typically split upfront fees and backend income 50/50. Non-exclusive deals often pay the writer 25% of the upfront fee plus 100% of the writer's share. Some exclusive libraries pay $250–1,000 per track upfront ([Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/music-sync-libraries); [Music Connection](https://musicconnection.com/?p=100014)). These are secondary sources.
- Game composers: freelance rates average **$1,871/min (AAA)** and **$618/min (indie)**, with a $407 indie median ([GameSoundCon 2025](https://www.gamesoundcon.com/post/gamesoundcon-game-audio-industry-survey-2025)).

**AI risk [Fact].** Very high for library and background music.

- CISAC projects AI will take about 60% of library revenues by 2028 ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/cisacpmp-strategy-ai-study)).
- Epidemic Sound went "AI-first" in 2025, launching tools like Adapt, while growing only 3% and running a negative adjusted EBITDA margin ([Epidemic Group](https://corporate.epidemicsound.com/press-and-media/press-releases/2026/epidemic-group-publishes-2025-annual-and-sustainability-report-a-transformative-year-investing-significantly-into-ai-and-bringing-the-company-back-to-profitable-growth-in-2026/)).
- AI tracks flood streaming services, and 97% of listeners cannot tell them apart ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/04/20/deezer-says-44-of-songs-uploaded-to-its-platform-daily-are-ai-generated/)).
- [Inference] Custom scoring, where a director wants a human collaborator, is safer, but it is highly relationship-driven.

**How work is obtained [Inference].** Library submission is the most self-promotion-free path in this whole report: submit catalogs, no pitching, no client in the room. But income builds slowly over years and is now under AI price pressure. Scoring work depends on networking and reputation.

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** Library writing is a **good emotional fit** (open-ended, solitary, no client micromanagement) but a **poor economic bet** as a primary 2-year income. Keep it as a side or creative outlet and a long-tail asset.

## 7. Mixing and mastering (music)

**What the work is.** Balancing and finishing other artists' tracks.

**Pay and outlook [Fact].** BLS sound engineering technicians: $73,130 median, with the specialty flat or slightly declining ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm)). LANDR's AI mastering serves 5M+ creators, at about $9 for four tracks ([Loop Fans](https://music.loop.fans/ai/landr-ai), **single-source**).

**AI risk [Inference].** High at the budget end.

**How work is obtained [Inference].** Mostly through artist referrals and an online presence. Clients give subjective, iterative feedback ("make it punchier").

**Fit with Erica [Inference].** **Low–moderate.** The independent-client model leans on reputation and self-marketing, and the feedback is subjective and revision-heavy.

## 8. Science-adjacent audio

**Sonification.** NASA's Chandra X-ray Center has run a sustained sonification program since 2020 with SYSTEM Sounds (astrophysicist and musician Matt Russo plus sound engineer Andrew Santaguida) ([Chandra](https://chandra.cfa.harvard.edu/sound/listen.html)). [Inference] This is a tiny niche of a few practitioners and project grants. It is a wonderful **portfolio or passion project** that shows off creative-plus-science skills, but not a 2-year income source.

**Audio description (AD) narration and production.**

- The FCC is phasing AD requirements into 10 more TV markets each year through 2035 ([Verbit](https://verbit.ai/general/fcc-expands-audio-description-requirements-to-10-additional-markets-in-2025); [Wiley Law](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Expands-Audio-Description-Requirements-to-All-Television-Markets)).
- AD roles include writers, narrators, mixers, and QC specialists. Google research documents AD professionals' views on AI ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/made-by-people-described-by-people-audio-description-professionals-work-practices-and-perspectives-on-ai/)).
- The pay range sometimes cited ($40–80/hr freelance) comes from a career aggregator, so it is **unverified**.
- [Inference] AD narration is a neutral, precise voice role inside AD vendor rosters. It is behind the scenes, mission-driven, and steady, and it fits voice plus analytical skills. AI-voiced AD is an emerging threat.

**Audio QA and dataset work [Inference, not researched in depth].** Evaluating TTS and AI-music output, and recording voice datasets, exist as contract work. Its rate stability and ethics are uncertain. Worth a follow-up.

**Deprioritized [Inference]:**

- **Commercial/e-learning/IVR voice-over:** the ~1% booking ratio means constant rejection, the work is the most exposed to AI, and the best routes depend on agents and networking.
- **Freelance mixing and mastering:** reputation-marketing driven and squeezed by AI.

**Suggested 2-year sequencing [Inference]:**

1. **Months 0–6:** learn the DAW and ACX specs. Take paid proofing and editing work to build income and confidence on low-stakes, spec-based tasks. Record narration samples.
2. **Months 6–18:** narrate ACX titles (paid PFH where possible) and apply to publisher narrator and editor rosters and AD vendors.
3. **Months 18–24:** consolidate onto 2–3 roster relationships to reach full-time volume. Keep library music or a sonification project as the creative side.
