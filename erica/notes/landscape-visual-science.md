## 1. Scientific & medical illustration

**Day-to-day work.** Medical illustrators work with scientists and physicians to turn complex technical information into accurate visuals for education, research, patient care and marketing ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)). AMI says the field suits "detail-oriented individuals who genuinely enjoy and have natural ability in both art and science" who "enjoy working alone and in teams" ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)). The broader scientific-illustration field (natural science, botanical) works for museums, zoos, publishers, media outlets, animation firms and independent studios ([GNSI Careers](https://www.gnsi.org/careers)). A staff journal illustrator role at Cell Press, for example, is "primarily ... vector-based figures in Adobe Illustrator": drafting figures, refining them with editors and authors, and delivering finals, all under "frequent and demanding journal deadlines" ([Elsevier/Cell posting, 2026](https://www.tymblhub.com/scientific-illustrator-jobs-in-chennai-in-elsevier-2-to-6-years-jid-564565)).

**Pay.**

- AMI's 2022 survey puts the US median salary for a medical illustrator or animator at **$83,500** (range up to $170,000). The median gross income for self-employed illustrators is **$85,000**, and about 32% of salaried illustrators also freelance ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)). **[Dated: 2022 survey]**. These figures reflect AMI members, most of whom hold the accredited master's degree.
- The 2022 AMI pricing report had a 58% response rate (384 of 661 members) ([AMI 2022 Pricing & Business Practices Report](https://ami.memberclicks.net/assets/Committees/ProfessionalGuidelines/2022_AMI_Pricing_BusinessPractices_Report.pdf)).
- Non-accredited scientific-illustrator pay is less documented. I found no association salary survey for GNSI-type work, which is a gap.

**Demand / outlook.** The field is small: AMI estimates about 2,000 practitioners in North America ([ExploreHealthCareers, citing AMI](https://explorehealthcareers.org/careers/allied-health-professions/medical-illustrator-animator/)) **[Dated]**. AMI names growth areas in animation, interactive/UX, AR/VR, medical-legal work and research-lab molecular visualization ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)). In 2026, BioRender, a company that automates science figures, posted remote roles to build "a dedicated team of formally trained medical illustrators" ([BioRender posting via Dynamite Jobs](https://dynamitejobs.com/company/biorender/remote-job/medical-illustrator-animator)). Wirestock posted remote, entry-level freelance scientific illustrator work producing images for AI training data ([Wirestock posting, Oct 2026](https://www.designremotejobs.com/job/wirestock-freelance-scientific-illustrator-remote)).

**Entry barriers.**

- _Medical (accredited) route:_ There are five CAAHEP-accredited master's programs: Augusta, UIC, Johns Hopkins, RIT and Toronto. Each is two years and admits 4–16 students a year ([AMI Graduate Programs](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/education/graduate-programs/); [JHU MBI Admissions](https://medicalart.johnshopkins.edu/admissions-mbi/)). Johns Hopkins requires "pre-med" level science: general chemistry, vertebrate anatomy with dissection lab, vertebrate physiology, and one upper-level biology course. It also requires a realistic-rendering portfolio ([JHU MBI Admissions](https://medicalart.johnshopkins.edu/admissions-mbi/)).
- _Scientific/natural-science route (shorter):_ CSU Monterey Bay offers a certificate of 9 months of in-person study plus an internship, about 12–15 months in total. Past internship hosts include the Smithsonian, Scientific American and the Monterey Bay Aquarium ([CSUMB Science Illustration](https://csumb.edu/scienceillustration/)). RISD (3 years, part-time) and Yale Peabody also run certificate programs ([GNSI-NE program list](https://gnsi-ne.org/scientific-illustration-programs/)).
- Staff roles outside the US sometimes accept "a diploma or degree either in graphic design/scientific illustration, or in science ... with prior scientific illustration experience" ([NUS Mechanobiology Institute posting](https://jobsingapore247.com/scientific-illustrator-mechanobiology-institute-job303920)).

**Remote-friendliness.** Mixed. BioRender is "remote-first" ([BioRender](https://dynamitejobs.com/company/biorender/remote-job/medical-illustrator-animator)). Biotic Artlab hires contract illustrators remotely ([Biotic Artlab](https://biotic.bato.dev/jobs/medical-illustrator-contract/)). University and museum staff roles are often on site. AMI's 2022 survey comments mention "finding remote work" as a challenge ([AMI 2022 report](https://ami.memberclicks.net/assets/Committees/ProfessionalGuidelines/2022_AMI_Pricing_BusinessPractices_Report.pdf)).

**Getting work without self-promotion.** Salaried roles come through the AMI job board ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)) and through university, museum and publisher postings. Studios hire contractors through their art directors. AMI states plainly that _self-employed_ success requires "marketing and self-promotion" ([AMI Careers](https://ami.org/medical-illustration/enter-the-profession/careers/)). So the employed route is the one that fits her.

**AI-disruption risk: moderate-low for accuracy-critical work, higher for generic imagery.**

- University of Toronto BMC faculty testing image generators in 2025 found the results "confident yet highly inaccurate" and limited for novel phenomena ([U Toronto BMC, Mar 2025](https://bmc.med.utoronto.ca/news-events/2025/3/4/ai-in-mi-catastropportunity)).
- AMI's copyright comment warns that public generators "flood the market with acceptable, albeit inaccurate, imagery that can displace the demand for highly trained artists" ([AMI NOI comments](https://ami.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AMI_Comments_NOI-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf)).
- A 2024 review in a peer-reviewed journal expects AI to become "another tool" while noting "appreciable angst" ([Miller 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10887458/)).

**Fit [Inference].**

- **Strong** on art + science, solitude, and behind-the-scenes work.
- The briefs are tight (deadlines, accuracy). However, the standard is an _external, checkable truth_ (anatomy, data) rather than a client's taste. That may feel less like being judged as a person. This is speculative.
- Her analytical side is used directly.
- The accredited medical route (prerequisites plus a 2-year master's) likely **fails the 2-year income target** unless she already has the science courses.
- The certificate or self-built-portfolio route into scientific/journal/science-communication staff roles is plausible in about 2 years. Openings are scarce.

## 2. Data visualization / information design

**Day-to-day work.** Respondents to the DVS 2024 survey spend the most time "producing visualizations" and cleaning or preparing data ([DVS State of the Industry 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). Job titles span analyst, designer, developer, cartographer, journalist and researcher ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). Example postings:

- A remote healthcare "Senior Data Visualization Designer" sets dashboard design standards and is "not primarily responsible for developing dashboards or performing data analysis" ([posting, Oct 2026](https://remoterush.mysmartpros.com/job/4094787)).
- A remote CDC-contract specialist turns public-health analyses into "publication-ready graphs, tables, charts" ([posting, Oct 2026](https://wfhbeacon.up.railway.app/job/data-visualization-specialist-remote)).

**Pay.**

- DVS 2024 median annual pay: **$80,000–$99,999**. Median hourly: **$60–74**. The hourly median is lower for women, at $45–59 ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). The sample is self-selected (763 complete responses from 58 countries).
- Postings: $65K–$90K for a remote specialist ([WFH Beacon](https://wfhbeacon.up.railway.app/job/data-visualization-specialist-remote)), $80K–$110K for a remote federal-contract role ([WFH Beacon](https://wfhbeacon.up.railway.app/job/remote-data-visualization-specialist-analytics-reporting)), and $122K–$166K for a senior remote healthcare role needing 5+ years ([RemoteRush](https://remoterush.mysmartpros.com/job/4094787)).

**Demand / outlook.** The share of respondents for whom data visualization is the _primary_ focus rose from 30% (2020) to 42% (2024). About 3 in 4 work as employees and about 1 in 5 freelance ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). BLS has no single occupation for this work, so the outlook is not quantified. That is a gap.

**Entry barriers.** There is no licensing. Postings ask for a bachelor's degree in design, data, communications or a related field, "or 4 years of relevant experience in lieu of education," plus a portfolio ([RemoteRush](https://remoterush.mysmartpros.com/job/4094787)). The tools most used are Excel, Tableau, Python, Figma, R, Power BI, D3 and Illustrator ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). Respondents say the most useful way to learn is to "work through a project" (85%) ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). **[Inference]** Reaching an entry-level portfolio in Tableau or Figma plus Illustrator looks feasible in about 12–18 months of study. No source tested this.

**Remote-friendliness: high.** 49% of DVS respondents work fully remote, 39% hybrid and 11% on site ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)).

**Getting work without self-promotion.** The 76% who work as employees ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)) get roles through job boards and applications. All three example postings were standard online applications.

**AI-disruption risk: moderate, currently augmenting.** In the DVS 2024 survey, 37% used AI in their data-visualization work, up 13 points from 2023. Most used it for data prep, coding help or ideation rather than final visuals ([DVS 2024](https://www.datavisualizationsociety.org/soti-report-2024)). One 2026 product-design posting explicitly wants "experience using AI to expedite design processes" ([Northbeam posting](https://remoshift.com/job/senior-ux-visual-designer-a12515db)).

**Fit [Inference].** **Very strong** on the creative-plus-analytical blend, remote work, behind-the-scenes work and employed income, and it fits a 2-year target. The briefs are problem-driven: "make this data clear" leaves room for her own design judgment. The risk is that some roles are mostly dashboard plumbing (Power BI and SQL), which may bore her. She should target "designer" and "information design" roles over "analyst" ones. A science or health niche (public health, research institutes) connects to her science interest.

## 3. Art conservation & restoration (and the technician route)

**Day-to-day work.** Conservators examine, analyze, stabilize and treat cultural objects ([WUDPAC](https://www.udel.edu/academics/colleges/cas/units/departments/art-conservation/graduate-programs/art-conservation-ms/)). Technicians do housing, mat cutting, mending, cleaning and basic stabilization under a conservator. One example covers "custom housing," "basic stabilization," and "manual dexterity ... with precision" ([Newberry Library posting, 2026](https://jobs-in.us/conservation-technician_chicago-c429951/2026-08-newberry-library_i4174831940)). Another lists cleaning, tape and stain removal, and mending tears at the National Library of Medicine ([Computercraft/NLM posting](https://computercraft.applytojob.com/apply/1Ieoz97Mp4/Library-Conservation-Technician)).

**Pay.**

- BLS reports a median of **$51,440** for museum technicians and conservators in May 2025, and **$60,330** for the broader archivists, curators and museum workers group ([BLS OOH, live fetch](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/curators-museum-technicians-and-conservators.htm)).
- Technician postings: UNC $50,280–$56,419 ([UNC](https://library.unc.edu/job-postings/conservation-technician/)); NLM contractor $60,000–$70,000 ([Computercraft](https://computercraft.applytojob.com/apply/1Ieoz97Mp4/Library-Conservation-Technician)); San Francisco Library Conservation Technician I $88,088–$107,068 for 2026 ([SF classification 3620](https://careers.sf.gov/classifications/?classCode=3620)), which is a high-cost-of-living outlier.

**Demand / outlook.** The broader museum-workers group is projected to grow 4% over 2025–35, with about 4,300 openings a year ([BLS OOH, live](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/curators-museum-technicians-and-conservators.htm)). The earlier snapshot showed 6% for 2024–34, which BLS has since revised. Only 15,700 people held museum technician and conservator jobs in 2024 ([BLS snapshot](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/curators-museum-technicians-and-conservators.htm)), so the field is small.

**Entry barriers.**

- _Conservator:_ "typically need a master's degree," with graduate programs lasting "2 to 4 years," including an internship ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/curators-museum-technicians-and-conservators.htm); [AIC Graduate Programs](https://www.culturalheritage.org/careers/become-a-conservator/graduate-programs)). Prerequisites usually include a year each of general and organic chemistry with lab, 4–6 art history and anthropology courses, studio art courses, and a dexterity portfolio ([AIC Pre-Program](https://www.culturalheritage.org/careers/become-a-conservator/pre-program)). Winterthur/Delaware requires **400 hours** of supervised conservation experience and a chemistry assessment ([WUDPAC](https://www.udel.edu/academics/colleges/cas/units/departments/art-conservation/graduate-programs/art-conservation-ms/)). NYU, by contrast, "actively discourage[s]" unpaid pre-program internships ([NYU IFA](https://ifa.nyu.edu/study/ms-ma-admission.html)). Admission is "highly competitive" ([AIC](https://www.culturalheritage.org/careers/become-a-conservator/graduate-programs)).
- _Technician:_ AIC calls the technician role "itself ... a rewarding career path" as well as a feeder into graduate school ([AIC Pre-Program](https://www.culturalheritage.org/careers/become-a-conservator/pre-program)). Requirements vary: Harvard Art Museums says "relevant education beyond high school is desirable" ([Harvard posting, 2026](https://careers.harvard.edu/job/conservation-technician-in-cambridge-ma-united-states-jid-1019)), while the Newberry requires a bachelor's degree plus book or paper arts training ([Newberry](https://jobs-in.us/conservation-technician_chicago-c429951/2026-08-newberry-library_i4174831940)).

**Remote-friendliness: none.** All the work is on site and hands-on, for example "Fully On-[site]" at Harvard and "exclusively onsite" at the Newberry. Relocation is required.

**Getting work without self-promotion.** Through postings on the AIC Career Center and Global Conservation Forum and on institution sites ([AIC](https://www.culturalheritage.org/careers/become-a-conservator/pre-program)). AIC notes that pre-program spots are "often arranged by contacting a conservation lab," which is a form of outreach but not cold sales.

**AI-disruption risk: low.** The work is physical treatment of unique objects. I found no 2024–26 evidence of displacement **[Inference from nature of work; no direct source]**.

**Fit [Inference].**

- **Strong** on solitude (lab work, one supervisor), behind-the-scenes work, painting skill, the science interest (chemistry) and low AI risk.
- Instructions are prescriptive ("as assigned"), but the work is methodical, with a different emotional texture than creative-taste approval.
- The full conservator route fails the 2-year target. The technician route can work in about 2 years for income in the $50–70K range if she relocates.
- She should check whether she has the chemistry prerequisites or a degree. Erica's education history is not in the brief.

## 4. Natural-history museum preparation (fossil prep) & paleoart

**Day-to-day work.** Mechanical and chemical fossil prep (air scribes), molding and casting, documentation, some fieldwork, and often public outreach ([Princeton posting, 2026](https://research-princeton.icims.com/jobs/22265/fossil-preparator/job); [NC Museum of Natural Sciences DinoLab](https://spnhc.org/dinolab-fossil-preparator/)).

**Pay.** NC DinoLab $41,206–$56,660 ([SPNHC](https://spnhc.org/dinolab-fossil-preparator/)); Princeton $41,000–$60,000 ([Princeton](https://research-princeton.icims.com/jobs/22265/fossil-preparator/job)); USU Eastern $43,000 for a 3-year term ([SPNHC](https://spnhc.org/fossil-preparator/)); Florida Museum $40,000–$65,000 ([UF](https://teams-titles.hr.ufl.edu/teams-title/flmnh-museum-preparator/)); Dickinson, ND $21.33/hr with no degree required but 1–3 years of prep experience ([Dickinson](https://www.governmentjobs.com/careers/dickinson/jobs/newprint/3216415)).

**Entry barriers.** Usually a bachelor's degree in geology or biology plus 6–24 months of hands-on prep experience, often gained by volunteering ([USU](https://spnhc.org/fossil-preparator/); [Princeton](https://research-princeton.icims.com/jobs/22265/fossil-preparator/job)).

**Remote:** none. **AI risk:** low **[Inference]**.

**Fit [Inference].** Patient, solitary, science-rich, hands-on work. But the DinoLab role is explicitly "public-facing," with tours and presentations, and openings are rare and pay is modest. Paleoart, meaning illustration of extinct life, is a sub-niche of scientific illustration. I found no reliable pay data for it, so treat it as a style specialization within §1, not a separate career.

## 5. Illustration: editorial, children's books, licensing / surface pattern

**Day-to-day work.** Commissioned images for publications, books and products. Surface designers deliver "full pattern collections" with repeat-ready, layered files ([Art of Sketching, 2025](https://artofsketching.com/art-licensing-for-surface-pattern-designers/)).

**Pay.**

- BLS reports a median of **$55,490** (May 2025) for fine artists, including painters, sculptors and illustrators. **58% are self-employed** ([BLS Craft & Fine Artists, live](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/craft-and-fine-artists.htm)).
- _Children's books:_ a Big 5 picture-book illustration advance is typically $8,000–$20,000 per project ([WhatShouldICharge](https://whatshouldicharge.io/illustrator/publishing-clients)) **[Single-source]**. One working illustrator with 40 book projects over ten years reports that "only four books have actually earned out their advance" ([Anoosha Syed, Jan 2025](https://anooshasyed.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-an-illustrators-salary-1f0)) **[Single practitioner]**.
- _Licensing:_ Graphic Artists Guild benchmarks are cited as 3–5% royalties for mass-market products and 5–10% for specialty products ([Chris Wilson Studio, 2025](https://chriswilsonstudio.com/art-licensing-royalty-rates/), secondary citation of GAG). Flat fees run about $300–$2,000 per design ([Art of Sketching](https://artofsketching.com/art-licensing-for-surface-pattern-designers/)).

**Demand / outlook.** BLS projects craft and fine artist employment to _decline 1%_ over 2025–35, with about 4,300 openings a year ([BLS, live](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/craft-and-fine-artists.htm)).

**Getting work without self-promotion.**

- **Agents and reps exist and suit her.** Illustration reps commonly take **25–30%** of fees. Literary agents for author-illustrators take 15% ([Page & Provenance](https://www.pageandprovenance.com/provenance-studio/illustrators)).
- Licensing agencies take about **30–50% of royalties** but "can get you in front of companies you couldn't reach alone" ([Art of Sketching](https://artofsketching.com/art-licensing-for-surface-pattern-designers/)). The Graphic Artists Guild publishes a model designer–agent agreement ([GAG](https://graphicartistsguild.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/PEGs16-Surface-Pattern-Designer-Agent-Agreement.pdf)).
- Catch: you still have to _get_ the agent, usually through SCBWI conferences or portfolio submissions ([Page & Provenance](https://www.pageandprovenance.com/provenance-studio/illustrators)). Even an established illustrator says illustration income stalled with "zero outreach" ([Syed, Dec 2025](https://anooshasyed.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-an-illustrators-salary-99b)).

**AI-disruption risk: high and documented.**

- A Society of Authors survey (Jan 2024, 787 respondents) found **26% of illustrators had already lost work to generative AI** and 37% said their income had fallen in value ([SoA, Apr 2024](https://societyofauthors.org/2024/04/11/soa-survey-reveals-a-third-of-translators-and-quarter-of-illustrators-losing-work-to-ai/)).
- An Association of Illustrators survey (early 2025, 6,844 illustrators) found **32.4% had lost work**, averaging **£9,262** per affected artist ([AOI](https://theaoi.com/news/the-aoi-ai-survey-results)).
- Agents report clients extending finished illustrations with AI tools outside the license ([Design Week, Oct 2025](https://www.designweek.co.uk/how-the-illustration-industry-is-grappling-with-ai-a-special-report/)).
- A CHI 2025 study argues that style transfer works as "supply-chain optimization" that lets clients extract value from an illustrator's style ([Porquet et al., CHI 2025](https://dlnext.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713854)).
- These are UK surveys. US figures may differ.

**Fit [Inference].** Editorial work has tight briefs and fast deadlines, so it is a poor fit. Children's books allow long solo projects, but income is lumpy and slow, so they are unlikely to give full-time income within 2 years. Licensing through an agent is the most "no self-promotion" option, but it is slow to compound, so treat it as a **side stream**.

## 6. Print-on-demand (POD) & art licensing marketplaces

**Facts.**

- Redbubble's default artist margin is 20%, set by the artist. Typical earnings are about $2–5 per t-shirt. Society6 pays about 10% by default ([The Artillerist](https://theartillerist.com/post/etsy-vs-redbubble-vs-society6-for-artists)).
- Society6 reportedly cut many artist accounts in March 2025 and moved to curated, standardized royalties ([Store Score, 2026](https://blog.storescore.dev/society6-vs-redbubble-for-etsy-art-sellers-what-changed-in-2025-and-who-still-controls-your-margin/)) **[Single-source]**.
- Society6 earnings "often [run] $50–$200/month" unless the artist drives their own traffic ([The Artillerist](https://theartillerist.com/post/etsy-vs-redbubble-vs-society6-for-artists)).
- One calculator estimates $500/month needs about 107 Redbubble shirt sales a month and a 200+ design catalog ([CreatorMargin](https://www.creatormargin.com/pod-royalty-calculator-2026)) **[Single-source]**.

**Fit [Inference]: poor as a main income.** POD rewards high volume, trend chasing and driving your own traffic, which means self-promotion. It is fine as a low-effort outlet for existing paintings.

## 7. Painting commissions & gallery representation

**Facts.**

- Gallery consignment is most commonly 50/50 or 40/60 (artist/gallery) ([ATALM model consignment guide](https://www.atalm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/CONSIGNMENT-contract-2019.pdf)).
- Gallerists say they want artists who bring "a large audience" or fill a gap, and advise attending fairs and openings and researched email outreach ([NYFA, Mar 2025](https://www.nyfa.org/knowledge-base/five-things-to-know-gallery-representation-for-visual-artists/)).
- 58% of craft and fine artists are self-employed, and employment is projected to fall 1% ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/craft-and-fine-artists.htm)).

**Fit [Inference]: poor for full-time income in 2 years.** It requires networking and self-presentation, which are the core of what she wants to avoid, and income is irregular. Her own note that painting is _draining_ argues against making it the full-time job. Keep painting as personal practice and an occasional sales outlet.

## 8. Product / brand photography

**Facts.**

- BLS reports a median of **$44,660** (May 2025), with employment projected to **decline 1%** over 2025–35 and **67% self-employed** ([BLS Photographers, live](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm)).
- The earlier snapshot showed $42,520 and +2% for 2024–34 ([BLS snapshot](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Media-and-Communication/Photographers.htm)).

**AI-disruption risk: high, with concrete 2025 evidence.**

- Zalando: about 70% of editorial campaign images were AI-generated in Q4 2024, and costs fell about 90% ([Reuters, May 2025](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/zalando-uses-ai-speed-up-marketing-campaigns-cut-costs-2025-05-07/)).
- Unilever: "digital twins" of packaging gave up to 55% savings ([Unilever, Mar 2025](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2025/unilever-reinvents-product-shoots-with-ai-for-faster-content-creation/)).
- OTTO says AI is replacing model photography, with up to 60% cost savings ([E-commerce Germany, Oct 2025](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/otto-replaces-model-photography-with-ai/)).
- Hugo Boss launched AI product content globally ([Yahoo/Sourcing Journal, Jan 2025](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/hugo-boss-uses-gen-ai-171128789.html)).

**Fit [Inference]: poor.** The field is shrinking, mostly self-employed (so client-hunting), and in the direct path of AI. Her photo skill is better used inside a content-producer role (§9).

## 9. Behind-the-scenes brand content producer (employed, not an influencer)

**Day-to-day work.** Concepting, shooting, editing and delivering social video and photo assets for a brand. P&G's in-house studio COHO wants someone to "produce, shoot, and edit social content from concept to final delivery" and to "work solo" ([P&G posting, Jul 2026](https://pg.wd5.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/1000/job/Social-Content-Creator_R000155963)). The National Geographic Museum's Social Content Producer is the "boots on the ground" creator. A separate Social Media Manager owns strategy and community ([NGS posting, Sep 2026](https://ngs.wd1.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/ngs_external_career_site/job/Social-Content-Producer_R2107)).

**Pay.**

- P&G: **$85,000–$122,200** with 3+ years ([P&G](https://pg.wd5.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/1000/job/Social-Content-Creator_R000155963)).
- National Geographic Society: **$80,750–$85,000** ([NGS](https://ngs.wd1.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/ngs_external_career_site/job/Social-Content-Producer_R2107)).

**AI-disruption risk: moderate.** Brands are pushing more volume per person. Acquisition.com describes "AI-native production systems" ([GrowthRoles](https://growthroles.com/jobs/acquisitioncom-social-media-producer-las-vegas-cacd3faf401d2f7d)). The content volume demand appears to be rising, not falling **[Inference]**.

**Fit [Inference]: mixed, but the fastest route to full-time pay.** She already has paid experience, so it uses her current skills. Roles at museums, science organizations or nonprofits, like NatGeo, match her interests. The pace, trend-chasing and on-camera requirements could trigger stress and procrastination. She should screen for "behind the camera only" roles with a separate strategist, which fits her preference for working with one other person.

## 10. Museum exhibit design / exhibition graphics

**Facts.** Pay ranges vary widely:

- Museum of Arts and Design (NYC): $72K–$75K, on site, 3+ years, CAD and SketchUp ([NYU Museum Studies board, 2025](https://wp.nyu.edu/gsas-museumstudies_jobs_internships/2025/05/23/exhibition-designer-at-the-museum-of-arts-and-design-2/)).
- C&G Partners exhibition graphics: $70K–$85K, 3–5 years ([IDSA board, 2025](https://supportfinity.com/skilled-people/find-work/museum-exhibition-graphic-designer/EX-06D21319)).
- NY State Museum graphic design: $62,477–$75,686, up to 30% telework ([StateJobsNY](https://statejobs.ny.gov/public/vacancyDetailsPrint.cfm?id=226781)).
- North Carolina museums: $32,639–$46,383 ([NC Museum of Art](https://www.governmentjobs.com/careers/northcarolina/jobs/newprint/4915192)) and $34,270–$42,431, including ladder and installation work ([NC DNCR](https://www.governmentjobs.com/careers/northcarolina/jobs/newprint/5038738)).

For context, BLS reports a graphic designer median of **$62,960** (May 2025), projects employment to decline 2% over 2025–35, and states that AI tools are "projected to ... reduce the need for these workers" ([BLS Graphic Designers, live](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/graphic-designers.htm)).

**Fit [Inference]: moderate.** It mixes science and art (natural-history exhibits). However, it is team-heavy (curators, fabricators, architects), on site, and needs CAD/3D skills plus 2–3 years of museum experience. It works better as a later move, from scientific illustration or data visualization, than as an entry point.
