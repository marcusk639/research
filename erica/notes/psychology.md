# Psychology notes: Erica's creative-career plan

> **Read this first.** These notes give background for a career plan. They are **not clinical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment plan.** Nothing here says Erica has any condition. Research findings describe group averages, and most of the links are correlations rather than proof of cause. Decisions about therapy belong to Erica and a licensed clinician she chooses. Compiled 2026-10-12.

**Evidence labels used throughout:** `[META]` meta-analysis or systematic review · `[RCT]` randomized controlled trial · `[EXP]` lab or field experiment, not a clinical trial · `[OBS]` observational, correlational, or diary study · `[GUIDE]` clinical practice guideline · `[EXPERT]` expert opinion, theory, or practitioner book · `[ANEC]` anecdotal or community practice.
Text marked **Inference:** is our reasoning, not a finding from a source.

## 1. Procrastination as an emotion-regulation problem

### What the research says

- Sirois & Pychyl argue that procrastination is a failure of self-regulation that "has a great deal to do with short-term mood repair and emotion regulation." People put off tasks they find aversive (boring, frustrating, meaningless, or unstructured) to feel better now, and their "future self" pays the cost ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013, _Soc. & Personality Psych. Compass_](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/)) `[EXPERT — theoretical review of OBS/EXP work]`.
- The same review says that **negative mood about a task**, and about past procrastination, tends to increase further procrastination. Reducing negative feelings about past procrastination tends to reduce it ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/)) `[EXPERT]`.
- In four samples, trait procrastination had a moderate negative correlation with **self-compassion**. Low self-compassion partly explained why procrastinators feel more stressed ([Sirois 2014, _Self and Identity_](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91791/)) `[OBS, with a small internal meta-analysis]`.
- **Self-forgiveness:** 119 students who forgave themselves for procrastinating before their first exam procrastinated less before their second. Lower negative affect explained the effect ([Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886910000474)) `[OBS, prospective]`.
- **Perfectionism:** _Perfectionistic concerns_ (fear of mistakes, worry about others' judgment) correlate positively with procrastination (r = .23, k = 43, N ≈ 10,000). _Perfectionistic strivings_ (high personal standards) correlate negatively (r = −.22) ([Sirois, Molnar & Hirsch 2017, _Eur. J. Personality_](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/112533/)) `[META]`. Concern about mistakes is the part that goes with procrastination. High standards on their own do not.

### Interventions and how strong the evidence is

| Intervention                                                     | What the evidence shows                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Strength                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **CBT for procrastination**                                      | In RCTs against inactive controls, the overall effect of psychological treatments was small (g = 0.34) with high heterogeneity. A CBT subgroup of three studies (N = 236) reached g = 0.55 with no heterogeneity. Studies were few, small, and at high risk of bias ([Rozental et al. 2018, _Front. Psychol._](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6125391/)). A broader meta-analysis (24 studies, N = 1,173) found a large pre-post reduction that held at follow-up, with CBT reducing procrastination the most. Almost all samples were students ([van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X18300472)). A third RCT meta-analysis (12 studies, N = 646) found g = 1.18, with larger effects for in-person delivery and student samples ([Malouff & Schutte 2019](https://rune.une.edu.au/entities/publication/e97dd2e3-1473-4ae8-aab6-a02798461c3f)). | `[META]`: moderate. Promising, but the trials are small and mostly in students |
| **ACT / acceptance-based**                                       | ACT works across anxiety, depression, and transdiagnostic problems. It is generally better than waitlist and treatment-as-usual and roughly equal to CBT (20 meta-analyses, n = 12,477) ([Gloster et al. 2020](https://contextualscience.org/article/empirical_status_acceptance_commitment_therapy_review_metaanalyses_pages_181192)). For procrastination specifically, one RCT found **no group difference** between ACT-style and time-management interventions. The ACT intervention worked best for people who strongly valued the goal ([Glick & Orsillo 2015](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037/xge0000050)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | General ACT: `[META]`, strong. ACT for procrastination: `[RCT]`, weak or mixed |
| **Self-compassion / self-forgiveness**                           | Correlational and prospective links to less procrastination and less stress (see above). There is no dedicated procrastination RCT meta-analysis yet. Sirois has registered a meta-analysis on the topic ([project record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/4vrcbl9bmbd)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | `[OBS]`: moderate for association, weak for causation                          |
| **Implementation intentions** ("If X happens, then I will do Y") | Across 94 tests, the effect on reaching goals was medium to large (d = .65). Helps with getting started, shielding a goal from distraction, and disengaging from failing plans ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f)). These are general goal studies, not procrastination-specific ones.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | `[META]`: strong for goal attainment generally                                 |
| **Time-boxing / Pomodoro**                                       | One-day randomized study (N = 87 students). Compared with breaks taken at will, scheduled breaks (24/6 or 12/3 minutes) gave less fatigue and distraction and more concentration and motivation, with the same task completion. The authors note that Pomodoro had "never been investigated empirically" before this ([Biwer et al. 2023, _Br. J. Educ. Psychol._](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjep.12593)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | `[EXP]`: single small study, weak to moderate                                  |
| **Body doubling** (working with someone else present)            | Comes mainly from ADHD communities. A survey of 220 people describes how it is used, but it does not test whether it works ([Eagle et al. 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3597638.3614486)). An EEG study (N = 26) found **no significant effects** and no harms ([ACM ASSETS 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3759743)). A VR study (N = 12) found faster task completion, and its authors note that "no peer-reviewed controlled trials" exist yet. They also warn that **being observed can increase anxiety for people who fear judgment** ([arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12153)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | `[ANEC]` / pilot `[EXP]`: **weak**                                             |

## 2. Childhood emotional abuse, parental criticism, and adult self-criticism

**Caveat:** Every study in this section is correlational and mostly uses adults' recall of childhood. These studies cannot show that one person's history caused a particular outcome. They show patterns across many people.

- **Self-compassion:** Across 20 studies (N = 6,877), childhood maltreatment was linked to lower self-compassion (r = −.28). Emotional abuse in particular showed a moderate link (r = −.28). Physical and sexual abuse showed smaller links ([Trauma, Violence & Abuse meta-analysis 2021](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/15248380211043825)) `[META]`.
- **Self-esteem:** Across 254 studies, emotional abuse was linked to lower self-esteem (r = −.23). The result was not explained by publication bias ([Zhang et al. 2022, _Trauma, Violence & Abuse_](https://bishtref.com/articles/10.1177/15248380221129587)) `[META]`.
- **Adult mental health:** In 90 studies (N ≈ 132,000), childhood emotional abuse and neglect were linked to adult depression, anxiety, and other symptoms. The links held after controlling for other types of abuse. Note that the pooled depression/anxiety estimate (d = 0.36) shrank sharply after correcting for publication bias ([Xiao et al. 2022, _Trauma, Violence & Abuse_](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/15248380221122816)) `[META]`.
- **Self-criticism and attachment:** Insecure attachment, especially _anxious_ attachment, goes with more self-criticism. The authors suggest that self-blame can be a way to keep the caregiver seen as good and the world seen as predictable ([Rogier et al. 2023, _Pers. Indiv. Diff._](https://exa.ai/library/publication/86xvptw0pq5)) `[META]`.
- **Parental criticism and perfectionism:** Perceived parental criticism had a **large** correlation (about r = .53) with _socially prescribed_ perfectionism (the belief that others demand perfection from you). Its links to self-oriented perfectionism were small ([Curran & Hill 2022, _Psychological Bulletin_](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115289/)) `[META]`. A second meta-analysis (46 studies, N = 13,364) found the same pattern ([Smith et al. 2022, _J. Res. Personality_](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092656621001173)) `[META]`.
- **Imposter feelings and fear of failure:** Fear of failure was the strongest predictor of impostor feelings in working adults (β = .75) ([Noskeau et al. 2021](https://exa.ai/library/publication/xyqrt6jyz61)) `[OBS]`. In two samples, impostor feelings were predicted by fear of failure, fear of success, and low self-esteem, and they went with less career planning and career striving ([Neureiter & Traut-Mattausch 2016, _Front. Psychol._](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00048/full)) `[OBS]`. Evidence that childhood adversity leads to impostor feelings is thin. One survey found only an indirect link, running through emotion dysregulation and attachment anxiety ([Day & Blackhart 2023](https://exa.ai/library/publication/2zzc15g12dv)) `[OBS, student/unpublished work]`.

**Inference:** One chain fits the literature: harsh parental criticism → socially prescribed perfectionism, self-criticism, and low self-compassion → fear of failure and impostor feelings → avoiding the task to repair mood (procrastination). Each link has meta-analytic or good observational support. Nobody has tested the whole chain in one study, and it is a hypothesis about Erica, not a finding.

## 3. Therapy approaches for a harsh inner critic and childhood emotional abuse

| Approach                             | Evidence level (honest)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trauma-focused CBT, CPT, PE**      | **Strongest evidence for PTSD.** The APA guideline gives these a _strong_ recommendation ([APA PTSD guideline summary](https://www.apa.org/ptsd-guideline/treatments/recommendations-summary-table.pdf); [2025 update](https://www.uwyo.edu/wind/_files/docs/echo/ic-clinical-toolkit/treatment-guidelines/ptsd-apa-2025.pdf)) `[GUIDE]`. Caveat: these were tested on people with PTSD. Childhood emotional abuse does not necessarily mean PTSD. Whether these protocols fit is a question for a clinician.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **EMDR**                             | APA rates it _conditional_ (second line) ([APA treatments page](https://www.apa.org/ptsd-guideline/treatments)) `[GUIDE]`. In head-to-head studies it was about as effective as trauma-focused CBT ([Seidler & Wagner 2006, _Psychol. Med._](https://www.emdria.org/resource/comparing-the-efficacy-of-emdr-and-trauma-focused-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-in-the-treatment-of-ptsd-a-meta-analytic-study-psychological-medicine/)) `[META, small: 7 studies]`. Whether the eye movements themselves add anything is still unclear.                                                                                                                                         |
| **Compassion-Focused Therapy (CFT)** | Built specifically for shame and self-criticism. In 15 RCTs and pilot RCTs, CFT improved self-compassion (g = 1.12 vs waitlist, high heterogeneity) and self-reassurance (g = 0.51). The reductions in "hated self" and "inadequate self" were **not statistically significant** against controls. Evidence against active treatments is mixed, and 7 of 15 studies were of unclear quality ([Millard et al. 2023, _J. Affect. Disord._](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165032723000228)) `[META]`: **moderate and still emerging.**                                                                                                                    |
| **Schema therapy**                   | Targets long-standing patterns learned in childhood (e.g., "defectiveness" or "failure" schemas). Strongest in personality disorders: 8 RCTs gave g = 0.36 ([meta-analysis 2023, PubMed 37402124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37402124/)), and a large multicenter RCT (N = 323) found it beat treatment-as-usual ([Bamelis et al. 2014, _Am. J. Psychiatry_](https://www.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518)) `[META/RCT]`. Evidence outside personality disorders is still developing ([J. Pers. Disord. 2023 review](https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/pedi.2023.37.2.262)). **Moderate**, mainly for personality disorders.              |
| **ACT**                              | Broad, strong evidence across conditions ([Gloster et al. 2020](https://contextualscience.org/article/empirical_status_acceptance_commitment_therapy_review_metaanalyses_pages_181192)) `[META]`. Not trauma-specific. Its focus on values and acting while still afraid fits procrastination and fear of being seen.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Internal Family Systems (IFS)**    | **The evidence is thin.** A 2025 scoping review found 27 studies: 17 case studies, only **2 RCTs** (both proof-of-concept and small), and no controlled trials for PTSD ([Buys 2025, _Aust. Psychologist_](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13284207.2025.2533127)). The PTSD study was an uncontrolled pilot ([Hodgdon et al. 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10926771.2021.2013375)), and an online group version had N = 15 and no control group ([PARTS 2024](https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2024-98110-001.pdf)). It is popular and generally well tolerated, but it is **not established** as effective. `[pilot RCT / OBS / EXPERT]` |

### Finding a trauma-informed therapist (practical)

- **Screening questions** can follow SAMHSA's six trauma-informed principles: safety, trustworthiness and transparency, peer support, collaboration, empowerment/voice/choice, and cultural awareness ([SAMHSA 2014](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/sma14-4884.pdf)) `[GUIDE]`. For example, ask: "How do you pace trauma work?", "What happens if I need to slow down?", and "How do we decide on goals together?"
- **Ask about specific training** (CPT, PE, EMDR, CFT, schema therapy) instead of accepting "trauma-informed" as a label. **Inference:** "Trauma-informed" describes an approach to care, not a credential.
- **Lowering the cost in the US:**
  - [Open Path Collective](https://openpathcollective.org/pricing-and-eligibility-for-affordable-therapy/): US$40–70 per individual session, US$30 with supervised student interns. Check the site for its membership fee and income eligibility.
  - [HRSA-funded community health centers](https://www.hrsa.gov/get-health-care/affordable/eligibility): sliding-scale fees, open to anyone with or without insurance ([locator](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov/)).
  - **Inference / general practice:** Other options are university training clinics, in-network providers through insurance, employer EAP sessions, and group-format CFT or ACT (often cheaper; the group IFS pilot above was designed partly to save cost).

## 4. Self-determination theory and creative motivation

- **Rewards and control:** A meta-analysis of 128 experiments found that _expected, tangible_ rewards tied to doing or finishing a task reduced free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = −0.40 / −0.36). **Positive informational feedback increased it** (d = +0.33) ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, _Psych. Bulletin_](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan_Meta.pdf)) `[META]`. This is the main evidence that controlling conditions can undermine interest.
- **Amabile's intrinsic motivation principle:** People are most creative when driven by "interest, enjoyment, satisfaction, and challenge of the work itself." In her experiments, **expected evaluation, surveillance, constrained choice, and contracted-for reward** reduced creativity as rated by artist judges ([Amabile, _Big C, Little C_ working paper](https://studyres.com/doc/52822/big-c--little-c--howard--and-me--approaches-to-understand...)) `[EXP]`. Her later work refines this: some extrinsic motivators can _add to_ intrinsic motivation ("motivational synergy") when they inform or enable rather than control ([same source](https://studyres.com/doc/52822/big-c--little-c--howard--and-me--approaches-to-understand...); [Fisher & Amabile 2023](https://exa.ai/library/publication/3vnwqrrwmjt)) `[EXPERT]`.
- **Deadlines:** In more than 9,000 diary entries, extreme time pressure usually reduced creativity. The exception was "on a mission" days, where people had a single focus and felt the urgency was meaningful. High pressure combined with fragmented demands ("treadmill" days) was the worst case ([Amabile, Hadley & Kramer 2002, _HBR_](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12195920)) `[OBS]`.
- **Structure that helps:** Teacher autonomy support and structure _each_ predicted student engagement. The title sums it up: "not autonomy support _or_ structure but autonomy support _and_ structure" ([Jang, Reeve & Deci 2010, _J. Educ. Psychol._](https://www.scienceopen.com/document?vid=db5771b3-ec55-4ccd-8ed0-b0fbea3d54b1)) `[OBS]`. Structuring creative work reduces anxiety, but only when people **do not feel evaluated** ([Fisher & Amabile 2023](https://exa.ai/library/publication/3vnwqrrwmjt), citing Byron et al. 2010) `[EXPERT/META-cited]`.
- **Goals she chooses herself:** People pursuing _self-concordant_ goals, meaning goals that match their own interests and values, put in more sustained effort, were more likely to reach them, and gained more well-being when they did ([Sheldon & Elliot 1999, _JPSP_](http://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482)) `[OBS, longitudinal]`.

## 5. Work design for this profile

- **Small wins / progress principle:** In nearly 12,000 diary entries from 238 knowledge workers, _making progress on meaningful work_ was the single biggest difference between people's best and worst days. Praise without real progress did little and could breed cynicism. Criticism of trivial issues after real progress caused anger and sadness ([Amabile & Kramer, _The Progress Principle_](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=40692); [HBR 2007](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=24239)) `[OBS]`.
- **Feedback design:** Positive _informational_ feedback raises intrinsic motivation ([Deci et al. 1999](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan_Meta.pdf)) `[META]`, and _expected evaluation_ lowers creativity (Amabile, above) `[EXP]`. **Inference:** In early stages, feedback should be private, specific, requested by Erica, and from people she picks. Generating ideas should be kept separate from being judged on them.
- **An exposure ladder for sharing and selling work:** Exposure-based CBT has large effects for social-evaluative fear. Individual CBT gave SMD −1.19 against waitlist, and supported self-help (−0.86) also worked ([Mayo-Wilson et al. 2014, _Lancet Psychiatry_](<https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(14)70329-3/fulltext>)) `[META]`. **Inference:** Using this as a _career_ tool, with graded steps from a private draft to one trusted viewer to a small group to public posting to selling, is an extrapolation. It was not tested on artists, and these trials were in people diagnosed with social anxiety.
- **Accountability without control:** Implementation intentions `[META]` and fixed work and break blocks `[EXP]` support follow-through. Being watched and having choices constrained hurt creativity `[EXP]`. **Inference:** Accountability works best when Erica designs it herself and it reports on her _process_ ("I did my two blocks"), not on judgments of the _product_.
- **Passion and burnout:** Across 94 studies, _harmonious_ passion (an activity that sits in balance with the rest of life) correlated with positive affect, flow, and performance, and had a **large negative correlation with burnout**. _Obsessive_ passion (feeling compelled, with self-worth tied to the activity) correlated with both positive and negative outcomes, including negative affect and rumination ([Curran, Hill, Appleton, Vallerand & Standage 2015, _Motiv. Emot._](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/2015_CurranHillAppletonVallerandStandage_MotivEmo.pdf)) `[META]`. **Inference:** Painting that is "absorbing but draining" deserves watching. Signs to watch for are losing track of time _and_ being unable to stop without guilt. These are risks to monitor, not something to label.

## 6. The partner's role (directly relevant: her husband is helping drive this)

- **Secure base:** In a study that combined observation with an experiment, _responsive, nonintrusive_ support of a partner's goals and exploration predicted the recipient's happiness, self-esteem, and confidence in reaching specific goals ([Feeney 2004, _JPSP_](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15535776/)) `[OBS + EXP]`. In 167 married couples, three features of a secure base predicted exploration: **availability, non-interference, and encouragement** ([Feeney & Thrush 2010](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wzvkndpryzp)) `[OBS]`.
- **Dependency paradox:** A partner accepting someone's dependence _when needed_ predicted _more_ independent goal-striving six months later, not less ([Feeney 2007, _JPSP_](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6524611_The_dependency_paradox_in_close_relationships_Accepting_dependence_promotes_independence)) `[OBS + EXP]`.
- **Invisible support:** In couples facing a major stressor (the bar exam), support the recipient _didn't notice_ predicted better adjustment ([Bolger, Zuckerman & Kessler 2000, _JPSP_](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11138764/)) `[OBS, diary]`. A later synthesis adds an important nuance. Invisible support helps most **when the recipient is worried about being evaluated**, because it protects her sense of competence. _Visible_ support helps when she wants to take action or is acutely distressed and asks for help. Practical help draws more attention to incompetence than emotional help ([Zee & Bolger 2019, _Curr. Dir. Psychol. Sci._](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/0963721419835214/)) `[EXPERT review of EXP/OBS]`.

**Inference for the husband:** He would do best as the _secure base_, not the _project manager_. That means being available, not interfering, encouraging her, and handling logistics quietly. He should offer visible help when she asks for it. He should avoid evaluating her work or tracking her deadlines unless she has designed that role for him.

## Implications for Erica's career plan (INFERENCE, not findings)

1. **Treat procrastination as a feelings problem, not a discipline problem.** Plans should lower how aversive starting feels (tiny first steps, if-then plans, fixed work/break blocks) and should include self-forgiveness after slips, not punishment.
2. **Use structure she chooses herself, not briefs others impose.** Erica sets her own process goals (e.g., "two 25-minute painting blocks on Tue/Thu") inside a loose two-year direction. Avoid client-style briefs early on and add them gradually once her confidence is stronger.
3. **Keep making and judging separate.** Feedback should be private, specific, requested by her, and from 1–2 trusted people. Public exposure and selling move up a graded ladder at a pace she controls.
4. **Track small wins.** Use a brief daily progress log (what moved forward). This is the most direct evidence-based lever on motivation in creative work.
5. **Treat therapy as a parallel track, not a requirement of the plan.** If she wants it, CFT or ACT fit the inner-critic and procrastination picture. Trauma-focused CBT or EMDR fit if a clinician finds trauma symptoms. Schema therapy fits long-standing "I'm awful at everything" beliefs. IFS is popular, but its evidence is weak. Low-cost routes exist (Open Path, HRSA centers, training clinics).
6. **Protect harmonious passion.** Turning a passion into income risks making it obsessive. Build stopping points into painting, keep some creative work non-commercial, and watch for guilt and rumination.
7. **Her husband acts as a secure base.** Mostly invisible, practical support; encouragement; no evaluating her work. Accountability only in forms she designs.
8. **What's unknown:** Most procrastination evidence comes from students. No study tests this whole package for adult creatives with histories of emotional abuse. Treat the plan as an experiment: review monthly and adjust.
