# Work structure: employment vs freelance vs own brand (for Erica)

**Date:** 2026-10-12 · **Scope:** Which work structure fits Erica's goal of full-time income within about 2 years, her dislike of self-promotion and cold calling, her preference for working alone or with one other person, her stress under tight briefs, and her habit of procrastinating when afraid.
**Method:** Web research (Exa search plus full-page reads). The Firecrawl tool was out of credits. Sources read in full: The Creative Independent artist survey, CNBC's piece on 2026 ACA premiums, and Leapers' 2025 freelancer mental-health report. Two more were read in detail through long excerpts: Richard Pryn on production-music income and the Ariely & Wertenbroch paper together with its 2026 replication.
**Conventions:** **[Fact]** means a cited claim. **[Inference]** means my reasoning from the evidence. **[Single-source]** flags a claim backed by only one source. **[Older]** flags a source from before 2024.

---

## 1. Headline findings

1. **[Fact]** Most visual artists who earn a living get most of it from freelance or contract work, or from a job, not from selling their own art. In The Creative Independent's survey of 1,016 artists, 61% named "freelance/contract work" as a top-three income source. 42% named a job related to their practice, and only 12% named gallery sales. Only 17% earned 75–100% of their income from their art ([TCI, 2018](https://thecreativeindependent.com/artist-survey/)). **[Older]**, but it is the best survey of its kind and later data points the same way.
2. **[Fact]** Own-brand income on creator platforms follows a power law: a few earners take most of the money. An academic analysis of Patreon found 115,183 of 139,366 campaigns received no pledges at all. Among active campaigns the median was about $31 a month, and only the top 1% made $2,500 a month or more ([Regner, 2020](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wnv5qsn65ch)). Concentration _within_ each topic rose: the Gini coefficient, a 0–1 inequality measure, went from 0.30 in 2015 to 0.60 in 2020 ([El Sanyoura & Anderson, ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/download/19338/19110/23351)). **[Older]**
3. **[Fact]** Catalog and royalty income (music libraries, art licensing) is real but slow. The Production Music Association suggests five to eight years to reach full-time income from library royalties, as cited in a 2026 summary ([NotNoise, 2026](https://notnoise.co/blog/production-music-libraries-passive-income)). One working library composer went full-time after about seven years part-time, and says tracks usually earn nothing for one to two years ([Pryn, 2026](https://richardpryn.com/the-honest-truth-about-production-music-income-in-2026-with-real-numbers-from-my-career/)).
4. **[Fact]** There are established ways to get paid creative work without selling yourself. Creative staffing agencies employ you on a W-2, so taxes are withheld and you get benefits ([Creative Circle](https://www.creativecircle.com/candidate-experience/), [Aquent](https://aquent.com/services/temporary-staffing)). Illustration, licensing and voice agents find and negotiate the work for a commission ([Handsome Frank via True Grit](https://www.truegrittexturesupply.com/pages/jon-cockley-handsome-frank-agent-interview), [ALI](https://www.artlicensing.com/for-artists/artist-faq/), [Voices.com](https://www.voices.com/blog/what-is-talent-agent/)). Studios also subcontract work out without their clients knowing ("white-label").
5. **[Inference]** The best fit for Erica keeps a third party between her and the market: someone else finds the client and sets the deadline, and she controls how the work gets done. A creative staffing agency (W-2 contract work) or a part-time in-house role, plus a slowly growing catalog on the side, is the most likely way to reach full-time income in two years. Building her own brand alone is the least likely.

## 2. Structure-by-structure evidence

### 2.1 Employment (in-house, W-2, full- or part-time, remote)

**Income and stability**

- **[Fact]** BLS May 2025 median wages for employees: graphic designers $62,960, illustrators $55,490, photographers $44,660, music producers and audio engineers $73,130 ([Slice, citing BLS](https://tryslicemedia.com/data/creative-pay-2026)). **[Single-source aggregator, cites BLS]**
- **[Fact]** The 2024 median for craft and fine artists was $56,260. 57% of them are self-employed, and "many hold another job in addition to their work as an artist" ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/craft-and-fine-artists.htm)).
- **[Fact]** BLS wage tables leave out self-employed workers. Self-employment runs at about 59–60% in art occupations versus about 6% across all occupations ([BLS Career Outlook](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2018/article/creative-careers-update.htm)). **[Older]**

**Remote availability**

- **[Fact]** Estimates of remote design jobs vary a lot. One 2025 analysis puts them at about 21% of design roles, down from 30% in 2022 ([Bridgeway](https://www.bridgewaysolution.com/blog/remote-design-jobs-navigating-the-hybrid-shift-in-2025)). Another tracker finds 44% fully remote among listings that state a work model, and that count is skewed toward product design ([LandEarly](https://www.landearly.com/job-market/design)). Both are aggregators, so treat them as rough.
- **[Fact]** On Indeed, remote graphic-designer postings mostly pay $20–$44 an hour. About 17% are part-time and 38% mention flexible schedules ([Indeed, updated 2026-07](https://www.indeed.com/q-remote-graphic-designer-jobs.html)). **[Single-source]**

**Benefits**

- **[Fact]** Under the ACA, only employers with 50 or more full-time-equivalent staff must offer health coverage, and only to people averaging 30 or more hours a week. They do not have to cover part-time staff ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/identifying-full-time-employees), [IRS Q&A](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/questions-and-answers-on-employer-shared-responsibility-provisions-under-the-affordable-care-act)).
- **[Inference]** A part-time job will not reliably come with health insurance. A job at 30 or more hours with a large employer usually will.

**Self-marketing required:** Low after hiring. Getting hired takes a portfolio, a résumé and interviews, but no ongoing promotion.
**Isolation:** Lowest of the three. Even remote roles come with a team.
**Autonomy:** Mixed. Briefs come from the employer. Per the Amabile research in §3, that can actually help, if the goals are clear and stable and she controls how she works.

### 2.2 Freelance / client work

**Income and stability**

- **[Fact]** Upwork's 2025 survey of 3,000 skilled US knowledge workers found full-time freelancers' median income was $85,000, against $80,000 for full-time employees. "Moonlighters" (people with a job who also freelance) earned about $40,000 extra from freelancing ([Upwork Research Institute, 2025](https://www.upwork.com/research/future-workforce-index-2025)). Caveat: the sample was limited to "skilled" workers above an earnings threshold, and Upwork is a platform with a stake in the result.
- **[Fact]** MBO Partners counts 72.9 million US independents in 2025, of whom 5.6 million earn more than $100K. 63% say they are independent fully by choice ([MBO Partners, 2025](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence)). That means about 92% earn under $100K. MBO is also a vendor in this market.
- **[Fact]** Marketplace rates for creative work are low because buyers worldwide can hire anyone. Upwork's worldwide ranges are about $15–35 an hour for graphic designers, $25–45 for photographers and $25–55 for content creators ([Slice, citing Upwork](https://tryslicemedia.com/data/creative-pay-2026)). US benchmark guides put mid-level brand and graphic design at about $45–130 an hour ([FreelanceRateLab, 2026](https://freelanceratelab.com/guides/freelance-rate-by-industry)). That guide's methodology is unclear, so treat it as rough.
- **[Fact]** Generative AI cut demand in exactly her areas. After DALL-E 2 and Midjourney launched, image-related job posts on a large platform fell 17% ([Demirci et al., Management Science 2025](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420)). A separate study of Upwork found design and image freelancers lost jobs and earnings, and the top earners were hit hardest ([Hui, Reshef & Zhou, Org. Science 2024](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2023.18441)).
- **[Fact]** Freelancer conditions softened in 2025. In a 3,571-person survey, the share rating their economic situation good fell from 53% to 46% ([freelancermap 2025](https://www.freelancermap.com/files/survey/freelancer-study-2025.pdf)). This survey is mostly German/European.

**How freelancers actually get work (the self-marketing question)**

- **[Fact]** Referrals, word of mouth and reputation are the most common source of work (67%). Then come social media (44%), marketplaces (29%) and job sites (27%) ([Freelance economic impact report 2024](https://motamem.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/freelance-economy-2024.pdf), [Freelance Informer](https://www.freelanceinformer.com/news/word-of-mouth-still-top-way-to-land-freelance-work/)).
- **[Fact]** Among UK cultural freelancers, 81% found work through personal recommendations and 56% through repeat bookings ([Cultural Freelancers Study 2024](https://exa.ai/library/publication/41xp0ks65vr)).
- **[Fact]** Ways around self-marketing:
  - **Creative staffing agencies.** Creative Circle makes freelancers its W-2 employees, with weekly pay, "no invoices or hounding past-due clients", and access to health benefits and a 401(k). Recruiters bring roles to you, there is no cost to the candidate, and remote roles are included ([Creative Circle](https://www.creativecircle.com/candidate-experience/)). Aquent says the same: talent are its employees, with weekly pay and quick benefits eligibility ([Aquent](https://aquent.com/services/temporary-staffing)). Caveat: neither guarantees any work.
  - **Illustration agents.** They source the work, negotiate, and handle project management and invoicing. Commission is typically 20–35%; Handsome Frank charges 33%, or 25% for editorial ([True Grit, 2025](https://www.truegrittexturesupply.com/blogs/news/how-to-get-an-illustration-agent)). Agents say representation usually comes _after_ an artist has a track record.
  - **Voice-over agents.** Commission is about 10–20%. They bring auditions you could not get on your own, but they sign voices that are "demonstrably bookable" ([Voices.com](https://www.voices.com/blog/what-is-talent-agent/), [OnSet Report](https://onsetreport.com/article/voice-over-agents-when-you-need-one-and-how-to-get-one)). One practitioner says most of his bookings came from his own efforts and only a small share from agents ([Such A Voice](https://www.suchavoice.com/blog/2018/12/20/what-is-a-talent-agents-job/)). **[Older]**
  - **White-label subcontracting.** Agencies and solo studios buy production capacity that "works under your brand" and never talks to the end client ([Delesign](https://www.delesign.com/agency), [CreativCurrent](https://www.creativcurrent.com/industries/agency-partner)). These are vendor pages, but they show the model exists. **[Inference]** A freelancer can be the person behind one or two studios and never pitch an end client.

**Benefits:** Self-employed workers buy their own insurance on the ACA marketplace. See §2.4.
**Isolation:** High (see §2.4).
**Autonomy:** High over when she works. Over the work itself it depends on the client, and tight briefs are common on marketplaces.

### 2.3 Own brand / products (original art, licensing, print-on-demand, music library, Patreon)

**Income distribution (power law)**

- **[Fact]** Patreon: see §1. In 2017 Graphtreon found only about 2% of creators earned federal minimum wage or more ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2018/01/02/patreon-content-creators-monthly-minimum-wage/)). **[Older]** I found no newer public distribution data.
- **[Fact]** Etsy: about $11.92B in gross sales across about 5.6M sellers in 2025 averages to about $2,130 per seller per year in _gross_ sales, and that average is pulled up by large shops ([EarnFacts, from Etsy FY2025](https://earnfacts.com/etsy-print-on-demand-income/)).
- **[Fact]** Print-on-demand: since September 2025, Redbubble's Standard tier takes 50% of the artist's margin. Most Redbubble artists earn $0–50 a month, and 200+ designs in a popular niche might earn $200–1,500 a month ([Wrench & Wallet, 2026](https://wrenchandwallet.com/how-to-make-money-print-on-demand/)). Society6 artists typically see $50–200 a month unless they bring their own traffic ([The Artillerist](https://theartillerist.com/post/etsy-vs-redbubble-vs-society6-for-artists)). Both are blog estimates, so treat them as rough.

**Licensing (art)**

- **[Fact]** Royalties run about 2–5% on greeting cards and gift wrap, 3–8% on household goods, and 10%+ on posters and prints ([Gather Dream Create](https://gatherdreamcreate.com/business-advice/art-licensing/self-represent-or-art-agency/)). Agencies commonly take 30–50% of royalties ([CatCoq, 2025](https://catcoq.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-art-licensing-agencies)). Art Licensing International pays artists 50%, quarterly, and has paid some artists for 20+ years ([ALI FAQ](https://www.artlicensing.com/for-artists/artist-faq/)).
- **[Fact]** Royalty payments can arrive 3–18 months after a deal ([Sars Art, 2024](https://sarsart.org/how-to-license-your-artwork-a-complete-guide-to-art-licensing-success-2024/)).

**Music library / sync** (sync = licensing music to film, TV, ads or video)

- **[Fact]** One 2026 estimate says three to seven years to full-time income: year one $0–5K, year three $20–50K, and $80K+ around year five, with hundreds to thousands of tracks in the catalog ([DropCue, 2026](https://dropcue.app/blog/sync-licensing-income-2026)). DropCue is a vendor, but its numbers agree with the PMA and Pryn.
- **[Fact]** Pryn: "Production music is not passive income. It is income that is asynchronous to your action. You work hard for free, then 2 years later you get paid." His back catalog only began earning as a whole about 10 years after he started ([Pryn, 2026](https://richardpryn.com/the-honest-truth-about-production-music-income-in-2026-with-real-numbers-from-my-career/)).
- **[Fact]** Stock-music sites pay about $10 per license, with exclusive libraries paying $50–300 per song as a buyout ([StockMusicMusician](https://www.stockmusicmusician.com/blog/stock-music-licensing-earnings-calculator)). **[Single-source]**

**Self-marketing required:** Patreon, Etsy and print-on-demand mostly reward the artist's own traffic; Society6 income is "rarely" significant without it ([The Artillerist](https://theartillerist.com/post/etsy-vs-redbubble-vs-society6-for-artists)). **Libraries and licensing agencies are the exception.** Once a library or agency signs her, its staff does the selling. Her job is to hit the library's briefs and keep producing.

- **[Fact]** In the TCI survey, artists spending 3+ hours a week on self-promotion did _not_ feel more financially stable (median stability 4, against 5 for those spending 1–2 hours). Only 26% credited marketing their own work as a leading factor in their stability; relationships and support networks ranked highest ([TCI](https://thecreativeindependent.com/artist-survey/)). This is a correlation, not proof of cause.

**Autonomy:** Highest. **External deadlines:** Almost none for self-directed products. Libraries and licensing agencies do issue briefs and calls for collections.

### 2.4 Cross-cutting: health insurance and isolation (US)

- **[Fact]** The enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired on 2025-12-31. In 2026, single people earning above 400% of the federal poverty level (about $62,600) get no subsidy at all. This is the "cliff" ([BenefitKarma](https://benefitkarma.com/articles/benefits-in-the-news/the-aca-subsidy-cliff-is-back-in-2026), [CRS R48290](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48290)). KFF estimated subsidized enrollees' premium payments would rise 114% on average, and the average deductible rose from $2,759 to $3,786 ([BenefitKarma citing KFF](https://benefitkarma.com/articles/benefits-in-the-news/the-aca-subsidy-cliff-is-back-in-2026)).
- **[Fact]** Example: a 26-year-old bookstore manager who also creates content saw her NY bronze-plan premium go from about $147 to $849 a month. As of March 2026, efforts in Congress to restore the subsidies had stalled ([CNBC, 2026-03-25](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2026/03/25/how-much-aca-premiums-spiked-after-enhanced-subsidies-ended.html)). **Re-check before advising**, because the law may have changed since March.
- **[Inference]** For a self-employed person in 2026, health insurance is a real cost. At a $60–70K target income she would sit right at the cliff. A W-2 arrangement with benefits (an employer or a staffing agency) is worth thousands of dollars a year more than the headline rate suggests.
- **[Fact]** Isolation: 19.2% of UK self-employed felt lonely "often or always", against about 7.7% of the general population. People who work "mostly remote + mostly alone" had the worst wellbeing. The analysis concluded that "social interaction itself, not location, is the key protective factor" ([Leapers 2025](https://www.leapers.co/research/2025/report/)). This is a UK, self-selected sample.

## 3. Psychology evidence relevant to her profile

- **[Fact] Freedom over how, not what.** Amabile: "the key to creativity is giving people autonomy concerning the means – that is, concerning process – but not necessarily the ends… clearly specified strategic goals often enhance people's creativity" ([Amabile, "How to Kill Creativity", HBR 1998](https://www.pickardlaws.com/myleadership/myfiles/rtdocs/hbr/HowToKillCreativityHBR0998.pdf)). Bailyn's terms, used in Amabile's R&D work: "strategic autonomy" (choosing the goal) can sit with the manager, while "operational autonomy" (choosing the method) is what creative people need ([Amabile & Gryskiewicz, 1987](https://exa.ai/library/publication/y3gr5m5vc2v)). **[Older]**, but the framework is still the standard one.
- **[Fact] Fear of judgment.** Fear of negative criticism can undermine the intrinsic motivation creativity depends on ([Amabile et al., 1996, AMJ](https://innovationnorth.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Amabile-et-al_1996_AMJ.pdf)). Fake or impossibly tight deadlines leave people feeling over-controlled ([Amabile, HBR](https://www.pickardlaws.com/myleadership/myfiles/rtdocs/hbr/HowToKillCreativityHBR0998.pdf)). **[Older]**
- **[Fact] External deadlines and procrastination (contested).** Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002) found people self-impose deadlines but set them badly, and that evenly spaced _external_ deadlines produced the best performance ([Psych Science 2002](https://web.mit.edu/ariely/www/MIT/Papers/deadlines.pdf)). **However,** a July 2026 replication of the key study found _no_ effect of deadline condition and lots of drop-out ([Hyndman & Bisin, Psych Science 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09567976261460772)). **[Inference]** The idea that external deadlines fix procrastination is now weakly supported in general. It may still hold for an individual, and her own history is better evidence than this literature.

## 4. Fit analysis (all **[Inference]**, grounded in the facts above)

**Her constraints, restated as design requirements:**

1. **A third party supplies who pays and when it's due.** She avoids self-promotion and cold calling, and fear shows up as procrastination. A client, recruiter or agent who brings the work and sets the deadline removes the two hardest steps.
2. **She controls how the work gets done, with briefs that are clear but not prescriptive.** This is Amabile's "strategic vs operational autonomy" split. Her stress under tight briefs points to work defined by outcome ("a 3-image series for X, mood Y") rather than by spec.
3. **Low social load but not total isolation.** She prefers one collaborator; Leapers finds working alone is the riskiest wellbeing profile. One editor, art director or producer she works with regularly fits both.
4. **About $50–70K within two years, ideally with benefits.** BLS employee medians in her fields are $45–73K. Catalog income realistically takes three to eight years. Own-brand income is power-law distributed.
5. **Low-trust-building.** Given her history, repeated small wins with a stable counterpart (one art director, one library) are better than environments built on frequent public rejection, such as marketplace bidding or social-media metrics.

**Scoring (H/M/L = high/medium/low fit):**

| Structure                                    | Someone else brings work | Freedom over how          | Deadlines set externally | One stable collaborator | Full-time income in 2 years       | Benefits |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------- |
| Full-time in-house (remote)                  | H                        | M (depends on employer)   | H                        | H                       | H, if hired                       | H        |
| Part-time in-house                           | H                        | M                         | H                        | H                       | M (only part of income)           | L–M      |
| Creative staffing agency (W-2 contracts)     | H                        | M                         | H                        | M–H                     | M–H                               | M–H      |
| White-label subcontract to 1–2 studios       | H, once set up           | M–H                       | H                        | H                       | M                                 | L        |
| Retainer with 1–2 anchor clients             | M (needs referrals)      | M–H                       | H                        | H                       | M                                 | L        |
| Open marketplace freelancing (Upwork/Fiverr) | M (bidding = selling)    | L–M (prescriptive briefs) | H                        | L                       | L–M (low global rates, hit by AI) | L        |
| Illustration/voice agent                     | H, once signed           | M–H                       | H                        | M                       | M (needs track record first)      | L        |
| Licensing agency / music library             | H, once signed           | H                         | M (open calls, briefs)   | M                       | L in 2 years, H over 5–8          | L        |
| Own brand (Patreon/Etsy/POD)                 | L                        | H                         | L                        | L                       | L (power law)                     | L        |

**Why the "obvious" creative answer is a poor fit:** Own-brand products give the most autonomy but also need the most self-promotion and have the fewest external deadlines, which are the two things she most needs supplied. The income distribution is extremely skewed. For someone who is self-critical, it also turns every sales metric into a public verdict on her worth.

**Why pure open-market freelancing is also a poor fit:** Bidding on marketplaces is selling. Marketplace briefs are often prescriptive. Rates are globally compressed. AI has cut image-work demand by about 17%.

**The shape that fits:** a **go-between model**, meaning a recruiter, agent, studio or library sits between her and the market, plus **a slow-growing catalog as a long-term asset**.

## 5. Recommendation

**Primary path (years 0–2): "W-2 base plus catalog"**

1. **Get a W-2 base of 25–40 hours a week, through either:**
   - **(a) a creative staffing agency.** Register with Creative Circle, Aquent and similar (Vitamin T is Aquent's). Recruiters bring roles; she is a W-2 employee with weekly pay and access to benefits. She needs an ATS-friendly résumé and a portfolio, not a social-media presence. Or:
   - **(b) a remote or part-time in-house role** where the work is defined by goals, not specs. Examples are content and photo for a brand or museum's social channels, or visuals for science communication. This plays to her art-plus-science interests, but those openings are an inference, not something I researched. Target employers with 50+ staff and 30+ hours a week if she needs health coverage.
2. **Build a catalog on the side for a gatekeeper, not for an audience.** Submit to one or two production-music libraries (music) and/or one art-licensing agency (paintings). The library or agency does the selling, issues briefs and calls for collections that serve as gentle external deadlines, and pays for years. Expect little income from this in years one and two; it is for years three to eight.
3. **Grow toward one or two anchor relationships.** The aim is a retainer, or white-label work for one small studio, that grows out of a staffing placement or a referral. That fits "work alongside one other person" and needs no public self-promotion.
4. **Later (year 2+), once there's a track record:** approach one illustration agent or voice-over agent. Agents sign people who are already "bookable", and commissions of 10–35% buy her out of the sales work entirely.

**Avoid as primary income:** Patreon, print-on-demand, Etsy and any model where sales depend on her own social reach. Also avoid open-bid marketplaces. At most, treat these as low-effort extras.

**Make the structure fit her psychology:**

- Ask for, or negotiate, **outcome briefs**: goal, audience, mood, deadline, with freedom over method. This applies Amabile's means/ends split.
- Choose arrangements with **regular check-ins with one person**: a recruiter, an art director, a library's A&R. This tackles isolation and gives some accountability. The evidence on external deadlines is now mixed, so test it on herself rather than assume it works.
- Expect **health-insurance costs** in any 1099 period (paid as an independent contractor, no withholding or benefits). 2026 has no subsidy above about $62.6K for a single person. That makes W-2 routes economically better than they look on headline rate.

**Open questions for Erica (these would change the recommendation):** Which medium is strongest commercially (painting vs music vs photo/social)? Does she have a portfolio ready for a recruiter? Does she need employer health insurance? Would she relocate for an in-house role at a museum, lab or studio?
