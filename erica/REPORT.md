## Purpose

Find a sustainable, full-time-income career path for Erica that fits her creative range
and the realities of fear and low self-trust rooted in childhood trauma.

- **Talents:** painting, acting, voice acting, music production (not instrumental
  performance), content creation, broad creative range
- **Self-description:** "very creative but also very analytical"; loves art, music and
  science
- **Income goal:** full-time income within 2 years
- **Location:** remote, or willing to relocate
- **Visibility:** prefers behind-the-scenes work, where the work is seen rather than her
- **Work style:** mostly alone; working alongside one other person is fine
- **Track record:** 50+ paintings, local stage plays, many Instagram reels, photography for
  social media. **Has been paid** for paintings and for social media content creation.
- **Training:** art lessons; an online Instagram coaching course
- **Equipment:** art supplies, microphone, laptop, recording equipment, violin
- **Does not want:** promoting herself on social media; cold calling
- **Work structure:** open; compare employment, freelance/client work and own brand
- **Involvement:** Erica is on board and participating (see `INTAKE.md`)

### Profile tensions to design around

- **Prescriptive briefs stress her** ("boxes me in") but **fear shows up as
  procrastination**. Correction to the initial assumption: the answer is not more imposed
  structure. It is autonomy over _how_ with some external anchor for _when_.
- **Painting is both where she loses track of time and draining.** That is a signal that
  it may work better as a protected practice than as the sole income source.
- **No self-promotion, no cold calling.** Paths must get work through employers, agents,
  representatives, platforms or catalogs, not personal marketing.
- Unknowns (she answered "I don't know"): weekly hours available, which feedback is hard
  to hear, what an ideal workday looks like. These are worth finding out by trying things
  rather than answering in the abstract.

### Executive summary

The most realistic path to full-time income within 2 years is **a job with a steady
paycheck, plus building toward one main career**, not a creative business built on her
own audience. The evidence is consistent: most working artists earn mainly from contract
work or jobs, not from selling their own work. Income from Patreon, Etsy and
print-on-demand goes mostly to a small top tier. Catalog income from music libraries and
licensing takes 5–8 years to build (`work-structure.md` §1).

The strongest long-term fits for someone who is "creative but also analytical" and loves
art, music and science are **science and data visual communication** and **audio
post-production plus audiobook narration**. Both are mostly solitary and remote-friendly,
reachable through employers or rosters instead of self-promotion, and more resilient to
AI than most creative work.

### Step 2: Work structure

Recommendation (inference): **a steady salaried base plus a gatekeeper.**

1. **Years 0–2: a 25–40 hour/week salaried (W-2) base**, through a creative staffing agency
   (Creative Circle, Aquent/Vitamin T), where recruiters bring roles to her, or a remote or
   part-time in-house role defined by goals rather than detailed specs.
2. **Alongside it, a portfolio or catalog that someone else sells**: a library, a
   licensing agency, narrator rosters. Later, one agent (10–35% commission) once she has a
   track record. This keeps a third party between her and the market, so she never cold
   calls or self-promotes.
3. **Brief style matters more than job title.** Look for or negotiate _outcome briefs_
   (the goal, audience, mood and deadline) that leave the method to her. Creativity
   research consistently finds people do best when someone else sets the goal and they
   control the method (Amabile; `work-structure.md` §3).
4. **Health insurance tilts toward a salaried job.** Enhanced ACA subsidies lapsed in 2026.
   A single person earning above about $62.6K gets no subsidy, so employer coverage is
   worth more than the pay rate suggests. **This is policy-volatile; re-check it before
   relying on it.**

### Step 3: Psychology. Context, not clinical advice

Evidence strength is labeled in `psychology.md`. The main implications (inference):

- **Procrastination is mostly short-term mood repair, not laziness.** She avoids the bad
  feeling a task triggers (Sirois & Pychyl). The fix is to make starting feel less
  threatening, not to add pressure. If-then plans ("if it's 10am, I open the project
  file") have strong evidence (d = .65 across 94 tests). Self-forgiveness after a slip
  predicted less procrastination next time.
- **Let her choose the structure; don't impose it.** Controlling deadlines and expected
  evaluation tend to undermine creativity. Structure she chooses herself (her own process
  goals, with a flexible direction) supports it. This explains her intake answer that
  briefs "box me in".
- **Keep making and judging separate.** Feedback should be private, specific, asked for by
  her, and from 1–2 trusted people. Public exposure and selling should move up a gradual
  ladder at a pace she sets.
- **Small wins are the strongest motivator.** A brief daily log of what moved forward is
  the most direct evidence-based tool (Amabile & Kramer).
- **Protect painting.** Painting is both absorbing and draining, which fits the risk of a
  passion turning obsessive once it becomes income (Vallerand). Keeping some work
  non-commercial lowers burnout risk.
- **Therapy can run in parallel; it isn't a requirement of the plan.** If she wants it,
  Compassion-Focused Therapy or ACT fit the inner-critic and procrastination pattern.
  Trauma-focused CBT or EMDR fit if a clinician finds trauma symptoms. IFS is popular, but
  its evidence is thin. Low-cost options: Open Path ($40–70 a session), HRSA sliding-scale
  centers, training clinics.
- **For Marcus: be a secure base.** Be available and encouraging, offer mostly invisible
  practical support, don't evaluate her work, and keep accountability only in forms she
  designs. Visible help works best when she asks for it.
- **Caveat:** most procrastination research uses students. Treat the plan as an
  experiment and review it monthly.

### What the new facts changed

- **Data visualization is not an entry point.** None of the 8 sampled postings were
  entry-level; most required a bachelor's or 4+ years of experience. It moves from first
  job to a longer-term direction.
- **Scientific illustration and conservation are closed for now.** Staff
  science-illustration postings mostly require graduate degrees, and the CSU Monterey Bay
  certificate requires a bachelor's to enroll. Collections technician jobs are mostly
  gated by a bachelor's or hands-on experience.
- **Content and multimedia jobs at health systems and universities are the open door.**
  8 of the 9 sampled US postings accepted an associate's degree or "equivalent
  experience," or listed no degree. These employers usually offer benefits, and her paid
  social-content work counts as experience.
- **Radiologic technology is a career pivot, not quick money.** She would first need
  about two semesters of science prerequisites. Admission is selective (Portland CC
  takes 38 of 350+ applicants), followed by a full-time, daytime program of 22–24
  months. Her first rad-tech paycheck would come around 2029–2030. Her business
  associate's should satisfy the certifying board's (ARRT) degree requirement, provided
  her school is regionally accredited. Median pay is $80,110 (BLS, May 2025).
- **Faster healthcare jobs exist.** Patient access/registration hires within weeks and
  uses her business degree. Sterile processing takes about 4 months of training ($2–4K),
  involves no patient contact, and usually comes with hospital benefits.

### First 90 days

Erica sets the pace, and the weekly hours are her choice. Each step is deliberately
small.

**Weeks 1–3: get ready to apply**

- One résumé that frames her paid content work as professional experience.
- One portfolio of 8–12 pieces (paintings, content work, a voice sample), with no photo
  work and nothing showing her on camera.
- Register with Aquent/Vitamin T, and ask them directly about health coverage.
- Make a short list of target employers: health systems, universities, science museums
  and science nonprofits, in her current area plus 1–2 places she'd consider moving to.

**Weeks 2–10: apply, and run the two experiments**

- Aim for a few applications a week, set by her as an if-then plan (e.g., "If it's
  Tuesday after lunch, I send one application").
- Run one creative experiment at a time, about 2 weeks each, with a few hours a week of
  creative work.
- Keep a short daily log with three entries: what moved forward, whether she lost track
  of time, and whether it drained her.

**Day 60 checkpoint**

- If there are no interviews, adjust the résumé and widen the search.
- If money is tight, start applying for patient access roles, or enroll in a sterile
  processing course, in parallel.

**Day 90 review, together, in a format she chooses**

- Which experiment did she enjoy more? Pick one long-term direction for the next 6
  months.
- Is the base job working, or is it on track?
- Adjust the plan; it is meant to change.

### Guardrails from the psychology research

- **Procrastination is a cue, not a failure.** It usually means the next step is too big
  or too exposed. Make the step smaller; don't add pressure. Self-forgiveness after a
  slip predicts less procrastination next time.
- **Feedback on her creative work** comes only when she asks for it, and only from one or
  two people she picks.
- **Marcus's role:** practical support (logistics, reviewing applications if she asks),
  encouragement, and no evaluating her art. Accountability only in forms she designs.
- **Therapy is optional, not a condition of the plan.** If she wants it,
  Compassion-Focused Therapy or ACT fit this picture. Employer insurance from a Track 1
  job would make therapy cheaper.
