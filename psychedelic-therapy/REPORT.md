# Psychedelic Therapy

> Status: draft · Started: 2026-08-04 · Last updated: 2026-08-04

## Research Question

What does the evidence actually support for psychedelic-assisted therapy across PTSD,
treatment-resistant depression, alcohol/substance use, and anxiety/end-of-life distress;
what routes to treatment legally exist today; what the practice and market look like;
and which medication combinations are dangerous, merely blunting, or fine.

**This is a landscape review, not medical advice.** Nothing here substitutes for a
prescriber who knows the full medication list and history. The interaction section in
particular describes what the literature reports, not what any individual should do.

---

## Bottom Line

1. **Only one thing in this category is FDA-approved and available today: esketamine
   (Spravato) and off-label ketamine.** Everything else — psilocybin, MDMA — is
   available in the US exclusively through a clinical trial or a state-legal
   supervised-use program in Oregon or Colorado. There is no legal medical psilocybin
   or MDMA anywhere in Louisiana.

2. **The evidence is real but weaker than the public conversation suggests.** The
   single most important methodological finding is that placebo groups in psilocybin
   trials improve _far less_ than placebo groups in SSRI or esketamine trials — 0.50
   vs 1.00 vs 1.12 standardized mean change on MADRS ([Hieronymus et al., _JAMA Network
   Open_, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41520839/)). Participants can tell
   whether they got the drug, and disappointment appears to depress the control arm.
   That inflates the measured advantage. A living systematic review rates the evidence
   for serotonergic psychedelics in depression **"very low certainty," with 83.3% of
   trials at high risk of bias** ([Højlund et al., _European Neuropsychopharmacology_,
   2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41205366/)).

3. **MDMA for PTSD is the strongest case, and the FDA still rejected it.** Two Phase 3
   trials hit their endpoints with large effects. The FDA rejected the 2024 New Drug
   Application and required an additional Phase 3 trial. Approval is years out, if it
   comes.

4. **The medication question has a counterintuitive answer.** The dangerous combinations
   are narrower than most people assume (MAOIs, and lithium's seizure signal); the
   _common_ problem is different — SSRIs and SNRIs blunt psilocybin's effects, and
   that blunting appears to **persist 3–6 months after stopping the drug**
   ([Gukasyan et al., _J Psychopharmacology_,
   2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37291890/)). This is a planning problem, not
   just a safety one.

5. **Two Phase 3 trials are recruiting in New Orleans** — see [Access Routes](#5-access-routes--what-actually-exists).
   That is the nearest legal path to a classic psychedelic. Caveat: primary completion
   is listed as **August 2026**, so enrollment may already be closed. Verify first.

## 1. The Methodology Problem (read this before the efficacy numbers)

Every effect size below should be read through three defects that the field's own
researchers now openly flag:

**Functional unblinding.** Psychedelics are unmistakable. In trials, participants and
therapists both correctly guess assignment at high rates. A 2026 _Molecular Psychiatry_
review of MDMA's neurocognitive profile states the problem plainly: MDMA's distinctive
effects "complicate trial design by compromising blinding and inflating expectancy,
necessitating their quantitative assessments"
([PMID 41951837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41951837/)).

**Depressed control arms.** Hieronymus et al. compared control-group outcomes across
17 trials — 4 psilocybin (n=373), 11 SSRI (n=4,014), 2 esketamine (n=573). Control
participants in psilocybin trials improved substantially less than controls in SSRI
or esketamine trials; response rates ran 14 percentage points lower than SSRI controls
and 23 points lower than esketamine controls. Their conclusion is that psilocybin's
antidepressant efficacy "might be overestimated." A drug looks better when the
comparison group is doing worse than comparison groups normally do.

**Therapy is bundled with drug.** Nearly every trial delivers the compound inside a
multi-session psychotherapy protocol. Almost none isolate the drug's contribution.
A 2026 analysis of 7 RCTs (522 participants) found effect sizes moved with
_methodological_ choices — bodyweight-adjusted dosing, longer preparation and
integration, non-manualized psychotherapy — not just with the drug
([Syed et al., _Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews_,
2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41587629/)).

None of this means the treatments don't work. It means the honest confidence interval
is wider than headlines imply, and the therapy component may be carrying more weight
than the molecule.

## 2. Clinical Evidence by Indication

### PTSD — MDMA-assisted therapy

The best-evidenced psychedelic indication, and the one that got closest to approval.

|                             | **MAPP1** (2021)                              | **MAPP2** (2023)                     |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Journal                     | _Nature Medicine_                             | _Nature Medicine_                    |
| Participants                | 90                                            | 104 (53 MDMA / 51 placebo)           |
| Severity                    | Severe PTSD                                   | 73.1% severe, 26.9% moderate         |
| CAPS-5 change (MDMA)        | −24.4 (SD 11.6)                               | −23.7 (95% CI −26.94 to −20.44)      |
| CAPS-5 change (placebo)     | −13.9 (SD 11.5)                               | −14.8 (95% CI −18.28 to −11.28)      |
| Effect size                 | d = 0.91                                      | d = 0.7                              |
| Functional impairment (SDS) | d = 0.43, P=0.0116                            | d = 0.4, P=0.03                      |
| Outcome                     | P < 0.0001                                    | ~71% no longer met PTSD criteria     |
| Severe adverse events       | No abuse-potential, suicidality, or QT signal | 9.4% MDMA vs 3.9% placebo; no deaths |

Sources: [MAPP1, PMID 33972795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33972795/) ·
[MAPP2, PMID 37709999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37709999/)

**Regulatory reality.** FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation in 2017. The FDA
**rejected the New Drug Application in 2024 and required an additional Phase 3 trial.**
The living systematic review rates MDMA for PTSD at **moderate certainty** — the highest
grade any psychedelic indication earns in that review, and still short of approval.

A mediation analysis of 82 patients found the effect ran through _self-compassion_:
changes in compassionate and uncompassionate self-responding fully mediated MDMA-AT's
effect on PTSD and depressive symptoms — though notably **not** on substance use
outcomes ([_European Journal of Psychotraumatology_,
2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40331914/)). Worth knowing for the PTSD+AUD
comorbid case: the mechanism that helps PTSD may not carry to the drinking.

### Treatment-resistant depression — psilocybin

Two meta-analyses published within a year reach materially different conclusions,
which is itself the finding.

**The optimistic read** ([Kishi et al., _Journal of Affective Disorders_,
2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41876058/)) — 6 RCTs, standard 25 mg dose:

- Depression severity: **SMD −1.05** (95% CI −1.60 to −0.50) — a large effect
- Response at 2–3 weeks: **RR 2.34** (95% CI 1.52–3.60)
- Remission at 2–3 weeks: **RR 3.38** (95% CI 1.88–6.08)
- Sustained response at 6–12 weeks: **RR 2.61**
- Adverse effects: nausea (RR 10.20) and headache (RR 2.06), resolving within 1–9 days
- Authors' own caveat: "considerable methodological heterogeneity across current trials"

**The skeptical read** ([Højlund et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41205366/))
— 30 RCTs, 1,480 participants:

- Psilocybin/ayahuasca/LSD in MDD: **SMD −0.62** (95% CI −0.97 to −0.28), I² = 55%
- **Certainty of evidence: very low**
- **83.3% of trials at high risk of bias**
- No difference in all-cause discontinuation between active and control

The gap between SMD −1.05 and −0.62 is the difference between "transformative" and
"comparable to existing treatments." Both are defensible from the same literature.

### Alcohol and substance use

Weaker and earlier than the depression or PTSD literature — mostly Phase 2, with the
first Phase 3 appearing outside the US.

| [NCT06349083](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06349083) | Psilocybin               | 2     | Alcohol use disorder         | NYU Langone           |

The Iowa trial is unusual and worth watching — it puts psilocybin head-to-head against
ketamine rather than placebo, which is exactly the comparison the field has avoided.

### Anxiety and end-of-life distress

The oldest modern literature (Griffiths and Ross, 2016) and still among the more
durable-looking signals, but small. Højlund et al. graded psychedelics for anxiety
disorders at **very low certainty**, same as depression. Active work is concentrated
in oncology settings — MD Anderson is running two Phase 2 trials in cancer patients
([NCT06801041](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06801041),
[NCT06200155](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06200155)).

### Ketamine and esketamine — the approved comparator

Worth taking seriously rather than treating as the consolation prize. It is the only
option here that is legal, prescribable, and sometimes covered by insurance today.

Real-world community clinic data, n=63 ([Bellanti et al., _Frontiers in Psychiatry_,
2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501650/)):

|                | Intranasal esketamine (n=37) | IV ketamine (n=26)         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Baseline PHQ-9 | ~18                          | ~18                        |
| Mean reduction | 10.31 ± 5.59 (**d = 1.84**)  | 9.50 ± 5.69 (**d = 1.67**) |
| Response rate  | 64.9%                        | 69.2%                      |
| Remission      | 32.4%                        | 23.1%                      |
| Completion     | >90%                         | >90%                       |

No significant difference between routes. Note those effect sizes are _uncontrolled_
— not comparable to the controlled SMDs above.

Other findings:

- A 16-week Australia/NZ cohort (n=84) found a **33.3% response rate**; responders
  averaged 71.2% HAMD-17 improvement, with quality-of-life gains of 29.5% and work
  productivity gains up to 35.9% ([_J Psychiatric Research_,
  2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42494735/)). The gap between 33% response and
  65% above reflects how much cohort selection drives these numbers.
- For acute suicidality, a Spanish prospective cohort (n=62) reported **NNT = 1.35**
  for reducing high suicide risk versus alternative pharmacotherapy
  ([_Clinics and Practice_, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42346193/)). If
  suicidality is in the picture, this is the fastest-acting evidenced option.
- European expert consensus (30 psychiatrists, ≥80% agreement) recommends maximizing
  to **84 mg weekly** during a 4–12 week acute phase, 6–9 months continuation, and
  ≥12 months maintenance depending on relapse risk
  ([_European Neuropsychopharmacology_, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42468081/)).

## 3. Medication Interactions

The safety core. Derived primarily from the systematic review by
[Sarparast, Thomas, Malcolm & Stauffer, _Psychopharmacology_,
2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35253070/) — 163 search terms across 22
medication classes and 135 drugs, yielding 40 publications (26 RCTs, 3 epidemiologic
studies, 11 case reports) — supplemented by pharmacokinetic and survey work.

### Hard contraindications

| Combination                        | Problem                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MAOI + MDMA**                    | Serotonin syndrome; **documented fatality with moclobemide**. This is the unambiguous do-not-combine.                                                                  |
| **MAOI + psilocybin**              | Psilocin is partly metabolized by monoamine oxidase A; MAOI inhibition raises exposure unpredictably.                                                                  |
| **Lithium + classic psychedelics** | **Seizures** reported. The evidence base is user reports and case series rather than trials, but the signal is consistent enough that trials exclude lithium outright. |

### Attenuating — not dangerous, but they blunt the effect

This is the most practically consequential section, and the least discussed.

| Class              | Finding                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SSRIs**          | Reduce psilocybin effects — probability 0.47 of weakened response. Also attenuate MDMA; citalopram reduces MDMA's acute cardiovascular effects; paroxetine inhibits immune effects. |
| **SNRIs**          | Comparable attenuation, probability 0.55. Duloxetine inhibits MDMA effects in vivo.                                                                                                 |
| **Bupropion**      | **Minimal interaction — 0.29.** Non-serotonergic mechanism appears protective. The notable exception.                                                                               |
| **Antipsychotics** | Haloperidol reduces MDMA subjective effects. Broadly, 5-HT2A antagonists blunt classic psychedelics — that is the receptor being targeted.                                          |

**The washout finding that changes planning.** Gukasyan et al. (2023) surveyed people
who had discontinued antidepressants before taking psilocybin. Attenuation
**persisted 3–6 months after stopping** the SSRI/SNRI. Fluoxetine's long half-life
does not explain it — the authors point to receptor-level adaptation. Practical
implication: "stop the SSRI two weeks before" — the standard trial washout — may be
insufficient to restore full response. Anyone planning around this should assume a
months-long timeline, not a weeks-long one, and weigh that against the relapse risk
of a long unmedicated stretch.

### Cardiovascular and other agents

Multiple adrenergic drugs modulate MDMA's physiological effects: carvedilol blocks
thermogenic and cardiostimulant effects, clonidine modulates pharmacodynamics,
reboxetine reduces stimulant effects. MDMA raises blood pressure and heart rate;
uncontrolled hypertension and arrhythmia are standard trial exclusions.

### Pharmacokinetics worth knowing

Psilocybin is a prodrug, rapidly dephosphorylated to psilocin
([Meshkat et al., _Pharmaceutics_, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40284409/),
14 studies, 112 healthy humans):

- Tmax 1.8–4 hours oral · half-life 1.5–4 hours
- Oral bioavailability 52.7 ± 20%
- Volume of distribution 277–1,016 L (extensive)
- **Metabolized by CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and MAO-A** — so CYP2D6 polymorphism and any
  strong CYP3A4 inhibitor or inducer will shift exposure. The review explicitly flags
  "potential for drug-drug interactions."

### Benzodiazepines, stimulants, cannabis

Thinly evidenced. Benzodiazepines are widely reported to dampen or truncate the
experience (they are the standard rescue medication in trials, which tells you the
direction). Stimulant interactions with MDMA are pharmacologically plausible —
additive sympathomimetic load — but poorly characterized in controlled work.
Moderate-to-severe cannabis use disorder is a standard trial exclusion. Absence of
documented interaction here reflects absence of study, not demonstrated safety.

## 4. Psychiatric exclusions that apply regardless of medication

Consistent across essentially every protocol reviewed:

- Schizophrenia spectrum or other psychotic disorders
- **Bipolar I history** — and, in the Cybin Phase 3 protocol, a **first-degree family
  history** of schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, or bipolar I is disqualifying
- Current borderline personality disorder
- Significant liver disease, moderate-to-severe kidney disease, uncontrolled
  hypertension, cardiac arrhythmia

The family-history exclusion is stricter than most people expect and is worth checking
early, because it is disqualifying for trials regardless of everything else.

## 5. Access Routes — What Actually Exists

### Available in Louisiana today

**Ketamine and esketamine only.** Spravato (esketamine) is FDA-approved for
treatment-resistant depression, prescribed through certified REMS providers, and
sometimes insurance-covered. IV ketamine is off-label, typically cash-pay, and
delivered through freestanding clinics. There is no legal medical psilocybin or MDMA
in Louisiana, and no state program pending that this review identified.

### Clinical trials — including two in New Orleans

**The nearest legal access to a classic psychedelic compound.** Two Phase 3 trials of
**CYB003** (a deuterated psilocin analog, sponsored by Cybin) for major depressive
disorder list New Orleans sites:

- **[NCT06564818](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06564818)** and
  **[NCT06793397](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06793397)**
- Sites: **Atlas Psychiatric** and **DelRicht Research**, both New Orleans
- Contact: **877-361-4003** / **clinicaltrialsinfo@cybin.com**
- Started 17 Dec 2024; **primary completion listed as August 2026** — enrollment may
  be closed, verify before anything else

**Counterintuitive eligibility detail:** these trials _require_ participants to be on
a **stable dose of a single antidepressant with inadequate response** (<50%
improvement). Being on an SSRI is an entry criterion, not a disqualifier. But MAOIs,
tricyclics, mirtazapine, trazodone, moclobemide, and buspirone require 14+ day
discontinuation; St. John's Wort within 14 days and ketamine within 6 months are
exclusionary; and >10 lifetime psychedelic exposures or any use within 12 months
disqualifies.

Nationally, **37 psilocybin trials** and roughly two dozen MDMA trials are recruiting.
No MDMA trials were found in Louisiana or any neighboring state.

### VA pathway

The VA is now sponsoring its own trial, not merely permitting outside research:

- **[NCT07118839](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07118839)** — MDMA-Assisted
  Therapy for Veterans with PTSD and Alcohol Use Disorder, **Phase 2/3, sponsored by
  the VA Office of Research and Development**, recruiting from 18 May 2026
- Sites: **VA Connecticut Healthcare System (West Haven)** and **Providence VA Medical
  Center (RI)** — travel required from Louisiana
- Requires current VA enrollment, PTSD ≥6 months, AUD in past 3 months, prior
  evidence-based treatment, ability to abstain from alcohol 48 hours without medical
  detox, BMI 18–35
- **Excludes current antidepressants or antipsychotics** — a much harder bar than the
  Cybin trials
- Contacts: Erica.Eaton@va.gov · christy.capone@va.gov

Other VA-adjacent work: [NCT05790239](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05790239)
(MDMA for veterans, CA), [NCT06418178](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06418178)
(Bronx VA, dose optimization), [NCT06853912](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06853912)
(psilocybin for military veterans with PTSD+AUD, WA).

### State-legal supervised use — Oregon and Colorado

Not medical treatment. These are _supervised use_ programs: no diagnosis required, no
prescriber, no insurance, and no treatment claim. Cash-pay and travel-only from
Louisiana.

**Oregon** operates under Ballot Measure 109 / ORS 475A. Applications opened 2 January
2023; service centers began seeing clients in summer 2023. A July 2026 program news
item indicates **licensing fees may double** for service centers and facilitators —
a cost-pressure signal worth tracking, since it flows through to session pricing.

**Colorado** operates healing centers under the Natural Medicine Health Act.

_I could not retrieve current participation, cost, or adverse-event figures for either
program — see [Gaps](#7-gaps-and-what-i-could-not-verify)._

## 6. Practice and Market

**The category is bifurcating.** One half — ketamine/esketamine — is a real, operating,
reimbursable clinical business today. The other half — psilocybin and MDMA — is
pre-revenue in medicine and exists commercially only as Oregon/Colorado cash-pay
supervised use.

**Economics of the ketamine half.** Spravato runs through a REMS-certified setting with
required post-dose monitoring, which sets a floor on facility cost per session and
makes throughput the binding constraint. IV ketamine clinics are cash-pay and
competitive. The Australia/NZ cohort's 33.3% response rate is a more sober planning
number than the 65% from a self-selected community clinic sample; a business model
built on the higher number is exposed.

**Regulatory risk in the psychedelic half is not theoretical.** The FDA rejected the
MDMA NDA in 2024 despite two positive Phase 3 trials — the failure was not efficacy
but trial conduct and evidence quality. Every company in this space now carries the
knowledge that clean endpoints are not sufficient.

**The therapy component is the unresolved commercial question.** If effect is
substantially carried by preparation, session support, and integration — as the
methodological moderator analysis suggests — then the cost structure is therapist
hours, not drug units, and the margin profile looks like a clinic, not a pharma
product. Compounding this: non-manualized psychotherapy was associated with _larger_
effects, which is precisely the hardest thing to standardize, scale, or reimburse.

**Facilitator supply** is the practical constraint in Oregon and Colorado. Both built
new credentialing regimes from scratch, and rising licensing fees suggest the
programs are not yet self-sustaining at current volume.

## 7. Gaps and What I Could Not Verify

Stated plainly, because a research report that hides its holes is worse than a short one.

- **This session's web search budget was exhausted (200/200) before research began.**
  Everything above came from direct fetches of primary sources — PubMed E-utilities
  and the clinicaltrials.gov API — which worked well, but I could not run open-ended
  searches to discover sources I didn't already know to look for.
- **COMPASS Pathways' Phase 3 psilocybin results (COMP005/COMP006) are not included.**
  Their news and investor pages returned only navigation shells to the fetcher. This
  is a real gap — COMP360 is the leading psilocybin program and its Phase 3 readout
  is the most consequential recent event in the field.
- **Lykos Therapeutics' status past May 2025 is unverified.** Their newsroom surfaced
  only a Series B financing announcement (30 May 2025) and a note that the expanded
  access program for up to 50 PTSD patients is fully enrolled and closed. Whether a
  new Phase 3 has begun, I could not confirm.
- **Oregon and Colorado program numbers are missing.** Oregon publishes data through
  embedded Power BI dashboards that don't render to a text fetcher; the 2024 annual
  report PDF 404'd; Colorado's regulator returned 403.
- **Ibogaine is not covered.** Excluded deliberately — it carries genuine
  cardiac mortality risk (QT prolongation, documented deaths) that puts it in a
  different safety class, and Texas's state-funded ibogaine initiative is a distinct
  topic. Flagging it because it will come up in any search on this subject.
- **Cost figures throughout are absent** rather than estimated. I found no
  authoritative per-session pricing for Oregon/Colorado services or ketamine clinics
  in this pass and declined to manufacture numbers.

**Highest-value next pass:** COMPASS COMP005/COMP006 results, Lykos current
regulatory status, and Oregon/Colorado program data — all three need either a search
budget or the Power BI/investor endpoints fetched directly.
