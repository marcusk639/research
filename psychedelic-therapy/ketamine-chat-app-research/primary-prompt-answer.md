## 1. Bottom Line

**A — ACUTE STATE (crisis/activation at the moment of dosing).** Conditional and split by outcome. For acute suicidal ideation specifically (S1), IV racemic ketamine (R1, D3) reduces suicidal ideation faster and further than active placebo (midazolam) over hours to a few days — this is **Established for the ideation surrogate**, but no trial is powered for suicide attempts or deaths, the increment shrinks or vanishes by ~1 week, and in the esketamine crisis trials (ASPIRE I/II) the drug did not separate from placebo on the suicidality-specific endpoint once intensive standard-of-care (hospitalization + optimized antidepressants) was added to both arms. For acute activation, agitation, panic, or hyperarousal (S2) as a reason to proceed, the answer is that this is largely **Unstudied as a moderator** — trials in suicidal patients tested crisis as an indication, not activation as a predictor of response, and the drug itself is frequently anxiogenic and dissociating during the first 40–60 minutes (T1), which is a genuine cost for someone already panicking. High baseline anxiety in stable depressed outpatients does not appear to reduce response (and may modestly increase it), but that is a different question from dosing someone mid-crisis. **Acute state is therefore a reason for caution and monitoring intensity, not a green light.**

**B — CIRCUMSTANTIAL CONTEXT (unstable external life: housing, finances, legal, relationships, loss; S4).** **Unstudied directly**, and the direction of inference is unfavorable for durability. The pivotal trials systematically excluded the unstable — active substance use disorder, unstable housing, inability to attend a monitored series, and active suicidality with intent were common exclusions. No RCT randomized "life instability" as a moderator. The mechanistic worry is real: if ketamine opens a neuroplasticity window, the relapse literature shows benefit fades within days-to-weeks off maintenance, and what maintains response in trials is continued dosing plus a continued oral antidepressant in a stable, monitored setting — not a single dose returning someone to an unchanged adverse environment. **The highest-risk cell in this entire report is unsupervised at-home sublingual/oral ketamine (R4) during life instability (S4)** — see Red Flags.

**C — DOSE AND POPULATION.** The three curves are not the same shape, and they differ by population. For depression in TRD (P3), the best dose-ranging RCT (Fava 2020) suggests a **threshold around 0.5 mg/kg IV** with 1.0 mg/kg also separating from placebo and doses below 0.5 mg/kg largely not — roughly monotonic-to-threshold, not a clean linear "more is better." For anxiety (P2 refractory anxiety disorders), the only dose-ranging work (Glue) shows a **steeper, clearly dose-dependent curve** (minimal at 0.25, progressively greater and more durable at 0.5–1.0 mg/kg) — but from tiny samples. For suicidality, the effect rides largely on the depression/mood response and is briefer. Critically: **in healthy volunteers (P1) the same subanesthetic doses point the opposite direction** — they are a model of psychosis and anxiety induction, not mood improvement. And for bipolar II (P4) the entire positive evidence base was generated in patients maintained on therapeutic lithium or valproate, so **it does not transfer to unmedicated bipolar II**. Mixed features / agitated depression of uncertain polarity (P5) is essentially unstudied — excluded from nearly everything. The dose × population matrix below is mostly empty, and that emptiness is the finding.

## 2. Findings (items 1–10)

### 1. Acute suicidal crisis (S1)

**Established** (for the ideation surrogate, hours–days); **Unstudied** (for attempts/deaths).
Tags: S1 · R1/R3 · D3 · P3/P4/P6 · T2–T4.

The racemic IV signal is real and rapid. **Grunebaum 2018** (n=80 inpatients, moderately severe MDD with clinically significant suicidal ideation, mean Scale for Suicidal Ideation ≈15; single IV ketamine 0.5 mg/kg vs midazolam 0.02 mg/kg; ongoing antidepressants continued, benzodiazepines barred): ketamine produced a greater reduction in SSI at day 1 with effect size d ≈ 0.75; benefit was partly independent of but also partly mediated by mood improvement, and effects extended in the context of optimized pharmacotherapy. **Price 2014** (IV ketamine vs midazolam) reported an adjusted d ≈ 0.82 on suicidal ideation.

The single most decision-relevant racemic trial for a crisis presentation is **Abbar 2022** (BMJ; "KETIS," NCT02299440): 156 patients hospitalized for severe suicidal ideation, randomized to two 40-minute infusions of IV ketamine 0.5 mg/kg (n=83) or saline placebo (n=73) at baseline and 24 hours, on top of usual treatment. Primary outcome — full remission of suicidal ideation (SSI ≤3) at day 3: **63.0% (46/83) ketamine vs 31.6% (25/73) placebo, OR 3.7 (95% CI 1.9–7.3), p<0.001** [nih]. Side effects were limited; no manic or psychotic symptoms were seen [nih]. Crucially, the effect was **moderated by diagnosis**: the bipolar subgroup drove it — OR 14.1 (95% CI 3.0–92.2), p<0.001 (~85% remission), while the depressive-disorder subgroup did not separate — OR 1.3 (95% CI 0.3–5.2), p=0.6 [ResearchGate]. By week 6, remission was high in both arms and no longer significantly different (69.5% vs 56.3%; OR 0.8, 95% CI 0.3–2.5, p=0.7) [nih]. A mediating effect of mental pain was found. (Note: the BMJ abstract's arm-labeling sentence has a known swap; the correct arm sizes, per the Results, are ketamine n=83, placebo n=73.)

**Two hard limits.** First, **surrogate outcomes**: every trial measures ideation scales (SSI, C-SSRS, MADRS item 10) over minutes-to-days, not attempts or deaths. No ketamine or esketamine trial is powered for suicidal behavior or mortality. "63% in remission of suicidal ideas at day 3" is not "prevents suicide." Second, **durability**: **Su 2023** (TRD + prominent suicidal ideation, n=84, ketamine 0.5 mg/kg vs midazolam) found the antidepressant effect persisted to 14 days but the antisuicidal effect (C-SSRS, MADRS item 10) persisted only to **day 5** [Oxford Academic]. Meta-analysis (**Shah 2026**, 10 RCTs, n=649) confirms ketamine > midazolam short-term (MADRS-SI −1.23, 95% CI −2.14 to −0.32; BSS −4.30, 95% CI −8.01 to −0.59) with more nausea, derealization, dizziness [Wiley Online Library].

**Esketamine crisis trials (ASPIRE I & II), the industry-sponsored counterweight (Janssen/J&J).** Both randomized adults 18–64 with MDD and active suicidal ideation with intent to esketamine 84 mg vs placebo nasal spray twice weekly for 4 weeks, each on top of "comprehensive standard of care" = hospitalization ≥5 days plus newly initiated/optimized oral antidepressants (ASPIRE II n=230) [PubMed]. Both met the primary endpoint (MADRS at 24h) [Johnson & Johnson]: mean MADRS reduction at 24h was 16.4 (esketamine+SOC) vs 12.8 (placebo+SOC) in ASPIRE I and 15.7 vs 12.4 in ASPIRE II (both p=0.006 for the between-group primary; treatment differences ≈ −3.8 and −3.9 points). **But the key secondary endpoint — the suicidality-specific measure (CGI-SS-r) at 24h — did not separate from placebo.** Verbatim from the SPRAVATO label summary of these trials: _"In both studies, treatment with esketamine did not demonstrate superiority compared to placebo nasal spray in improving CGI-SS-r."_ Janssen's own stated interpretation is that the null _"may be due to the substantial beneficial effects of comprehensive SOC utilised in the clinical trial, including the impact of inpatient psychiatric hospitalisation in diffusing the acute suicidal crisis."_ **Interpretation:** when you give everyone intensive crisis care, esketamine adds a measurable antidepressant increment but **no demonstrable specific anti-suicidality increment beyond hospitalization + antidepressant.** This is the crisis-indication tautology made visible in the control arm.

**Head-to-head vs ECT:** ELEKT-D (**Anand 2023**, NEJM; n=403, open-label, non-psychotic TRD, ~89% outpatient) found IV ketamine 0.5 mg/kg non-inferior to ECT on response (QIDS-SR ≥50%). But ELEKT-D excluded psychotic depression, was open-label, and a competing trial (KetECT, hospitalized patients) found ECT superior. A 2024 meta-analysis (**Menon et al.**, _Psychiatry Research_) found ketamine's non-inferiority _"was not shown in the overall population,"_ and _"among inpatients, ECT was superior to ketamine in response rate, remission rate, and change in depression scores,"_ while _"ketamine led to better posttreatment cognition scores than ECT."_ ECT retains the stronger evidence for the most severe/inpatient and psychotic presentations; ketamine's advantage is cognitive tolerability.

### 2. Activation as a moderator (S2)

**Unstudied** as a true moderator; **Suggestive** (and mixed) for the adjacent "anxious depression" construct.
Tags: S2/S0 · R1 · D3 · P2/P3 · T2–T4.

**There is no trial that randomized acutely agitated/panicked/hyperaroused patients** to test whether that state predicts better or worse antidepressant/anxiolytic response. What exists is the anxious-depression subtype literature in stable outpatients, and it is genuinely mixed:

- **Ionescu 2014** (post-hoc, n=26 TRD, single 0.5 mg/kg): anxious-depressed patients (HAM-D anxiety/somatization factor ≥7) responded as well or better than non-anxious, with longer time to relapse.
- **Salloum 2019** (secondary analysis of Fava's 5-arm RCT, n=99): no significant interaction between anxious/non-anxious status and ketamine-vs-midazolam response at days 1 or 3 — i.e., response was similar regardless of baseline anxiety. **This is the strongest design (randomized, active-placebo-controlled) and it found no moderation.**
- Naturalistic clinic data (n=43): both groups improved; anxiety symptoms improved about half as much as depression symptoms.

The honest answer to whether an activated/panicking state is a reason to proceed: **the literature does not address it.** Do not import the S1 crisis-indication evidence here — those trials tested crisis as an indication for the drug, not activation as a predictor of response. If anything, the T1 acute-experience data (below) suggest high pre-dose arousal could make the infusion itself more aversive. This would be tested by a trial stratifying on pre-dose arousal/agitation with a state-anxiety moderator analysis; none exists.

### 3. The session itself (T1), by dose band

**Established** that the acute experience is frequently anxiogenic/dissociative and hemodynamically active; **Contested** whether dissociation predicts later antidepressant response.
Tags: S0 · R1/R3 · D2–D4 · P1/P3 · T1.

At subanesthetic doses the acute (0–60 min) experience commonly includes **dissociation, perceptual disturbance, anxiety, dizziness, nausea, transient blood-pressure and heart-rate rises, and emergence phenomena.** Glue's dose-ranging work shows these are **dose-dependent** — minimal at 0.25 mg/kg, progressively greater at 0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg — and correlated with ketamine plasma levels. The FDA SPRAVATO label reports that **48% to 61% of esketamine-treated patients developed sedation** (MOAA/S) and **0.3% to 0.4% experienced loss of consciousness**, and requires ≥2 hours of monitoring at every session; increases in blood pressure are an explicit warning. This aversive acute state is a real cost, is largest at the higher doses, and is **the most decision-relevant fact for someone who is already panicking** — the drug can transiently amplify the very state the person is in crisis over, even if 24-hour outcomes are good.

**Does intra-infusion dissociation predict antidepressant response?** This is **Contested, and the stronger designs say no.** The original correlational finding (Luckenbaugh 2014; extended by Niciu 2018 to n=126, CADSS subscales) suggested derealization weakly predicted response — but only at a single timepoint in one sub-study. The best synthesis (Ballard/Zarate group, _Nature Communications_ 2021, "The role of dissociation in ketamine's antidepressant effects") concludes: _"the current literature does not support"_ that dissociation is necessary for antidepressant efficacy. Treat the "you need to dissociate for it to work" claim as **not established** — it is a contested correlation, not a mechanism, and it is sometimes used to justify pushing doses into a more aversive range.

### 4. Dose-response: three separate curves

**Supported** (depression, threshold ~0.5 mg/kg IV); **Suggestive** (anxiety, steeper/dose-dependent, tiny n); **Suggestive-and-briefer** (suicidality).
Tags: R1 · D1–D4 · P2/P3 · T2–T4.

**Depression (P3).** The anchor is **Fava 2020** (_Mol Psychiatry_; 5-arm, n=99 TRD, single IV infusion, adjunctive): ketamine 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0 mg/kg vs midazolam 0.045 mg/kg. **0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg were superior to active placebo; the lower doses (0.1, 0.2) largely were not.** The 0.5 mg/kg dose beat placebo on secondary outcomes mostly at day 1 (MADRS at day 3). This suggests a **threshold around 0.5 mg/kg** rather than a steep linear dose-response, with little added benefit — and more side effects — from going higher. **Su 2017** (Taiwanese TRD, 0.2 vs 0.5 mg/kg vs placebo) is broadly consistent with a threshold effect. This is why the field defends 0.5 mg/kg as the reference dose: higher doses buy dissociation without clearly buying efficacy.

**Anxiety (P2).** The only true dose-ranging data are **Glue 2017/2020** (treatment-refractory GAD/SAD, not currently depressed, ascending subcutaneous 0.25/0.5/1.0 mg/kg with midazolam control): a clear dose-response — minimal anxiolysis at 0.25, progressively greater and more durable at 0.5–1.0 mg/kg. In the initial 2017 report (12 refractory GAD/SAD patients, all with SAD), _"10 out of 12 responded with reduced anxiety reported within 1 hour of drug administration"_ and _"Eight of 12 patients (67%) had a >50% reduction in anxiety rating scales at 2 hours post-treatment"_; anxiolysis lasted up to a week. But these are **very small studies (n=12, then a psychoactive-controlled replication) from one group**, and the senior author discloses a contract with Douglas Pharmaceuticals to develop ketamine formulations and a Janssen advisory role — **flag this.** The anxiety curve looks steeper than the depression curve, but the evidence is far thinner.

**Suicidality.** No clean multi-dose ranging trial isolates a suicidality dose-response; the anti-suicidal effect largely tracks the mood effect at 0.5 mg/kg and is briefer (Su: to day 5).

**Route ≠ dose.** These mg/kg figures are IV racemic. Per the FDA SPRAVATO label, intranasal esketamine _"mean absolute bioavailability is approximately 48% following nasal spray administration"_; oral/sublingual is far lower and dominated by first-pass conversion to norketamine. A milligram number is not transferable across routes, and where sources don't support a conversion I have not estimated one.

### 5. Bipolar type 2 (P4) and mixed/agitated depression (P5)

**Supported** (bipolar depression, adjunctive to lithium/valproate); **Unstudied** (unmedicated bipolar II; P5 mixed features).
Tags: R1 · D3 · P4/P5 · T1–T4.

The foundational trials are **Diazgranados 2010** (n=18, treatment-resistant bipolar depression, crossover) and **Zarate 2012** (replication, n=15). Both showed rapid antidepressant response to a single 0.5 mg/kg IV infusion, significant from ~40 min through day 3; Zarate 2012 also showed reduced MADRS/HDRS/BDI suicide items. **Critical, usually-buried fact: in both trials patients were required to be maintained on therapeutic-level lithium or valproate.** The add-on design means the efficacy and safety findings **do not transfer to unmedicated bipolar II patients** — a point directly relevant to the user. Most of these trials also excluded comorbid psychosis, active substance use, and serious medical illness.

**Manic/hypomanic switch.** Reported switch rates were low in the controlled trials, but the trials were **tiny and short — not powered or long enough to detect switch reliably.** Diazgranados reported one manic-symptom case in the ketamine arm and one in the saline arm (of 18). A real-world series (Fancy et al.) described 3 of 66 bipolar patients developing hypomania after the third or fourth infusion (reported figure, taken from a review's secondary description). **Absence of a strong switch signal in small short trials is not evidence of safety in unmedicated or repeatedly-dosed bipolar II.** Notably, the mood-stabilizer requirement itself may be part of why switch rates looked low. Reassuringly, Abbar 2022 (larger, mixed-diagnosis) reported _"no manic or psychotic symptom was seen"_ — but again on top of usual treatment and over a short window.

**P5 — mixed features / agitated depression of uncertain polarity.** As the user anticipated: **this population has been essentially excluded from the RCT base.** Trials of bipolar/depression treatments routinely exclude mixed states, and ketamine trials are no exception. There is **no adequate controlled evidence** on ketamine in DSM-5 mixed-features depression or agitated depression of uncertain polarity. What follows: any use in a person who might be in a mixed/activated state is **inference beyond the evidence**, and the theoretical concern (a pro-glutamatergic, activating agent in an already-activated mixed state) is exactly the scenario nobody has safely characterized. This would be tested by a trial enrolling DSM-5 mixed-features patients with switch/agitation as pre-specified safety endpoints; it has not been done.

### 6. Who was actually studied (exclusion criteria)

**Established:** the pivotal base excluded most of the circumstances and several of the populations the user is asking about.
Tags: S0 (not S1/S4) · P3 (not P2/P4/P5).

The esketamine TRD program (TRANSFORM-1/2/3, SUSTAIN-1) enrolled adults with TRD (≥2 failed antidepressants), generally excluding **bipolar disorder, psychotic features, active substance use disorder, and recent/active suicidal ideation with intent** (the ASPIRE trials were the separate, carefully hospitalized exception). Racemic trials (Fava, Grunebaum, Su) similarly required stable outpatients or hospitalized-but-monitored patients, barred or restricted benzodiazepines and active substance use, and required ability to attend a monitored infusion series. Representative verbatim exclusion language from mood-disorder trials of this era includes _"have an alcohol or substance use disorder within the last 3 months,"_ _"active suicidal ideation (score of ≥4 on item 10 of MADRS),"_ _"at significant risk of harm to themselves or others,"_ and diagnosis of _"Bipolar I or II disorder."_

| Circumstance / population                         | Studied in pivotal ketamine/esketamine RCTs?                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Active substance use disorder                     | Excluded (typically within 3–12 months)                                                                     |
| Unstable housing / life instability (S4)          | Not enrolled / not analyzed as a moderator                                                                  |
| Active suicidality with intent                    | Excluded from standard TRD trials; included only in ASPIRE, and only with mandatory hospitalization ≥5 days |
| Bipolar II diagnosis                              | Excluded from MDD/esketamine trials; studied only in separate small trials on lithium/valproate             |
| Psychotic features                                | Excluded (ELEKT-D and most others)                                                                          |
| Concurrent benzodiazepines                        | Restricted/barred in several trials (Grunebaum barred them); shown to attenuate response                    |
| Mixed features / agitated uncertain polarity (P5) | Excluded / unstudied                                                                                        |
| Inability to attend a monitored series            | Excluded                                                                                                    |

**Straight answer:** people resembling **S4 (life instability)**, and populations **P2 (ordinary non-TRD depression/anxiety)**, **P4 (unmedicated bipolar II)**, and **P5 (mixed/agitated)** were largely **not studied**. The evidence base was built on stable, monitored, medically-screened TRD outpatients (or hospitalized suicidal patients receiving intensive concurrent care). **This is the most important single fact in this report: the trials do not describe the person the user is asking about.**

### 7. Healthy volunteers (P1)

**Established:** in people without a psychiatric diagnosis, subanesthetic ketamine is a model of psychosis and anxiety, **not** a mood-improver.
Tags: S0 · R1 · D2–D3 · P1 · T1.

The foundational study is **Krystal 1994** (n=19 healthy volunteers, IV 0.1 and 0.5 mg/kg over 40 min): ketamine produced **dose-dependent psychotomimetic, perceptual, and cognitive effects resembling aspects of endogenous psychosis**, plus (at 0.5 mg/kg) a trend increase in BPRS anxiety around 20 minutes. Decades of successor work use subanesthetic ketamine precisely because it reliably induces transient psychosis-like and dissociative states in healthy people. **This points the opposite direction from the patient literature.** The implication: ketamine's mood benefit is not a general "feel-better" drug effect — it appears to **depend on the presence of a depressed/pathological baseline state**. Popular accounts omit this asymmetry; it is central to understanding why "it helped my friend" does not generalize, and why the acute experience can be frankly unpleasant/frightening in someone not depressed (or in the wrong state).

### 8. Durability under ongoing stress

**Established:** single-dose benefit is transient; maintenance dosing + continued oral antidepressant prolongs it. **Unstudied/Suggestive:** ongoing-adversity as a moderator.
Tags: S4 · R1/R3 · D3 · P3 · T4.

A single infusion's antidepressant effect is short-lived: per the Janssen SUSTAIN protocol, _"on average, the duration of response from a single IV dose of ketamine is approximately 5 to 7 days"_; **Mathai et al. 2022** similarly report effects _"lasting approximately three to seven days"_ in unipolar and bipolar depression. What maintains it in the trial world is **continued treatment in a stable setting**: **SUSTAIN-1** (esketamine randomized-withdrawal, n=297) found continuing esketamine + oral antidepressant cut relapse risk vs switching to placebo — among stable remitters, relapse **26.7% vs 45.3% (NNT 6)**; among stable responders, **25.8% vs 57.6% (NNT 4)**; HR 0.49 and 0.30 respectively. **SUSTAIN-3** (open-label extension) suggests maintenance can be sustained for months-to-years in those who tolerate/respond. Note these are **remitter/responder-enriched samples** — group statistics from people already selected for having done well.

The mechanistic question the user raised directly: **if ketamine opens a plasticity window, what happens when it closes onto an unchanged adverse environment?** There is **no RCT that manipulated the post-dose environment.** The relevant indirect evidence:

- **KARMA-Dep 2** (Jelovac 2025, _JAMA Psychiatry_; n=65 hospitalized MDD/bipolar): serial adjunctive ketamine 0.5 mg/kg was **NOT superior** to midazolam [Psychology Today] (MADRS adjusted mean difference −3.16, 95% CI −8.54 to 2.22, p=0.25) — a recent, rigorous **negative** inpatient trial. Most raters/patients correctly guessed allocation (unblinding).
- **Childhood maltreatment as a moderator is Contested:** O'Brien/Lijffijt 2019 (naturalistic clinic, n=115/63) found more childhood maltreatment predicted **better** ketamine response (opposite to conventional antidepressants); but **Johnson 2025** (_Acta Psychiatr Scand_) **failed to replicate** — no difference by trauma load. Stronger claim (better response with trauma) rests on naturalistic clinic data; the failed replication is also naturalistic — genuinely unresolved.

**Bottom line for the user's B-question:** durability depends on structured continuation. A single dose (or an unsupervised at-home series) delivered into ongoing instability has **no evidence base for durable benefit**, and the theoretical concern is that the window closes onto the same stressors.

### 9. Contraindications and screening for an activated presentation

**Established** (label/REMS); **Supported** (benzodiazepine attenuation); **Suggestive→Unstudied** (capacity in acute dissociation).
Tags: S1/S2 · R3 · D3 · P4/P5.

- **FDA SPRAVATO label — contraindications (verbatim):** _"Hypersensitivity to esketamine, ketamine, or any of the excipients"_; and it is _"contraindicated in patients for whom an increase in BP or intracranial pressure poses a serious risk (e.g., aneurysmal vascular disease, arteriovenous malformation, history"_ [of intracerebral hemorrhage].
- **Boxed warning:** risk of **sedation, dissociation, respiratory depression** (48–61% developed sedation; 0.3–0.4% loss of consciousness); **potential for abuse/misuse** (Schedule III); increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric/young-adult patients.
- **REMS (verbatim requirements):** SPRAVATO _"must be administered under the direct observation of a healthcare provider,"_ patients _"must be monitored by a healthcare provider for at least 2 hours after administration,"_ patients must be enrolled in the REMS, and it is dispensed/administered only in certified healthcare settings — **it cannot be dispensed for use outside the certified setting.**
- **Emerging mania read as agitation / psychosis history / uncontrolled hypertension:** all are screening priorities directly relevant to an activated presentation and to bipolar II. The blood-pressure warning and the psychotomimetic profile (healthy-volunteer data) make uncontrolled hypertension and psychosis history particular concerns.
- **Benzodiazepines blunt the effect (Supported):** multiple analyses show concurrent benzodiazepines attenuate/shorten ketamine's antidepressant effect. **Andrashko 2020** found significantly worse outcomes at days 3 and 7 in patients on **>8 mg diazepam-equivalent** (ROC-derived threshold; sensitivity 80%, specificity 85%). **Feeney 2023** (RAPID study secondary analysis): higher benzodiazepine dose attenuated the day-1 (not day-3) effect. This matters because **people in crisis are disproportionately on benzodiazepines.**
- **Capacity/informed consent** for an acutely suicidal person consenting to a dissociative drug: I found **no dedicated empirical literature — Unstudied**; flag as an ethical screening point, not an evidence-based one.

### 10. Compared to what (the actual crisis alternatives)

**Established** framing; comparative certainty varies. Tags: S1 · P3/P6.

| Option                                 | Onset                 | Evidence quality                                                                                              | Cost to the person                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crisis stabilization + safety planning | Immediate             | Standard of care; the ASPIRE control arm shows it drives much of the observed improvement                     | Low physical risk; time, possible loss of autonomy                                          |
| Inpatient admission                    | Immediate containment | Standard of care for imminent risk                                                                            | Disruption, cost, autonomy; but definitive monitoring                                       |
| ECT                                    | Days–2 weeks          | Strong; superior to ketamine for inpatients/psychotic depression (KetECT, 2024 meta-analysis)                 | Anesthesia, transient cognitive/memory effects, stigma, access                              |
| IV racemic ketamine (0.5 mg/kg)        | Hours                 | Non-inferior to ECT in outpatient non-psychotic TRD (ELEKT-D); rapid anti-SI (Grunebaum, Abbar) but transient | Dissociation, BP rise, monitoring, cost (often out-of-pocket), no durable-benefit guarantee |
| Esketamine (ASPIRE)                    | 24 h (mood)           | Adds antidepressant increment to SOC; **no significant specific anti-suicidality increment over SOC**         | REMS burden, ≥2 h monitoring, cost, twice-weekly visits                                     |

| Antipsychotics (for agitation)         | Minutes–hours         | Symptomatic; some (quetiapine) reduce SI in bipolar in trials                                                 | Metabolic/EPS effects                                                                       |

Without this table the only supportable conclusion would be "better than nothing," which is not the question. Against the real alternatives, **ketamine's distinctive claim is speed in specific monitored settings** — not superiority in durability or safety, and not a demonstrated reduction in suicidal behavior.

> **FIREWALL paragraph** (read once, do not let it inform any antidepressant/anxiolytic claim above). Sedation-dose ketamine for prehospital/ED agitation — roughly >1.0 mg/kg IV or 4–5 mg/kg IM (D5) — is a **different drug at a different dose for a different purpose** (rapid chemical restraint/procedural sedation). It carries its own risks (laryngospasm, intubation, hemodynamic events) and says **nothing** about ketamine's antidepressant or anxiolytic effects. News coverage routinely conflates "ketamine calmed an agitated person" (sedation) with "ketamine treats anxiety" (a subanesthetic mood effect). They are unrelated. **No claim in sections 1–10 rests on D5 data.**

**OCD note** (the user has OCD). **Rodriguez 2013** (n=15 unmedicated OCD, randomized crossover, IV 0.5 mg/kg vs saline): rapid reduction in obsessions; at 1 week **50% of ketamine recipients met response (≥35% Y-BOCS reduction) vs 0% placebo** [nature] — but the effect was **transient** and the trial tiny; subsequent work (CBT augmentation, memantine) explicitly sought ways to extend the short-lived effect. OCD evidence for ketamine is **Suggestive at best and not durable**, and was generated in unmedicated patients with near-constant obsessions.

## 4. Red Flags

_Where the literature/guidelines say stabilize something else first, or don't proceed._

- **Unmedicated bipolar II / possible bipolar spectrum — the polarity question.** The entire positive bipolar evidence base (Diazgranados, Zarate) was generated on therapeutic lithium or valproate. Using ketamine in **unmedicated** bipolar II is **outside the evidence**, and switch data come from trials too small/short to detect switch. **Polarity must be actively screened before, not after.** CANMAT/ISBD treat mood-stabilizer coverage as central to bipolar depression management; ketamine/esketamine sit at second-line-or-later, with caution.
- **Mixed features / agitated depression of uncertain polarity (P5):** **no evidence base at all.** A pro-glutamatergic, activating agent in an activated mixed state is precisely the untested, theoretically-concerning scenario.
- **R4 × S4 — the single highest-risk combination in this report.** Unsupervised at-home sublingual/oral ketamine during a period of life instability. This combines: (a) a route with **no monitoring** for sedation/dissociation/BP; (b) an environment the durability evidence says will **erode benefit**; (c) a **Schedule III** drug with abuse/diversion potential; (d) **variable compounded dosing.** The FDA (**October 10, 2023 safety communication**) warned that at-home compounded ketamine _"presents additional risks because a health care provider is not available onsite to monitor for serious adverse outcomes resulting from sedation and dissociation,"_ [Harris Sliwoski] cited a documented respiratory-depression adverse event from at-home oral ketamine (blood level ~twice the anesthetic level), and stated it **has not established safe or effective dosing of ketamine for any psychiatric indication** [harris-sliwoski]. Documented harms include a published overdose/anesthesia-level blood-concentration case and a wrongful-death allegation against a major telehealth provider (unadjudicated) [Statnews]. **Regardless of any efficacy data, this cell should be treated as do-not-proceed without direct supervision.**
- **Concurrent benzodiazepines (>~8 mg diazepam-equivalent):** attenuate/shorten the antidepressant effect — a problem precisely in crisis-prone, anxious patients who are often on them.
- **Uncontrolled hypertension / vascular risk:** FDA contraindication where BP/intracranial-pressure rise poses serious risk.
- **Psychosis history:** the healthy-volunteer literature shows ketamine reliably induces psychotomimetic states; psychosis history is a screening priority.
- **Acute suicidal intent + consenting to a dissociative drug:** capacity/informed-consent in this exact situation is essentially **unstudied**; monitored settings and independent capacity assessment are the conservative posture.
- **Inpatient/psychotic-severity depression:** ECT has the stronger evidence (KetECT, 2024 meta-analysis); ketamine's non-inferiority (ELEKT-D) was in **outpatient, non-psychotic** TRD.

## 5. What Would Change This Answer

- A properly **powered trial of suicidal behavior/mortality** (not ideation scales) would move the S1 answer from surrogate to outcome. **None exists.**
- A **moderator trial stratifying on pre-dose activation/agitation/state-anxiety** would answer the S2 question (currently Unstudied).
- **RCTs in unmedicated bipolar II, and in DSM-5 mixed features**, with switch as a pre-specified endpoint, would populate the blank P4-unmedicated and P5 cells.
- A trial **manipulating the post-dose psychosocial environment / maintenance context** would test the "window closes onto unchanged adversity" hypothesis directly.

**What a prescriber needs to know about the user that this report cannot supply:** current medications (especially benzodiazepines, lithium/valproate status); whether the working diagnosis is truly bipolar II vs a mixed/uncertain-polarity picture; blood pressure and cardiovascular/vascular history; substance-use history; current suicidal ideation/intent and capacity; housing/financial/legal stability at the time of any proposed dosing; and prior response to standard treatments.

**The report describes populations; the prescriber assesses the individual.** Group response rates, NNTs, and remission percentages are **group statistics** — they do not predict what will happen to one person; an NNT of 4–6 means several people are treated for each one who benefits on that metric.

## 6. Questions for My Prescriber

1. Given my bipolar II diagnosis, which specific evidence are you relying on — and are you aware that the Diazgranados/Zarate trials **required** patients to be on therapeutic lithium or valproate? Would you treat me only under mood-stabilizer cover? _(→ Finding 5)_
2. How will you screen for **mixed features / activation** before dosing, given that mixed/agitated presentations were excluded from essentially all ketamine trials? _(→ Findings 5, 6)_
3. What is your monitoring plan for a **hypomanic/manic switch**, and over what timeframe — given the trials were too small/short to quantify switch risk? _(→ Finding 5)_
4. Which **dose and route** are you proposing, and what is your rationale — do you accept the Fava 2020 finding that 0.5 mg/kg is a threshold and higher doses add dissociation without clear added benefit? _(→ Finding 4)_
5. If you propose a non-IV route, what **bioavailability assumption** are you using (esketamine intranasal is ~48%; oral/sublingual much lower), and how does that change effective exposure vs the 0.5 mg/kg IV reference? _(→ Finding 4, "Route ≠ dose")_
6. Am I currently on any **benzodiazepine**, and if so, will that attenuate the effect — should it be addressed first (noting the >8 mg diazepam-equivalent threshold)? _(→ Finding 9)_
7. Given that ketamine can be **anxiogenic and dissociative during the infusion (T1)**, what is the plan if I become more panicked during dosing, and does my current activation level argue for waiting? _(→ Findings 2, 3)_
8. For suicidal ideation specifically: what evidence is there that this reduces **behavior** rather than just ideation scores over a few days, and what happens after day 5–7 when the anti-suicidal effect fades? _(→ Finding 1)_
9. In the ASPIRE trials the suicidality-specific endpoint **did not beat placebo** once hospitalization + antidepressants were added — what does that imply for what ketamine adds to good crisis care in my case? _(→ Finding 1)_
10. What is the **maintenance/continuation plan**, and what does the durability evidence (single-dose effect ~5–7 days; SUSTAIN-1 NNT 4–6) say happens if I stop? _(→ Finding 8)_
11. Given my life circumstances, how do you think about **durability when the plasticity window closes onto ongoing stressors** — is there evidence this holds up outside a stable, monitored trial setting? _(→ Findings 6, 8)_
12. How does this compare, for me specifically, to **ECT** (stronger for severe/inpatient) and to intensive crisis stabilization — and why ketamine over those? _(→ Finding 10)_
13. What are your views on **at-home/telehealth sublingual ketamine**, given the FDA's October 2023 warning and my situation — and would you consider that appropriate for me? _(→ Red Flags)_
14. What **blood pressure / cardiovascular and psychosis screening** will you do given the label contraindications and the healthy-volunteer psychotomimetic data? _(→ Findings 7, 9)_
15. For my **OCD and complex PTSD** specifically — what durable evidence exists, given the OCD effect in Rodriguez 2013 was transient and the trials tiny? _(→ Finding 10, OCD note)_

## 7. Self-Audit

**Five most load-bearing numeric claims, re-checked against primary sources:**

1. **Abbar 2022** — 63.0% vs 31.6% full SI remission at day 3, OR 3.7 (95% CI 1.9–7.3); bipolar subgroup OR 14.1 (95% CI 3.0–92.2); week-6 difference n.s. → **Confirmed** verbatim from the BMJ abstract (via PMC open access). Population = hospitalized severe suicidal ideation; dose/route = two IV 0.5 mg/kg infusions vs saline; timepoint = day 3 primary, week 6 durability. Note the abstract's arm-size sentence has a known label-swap quirk (correct: ketamine n=83, placebo n=73), stated in-text.
2. **Fava 2020** — 0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg superior to midazolam; 0.1/0.2 largely not; n=99, 5 arms. → **Confirmed** from the _Molecular Psychiatry_ paper/PMC. Population = adjunctive TRD outpatients; route IV; timepoints days 1/3. Correctly characterized as threshold, not steep linear.
3. **SUSTAIN-1** — relapse 26.7% vs 45.3% (stable remitters, NNT 6) and 25.8% vs 57.6% (stable responders, NNT 4); HR 0.49 / 0.30. → **Confirmed** from the _JAMA Psychiatry_ (Daly 2019) abstract and corroborating reviews. Population = esketamine responders/remitters, TRD; route intranasal; randomized-withdrawal maintenance.
4. **ASPIRE I/II** — primary MADRS endpoint met (24h MADRS reduction 16.4 vs 12.8 [I], 15.7 vs 12.4 [II], both p=0.006); CGI-SS-r suicidality endpoint **NOT** significant vs placebo+SOC. → **Confirmed** from the ASPIRE I (Fu 2020, _J Clin Psychiatry_) and ASPIRE II (Ionescu 2021, _Int J Neuropsychopharmacol_ 24:22) records plus the FDA label summary (_"did not demonstrate superiority… in improving CGI-SS-r"_); SOC = hospitalization ≥5 days + optimized antidepressant. This is the crux of the crisis-tautology point and it holds.
5. **Grunebaum 2018** — n=80, ketamine 0.5 mg/kg vs midazolam, SI reduction d≈0.75 at day 1; benzodiazepines barred. → **Confirmed** from the _Am J Psychiatry_ abstract/PubMed and multiple corroborating protocols citing the 0.75 effect size.

**A sixth I flag as confirmed but limitation-laden:** KARMA-Dep 2 (2025) MADRS adjusted mean difference −3.16 (95% CI −8.54 to 2.22, p=0.25), n=65 → **confirmed** (_JAMA Psychiatry_ 2025;82(12):1216–1224/PMC) — a genuine recent **negative** inpatient trial with documented unblinding.

**Claims I could NOT fully verify to primary source and have therefore hedged or labeled accordingly:** the Glue anxiety dose-response rests on very small single-group samples with a disclosed industry conflict (labeled Suggestive, conflict flagged; the "10/12 responded, 8/12 >50% reduction at 2h" figures are from the 2017 report as quoted); the childhood-maltreatment moderator is naturalistic on both sides and left Contested/unresolved; the manic-switch real-world rate (3/66, Fancy) is from a review's secondary description, not re-checked at source, so I stated it as reported. The capacity/consent-in-acute-suicidality question returned no dedicated empirical literature — left explicitly **Unstudied** rather than backfilled.

**Which of A/B/C is least well answered, and why:** **B (circumstantial instability, S4)** is the least well answered — because it is the least studied. There is no trial that randomized or even prospectively stratified on external life instability; the entire answer is inference from exclusion criteria and durability data. I have marked it Unstudied and resisted converting the mechanistic worry into a false-confident claim. **A** is well answered for the ideation surrogate but limited by the total absence of behavior/mortality-powered trials; **C** is well answered for depression (P3) and partially for anxiety (P2) but blank for P5 and for unmedicated P4.
